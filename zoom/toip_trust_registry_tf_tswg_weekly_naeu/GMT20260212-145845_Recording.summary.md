### Standardizing Trust Registry Systems

The group discussed the need for a standardized trust registry system, with Scott emphasizing the importance of conformity and consistency, while Darrell suggested partnering with COG to develop this guidance since the Treasury Task Force may not be the appropriate venue. Carly proposed creating an instructional "how-to" document for setting up trust registries, which would serve as an educational resource. The discussion also touched on the IPTC's existing trust registry for COG validation, with Will noting Charlie Halford's involvement in pushing for its development.

### Trust Registry Protocol Adaptation

The group discussed metadata specifications and trust registry protocols, with Darrell and Scott agreeing to continue working on adapting existing standards rather than reinventing them. Scott explained that the EU's EITIS protocol, while over-engineered, provides a useful model for cross-border trust recognition, though COG would not need to address national ID verification aspects. The discussion concluded with Scott expressing a desire to transition away from Mozilla's trust registry for SMI and content signing signatures, aiming to replace it with IRA by the end of next year.

### Domain Expertise for Trust Registry

Tim expressed his need to thoroughly understand the domain and business requirements before providing feedback on the Trust Registry Query Protocol and Creator Assertions working group. He emphasized the importance of keeping options open and being intellectually honest in his assessment. Scott suggested that Tim join the DIF task force to contribute his expertise in solving the identified problems, highlighting the need for diverse perspectives in addressing user concerns.

### Metadata Trust and Community Control

The discussion focused on the challenges of regulating metadata tag issuance and managing trust registries. Tim expressed confidence in the technological mechanisms but emphasized the importance of ensuring that solutions meet community needs without centralized authority control. He highlighted the need to identify hidden dependencies that could cause issues in the future, drawing parallels with certificate authority challenges. The conversation underscored the importance of allowing communities to define their needs independently of technological infrastructure.

### Trust Registry Governance and Standards

The group discussed trust registry management and governance, with Scott explaining that while there needs to be a structured standard for interoperability, the actual data content can be distributed. Tim raised concerns about geopolitical risks of decertification and emphasized the importance of having clear governance principles, while Scott shared his experience with X509 and CA browsers, noting that while CAs are not going away, they are focused on their own means rather than public trust.

### Hybrid Credentials Standardization Discussion

Scott and Darrell discussed the emergence of hybrid verifiable credentials and the importance of a standardized approach, like the CA approach, which provides a recipe for creating credentials. They emphasized the need for interested parties to expose their systems of record and build on this framework. Scott highlighted the urgency of developing signing capabilities, as some COG members are facing desperate situations due to a lack of signing capacity. They also touched on the distinction between signing capabilities and verifiable credentials, with Scott expressing a desire to disrupt the current system by separating signing capabilities from identifiers.
