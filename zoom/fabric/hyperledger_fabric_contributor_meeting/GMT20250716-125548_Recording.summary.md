## Details

### Technical Setup and Meeting Preparation

The meeting began with Yacov checking audio and video connections with James and Tatsuya. After a brief delay, Jim offered to share his screen, which Yacov confirmed was working. The group prepared to start the discussion, with Yacov mentioning he would paste a link in the chat for reference.

### Hyperledger Fabric Version Updates

Yacov provided an update on Hyperledger Fabric, noting that version 2.5 is mature and stable, while version 3.1 was recently released with critical bug fixes. He encouraged users to upgrade to the latest version and mentioned that new Fabric X repositories have been open-sourced. Yacov also highlighted that while IBM still maintains about half of the Fabric project, the number of IBM maintainers has decreased over time.

### FabricX vs Firefly Architecture Discussion

Yacov clarified that the IBM-developed code for FabricX is still in early development and advised against using it for commercial purposes, though it is legally available under the Apache license for learning and testing. Jim noted the differences between FabricX and Firefly, highlighting Firefly's extensive pluggable architecture with interfaces for various services, which Jim found more flexible than Fabric's current design. Yacov acknowledged some limitations in Fabric's pluggability but mentioned features like pluggable endorsement and validation, suggesting that while some aspects of Fabric are not fully pluggable, others are quite flexible.

### Hyperledger Fabric Identity Limitations

Jim and Yacov discussed the limitations of the Hyperledger Fabric platform, particularly its lack of pluggable identity management options. Jim highlighted that while Fabric is well-architected as an L1 platform, its non-interface-based design restricts the ability to swap out certain features, unlike Firefly, which offers more flexibility. Jim also explained his work with Trustgrid on a decentralized SSI (Self-Sovereign Identity) system, emphasizing their goal to enable offline transactions and a more decentralized identity framework.

### Platform Performance and Scaling Discussion

Jim and Yacov discussed the performance capabilities of different platforms, focusing on scaling and transaction processing. Jim argued that decentralized databases can achieve higher TPS than DLTs, while Yacov suggested looking at Fabric X for scaling DPS. They clarified the distinction between distributed and decentralized systems, with Jim emphasizing the importance of offline transactions and the concept of separation of commit and finalization in a network.

### Ethereum Scalability and Fabric Integration

Jim discussed the limitations of Ethereum's architecture, particularly its slow performance and built-in gas fees, and highlighted how platforms like Arbitrum have worked around these constraints. He expressed interest in exploring alternative architectures that offer better scalability and flexibility, such as integrating distributed databases with Fabric to handle high-throughput payment applications. Yacov suggested that Jim should open an issue on the FabricX GitHub repository to propose plugable authentication and share his use case, which Jim agreed was the appropriate channel for feedback.

### Hyperledger Fabric Kubernetes Setup Discussion

Karthikeyan discussed the setup of a chain code running on a Kubernetes system with a multi-peer configuration, using Hyperledger Fabric 2.5.5 and a Fabric Gateway client. He explained the option to configure one or three peers from the client side and inquired about how the distribution occurs when multiple peers are configured. Yacov, who has experience with the node and peer aspects but not the client side, suggested tagging a specific individual in the Discord group for further clarification on the Fabric Gateway client's behavior.
