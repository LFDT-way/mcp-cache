### Cryptographic Protocols and Zero-Knowledge Proofs

Mitchell clarified that the positions discussed were aligned with existing drafting agreements from the root registry rather than introducing new protocols. He also shared an update about the Ethereum Foundation moving away from Poseidon hashes to SNARKs, which Denis noted may not be post-quantum resistant. Drummond warned that the task force would need to find ways to explain complex cryptographic concepts to non-experts, suggesting analogies and clear explanations would be important for building confidence in the work. Denis compared zero-knowledge proofs to smart contracts, explaining they function like trusted code that can be verified.

### Zero-Knowledge Proofs and Requirements

The group discussed zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) and circuits, with Denis explaining that circuits are essentially programs or code, while Mitchell emphasized making the AI-first approach to handle the complex mathematical aspects. The conversation then shifted to reviewing Sankarshan's V0.4.0 requirements draft, which establishes three key documents: requirements (semantic source of truth), decisions (operationalizes requirements), and registers (implementation view). The team needs to decide whether to adopt the entire draft as official V0.4 or implement only the semantic core upstream while keeping the detailed implementation in a separate guide.

### ZKP Circuit Version 0.4.0 Progress

The team agreed to proceed with version 0.4.0 of the ZKP circuit, with Mitchell confirming it should be promoted to draft status due to incorporating feedback from previous discussions. Denis presented a comprehensive implementation of probabilistic sampling that includes proof of credential connection, issuer verification, and trust registry validation, demonstrating it works in 6 seconds on a real device for 29 selected credentials from thousands. The team discussed connecting with Berkeley's Hart team, with Drummond agreeing to facilitate an introduction through Sanjim on Signal to coordinate their participation in future meetings.
