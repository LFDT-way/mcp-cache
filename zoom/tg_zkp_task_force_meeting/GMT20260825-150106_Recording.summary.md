## Details

### ZKPs Development Progress Meeting

The team met to discuss ZKPs, with Scott leading the meeting and taking notes. They reviewed recent developments including the merging of version 0.4 of the requirements document and updates from the Credentials Task Force call. The group discussed the need to create a prioritized list of required proofs and agreed to store this information in a shared repository, while also noting that Glenn posted a strong delegation design note and the Berkeley team is scheduled to connect on September 8th.

### ZK Task Force Post Viewer

Mitchell shared a system he developed called the ZK Task Force Post Viewer, which uses a protocol to summarize and respond to discussion posts. He demonstrated how the system generates responses to specific issues, including one regarding Glenn's thread and another about circuits for signed documents. Mitchell also mentioned updates on cryptography trends, noting a shift from lattice-based to hash-based PQC, and expressed interest in continuing to use and improve the HTML documentation format for summarizing work.

### ZKP Implementation Strategy Meeting

The team agreed to stop assuming ZKP fixes issues wholesale and instead focus on issuing real ZKPs for real credentials using OpenVCT. Drummond suggested creating a repository for the ZKP spec to maintain provenance, which the team agreed to set up without immediate pressure on timing. Glenn will deliver a first real ZKP with tight constraints for OpenVCC creation and presentation by tomorrow, and the team will develop a prioritized proofs list with real-world utility to target for ZKP production, including liveness and biometrics proofs.

### Berkeley Team Scheduling Discussion

The team discussed scheduling a call with the Berkeley team for September 8th, with Mitchell noting there may be timing changes depending on Sanjam's response. Drummond explained the process for scheduling meetings through support@lfdcentralizetrust.org with 24-48 hours notice. The group also discussed construction selection inputs and Mitchell mentioned exploring ZKP tooling differences in a future recipe document, noting that ZKP hasn't had its circuit verified since 2024 and has different construction methods than ZKIT.

### ZKKit Implementation Discussion

The team discussed using ZKKit, an open-source repository for compiling ZKP circuits, as an alternative to agent-written scripts. Mitchell agreed that referencing verified human-written versions of circuits should be part of the recipe book. Drummond inquired about the provenance and maintenance of ZKKit, to which Denis responded that while it may not be widely used, it would be better than creating custom scripts from scratch. The discussion concluded with a brief mention of backlog items and design time decisions from the CROS task force work.

### House of No Names Design

The team discussed delegation topics and a concept called the "House of No Names" as a potential design approach that balances transparency and privacy. Mitchell explained that verifiers would perform no live lookups under this approach, which would represent a boundary for the House of No Names design. The discussion also touched on the evolution of work moving from writing to reading activities, and Mitchell mentioned a Git discussion system that could be implemented early on. The conversation ended with an update on Dennis's probabilistic sampling prototype, though no new information was provided about its current status.

### Credential Verification System Technical Details

Denis presented technical details about a credential verification system, explaining that approximately 29 credentials need to be sampled from around 4,000 total credentials to prove trust with 95% confidence using a 10% threshold. Mitchell suggested incorporating these percentages and proof timing metrics into documentation to help explain trade-off choices to stakeholders making governance decisions about trust graph composition. Mitchell also discussed the concept of interleaving and visibility ratios, which allows users to control how much of a hash is visible in different relationship contexts, from fully public to private affairs.

### GDC Presentations and Geneva Meetup

The team discussed upcoming GDC presentations and a meetup in Geneva. Mitchell explained his concept of 70% public responsibility in relationships using a proverb example, while Drummond clarified details about the Geneva meetup scheduled for Monday afternoon from 1-5 PM. Mitchell shared information about two GDC workshops he will host on September 2nd and 3rd, including one on post-quantum cryptography with a Google Quantum researcher and another on wallet security. The team confirmed they would not hold their regular call next week due to travel schedules, with Drummond leaving for Europe on Friday night and Scott arriving Monday at 11 AM.
