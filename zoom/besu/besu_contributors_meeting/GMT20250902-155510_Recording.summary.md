## Details

### Baysuit Team Monthly Contributor Call

The monthly contributor call for the Baysuit team began with Justin providing housekeeping updates, including an antitrust notice and meeting recording information. Miguel introduced himself as a new team member from Alastria and mentioned he would present about the P.2.5.6 issue later in the meeting. Justin suggested allocating the latter part of the call to discuss P.2.5.6, allowing the team to focus on this specific topic after addressing other minor news items.

### Fusaka Upgrade and Project Updates

The meeting covered updates on the Fusaka upgrade, which is likely to be delayed to early December, with no current concerns on the El side. Discussions included the removal of proof of work, with Matthew noting that while it's still on the agenda, there's no strong opinion on supporting Click, and it's not a priority. Justin mentioned that the Glamsterdam planning is progressing well, with headliners selected and exterior contributions for Bal in progress. The team also discussed the open Project Charter PR, urging maintainers to approve or raise issues, and Matthew mentioned working on archive state proofs with Karim. The conversation ended with a brief mention of the upcoming dev connecting in Argentina, where Justin noted there might not be a significant presence from the consensus team.

### Alastria: Spain's Blockchain Ecosystem

Miguel introduced Alastria, a blockchain platform association in Spain that aims to foster blockchain adoption and strengthen public-private blockchain ecosystems. He explained that Alastria has over 90 documented use cases, including identity preservation and tokenization, and currently maintains two production networks: the T Network and a Hyperledger Besu network. Miguel discussed their new project funded by the Spanish blockchain services infrastructure, which aims to provide SLAs and regulatory compliance for corporate and public administration use cases by December 31, 2025, with a potential move to June 2026.

### P.2,5,6 Implementation and Roadmap

Miguel discussed the implementation of P.2,5,6, which is mandatory and natively supported by Besu. He mentioned that they have been deploying test networks but wanted to clarify the roadmap and status of this support. Miguel also mentioned conversations with the European Commission and asked if other contributors had implemented P.2,5,6, seeking clarity on whether this was a casual or structural occurrence.

### EVM R1 Curve Support Discussion

The group discussed the support for the R1 curve in transaction signatures for the Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM). Gary clarified that Besu supports R1 as the native curve for private chains, mentioning Mastercard as a client using this curve. He noted that while R1 is implemented, its adoption is limited, and there have been some issues with P256 that have been recently fixed. Gary also mentioned that the Fujika Fork will support P256 signature verification, which is expected to increase its use, especially with account abstraction and hardware enclaves supporting R1.

### Full Kosaka Project Status Update

Miguel discussed the current status and roadmap for the Full Kosaka project, which is expected to be completed by the end of the year. Justin clarified that Fugasaka is delivering the precompile to Gary, and mentioned that transaction signatures are already a configurable option in Besu. Matthew raised a point about 6. SEC P. 256 r. 1, noting that enterprise users working with HSM vendors are forming opinions about which signatures are more secure, and expressing interest in using r. 1 due to perceived improved security.

### P.256 and Web3 Signer Implementation

The team discussed the implementation and testing of P.256 and Web3 signer features, with Miguel raising concerns about potential ecosystem adoption challenges. Gary suggested that the best way to contribute would be to exercise R.1 as a primary chain curve through test networks and performance testing. Justin agreed to reach out to the Web3 signer team to gather information about P.256's placement on their roadmap and would provide updates via Discord. The group confirmed that communication would continue through the Discord channel, and Miguel expressed commitment to contributing to the project's development.
