The meeting focused on the migration of Besu repositories to a new organization under GitHub, including Besu, Besu Homebrew, Besu Native, and Besu Stateless. Justin explained that this transfer of ownership to the Besu ETH organization was completed to enhance control over repository management and reduce reliance on LFTT. Matthew discussed ongoing work related to QBFT, including fixes for block period transitions and validator set migration options for enterprise blockchains. He mentioned that testing in Cancun has been successful and that the team is grateful for recent fixes contributed by the community. Matthew also highlighted the BFT acceptance test, which has been integrated into a GitHub action for easier regression testing, and mentioned plans to potentially expand this test to include Cancun and Prague forks. The team is currently conducting specific testing of version 26.2 in preparation for its rollout.

### Hyperledger Branding Strategy Discussion

The team discussed the need for a holistic branding plan to address the removal of "Hyperledger" from various project elements. Justin emphasized the importance of planning for changes like package structure and domain names, acknowledging the risks involved. David and Matthew agreed on the goal of dropping "Hyperledger" where possible, with Matthew noting that Besu is now primarily referred to as "Besu" in presentations and documentation. The team decided to focus on the mechanics of the rebranding process, with David planning to consult internally and potentially form a task force to assist with the effort.

### Remote Collaboration Strategy Discussion

Jessica discussed challenges with gathering maintainers' opinions for a blog post and sought advice from Justin on how to coordinate remote collaboration. Justin explained that he typically uses collaborative documents and relies on a champion to collect input, with minimal synchronous meetings. Jessica shared her goal of getting input from 10 maintainers across different sub-projects of HIRO, and Justin suggested setting a deadline to encourage timely responses from those who care most about the content.
