## Overview

The May 12th Contributor Call focused on addressing the significant increase in pull requests, particularly those generated by AI, and discussing architectural updates. The team reviewed migrating to JSpecify across Besu to replace JSR305 annotations, updated Java version requirements to 25, and discussed removing experimental flags for empty block period seconds. A major portion of the discussion centered on managing the high volume of PRs and their quality, with participants sharing perspectives on balancing review work with development, detecting long-term contributors, and potentially requiring AI usage disclosure through the DCO. The team also discussed enterprise-specific overrides for transaction limits and improvements to BFT transition behaviors in the protocol schedule. Additional updates included ongoing work to migrate documentation away from Hyperledger branding and plans to transition the Twitter handle to @Besu_ETH.

## Details

### JSpecify Migration Discussion Meeting

The meeting focused on discussing the migration to JSpecify across Besu as a replacement for JSR305 annotations for null checks. Justin introduced the topic and asked for thoughts and concerns about implementing this change more broadly across Besu. No specific decisions or next steps were outlined in the provided transcript segment.

### Java 25 Update Discussion

The team discussed updating to Java 25, with Fabio confirming they are already running Java 25 in the Docker image and planning to require it for building bassoon soon. Matthew inquired about maintaining compatibility with Java 21, and Justin confirmed both versions would be supported temporarily. The team also discussed the removal of the experimental flag for "empty block period seconds," allowing users to migrate to the non-experimental version. The conversation ended with Justin raising concerns about the large number of open PRs and the need for more sustainable review processes, inviting the team to share ideas on how to address this issue.

### AI Contribution Management Discussion

The team discussed managing AI-assisted contributions to their project. Fabio expressed concerns about PR quality and inflow management, suggesting the need to identify long-term contributors and potentially requiring AI disclosure in contribution rules. David shared insights from Linus Torvalds about the evolution of maintainer roles in open source projects, while Luis emphasized that the approach should depend on a project's architectural maturity. The discussion concluded with considering whether to modify their DCO to require disclosure of AI usage, though this point remained unresolved.

### DCO Policy Documentation Review

The team discussed documentation around the DCO (Developer Certificate of Origin) policy and its application to agents. David shared a new documentation resource that the TAC had created, though Justin noted it doesn't currently address the role of agents in the DCO. Matthew mentioned a TACPR proposal for wider guidelines that projects could follow, which is still under discussion and seeking feedback from project maintainers. The group agreed to review and provide comments on the proposal, particularly regarding how agents should be handled in the DCO process.

### Hyperledger Rebranding Strategy Discussion

The team discussed removing Hyperledger branding, with Justin reporting they are migrating documentation to basueath.org and working with ConsenSys staff on rebranding efforts. They also addressed the Twitter handle transition, confirming they would move forward with @BazooETH while maintaining the current handle temporarily to avoid losing followers. The team agreed to proceed with these changes, though specific implementation details and discussions with social media teams are still pending.

### Beizu-Project/ETH Project Management Discussion

The team discussed handling a project with Beizu-Project/ETH, with David confirming Ben's previous offer to manage it and agreeing to check with him about proceeding with Beizu_ETH. Justin confirmed they were okay with moving forward if Ben could handle it. Matthew briefly mentioned a potential enterprise-specific enhancement for handling large batched transactions, though he noted this was less common on public mainnet.

### Enterprise System Limit Implementation Discussion

The team discussed implementing an enterprise-specific edition to address the Fusaka limit in the system. Justin confirmed that while the limit currently exists as an artifact of using the Engine API, it should be configurable and potentially subject to protocol governance. The group agreed that with the upcoming Glamsterdam release expected to increase gas usage significantly, implementing this capability would be valuable for enterprise users.

### Transaction Limits Protocol Discussion

The team discussed transaction limits and their configuration in the protocol schedule. Matthew and others noted that while the current system has limits baked in, there's no clear way to override them. Luis and Matthew agreed that these limits are important for transaction parallelization and avoiding state congestion. The group also identified potential UX improvements needed for BFT transitions, particularly around the handling of block numbers versus timestamps in the Genesis file, though no specific action items were assigned.
