### Task Force Restructuring and Updates

The meeting covered updates on various task forces and working groups. Drummond announced that two task forces, AI and Human Trust, and Decentralized TrustGraphs, have become separate working groups. He also mentioned that the Technology Architecture Task Force and Trust Spanning Protocol Task Force are now asynchronous, while the Trusty Task Force and SCID Task Force continue to meet regularly. Darrell provided an update on the Trustworthy Query Protocol, which is in public review and expected to move to a working group approved deliverable stage if no major changes are needed. Drummond noted that the steering committee is reviewing the first specs for Trust REP approval, marking a significant step in the process.

### Google Form for Review Reports

Scott presented a new Google form designed as a checklist and standard format for review reports to ensure due diligence in working groups. Drummond highlighted the comprehensive nature of the checklist, which covers editorial quality, metadata, ancillary materials, and publishing readiness, among other aspects. Scott explained that the form would allow data capture on a Google Sheet and notify steering committee members when completed. The group discussed making the format available to other working groups for feedback and potential adoption.

### SCID-Based DID Method Updates

The meeting discussed updates on several task forces and specifications. Drummond reported that the Trust Spanning Protocol Task Force had moved to asynchronous work due to the maturity of the spec and the existence of two implementations. He also provided an update on the DIDSCID Task Force, explaining that it is a DID meta method based on self-certifying good methods and is gaining recognition for its advantages. Drummond mentioned that DIDSCID is in the queue for review by the DIF Did Methods Working Group but has decided to wait until other SCID-based methods are reviewed first. He also introduced a new SCID-based DID method called Did Sal for cryptographic event log, which has sparked discussions about its differences from other SCID-based methods.

### DID Working Group Progress Updates

The meeting focused on updates and decisions regarding various task forces and working groups. Drummond explained the status of the DID Methods Working Group, noting that only SKID-based methods had been processed until the introduction of DID:Sell, and mentioned Affinity's second implementation. Darrell emphasized the importance of time over additional features, advocating for the completion and approval of existing specifications. Drummond discussed the progression of specifications through the working group and steering committee approval processes, suggesting a potential wave of specs to be finalized this quarter. The High Assurance DID Task Force was reported to be inactive due to the unavailability of its leader, Alex Tweetdale, with no updates from Scott. Drummond proposed switching the working group meetings to a quarterly schedule, suggesting the first Tuesday in April for the next meeting, and invited feedback or objections.

### Verifiable Dossier Task Force Updates

The meeting discussed the status and future of the high-assurance bid specification, with Scott agreeing to check with Eric about its potential reuse in the Creator Assertions Working Group and trust registry initiatives. Daniel Hardman presented a new concept called "dossier" for efficiently referencing and assembling various types of evidence, including verifiable credentials, in applications like secure phone calls, crime scene investigations, and financial audits. The Verifiable Dossier Task Force, hosted by the Cary working group, is developing specifications to handle evidence of different types, with a focus on assembly and communication mechanisms rather than interoperability.

### GSMA Project and SMS Routing

The meeting focused on discussions about SMS routing, trust in communication networks, and a GSMA Foundry project. Reza shared his experience with SMS routing at Real Networks and highlighted the challenges of building trust into communication technologies. Daniel Hardman explained a GSMA project that will demonstrate verifiable international calling using a simplified dossier concept, involving major players like Google, Apple, and Telefonica. The group also discussed technical issues with Specup and the availability of SpecMT for building glossaries. Drummond mentioned the quarterly meeting schedule and upcoming events, while Makki inquired about sharing the decentralized dossier with telecom operators.
