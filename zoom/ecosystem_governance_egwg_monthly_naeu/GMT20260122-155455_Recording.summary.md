The workshop discussed governance models for digital technologies in Canada and Europe, highlighting the decentralized nature of Canada's approach compared to the EU's top-down model. Participants emphasized reciprocity, transparency, and domestic accountability as key factors for interoperability between the two jurisdictions. The group agreed to take a use case-driven approach, focusing on B2B and workplace applications as less contentious pilot areas, while acknowledging the polarized public perception of digital identity in Canada.

### Canada-EU Identity Interoperability Discussion

Keith discussed the differences in how driver's licenses are recognized as identity documents in Canada and Europe, highlighting the need for interoperability between these jurisdictions. He emphasized the importance of establishing pilots to test governance mechanisms and trust, while acknowledging the variations in how identities are verified across the two regions. Keith also called for a formal Canada-EU mutual recognition agreement based on the UN model law to ensure the legal effect of identity systems and trust services across both regions. He concluded by discussing the need for conformity assessment and assurance mapping, including the recognition of third-party evaluators and the acceptance of certificates issued in one region in the other.

### Canada-EU Digital Trust Alignment

Keith discussed the principle of functional equivalence in achieving mutual recognition between Canada and the EU, allowing for cross-border flow of credentials and trust services while preserving jurisdictional sovereignty. He emphasized that technical trust alone is insufficient, highlighting the importance of social trust, voluntary participation, transparency, and meaningful consent in the Canadian context. The workshop proposed creating a joint Canada-EU working group to focus on legal and technical alignment, as well as a shared middleware layer for functional equivalence. Keith also noted that Canada and the EU already have a trusted trade relationship through CETA, which could be extended to include digital credentials and trust services within its conformity assessment protocol.

### Digital Trust Services Collaboration Update

He emphasized the need for continuous technical collaboration, shared operational pathways for validation and credential exchange, and the use of ISO 17029 methodology for verification. The group discussed establishing trust between Europe and Canada through business-to-business (B2B) relationships rather than personal interactions, as B2B interactions tend to be less contentious. Keith emphasized that while technology can address technical issues, trust issues ultimately stem from governance and management decisions, suggesting that both regulatory frameworks and conformance assessments are necessary for building trust.
