## Overview

The Ecosystem and Governance Working Group meeting focused on a presentation by Andrew, Tim, and Daniel about Open Verifiable Communications, a project aimed at restoring trust in telecommunications by implementing decentralized identity credentials. The presenters demonstrated how their system uses verifiable credentials, LEIs, and ACDCs to enable transparent communication between calling parties and terminating carriers, with a specific demo showing Microsoft calling a Spanish number with verified identity and brand information. The technology leverages VVP (Verifiable Voice Protocol) specifications and involves a governance authority, policy administrators, and vetting agents to create portable credentials that can be verified across different network operators. The project is moving to operational implementation with UK operators as a pilot, with plans to have operational improvements based on decentralized credentials within six months.

### Open Verifiable Communications Project

Andrew and Tim presented on Open Verifiable Communications, a project aimed at restoring trust in telecommunications by addressing calling party impersonation fraud. They explained that the current PSTN system lacks proper identity verification between carriers, making it easy for bad actors to impersonate others. The project, developed in partnership with GSMA Foundry, demonstrates end-to-end verifiable calling using decentralized identity technologies. The presentation included a planned demo of their solution shown at Mobile World Congress, though technical difficulties with screen sharing prevented the demo from being completed during this meeting.

### Call Verification System Demo

The meeting focused on demonstrating a new call verification system that uses rich credentials to prove the identity and authority of call originators. Tim and Andrew presented a demo showing how Microsoft and Rich Connections could verify calls across different networks, with credentials being checked at various points in the call flow. The system uses a global governance authority, policy administrators, and vetting agents to validate the rights of organizations to use specific phone numbers, brands, and communication methods. Daniel explained the technical implementation, which relies on standards like LEIs and the Verifiable Voice Protocol (VVP), and mentioned that Provenant is working on an open source test harness for VVP compliance. The team announced they are starting a pilot project with UK operators next week to implement portable vetting based on verifiable credentials, with a goal of achieving operational improvements within 6 months.
