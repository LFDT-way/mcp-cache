## Overview

The meeting focused on David Condry's presentation of his Writer's Logic protocol, which uses cryptographic proof of effort to verify authorship and detect AI-assisted writing. David explained how his system creates checkpoints through writing sessions by hashing document states and recording behavioral patterns including typing timing and editing patterns. The protocol monitors paste events and allows writers to attest whether pasted content was AI-assisted or reference material, with the evidence supporting or contradicting these claims. The system exports results as C2PA manifest files and generates writer's attestation reports that provide forensic recommendations while allowing receiving parties to make final decisions. Participants discussed potential applications in education for exam proctoring and detecting AI-generated content in academic submissions. The group explored connections between David's work and existing frameworks like C2PA and COG, with Drummond suggesting that COG might be the appropriate home for developing governance frameworks around the process. The discussion highlighted the need for standardized governance to support multiple detection systems and establish policies for different industry use cases.

## Details

### Antitrust Policy and Trust Presentations

Carly then welcomed David Condry from Writer's Logic, who was set to present on cryptographic proof of effort related to the Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Labs.

### Writer's Logic Protocol Presentation

David presented his Writer's Logic protocol, which aims to provide cryptographic proof of authorship and evidence of AI assistance in writing through a system of checkpoints and hashes that track writing sessions. The protocol monitors paste events and allows writers to categorize content as human-written, AI-assisted, or reference material, exporting the results as a C2PA sidecar file. David explained that the protocol is not an AI detector but infrastructure that can provide forensic evidence of the writing process, and he is seeking support to present a BOS proposal to the IETF for adoption.

### AI Writing Detection Tool Launch

David presented his work on developing an AI detection tool called the "writer's attestation report" (WAR) to establish governance for AI-assisted writing processes. He explained that writers are now "at war" with AI detection as the method of writing has become more important than the quality of writing itself. David announced he would be releasing a desktop application on writerslogic.com this week that works across multiple editing platforms including Microsoft Word, Pages, Visual Studio Code, and Scrivener, with a browser extension for web-based editors.

### Keyboard Input Security System Discussion

David explained that his system currently only supports keyboard input and does not account for speech-to-text features, as implementing this would create security vulnerabilities. He noted that the system can detect when AI-generated content is being retyped into documents through behavioral pattern analysis and timing between keystrokes. Drummond inquired about how the C2PA manifest handles written material, to which David responded that it tracks behavioral patterns and timing rather than the actual keystrokes.

### Proof of Effort Protocol Demonstration

David demonstrated a proof of effort protocol that monitors both behavioral patterns and screen content to deter fake evidence creation. The system records editing patterns, creates checkpoints with hash verification, and compiles data into a Merkle tree to prevent tampering. Makki asked about how the system handles content copied from LLMs and pasted into documents, inquiring about classification and tagging of such content.

### AI Content Detection System

David explained the current AI detection system's approach to pasted content, which monitors editing behavior to determine if text was written by AI or a human. Makki suggested exploring techniques from existing systems like Turnitin to enhance the detection capabilities. Carly inquired about biometric identification possibilities, but David clarified that the hashed and combined nature of the data makes individual writing patterns difficult to extract. David's next step is to seek support from the Linux Foundation's Decentralized Lab to present the protocol to IETF for adoption.

### Digital Governance Framework Discussion

Carly clarified that while the group doesn't govern specific projects, they can help describe the process for creating governance frameworks for digital ecosystems. Drummond explained that TrustRover provides tools and models for creating governance frameworks but doesn't author them themselves, emphasizing that the governing body would be an external entity like Writer's Logic or IETF. The discussion highlighted that David's work with Writer's Logic spans both COG and C2PA frameworks, with COG being more focused on writer attestation while C2PA handles the broader process.

### Governance Framework and Applications Discussion

Drummond suggested that Cog might be the right home for producing and publishing a governance framework, and recommended starting discussions with Eric rather than Scott. Carly discussed potential applications in education, particularly for proctoring exams and detecting AI-generated content in student assignments, while also expressing concerns about Turnitin's copyright requirements. David shared insights about writer concerns regarding process evidence and explained that his application was designed to work offline to address privacy concerns.

### Writer Privacy Verification System

David explained his system for maintaining writer privacy while verifying content authenticity through Merkle trees and hashing. Eric mentioned he would discuss this topic with a journalist from The Economist at dinner. Carly suggested using the proof system as a screening mechanism for journal submissions, and David agreed that different publishers should be able to set their own policies. Makki mentioned they are developing governance principles and a self-assessment tool expected by August or September.
