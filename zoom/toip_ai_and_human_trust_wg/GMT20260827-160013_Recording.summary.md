Wenjing shared information about high AI adoption costs, noting that some startups are spending up to $100K per employee annually on AI, and mentioned Meta's projected $10 billion annual AI spending.

The conversation then shifted to implementation of a controller, with Steve recommending everyone check out a new deep-sea carnis tool that uses a plugin-based approach, which could help achieve 80% of their implementation goals.

### Technical Stack Diagram Discussion

Steve and Wenjing discussed a technical stack diagram, focusing on the DeepSeek harness and verifiable trust agent subsystem. Steve explained that the harness would handle controller and deterministic workflows, while the embedded verifiable trust agent subsystem would manage interactions with outside parties through TSP and VTA. Drummond asked about delegation credentials, to which Steve responded that they would be held in the verifiable relationship record.

### VTA System Architecture Placement Discussion

The team discussed the placement of verifiable trust agents (VTAs) in the system architecture, with Steve questioning whether they should be in Layer 1 or Layer 3. Drummond and Wenjing clarified that VTAs are implemented as software components that run the entire stack and operate at the party level rather than a specific layer. Wenjing explained that each agent implements the whole stack and communicates with other agents using lower-level protocols like TSP, creating a nested architecture where different agents interact with each other while maintaining their own complete stack implementation.

### TA and VTA Security Architecture

The group discussed the architecture and security considerations for implementing TA (Trust Agent) and VTA components. Steve explained that VTAs operate within a secure environment handling communication and verification tasks, while verifiable relationship records are maintained outside the VTA in larger TE agents. Wenjing advised that while the protocol doesn't mandate separating components, it would be a good security practice to isolate critical functions, though Steve indicated he needs to build the system first to better understand priorities. The discussion concluded with Wenjing reviewing proposed deliverables in Appendix A of Steve's document, though the conversation ended before finalizing the deliverables discussion.

### Working Group Deliverables Alignment

The team discussed four potential deliverables for their working group, with Drummond and Steve agreeing that joint work would be beneficial. They aligned on creating companion documents including a diagram and vocabulary to describe the architecture (item A1), defining agent roles and cryptographic capabilities (item A2), and building a comprehensive roll record provenance state following recovery profile (item A3). Items A4 and A5 regarding TEA/VTA binding and signing authority were deemed to be already in progress, with someone named Glenn working on related subsystems.

### Agent Cards in VDS Specification

The team discussed the concept of agent cards as part of the verifiable data structure (VDS) specification, clarifying that these cards describe agents rather than humans. Drummond explained that while the original "our card" concept focused on human digital business cards, the evolution into VDS allows for both human and agent cards, with agent cards specifically supporting delegation credentials. Wenjing emphasized the importance of maintaining clear distinctions between human and AI agent interactions to avoid dangerous equivalence, noting that contracts with humans and agents should be constructed differently even when using similar terminology.

### Relational AI Trust Framework Discussion

The team discussed the philosophical underpinnings of relationships in relational AI, focusing on how trust graphs will redefine identity and the need for different types of verifiable relationship credentials (VRCs) based on relationship types. Steve suggested using data delegation as a simple example of fiduciary duty, where parties entrust data usage with specific terms and conditions, including policy gates for handling ambiguous cases. The group agreed to continue exploring this example in future meetings, with plans to discuss TEA protocol aspects in the next session.
