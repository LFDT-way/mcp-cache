Drummond confirmed this meant users could add trust and delegation capabilities without affecting their current MCP usage. Wenjing noted that while the MCP protocol remains unchanged, the agent code would need modifications for authorization and trust decision-making functionality.

### TEA Protocol Integration Discussion

Wenjing explained that a composite TEA involves both initiator and responder roles being TSP endpoints identified by VIDS, where the agent combines multiple components including MCP and authorization protocols. Drummond confirmed that the TrustRP stack's TSP transport protocol would support their authenticated delegation requirements from person or community nodes to agents. Steve mentioned working on an additional artifact for delegation purposes that would complement the TEA specification. The discussion focused on how agents would integrate TEA with other protocols, with Wenjing noting that while the ACDC content is covered in the draft, the remaining work involves describing integration with other agent protocols.

### Verifiable Capabilities in ACDC Systems

Wenjing explained the concept of verifiable capabilities in ACDC (Attribute-Based Credential Delegation) systems, distinguishing between capabilities that can be verified before action and accountability requirements that can only be verified later. She provided examples using API access with keys as capabilities, explaining that verification ensures the authenticity of the authorization source rather than validating the keys themselves. Wenjing also described how delegation can add constraints and duties to capabilities, with some conditions being checked immediately and others verified after execution.

### Protocol Duty Definition Discussion

Wenjing and Steve discussed the need to distinguish between different types of duties and obligations in their protocol design. They explored terminology options, with Steve suggesting "deterministic" and "non-deterministic" duties as alternatives to "duty" itself. Carly raised concerns about the complexity of defining conditions, particularly around currency and financial obligations, highlighting how even simple transactions can become messy due to variables like exchange rates and shipping costs. The discussion concluded with Wenjing emphasizing the importance of having clearly defined conditions that can be verified, while acknowledging that some aspects will necessarily involve judgment and may lead to disputes that require accountability mechanisms through logged decision-making.

### AC/DC Information Storage System

Wenjing explained that all information is stored in AC/DC and discussed two main types of information: those with clear JSON schemas and those with ad hoc or non-formalized language. She confirmed that agent facts or "aging cards" under A2A could also be placed in an AC-DC envelope. The team plans to continue working on the draft, with the goal of completing it by the end of summer, and Steve and Wenjing discussed GitHub procedures for editing and submitting pull requests.

### AI Harness Definition Discussion

The group discussed the definition of "harness" in the context of AI, with participants sharing different perspectives on the term. Wenjing and Steve explained that a harness is essentially a piece of software that calls or interfaces with an AI model, acting as a middle layer between users and the model. They clarified that while the term is not well-defined across the industry, it typically refers to the software component that processes user input and sends it to the AI model, with the ability to manipulate or modify that input before it reaches the model. Steve agreed to propose a topic about duties for the next meeting, to be discussed two days in advance.
