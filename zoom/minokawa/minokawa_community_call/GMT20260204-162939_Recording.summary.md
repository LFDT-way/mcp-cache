Kevin introduced the Compact Improvement Process (COIP), explaining its lifecycle and comparing it to other improvement processes in the blockchain and programming communities. He proposed using the term "COIP" for Compact Improvement Proposal, suggesting it as a community-driven way to propose new features and document design decisions.

### Compact Language Community Process Overview

Kevin explained the naming confusion between Minokawa and Compact, clarifying that Minokawa is the open source organization developing the Compact language, and the Midnight Foundation initially prohibited using "Compact" for open source projects but later allowed it due to confusion. He outlined the Community-Owned Improvement Process (COIP) lifecycle, which begins with an idea from anyone in the community, including maintainers, and involves engaging with the Technical Steering Committee (TSC) for approval or rejection of proposals. Kevin emphasized that not every improvement to Compact requires a COIP, as simple changes like bug fixes, performance improvements, and refactoring do not need formal proposals.

### Python COIP Process Overview

Kent explained the process for Python's Community Improvement Proposal (COIP) system, detailing how maintainers decide if a feature requires a formal proposal, the steps for drafting and submitting a COIP, and the TSC's role in reviewing and accepting proposals. The process involves a first draft, TSC consideration, public comment period, and final acceptance or rejection, with accepted proposals representing the community's agreement to implement the proposal. Kent emphasized that COIPs are not living documents and should not be updated after acceptance.

### Compact Roadmap and Contributor Insights
