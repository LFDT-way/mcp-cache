## Details

### GitHub Fork Build Failure Resolution

Jonathan discovered that his fork of the Compaq repository was causing build failures due to automated workflows running on his GitHub account. He explained that as a non-maintainer, he needed to use a fork to create PRs, which automatically cloned all the actions and builds. Jonathan resolved the issue by disabling automated actions on his fork.

### Phase 2 and 3 Swap Issues

The team discussed issues with Phase 2 and Phase 3 being swapped again, with Kent noting that while Bob and Gianluca were aware of the changes, they needed to fight against this modification. Jonathan explained that the proposed change removes the delivery of a MIP and instead delivers the entire working implementation, which was not just a minor wording change. The discussion then shifted to a potential commitment to ZKRv3, with Parisa raising questions about whether there were any obstacles to this commitment and whether management and Midnight Foundation had been informed about the associated risks.

### ZKR Version Transition Planning

The team discussed the transition from ZKRv2 to ZKRv3, with concerns raised about stability and testing. They agreed to maintain both versions for now, with ZKRv3 being used for features like SECP support while events continue to support ZKRv2. The team decided to schedule a meeting with the ledger team to coordinate the transition plan, and Parisa volunteered to follow up on this matter. The release schedule was also reviewed, with a security fix releasing as 0.31.1 tomorrow, followed by 0.33.0 next week supporting Ledger 9, and 0.32 the week after as the final Ledger 8 release.

### Contract Interface Updates Discussion

The team discussed updates to contract interfaces and types, with Jonathan presenting a PR that simplifies the language to be more consistent with the reference manual by using contract types instead of interfaces. They debated the use of the word "implements" in the syntax and considered alternatives like "has type," but ultimately decided to keep the current approach. The discussion also covered recursion handling, with Kent and Joseph clarifying that while recursive contracts are currently undefined, Joe has implemented checks to prevent them and the compiler may signal errors statically or at runtime. Joseph noted that the current compiler behavior correctly handles contract disclosure and witness restrictions, and the team agreed to relax the witness restriction to only apply to circuits within the interface definition rather than to the contract as a whole.

### Contract Standardization Discussion

Jonathan discussed a well-written problem statement submitted by someone regarding the need for a standardized representation of compiled contracts beyond the current TypeScript/JavaScript targets. The proposal suggests using Rust for execution and highlights the lack of a spec for contractinfo.json, which Seba had questioned regarding tooling. Joseph suggested exploring a WASM retargeting option as an alternative to executable ZKIR for safe execution of untrusted code in browser environments. Parisa confirmed adding the link to the agenda for team review.
