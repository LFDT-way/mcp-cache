## Details

### UX Task Force Setup Meeting

The meeting focused on setting up a UX task force, with Nicky leading the discussion and sharing a prepared wiki page and Miro board. The group discussed the agenda, which included introductions, sharing hopes and concerns about the task force's objectives, and going through antitrust notices. Nicky asked if anyone would be interested in co-chairing the task force, though no decisions were made on this point.

### UX Task Force Formation Discussion

Nicky and Daniel discussed the formation of a UX task force for the Decentralized Trust Graph, with Nicky emphasizing that the group's focus should align with broader working group needs rather than individual strategies. Drummond explained that task forces operate as self-organizing subgroups of working groups, with seven currently active, and clarified that any deliverables from task forces must be approved by the working group before moving up the TrustRFP stack to Layer 4. The discussion focused on defining the scope of human experience and user experience work beyond just technical screens, with Drummond noting that while UX is often reduced to screen design, there's more complexity involved in customer journey design and user guides.

### Digital Trust and Implementation Challenges

Daniel explained his background as a consultant specializing in digital trust and analytics, highlighting his research on trust models and an AI fairness platform he's developing. He emphasized the importance of considering human trust factors alongside technical solutions, citing Switzerland's eID system as an example where a sophisticated technical solution failed due to governance and trust issues. Carly added that user experience and ease of adoption are crucial factors for successful implementation, regardless of technical quality.

### Human Trust Experience Design Framework

Daniel discussed creating a "human trust experience layer" that would include new approaches to UX trust design principles and interaction patterns to help users better understand and cope with trust signals in a changing environment. Rob shared his interest in applying these concepts to a decentralized peer-to-peer content distribution system similar to Substack or Medium, where authors could build verifiable trust networks with their readership. The group discussed how their work might naturally lead to requirements for lower levels, with Drummond suggesting the effort might be more appropriately called a "human experience task force" rather than just UX-focused.

### Trust Network User Experience Planning

Rob expressed his hope for the task force to visualize a real trust network user experience and emphasized the importance of simplicity and ease of use without creating a new platform. He feared potential issues with governance and rules of conduct in an ecosystem without platform incentives. Carly, a Trust Over IP steering committee co-chair, highlighted the need for a clear use case and user experience to ensure adoption and avoid the work being lost as attention shifts to other initiatives.

### Human Experience Task Force Planning

The group discussed establishing a human experience task force focused on verifiable trust agents (VTAs) and verifiable trust communities (VTCs) within the Decentralized Trust Graph framework. Drummond described VTAs as potentially transformational for digital life, comparing them to the impact of browsers, while emphasizing the need to address both user experience and developer experience. Daniel presented research questions organized around six key areas: trust formation, representation, explanation, control, evaluation, and interaction, highlighting the importance of balancing transparency with user comprehension and addressing both technical and human-centered aspects of trust. The group agreed to continue discussions asynchronously and schedule dedicated task force sessions to further develop the human experience framework.
