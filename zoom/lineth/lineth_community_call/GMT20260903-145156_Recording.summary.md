## Overview

The Lineth Community Call for September 3rd, 2026, was the first of its kind, focusing on technology updates and community interaction. Simon and Julien led the discussion, with Sean moderating. The main topic was the ongoing transition from direct arithmetization to RISC-V, which Julien explained would impact performance, cost, and throughput, with a target completion around late October or early November. Julien detailed the reasons for the pivot to RISC-V, citing maintenance concerns and standardization. The call also covered hardware requirements for proof generation, with Julien noting current CPU focus and future GPU acceleration. Questions from the community, including Coenie, addressed proof generation times, hardware needs, and the architecture of the proof pipeline. The team mentioned work on enterprise features like HSM integration and topics like privacy and interoperability. The next community call was scheduled for the first Thursday in October.

## Details

### Lineth Community Call Opening

Sean welcomed attendees to the first Lineth Community Call and noted that the meeting would start in a few minutes to allow time for refreshments. He informed participants about the Linux Foundation antitrust policy and code of conduct, emphasizing the importance of adhering to these guidelines during the meeting. The call was scheduled to begin shortly after additional participants joined, including Simon and Julien.

### Linif Community Call Overview

Simon explained that the community call aims to foster interaction between maintenance teams and the community, particularly for those interested in developing with Linif or building use cases. He outlined that the call will be held monthly and encouraged participants to ask questions either via Zoom or chat. Simon also provided context on Linif's mission to provide open technology in a neutral environment, avoiding vendor lock-in, and mentioned the transition from direct arithmetization to a new approach, though the details were not completed in the transcript.

### RISC-V Transition Update

Julien provided an update on the RISC-V transition, explaining that it will continue for about 2 months with a targeted delivery of late October to early November. The transition includes implementing their own ZK VM to replace the previous ZK EVM, which will address data availability limitations and significantly increase throughput. The team will begin security and audits in mid to late September, running parallel to performance improvements, with the goal of achieving orders of magnitude improvement in cost per transaction.

### Linear Stack Technology Updates

Julien clarified that the community call focused on Linear stack technology rather than token or IPO discussions. He answered that while current hardware requirements include large machines with several hundred GB of RAM and CPU-only processing, the team is working on GPU acceleration expected to be available in December or January. Julien noted that hardware requirements are still evolving as they work on risk 5 and performance improvements, with the best machines likely to change frequently based on ongoing developments.

### Performance Optimization Progress Update

Coenie reported significant performance improvements from Julien's recent optimizations, allowing proofs to be generated in 27 minutes using a 256GB machine instead of a terabyte machine. Julien explained that the current cost per transaction is approximately half a cent for transfers, with plans to reduce this by a factor of 10 by early Q1 through the new Risk 5 architecture and sharding improvements. The team is also working on Fast Confirmation Rules (FCR) to accelerate deposits, targeting reduction from 13-19 minutes to a few tens of seconds once layer one finality is achieved.

### GPU Acceleration and Proof Optimization

Julien discussed GPU acceleration efforts, noting they achieved a 4x cost reduction for aggregation and compression proofs but not for execution proofs. He mentioned working on enterprise Linux integration with HSMs and cloud certification, along with privacy and interoperability features. The discussion touched on finalization processes, highlighting three main bottlenecks: data aggregation time, proof building, and layer-one finalization waiting time, with SCR offering potential improvements.

### Zig EVM Implementation Discussion

The team discussed implementing an EVM in Zig called "zoo" to support RISC 5 compliance, which will be used under the hood for proof generation while continuing to rely on Bizu for most chain operations. Coenie asked about the impact on the SNARK proof generation process, and Julien confirmed that while the high-level architecture would remain similar, the exact details should be verified by a cryptographer. The next community call was scheduled for the first Thursday in October, and participants were directed to join the Lineth Discord channel for ongoing discussions.
