### Open Tokenized Asset Standard Presentation

Mukul presented the current state of tokenized assets, highlighting fragmentation across different ledgers, jurisdictions, and asset classes that creates liquidity silos and duplicate compliance work. He outlined the solution proposed by the Open Tokenized Asset Standard (OTAS) project, which involves creating a protocol-agnostic interface layer that standardizes functions rather than asset representations, similar to existing financial protocols like SWIFT and FICS. The presentation covered four prioritized layers for the standard: settlement networks, identity, compliance, and asset metadata, with plans to discuss the post-trade lifecycle in more detail.

### Token Standards and Settlement Mechanisms

The team discussed legal aspects of settlements across different jurisdictions and regulatory frameworks, with Amit highlighting the need to address enforcement mechanisms. They compared token standards across different ecosystems including EVM, Sui, and Solana SPL, identifying four universal functions that appear across all platforms: who can hold or transfer tokens, transfer rules, how value changes hands, and asset servicing. The discussion focused on how Ledger answers these functions in mutually incompatible ways, which presents the core problem that needs addressing in their second iteration of the white paper.

### Real Estate Protocol Standardization Discussion

Michael asked about using the protocol for standardizing functions related to real estate records and compliance data sharing for registered investment advisors. Sarvesh suggested incorporating asset type attributes into the protocol specifications and leveraging existing security and compliance pillars. Mukul confirmed the protocol's asset-agnostic nature and mentioned existing real-world applications in ecosystems like Solana, while exploring potential collaboration opportunities with Michael's use cases.

### NIST OSCAL Compatibility Discussion

Mukul and Michael discussed the potential compatibility of their work with post standards, with Michael suggesting the NIST OSCAL format as a helpful tool for linking compliance standards together. They explored the use of NIST OSCAL in various compliance areas, including ISO, SOC 2, SEC, and FINRA. Sarvesh suggested adding specific use cases to their discussion points to help develop a proof of concept, highlighting the importance of understanding how protocols would work in practice, particularly in real estate applications.

### Blockchain Legal Finality Discussion

Mukul shared information about a new blockchain bill passed in Maharashtra for real estate use cases. Amit explained the distinction between technical finality and legal finality in blockchain transactions, noting that while technical finality is achieved through DLT, legal finality requires additional constructs around jurisdiction, time zones, and compliance requirements. Amit provided an example of how legal finality works in practice through governance and compliance specifications, illustrating how a transaction can achieve technical finality but still fail legal finality due to insolvency issues.

### Blockchain Legal Finality Challenges

The discussion focused on the challenges of achieving legal settlement finality in blockchain transactions, particularly when interacting with central banks and legacy payment systems. Amit explained that while technical finality provides atomicity through DLT, legal finality requires additional checks including insolvency verification and SPV approvals, creating complexity in heterogeneous systems. The group agreed to address these legal finality aspects in a dedicated section of the white paper, with Sarvesh emphasizing that the paper is designed as a community collaborative effort to gather input on the nine open questions rather than a final document.
