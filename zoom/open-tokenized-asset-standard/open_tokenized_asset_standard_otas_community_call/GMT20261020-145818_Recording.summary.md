## Overview

The October 20, 2026 Open Tokenized Asset Standard Community Call focused on reviewing the white paper version B0.2 and discussing proposed updates. Mukul presented key proposals including a redemption lifecycle for redeemable reference tokens, attested supply for authorization of token creation, and standard reason codes for transfer blocking. The team debated whether attestation should be part of OTAS standards or left to institutions, with Amit suggesting it should be tagged as pre-trade activity and included in asset metadata. Redwan offered to share the ERC7943 proposal with Edwin's working group for feedback, and Sarvesh emphasized the need for a high-level mapping structure or design document to better illustrate the workflow for community understanding. The meeting also included discussion about potential community outreach opportunities, with Amit mentioning the Hyperledger India chapter's interest in presenting OTAS at upcoming conferences.

## Details

### Antitrust Guidelines and Code Review

The meeting began with participants joining and informal conversation about Sean's recent break in Barcelona. Sean prepared to start the official meeting by sharing links in the chat, noting that the meeting would be recorded, and making Mukul a co-host. While waiting for Sarvesh to join, Sean proposed reviewing the antitrust guidelines and code of conduct with Mukul's agreement.

### OTAS White Paper B0.2 Recap

Mukul presented a recap of the OTAS white paper version B0.2, explaining that it proposes a common layer for describing basic token functions across different blockchains. The paper covers how tokens can conform to OTAS standards, distinguishes between technical and legal finality, and includes research on financial market infrastructure. Mukul noted that the SUI team contributed materials for their new token standard.

### Redeemable Reference Flows Proposal

Mukul presented a proposal for the redemption lifecycle of redeemable reference flows, introducing three main steps: request, fund, and claim. The proposal includes additional options for cancel and reject, with specific event standards to track these processes on blockchain systems. Redwan requested the link to the proposal, which was shared during the meeting.

### Redeemable Asset Lifecycle Proposal

The team discussed the proposal for introducing a lifecycle for redeemable assets, similar to how settlement lifecycles work. Sarvesh questioned whether the current redemption processes like request, fund, reject, cancel, and claim should be standardized in the specification, suggesting that specific rules for cancellation and rejection should be defined outside the spec as they vary by implementation. The group agreed that while a general flow could be useful, some items might be outside the scope and could be researched further.

### Attested Supply Proposal Discussion

Sarvesh updated Haris that the team is still working on cleaning up code before pushing it to the SUI team, with Mukul handling the cleanup work. Mukul discussed a proposal for attested supply, suggesting that new tokens should only be created with signed confirmations from independent parties like auditors or custodians. Amit suggested categorizing attested supply as a pre-trade activity rather than settlement-focused, and the group agreed this approach would be helpful for recording supply changes before actual trade execution.

### Asset Metadata Flow Discussion

The team discussed how asset metadata, including information about mintability, burnability, and supply, should flow through asset metadata rather than being handled at the settlement layer. Sarvesh announced that representatives from Digital Assets and Cantor Foundation might join the next call to provide insights on asset metadata and cross-chain information sharing. Redwan and Mukul discussed the technical aspects of adding metadata to ERC20 tokens on Ethereum, noting that while it's possible to add metadata through libraries, it increases contract size and gas requirements, which could be a concern for institutions.

### Financial Instrument Standards Discussion

The team discussed approaches for handling financial instrument representation across different networks and data storage systems, with Mukul suggesting keeping attestation as an optional field in standards to avoid complexity. They reviewed a PR for ERC7943 survey section submitted by Michael, with Redwan agreeing to share it with the working group for feedback. The group identified the need for a high-level mapping structure or design document to help explain the workflow to a broader audience, and discussed potential community outreach opportunities including a Hyperledger India chapter conference.
