## Overview

The September 22nd, 2026 Open Tokenized Asset Standard (OTAS) Community Call focused on reviewing recent updates to the OTAS white paper and demonstrating a cross-chain simulation between Ethereum and SUI. Mukul presented changes including the addition of permission asset standards, repositioning of closed loop tokens, and new sections clarifying where OTAS applies versus native mechanisms. The team demonstrated an end-to-end cross-rail DVP (Delivery Versus Payment) scenario involving a tokenized sovereign bond on Ethereum and a tokenized commercial bank deposit on SUI, showing how OTAS serves as a shared language at the seams between different blockchain systems. The discussion highlighted challenges with composability across different chains and the need for further work on identity and compliance aspects, with participants agreeing to collaborate on developing more comprehensive test suites and exploring implementation of OTAS interfaces across various blockchain platforms.

### Open Tokenized Asset Standard Call

Sean opened the September 22nd, 2026 Open Tokenized Asset Standard Community Call and provided necessary introductions regarding antitrust policies and conduct guidelines. Mukul experienced technical difficulties with presenting on VS Code and screen sharing, which Sean helped troubleshoot. Sean offered to assist Mukul by sharing his screen and navigating through the white paper on GitHub.

### Whitepaper Development Progress Update

Mukul updated the team on the whitepaper development progress, explaining that they now have three branches (main, whitepaper v01, and whitepaper v02) with the main branch being the active and default branch. He outlined recent changes including updates from Maestan Labs team regarding permission asset standards and address balances, as well as repositioning the closed loop token for in-app value. The team also addressed confusion about OTAS application versus native mechanisms by labeling steps in the flow and adding a new subsection on how raw on-chain tokens become OTAS objects.

### Cross-Blockchain Tokenized Bond Simulation

Mukul demonstrated a simulation between Ethereum and SUI for end-to-end tokenized bond and deposit transfers, explaining how OTAS serves as a shared language at the seams between different blockchain systems. The demo showed a cross-rail DVP flow following reference flows from A1 to A6, including scenarios for sanctions revocation and unconfirmed flow legs. Mukul highlighted the importance of token capability manifests, identity verification through verifiable credentials, and compliance interfaces that allow both rails to answer transfer proceed questions in the same vocabulary. The simulation demonstrated how the protocol handles different finality models between Ethereum and SUI, with both legs either completing successfully or being undone together to eliminate single-point failure risks.

### Composability Options Across Blockchains

The team discussed composability options outside of ETH and SUI chains, particularly regarding transactions within a banking perimeter. Mukul explained that while Solana could be used, there are limitations due to differences in permission and privacy, making it difficult to verify certain transactions completely. Sarvesh concluded that starting with SUI and ETH EVM chains would be a good approach, with potential for expansion and better understanding as the community grows.

### Blockchain Architecture Workflow Collaboration

Sarvesh and Mukul discussed collaborating with Harris and Maria from Meisten lab on developing an architecture workflow for a project involving ETH and SUI chains. They agreed to start by creating high-level specifications and building blocks before moving to simulation and testnet implementation. The team acknowledged the need to address identity and compliance aspects, with Sarvesh offering support on the compliance side and Harris offering to help with the GitHub repository and code sharing.

### Tokenized Bonds Test Suite Planning

Mukul proposed setting up a test suite for the cross-rail DVP of Tokenized Sovereign Bonds against Tokenized Deposits scenario, which Sarvesh suggested should be organized under one of the five pillars, likely settlement, with separate modules for different components like compliance and identity. Sarvesh emphasized making the implementation more generic as an interface rather than specific code, allowing people to use the OTAS interface while implementing their own modules. The team agreed to start with code sharing on GitHub and discussed publishing updates through the mailing list to maintain a historical record for new members.
