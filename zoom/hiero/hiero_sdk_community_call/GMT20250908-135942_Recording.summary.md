## Overview

The team discussed the process of working on Hierarchical Improvement Proposals (HIPs) and their implementation across different networks, with detailed explanations of the submission, approval, and development processes. ## Details

### Hierarchical Improvement Proposals Process

The team discussed the process of working on Hierarchical Improvement Proposals (HIPs). Sophie inquired about the steps involved in creating and submitting HIPs, as well as the role of the TSC in approving them. Jessica and Keith provided information on the submission process, noting that anyone can bring a HIP to the TSC for a vote. They clarified that HIP approval is necessary for the Hiero network, but separate approval is required for the Hedera network.

### Hedera Improvement Proposals Implementation Process

Keith explained the process for implementing Hedera Improvement Proposals (HIPs), noting that after approval by the Technical Steering Committee (TSC) and TECOM, work typically begins on the consensus node software and SDKs. He clarified that while HIPs outline high-level changes, detailed implementation plans are documented in SDK design docs, which anyone can create but are often authored by Robert or the Lime Chain team. Keith emphasized the importance of timing, as both consensus node and SDK changes are coordinated for specific Hedera releases, such as Release 66, ensuring parallel development and integration.

### HIPS Review and Approval Process

Sophie inquired about the process of creating and reviewing HIPS (Hierarchical Intellectual Property System) and sought clarification on the current status of approved HIPS. Keith explained that one or two HIPS are typically worked on each month, with block stream and block node HIPS being a major focus. Sophie expressed a desire to understand the post-approval process and timing, as she had recently attended TSC and community meetings. Keith acknowledged this gap in information and agreed to provide more details on actively worked-on HIPS and the overall process.

### Hashgraph HIP Project Timing Discussion

Keith explained that Hashgraph's HIP (High Importance Project) timing is dynamic and tied to consensus node releases, with flexibility to move between releases 66-68 as priorities change. He clarified that while HIP discussions are not publicly available, the public can participate by leaving comments and questions in the GitHub HIP channel. When asked about public meetings for HIPs, Keith suggested this was a question for Jessica to address, noting they could consider additional communication channels if needed.

### Community Engagement Strategies for HIP

The team discussed challenges in communicating public meetings to the community, with Keith and Jessica exploring options like blog posts and community calls. Jessica noted that blog reach is unclear and attendance at community calls is declining, while Keith emphasized that most HIP meetings focus on consensus node software implementation rather than SDK discussions. They agreed that while general HIP meetings could be useful, SDK-specific discussions would likely need separate dedicated meetings.

### Hiero Blog Updates Strategy

Jessica and Angelina discussed the Hiero blog situation, where Angelina suggested adding regular updates to provide project status and encourage community engagement. Jessica agreed that more frequent communication is beneficial but noted they lack visibility into blog viewership, with Hendrik working on implementing tracking tools. Sophie mentioned the current website is built on Hugo with CSS/HTML, and Angelina offered to explore metrics tools to gather usage statistics.

### Enhancing Community and Collaboration

Jessica discussed the importance of promoting community meetings and attracting external collaborators to maintain Hiero's graduated status. She emphasized the need for diversity in organizations contributing to the project, highlighting the role of the Technical Advisory Council in reviewing project status and guidelines. Jessica also mentioned the quarterly and annual reporting process to the LFDT, which includes assessing community growth and project activity. She concluded that improving communication with the external world is a key challenge that needs to be addressed.

### Meeting Attendance and Calendar Management

Sophie and Jessica discussed the importance of attending and staying updated on meetings, particularly the Technical Advisory Council Meeting scheduled for Thursday the 11th at 10 AM. Angelina recommended using a shared calendar to keep track of all projects and meetings, emphasizing its usefulness for staying organized. Jessica mentioned that the upcoming meeting would include the Q3 report for Hiero, which she was working on, and encouraged Sophie to attend.

### Hiero Node Software Development Updates

The team discussed the Hiero consensus node software, with Keith explaining that it's complex and divided into two main components: consensus mechanics and service functionality. Sophie and Jessica discussed improving SDK coordination, with Jessica agreeing to reach out to other SDK representatives for better attendance at meetings. Angelina reported on her research into web analytics for Hiero and inquired about the relevance of existing issues in the consensus node repository, which Keith confirmed were still valid. Sophie raised concerns about the blog content being dominated by Python SDK updates, and the team discussed the need for more diverse content contributions from other SDKs and teams.
