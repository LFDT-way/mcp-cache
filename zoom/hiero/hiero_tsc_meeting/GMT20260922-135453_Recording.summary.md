## Details

### TSC Maintainer Election Results Delay

Hendrik announced that the TSC maintainer election results cannot be declared due to technical issues preventing some maintainers from voting. The team identified that some people were unable to log in to the voting system with their GitHub accounts, potentially affecting the election outcome. Hendrik proposed re-running the election next week to ensure all eligible maintainers can vote properly.

### Helios Login Issues Resolution

Jessica reported three confirmed cases of login issues with the Helios tool and is working with Rai to develop an alternate login solution. The team agreed to reopen the election process, despite the two-week delay this would cause, and Jessica proposed improving communication about the login process through multiple channels including Discord, email, blog, and promoting Hendrik's video tutorial. Hendrik also announced he created documentation for handling tie votes in future elections, which will be brought to the TSC for voting in the coming weeks.

### Upcoming Events and AI Working Group

Jessica announced two upcoming events: Angie's presentation on code quality improvement using CodeRabbit tools, and the kickoff meeting for Hendrik's AI working group on October 6th. Hendrik clarified that the AI working group meeting would be an open discussion to explore how decentralized trust technology can be used for AI, identity, and payments, with potential output including a white paper. Ty inquired about accessing the calendar invite for the AI working group meeting, which Hendrik confirmed would be added to the Linux Foundation calendar system after the initial Meetup.

### Maintainer Cleanup and GitVote Issues

Jessica presented issues with cleaning up inactive maintainers due to failed pull requests and a lack of sufficient maintainers to vote on changes. Hendrik suggested postponing any cleanup until after the upcoming elections to ensure fairness. Hendrik also identified problems with the current GitVote tool, explaining that it doesn't align with their documentation requirements regarding voting algorithms, and proposed either fixing or forking the tool before proceeding with any maintainer cleanup.

### GitHub Maintainer Engagement Issues

The team discussed issues with low engagement in GitHub votes for repository maintainers, particularly noting that many previously active maintainers who left their companies within the last six months are not participating in votes. Hendrik explained that this creates problems where maintainers who still work on projects daily but no longer have company affiliation cannot vote, while former employees who were previously active maintainers remain in the system. Michael suggested improving communication channels, proposing a separate Discord server for maintainers or email lists to address the overwhelming nature of current communication tools like Discord and GitHub notifications.

### Hyro Project Communication Challenges

Richard explained that communication issues in the Hyro project stem from maintainers and committers using multiple platforms including Slack and Discord, creating confusion about where to discuss different topics. He noted that while the Python SDK team successfully uses Discord as their sole communication platform, the Hyro team faces challenges due to having both Slack and Discord, along with cultural and training issues around public versus private discussions. Richard acknowledged this was a known gap in planning from the project's launch and identified the need for better training to help the diverse team navigate these communication challenges.

### Maintainer Contact Challenges Discussion

Hendrik discussed challenges with contacting maintainers, noting that while GitHub connects GitHub accounts to Discord accounts, they don't have comprehensive lists of maintainers' email addresses due to privacy concerns. He explained that this makes it difficult to ping people for specific tasks, though security considerations around maintainer information are being addressed by Keith's work on secure onboarding processes. The conversation ended with Hendrik noting they lacked a quorum to vote on a presented HIP.

### Repository Moves and Security Proposals

The meeting focused on two main topics: repository moves and security proposals. Mark reminded the team about his proposal for operator-provisioner repo moves, which had not yet been voted on due to lack of quorum, and Hendrik agreed to handle an ASIC vote through Jessica and the TSC. The majority of the discussion centered on Keith's comprehensive security proposal for Hiero's open source repositories, which identified certain projects as security-sensitive due to their rapid release cycles and high-value funds at risk. The proposal included multiple security measures such as enhanced identity verification requirements for maintainers, GPG key registration controls, and separate approval processes for PRs and releases. The team discussed concerns about verification methods, with Hendrik questioning the reliability of LinkedIn and employment verification, while Diane suggested exploring existing security models from other open source projects like the Linux kernel before implementing new measures.
