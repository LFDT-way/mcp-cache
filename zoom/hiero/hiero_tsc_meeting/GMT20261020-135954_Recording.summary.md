Michael Garber raised concerns about AI agents creating multiple HIPs without proper vetting, citing a recent incident where someone created 7 detailed HIPs and became frustrated when they didn't receive adequate attention. Keith clarified that the previous presentation focused on verifying human identities for committer/maintainer access, not preventing fake HIP submissions by identified users. Diane explained that OpenVTC technology provides different verification levels for users, devices, and agents, and suggested starting a pilot with just user verification rather than mixing different identity solutions.

### Open Source Identity Verification Systems

The group discussed identity verification processes for open source contributions, with Hendrik proposing a QR code-based system using mobile wallets as a lightweight alternative to showing physical ID documents. Keith expressed concerns about the practical implementation of such a system, particularly for verifying contributors from different countries and regions. Michael Kantor suggested focusing on pre-qualification and AI tooling at the CI/CD level rather than identity verification, recommending the use of LLMs to triage and automatically handle issues to reduce maintainer workload. Daniel added that digital verification systems could be more effective than physical documents, especially for identifying legitimate contributors from regions where document forgery is common.
