The team discussed the definition and role of maintainers across different SDKs, with Keith expressing concerns about allowing maintainers from one language or SDK to approve PRs in unrelated projects where they lack expertise. Hendrik agreed to discuss the topic further and suggested defining what maintainers truly mean beyond just approving commits. Sophie presented data showing existing connections between maintainers across SDKs, particularly highlighting gaps in connections with the Python SDK. The discussion revealed tensions between security concerns and the need for cross-SDK collaboration, with Hendrik questioning the consistency of current proposals given existing practices.

### Emergency Repository Maintainer Handling Process

The group discussed handling emergency situations when repositories have unresponsive maintainers. Richard clarified that the Technical Steering Committee (TSC) has authority to resolve such issues, including deciding whether to punt projects to a sandbox category or appoint new maintainers. The discussion focused on balancing emergency needs with normal policies requiring 3-5 months of contributions before granting committer status, with Hendrik suggesting that in emergencies, the TSC could make exceptions and appoint new committers after reviewing meaningful contributions.

### TSC Maintainance and Process Updates

The TSC discussed maintainership and PR approval processes across SDKs, with Richard suggesting that when all maintainers are non-functional, the TSC should select replacements who ideally have project history but could include qualified candidates from other SDKs. The team confirmed they don't have a HIP ready to present at next week's Hiro Community Day, so Michael will instead provide an overview of the HIP process. Alex and Jessica raised concerns about the lack of engagement in monthly maintainers meetings and proposed conducting a mid-year review to assess project health and KPIs across Hiro, with Hendrik sharing progress on developing KPIs and metrics to track community activity and OpenSSF scores.
