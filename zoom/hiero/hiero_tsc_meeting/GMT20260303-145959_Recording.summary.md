### LF Decentralized Trust Evolution Overview

Hart discussed the evolution of LF Decentralized Trust, highlighting its expansion into privacy tools, cryptographic management, and ZK protocols. He predicted significant growth in open-source standardized smart contracts and digital public infrastructure in 2026, emphasizing the importance of open standards for resilience and portability in rapidly evolving technical landscapes. Hart also explained the role of Trust Over IP as a standards organization within LFDT, noting its consensus-driven, open, and lightweight approach, and the need for contributor agreements to protect IP.

### IP Management and Standards Discussion

Hart explained the options for IP management in repositories, highlighting the availability of both lightweight and heavyweight standards options, and emphasized the importance of disclosing any pre-existing intellectual property. Keith raised concerns about the lack of adoption and participation in TOIP standards by larger companies, suggesting that successful standards require broader industry engagement. Hart acknowledged these points and expressed optimism about the future adoption of digital identity standards, particularly with Google's involvement and ongoing initiatives.

### Tech Standards and Innovation Discussion

Keith discussed the importance of involving major tech companies and stakeholders in standards development, highlighting successful examples like MDL and FIDO. Hart mentioned watching the LFTT space for new standards efforts in tokenization and introduced the JDF as a stricter organization for advancing standards, contrasting it with CSL's more lightweight approach. Hart also highlighted recent innovations in labs, including OpenVTC for proof of personhood and a standardization project on generic ZK SNARKs, as well as the new open-source initiative Nightstream for developing a PQZKP system.

### Digital Credentials and Trust Systems

Hart presented on digital credentials and zero-knowledge proofs, discussing how they could be used to create a maintainer web of trust to prevent rogue attacks. He mentioned that the Linux Foundation's TOIP group is working on standardizing these reputation proofs. Hendrik asked about collaboration between LF and other foundations, and Hart explained that there are some challenges with the AAIF working group being closed to non-members. Giuseppe raised concerns about the adoption of web of trust systems, and Hart showed a demo of a H2H connection app that could potentially improve the UI. The conversation ended with Joseph presenting HIP 1398, which describes a temporary software deployment for running a Powers of Tau ceremony to generate data files for TSS.
