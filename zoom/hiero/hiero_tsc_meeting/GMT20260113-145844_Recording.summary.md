## Details

### AI-Driven Meeting Documentation Changes

The TSC meeting discussed changes to their meeting documentation process. Hendrik proposed moving from a pull request-based approval system to trusting AI-generated summaries and Brandon's review, with members agreeing to raise any concerns if issues are found. The group decided to remove the previous minutes approval topic from future agendas, and Richard suggested that since meetings are recorded, additional verification steps are unnecessary.

### TSC Meeting Recap and Updates

The team discussed implementing a 2-minute recap at the start of TSC calls to summarize previous discussions, which Hendrik agreed to prepare for the next meeting. Hendrik shared upcoming Hiero events, including maintainer days in Brooklyn and a conference in Brussels where he will give away non-permanent Hiero tattoos. Jessica presented a new guide on asynchronous voting using GitVote, explaining that it provides specific instructions for their configuration and encourages team members to review and contribute if needed.

### GitHub Pull Request Voting Process

Hendrik explained the process of opening and voting on a pull request to add a new committer to the Hiero mirror node project, highlighting the transparency and community feedback provided by GitHub's voting system. He also reminded attendees to join various Hiero meetings and encouraged participation in the Hiero Discord, noting its integration into the Python SDK. Lastly, Hendrik discussed the Hiero Improvement Proposals (HIPs) project board, asking Michael or Richard for updates on any new HIPs.

### Block Node Proposal and HIP Integration

The meeting focused on two main topics: the draft mode status of a block node discoverability proposal and the integration of roadmap initiatives with HIP (Hierarchical IP) processes. Joseph noted that the block node proposal was close to being ready for a vote but needed more review, while Hendrik confirmed it would be presented to the TSC in the near future. Richard and Alex discussed the relationship between HIPs and the roadmap, clarifying that HIPs are the process for specifying changes, while the roadmap serves as a public, transparent view of ongoing projects and future plans, with no requirement for roadmap items to go through HIPs first.

### Roadmap and HIP Integration Discussion

The team discussed the relationship between the roadmap and HIPs (Hierarchical Integration Patterns), clarifying that specification changes occur on the HIP side while the roadmap serves as a timeline for implementation. They agreed to keep the Hiero.org website roadmap marketing-focused and separate from technical epics, with Jessica suggesting it might need an update. The group also noted that a recent HIP presentation by Joseph had not yet been voted on, with the vote pending creation.

### HIP 1313 Voting and Access

The team discussed HIP 1313 and decided to create an asynchronous vote to allow all TSC members time to review and vote within the next 7 days. They also addressed access permissions for governance repo wikis, agreeing to ensure that all maintainers are added to the governance rights group to manage access effectively. Michael Garber mentioned that HCS specifications had been added to the Hiero ledger org, which was approved by the team as a positive step.

### HCS Integration Proposal Discussion

The group discussed the organization of HCS (Hiero Consensus Specifications) files within the HIPS repository. Michael Garber proposed integrating HCS standards as application standard tracks under HIP1364, similar to Ethereum's ERC approach, rather than creating a separate HCS folder. Michael Kantor and Hendrik agreed this was a good approach, and Michael Kantor suggested formalizing the proposal for an async vote next week. The discussion concluded with agreement to create a more generic folder structure that could accommodate future standards, similar to Ethereum's approach with ERCs.

### Expanding HIP Discussion Beyond SDKs

Sophie raised concerns about the dominance of HIP-related discussions in TSC calls, noting that while these topics are covered, they don't always reach the SDKs, particularly the Python SDK she maintains. Hendrik acknowledged this issue and explained that while some SDKs are maintained by Hashgraph and LimeChain, there is documentation available in the SDK Collaboration Hub for implementing HIPs. Sophie also inquired about the TSC's openness to discussing non-HIP-related topics, such as LFX mentorships, and asked if Hiero plans to participate in these mentorships.

### Linux Foundation Mentorship Collaboration

The TSC meeting addressed concerns about Linux Foundation mentorship opportunities, with Sophie highlighting that developers from their SDKs are applying to roles outside Hiero due to a lack of Hiero-based opportunities. Hendrik and Alex agreed that the TSC should help individual projects connect with LFDT for mentorship opportunities rather than dictating mentorship needs, and suggested both TSC and community meetings as platforms for discussing mentorships. Diane emphasized the importance of inviting Nate Waddington from LF to both TSC and community meetings to promote mentorships across all SDKs and sub-projects, while Hendrik noted that training materials being developed could serve as a foundation for future mentorship programs.
