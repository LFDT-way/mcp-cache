## Details

### Hiro TSC Meeting: HIP Proposals

Hendrik welcomed attendees to the Technical Steering Committee (TSC) meeting of the Hiro project at the Renox Foundation, emphasizing its open-source nature under the Linux Foundation. He outlined the meeting's structure, including the discussion of improvement proposals for the Hiro network, and provided information on accessing meeting recordings and the public calendar. Richard and Mark planned to present two HIPs (Hiro Improvement Proposals), with Richard taking the lead on the smart contract throttling HIP, which he noted was not yet ready for voting.

### Optimizing Gas Usage in EVM

Richard explained the limitations of using gas as a metric for measuring computation and payment in an EVM, noting its crudeness due to varying costs for different opcodes. He described a method to measure actual performance by executing opcodes repeatedly and calculating gas per second, highlighting significant variations in performance between different opcodes. Richard also discussed the need for a gas throttle to ensure network safety, limiting throughput to 300 TPS, despite the system's capability to handle up to 10,000 TPS without throttling. He concluded by sharing results from benchmarking a Uniswap port, which achieved around 1,000 swaps per second, translating to 150-300 million gas per second, indicating a potential 10x improvement in real-world utility.

### Consensus Node Transaction Throttling

Richard explained the consensus node process, detailing the pre-check and gossip stages for transaction handling. He highlighted the importance of front-end and back-end throttles, explaining that transactions stopped at pre-check are not charged, while those that proceed are. Richard also discussed the unique challenges of throttling EVM transactions due to unpredictable gas usage, noting that users may be charged even if their contract calls are not executed. He concluded by mentioning the rule requiring a gas limit for EVM transactions to help manage costs.

### Hedera Gas Cost Optimization Proposal

Richard proposed a new approach to smart contract execution on Hedera, replacing the current gas per second throttle with a more precise system based on actual operational costs (Ops cost). This change would eliminate the 80% gas charge and make Hedera more Ethereum-compatible, while still allowing for backend throttling based on actual operational capacity. The new system would measure and charge for gas usage according to Ethereum's official gas schedule, while simultaneously tracking and throttling based on Ops costs, which would allow for significantly higher transaction throughput and better network sustainability.

### Hedera Gas Estimation Strategy

Richard presented a new approach to gas estimation and throttling on the Hedera network, which Milan and Keith found promising. Richard explained how the system would reserve half a second for smart contracts while maintaining availability for other transactions. The team discussed the implications for EVM developers, noting that transactions under load could fail with a specific error, and the need to document this for users. Alex raised a question about potentially offering a native up-cost option for contracts, which Richard acknowledged as a possibility but noted the high demand for Ethereum compatibility.

### Enhancing Smart Contract Autonomy

Richard discussed the concept of developers running their own relays to enhance transaction signing and submission capabilities, which could improve network load anticipation and decision-making. He also presented a proposal for a new system contract that would enable smart contracts to schedule future calls, offering powerful capabilities such as rebalancing of DeFi positions, vesting claims, and autonomous smart contract execution. Richard explained the technical details of the proposed system, including capacity management, scheduling policies, and gas costs, emphasizing its uniqueness compared to other networks.

### Backward Compatibility and Governance Updates

The team discussed backward compatibility guidelines and their placement in the governance repo, with Richard explaining that these guidelines should be considered governance-related rather than feature-specific. They clarified that while the guidelines are intended to be followed, they are currently presented as recommendations rather than mandatory requirements, with the possibility of becoming more normative after 6 months of implementation. Jessica reported that she had received legal team approval for certain charter changes and will proceed with submitting those, while also working to consolidate the charter presence in either the Hiera or governance repo.
