## Agenda

Agent security relies on fragile software policies. Regulators demand verifiable compliance. We must shift to a post-quantum cryptographic baseline: ZK proofs for authorization and execution, Threshold Decryption for keys, collusion resistance, and SMPC for data privacy under adversarial conditions.

## Overview

The meeting focused on the development of the Zero Knowledge Boundary Control (ZKBC) standard for enforcing policy compliance in agent execution environments. Ada explained the concept of a sandboxed agent with a monitored interface that generates zero-knowledge proofs as an audit trail. The group discussed the scope of the standard, particularly its relationship to regulatory frameworks like the EU AI Act, and the need to clarify what is out of scope, such as log storage compliance. Andrew from Vera Anchor introduced his company's work on data provenance in scientific research and saw overlap with the group's goals. The group also discussed the need for a proof-of-concept implementation and the possibility of using platforms like GitHub or DIA for sharing and testing. They aimed to finalize the specification draft and prepare for a public request for comments in September.

## Details

### Vera Anchor Data Platform Introduction

Andrew introduced himself as the founder of Vera Anchor, a data provenance and verification platform focused on data, AI, and research workflows, particularly in compliance-driven industries like patient data sharing. He explained that Vera Anchor helps ensure reproducibility in scientific research by verifying data sources and execution. Andrew expressed interest in learning about the group's approach to cryptographic execution and agents to explore potential collaboration opportunities.

### ZKBC Compliance Scope Discussion

The team discussed their work on ZKBC (Zero Knowledge Boundary Control), which creates a sandbox environment for agents with policy enforcement and generates zero-knowledge proofs as audit trails. Ada explained that while the system provides enforcement, it does not automatically include the logging requirements needed for compliance with regulations like the EU AI Act, leading to discussions about scope boundaries. The team is considering adding clearer language to specify what is within and outside the scope of the standard, particularly regarding regulatory compliance frameworks.

### Gateway and Sandbox Security Challenges

Ada and Andrew discussed challenges in verifying the trustworthiness of gateways and sandboxes, particularly in ensuring that software and data remain secure and compliant. Ada emphasized the importance of defining data types and integration points to create reliable standards, while Andrew shared that their focus is on enabling local data processing to address data sharing issues in science without compromising security or compliance. DDB mentioned their work on similar sandbox implementations and offered to discuss further details.

### Specification Timeline and RFC Planning

The team discussed the timeline for their specification, agreeing to iterate on the current draft over the next couple of weeks before seeking public feedback through an RFC process in September. Ada mentioned that Open Matter is working on a research paper about Zero Knowledge work that will be submitted at the end of the month and aligned with the RFC timing. Michael noted that additional people have expressed interest in joining the working group and mentioned improving internal systems for onboarding new members.

### EUAI Ad Scope RFC Preparation

The team discussed preparing for an upcoming September request for comment (RFC) on the EUAI ad scope specification. Ada highlighted the need to address open comments from the working group and create a concrete example of zero knowledge proofs integration. Michael suggested adding diagrams to the specification and tracking deliverables in the working group page. The group also debated whether to include implementation verification in the specification scope, with Michael proposing to create an alpha version/POC in parallel with the RFC process for testing and feedback.

### Cryptographic Proofs Implementation Discussion

The team discussed implementation options for storing and distributing cryptographic proofs, with Michael suggesting GitHub as a potential platform where people could post their proofs as pull requests. Ada expressed interest in the social aspect of this approach, while Chris mentioned they could leverage DIA for the process. Michael also proposed using HOL points as a gamification mechanism, though Ada noted that social engagement might be more effective. The team agreed to further discuss these implementation ideas in their next meeting.
