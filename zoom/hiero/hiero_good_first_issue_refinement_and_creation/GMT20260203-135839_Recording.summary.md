## Overview

The meeting focused on discussing the management and refinement of "Good First Issues" (GFIs) across various repositories within the Hiero organization. Participants, including Angelina, Sophie, Robert, and Hendrik, explored challenges related to maintaining and documenting GFIs, such as mislabeled issues and a lack of templates in certain repositories. The team discussed potential solutions, including creating a filtered view for specific repositories on the Hiero website and implementing a candidate status for GFIs that are not yet ready. The conversation ended with an agreement to refine the process and explore options for improving the user experience for contributors.

### Refining Good First Issues Process

The team discussed refining the process for Good First Issues (GFIs) on the Hiero website. Sophie suggested making the meetings fortnightly and proposed changing undocumentedy GFIs to "Good First Issue Candidates" to give maintainers another chance to review them. Hendrik shared his experience trying to remove GFIs from certain repositories, noting it led to a long and controversial discussion. Robert and Hendrik agreed to consider reaching out to teams to confirm if they want to serve GFIs. The team also discussed creating a curated list of repositories that would appear on the GFIs website, ensuring only reliable and helpful issues are featured.

### Good First Issue Management Strategy

The team discussed managing "good first issue" labels across repositories, with Hendrik suggesting a filtered view for specific repositories known to be ready rather than implementing a global cleanup. Sophie Bulloch proposed automatically archiving or returning unassigned good first issues after two months, but Hendrik expressed concern that such automation might lead to resistance similar to previous experiences with pull request automation. The discussion concluded with agreement that while the current approach might not be ideal, it was necessary to maintain the Hiero experience, particularly given the Python SDK's limited number of available issues.

### Refining Good First Issue Recommendations

Sophie Bulloch discussed the importance of a link for recommending good first issues and proposed advertising it once someone completes a good first issue. She suggested updating the Python bots to recommend specific types of issues, such as C++ or Swift, rather than general Hiero good first issues. Hendrik and Robert agreed that no other teams had expressed interest in using good first issues, and they discussed the potential risks of using them in certain repositories, such as block nodes.

### Good First Issues Implementation Strategy

The team discussed the implementation of good first issues, with Hendrik explaining that while the concept is appealing, the current GitHub actions and workflow for the consensus note repository are too complex to support a good experience for external contributors. They agreed to focus initially on the SDKs and gradually expand to other repositories like Mirror Node, with Hendrik emphasizing the need to train all SDK engineers on good first issues. Sophie Bulloch noted that there are significant differences in perspectives about what constitutes a good first issue and the required level of documentation.

### Custom Good First Issue Filter

Sophie Bulloch and Hendrik discussed creating a custom page to filter and display good first issues from multiple repositories, aiming to integrate it into the Hiero webpage hosted at LFDT. They agreed to use a JSON file to add new repositories and potentially categorize issues by difficulty levels. Sophie mentioned updating the recommendation book for Python using the filtered link, which would help direct new contributors to available good first issues. Robert clarified that the "good first issue" label is crucial for tracking and attracting new contributors, and he planned to merge the changes after confirming they wouldn't disrupt existing workflows.

### GitHub Contributor Banner Settings

The team discussed GitHub's good first issue feature, which displays a banner to new contributors. They discovered that this banner is controlled by GitHub's user settings rather than repository settings, and cannot be permanently enabled or disabled by maintainers. Angelina found that enterprise-level announcement banners could be customized, but the team was unable to locate this feature in their GitHub settings.
