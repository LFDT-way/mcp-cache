## Overview

This monthly maintainer meeting focused on discussing governance changes for SDK teams and addressing ongoing challenges with GitHub maintainers and project scorecards. Jessica led the discussion about merging SDK teams into a simplified structure with separate maintainers and committers teams for both general SDKs and DSDKs, with Sophie suggesting a gradual migration approach rather than a hard cutoff to avoid breaking existing systems. The team discussed concerns about low OpenSSF scorecard ratings across repositories, with Daniel and Sophie highlighting that many maintainers were being blocked by GitHub maintainers and suggesting the need for better documentation and more diverse GitHub maintainer representation. The meeting also addressed the critical issue of low attendance at monthly maintainer meetings, with Daniel expressing frustration that only 4-5 people consistently attended despite having many active maintainers, and the group discussing ways to improve engagement and ensure better participation in decision-making processes.

## Details

### Hyro Community Day Planning

Jessica discussed the low attendance at monthly meetings and reached out to the marketing team to re-engage them in discussions beyond website topics. She presented the agenda for the upcoming Hyro Community Day and mentioned she would confirm if virtual attendance options would be available. The conversation also touched on travel plans for the event, with Jessica planning a long trip to Munich and Sophie noting the event would be convenient for her to attend.

### SDK Team Restructuring Discussion

Jessica and Sophie discussed attending a conference after Hyro Day, where Hendrik and others will be presenting. They agreed to connect Sophie with Allison if she's interested in attending. The main discussion focused on restructuring SDK teams, where Sophie suggested adding new teams rather than renaming existing ones to avoid disrupting current operations. The plan involves slowly working with maintainers to update their co-owners files during the migration process, with any personnel additions or removals being handled in separate PRs.

### Code Owner Process Changes Discussion

Sophie explained that proposed changes to code owners and review processes would be a breaking change affecting multiple repositories, including analytics and bots in the Python SDK. Jessica agreed to modify the PRs to add extra teams while keeping track of governance changes, and Sophie suggested making the SDK Collaboration Hub team the same as SDK maintainers and committers. Daniel raised concerns about ongoing blocks from GitHub maintainers, which Jessica acknowledged as a growing issue affecting multiple repositories and confirmed she had already brought to Hendrik's attention for internal discussion.

### GitHub Maintainer Diversity Discussion

Jessica and Sophie discussed increasing diversity in the GitHub maintainer team, with Jessica explaining that while LFDT cannot directly participate due to potential bias concerns, they are working to bring in community members like Sophie who have extensive project knowledge. They also addressed the need to clean up inactive maintainers from the team, with Jessica confirming that while the distinction between maintainers and members has no current functionality, they plan to address this cleanup after the migration process.

### Repository Scorecard Improvement Discussion

Daniel raised concerns about scorecards in repositories, noting that some repositories like the Python SDK need improvement. Jessica acknowledged this issue and mentioned that it would be discussed in the next TSC meeting, emphasizing that poor scorecard performance could affect the project's graduated status. Daniel suggested assigning someone to track scorecards and create issues when needed to improve engagement and remind maintainers of required changes. Sophie questioned the Linux Foundation's requirements for maintainers to meet minimum quality criteria, and Jessica mentioned that initially, projects seeking graduation were required to have perfect 10 scores on OpenSSF scorecards.

### OpenSSF Scorecard Implementation Challenges

The team discussed challenges with the OpenSSF scorecard implementation across repositories, noting that many scores are low and need attention from maintainers. Sophie and Daniel highlighted difficulties in implementing certain security features like fuzzing and signed releases, attributing this to inadequate documentation that requires significant research time. The group also addressed concerns about maintainer engagement, with Daniel expressing frustration about low attendance at monthly maintainer meetings despite having many active maintainers who don't participate in decision-making processes. Jessica acknowledged these issues and mentioned that TSC meetings now include reminders about meeting attendance, though enforcement remains a challenge.
