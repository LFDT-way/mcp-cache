### DID and ZKPs Evolution Discussion

Keith and Alexander discussed the evolution of DID methods and their adoption in the current cryptographic environment. Alexander noted that DID methods might be losing ground to PKI infrastructure due to complexity, while Keith suggested that zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) are becoming increasingly important, potentially replacing traditional DID-based credentials in many use cases. They also mentioned the "Longfellow of the key" project from Google as an example of how ZKPs are being integrated into existing standards like OpenID for VC.

### DID Adoption in Global Standards

Keith and Alexander discussed the limited adoption of DIDs (Decentralized Identifiers) in global standards, particularly noting that EU entities like UEDA primarily use X.509 trust models and PKI certificates due to ease of integration and government requirements. They agreed that legacy infrastructure considerations play a significant role in technology adoption decisions, with Alexander specifically mentioning how OpenID for VC protocols have an easier adoption path compared to DITCOM protocols due to existing enterprise integrations.

### DID Adoption and Fragmentation Challenges

Keith and Alexander discussed the limited adoption of DIDs (Decentralized Identifiers) and DID methods, with Keith expressing skepticism about the standard's fragmented nature due to the large number of methods (around 300) and lack of consensus. Alexander noted that while the technology offers flexibility and decentralization benefits, it creates challenges for enterprise verifiers who would need to support multiple methods. They both agreed that the current fragmentation makes adoption difficult, and Keith expressed disappointment that the DID Method Working Group did not successfully standardize methods to ease verifier implementation.

### DivDit Methods Working Group Challenges

Alexander and Keith discussed the limited participation in the DivDit Methods Working Group, noting that it primarily consists of six attendees who are not major enterprise players. They observed that open source communities typically lack significant involvement from large enterprises, which can impact adoption rates compared to when major companies like Google or Mastercard implement protocols. The conversation highlighted the challenge of achieving widespread adoption in the identity and authentication space, particularly when competing against enterprise solutions.
