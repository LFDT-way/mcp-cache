### ID Trust Platform Overview

Mahir presented an overview of ID Trust, an end-to-end identity platform developed over the past year. He explained the platform's modular architecture, which includes Hedera Consensus Service as the underlying trust layer, various SDKs, and services like register, resolver, and audit trail. Mahir highlighted that ID Trust has been battle-tested with 10,000 participants in the Africa Hecaton and is currently being updated to align with the OpenID Connect 1.0 specification. He also discussed the platform's applications, including Identity Wallet, Identity Manager, and a single sign-on service, and outlined potential use cases such as identity and access management, reusable KYC, and digital product passports. Mahir mentioned that organization management features are planned for next year but are not yet finalized.

### SSO Solution and Security Discussion

Mahir explained their SSO solution that allows users to present credentials through a QR code or deep link, resulting in an OAuth token if the credentials are valid. Baris and Alexander discussed the security implications of using VP tokens in the flow, with Alexander highlighting the potential risks due to the sensitive information they can contain. Mahir demonstrated the Identity Manager's features, including credential template creation, issuance, and verification, showcasing a custom feature that allows issuers to send information to the user during credential creation.

### OpenID Connect QR Code Implementation

Mahir explained how QR codes work in the context of OpenID Connect for VP, instructing wallets to fetch presentation definitions and verifier metadata. Keith inquired about the open-sourcing status of the underlying libraries, to which Mahir confirmed they are ready to be open-sourced. Mahir also mentioned ongoing hackathons and projects, noting that customer interest is spread across various use cases.

### Digital Product Passport Initiatives

Mahir presented on digital product passport initiatives, including self-sovereign identities and reusable KYC, with a focus on email verification credentials demonstrated through a hackathon project. Baris inquired about the compatibility of ZK snarks with selective disclosure, to which Mahir explained plans to add JWT support and noted limitations with Hedera's DID method. Alexander asked about testing the wallet and UI, and Mahir confirmed public availability except in China, explaining how Wallet Connect facilitates credential signing by arbitrary wallets with registered DID keys.
