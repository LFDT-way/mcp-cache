## Details

### HIRO Identity Projects Overview

Keith introduced the first monthly HIRO identity call, replacing the previous bi-weekly Hedera identity meetings. He explained that the new meeting aims to discuss innovations, product features, and roadmap items related to identity within the HIRO community. Keith and Diane discussed two new projects: the Identity Standards Repo, which will include DID method and non-creds method standards, and the Identity Community Hub featuring the Identity Workbench, a centralized tooling and documentation hub. They encouraged attendees to participate in upcoming TSC meetings to provide input on these projects.

### Identity Hub and Meeting Structure

The meeting discussed the Identity Hub proposal, which aims to create a common location for people to find and start with identity solutions, with a workbench as an initial project to help utilize different SDKs and plugins. Keith outlined the format for future meetings, including posting agendas a week in advance and encouraging attendees to provide feedback for improvement. Diane suggested promoting events and projects related to Hiero, and discussed the process of creating repositories and getting TSC approval for project contributions. The group also talked about upcoming presentations and events, including Mahir's ID Trust work presentation at the next meeting, and Micha's university coding session on November 25th.

### Hedera Identity Ecosystem Overview

Alexander presented an overview of Hedera's decentralized identity ecosystem and its integration with Open Wallet Foundation projects. He explained the benefits of Anoncredits, a privacy-preserving verifiable credential format, and discussed the technical aspects of Hedera's DID and Anoncreds implementations. Alexander also highlighted the integration plugins available for Acapai and Credo projects, showcasing their architecture and performance benefits. The presentation concluded with a roadmap of future developments in the Hedera decentralized identity space.

### Hedera DID Implementation Updates

The team discussed the finalization of Hedera DID and the introduction of Hedera-Anoncredits methods, which define how Anoncreds credentials metadata should be written and read from HCS. Keith highlighted the Hashgraph Association team's contribution to HIP around HederaDid version 2.0, which has been adapted and is now active. Alexander reviewed the implementation work, including the deprecation of legacy DID SDKs and the introduction of updated SDKs for Python and JavaScript. The team also discussed the recent release of the Hedera Creator plugin and the Hiero Identity Hub Initiative, which aims to provide a simple diagram for decentralized identity availability in Hiero.

### Creda and Akapai Technical Integration

Alexander discussed the scalability issues faced by Creda, which were resolved in recent years, and highlighted its customization options and robust scalability solutions. Keith provided context, noting that both Creda and Akapai are Open Wallet Foundation projects, and Alexander explained their technical architecture, including the use of Hedera DID methods and integration with the Hedera network. The discussion covered the plugins' architecture, emphasizing standardized extensibility and the integration of Hedera into these Open Wallet Foundation projects, with a focus on the technical implementation and use of HCS standards for data storage and integrity checks.

### HCS1 Integration and Credential Storage

The team discussed the implementation of HCS1, a community standard for writing and reading messages from the Hedera Consensus Service, for compatibility with data resolvers like Kiloscribe. They explored IDID document storage on HCS, utilizing event messages for CRUD operations and smart caching for efficient message retrieval. The integration of Hedera for Anoncreds credentials involved storing immutable resources as HCS1 files and using a separate topic for location registry entries with compression and timestamped message reading capabilities. Keith highlighted the potential for this model to support future ZKP standards, such as ZK SNARKs, by providing a framework for storing dynamic metadata on HCS. The conversation ended with a demonstration of the Acapai plugin and Credo, showcasing full-on credential flows with Hedera ID issuance, verification, and revocation.

### Credentialing Systems Demo Overview

Alexander demonstrated two credentialing systems: a Hedera-based plugin that supports both ACAP and OpenID protocols, and a Credo plugin demo with comprehensive UIs. The Hedera demo showed issuer-verifier and holder applications exchanging messages and issuing/reving credentials, while the Credo demo showcased issuing and verifying credentials using both anonymous credentials and OpenID protocols. Both demos used DITCOM protocols for connection establishment and featured UI templates for verification requests.

### Hedera Identity Tools Integration Discussion

The team discussed the integration of Hedera with identity tools like Credo and Bifold, aiming to provide customers with a quick start for building identity solutions on the Hedera network. Keith explained that while there are existing identity platforms like Micha and IDTrust, many customers seek guidance on getting started, which led to the development of these enabling tools. The conversation touched on the adoption of identity standards, with Keith noting that while there was anticipation for broad adoption of Akapai in certain regions, it has been mixed. Alexander mentioned that there is no official standard or tooling for Anancreds 2.0 yet, and the team is waiting for mass adoption or promise of adoption before pushing upgrades to 2.0 support.

### Hedera Identity Standards Discussion

The team discussed the potential adoption of Anonymous Credentials 2.0 on Hedera, with Keith suggesting a process similar to HIP762 where community support would drive investment in new standards. Alexander highlighted that Towngrids 2.0 is mentioned in their roadmap and promised to review recent updates on Anonymous Credentials 2.0. The discussion also covered Hedera's competitive advantages for decentralized identity use cases, including its reliability as a large and trusted network, and the introduction of new initiatives like the Hedera Identity Hub and Standards Repo.

### Hedera Network and Transaction Funding

Keith explained that while Hiero abstracts some aspects of the Hedera network, users still need HBAR to fund transactions on the network. He also mentioned that there are gas station projects that allow users to pay in fiat instead of holding HBAR. Baris requested a diagram showing the layers of protocols involved, and Keith suggested a future presentation on the details of non-creds. The conversation ended with Alexander and Diane encouraging attendees to reach out with any further questions.
