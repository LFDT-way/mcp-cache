## Details

### Meeting Duration Reduction Discussion

Sophie proposed reducing the duration of some meetings, particularly the Python SDK community call and office hours, to 30 minutes due to the time commitment of multiple meetings per week. Priyanshu agreed that reducing meeting time could help accommodate participants with full-time jobs. They discussed the possibility of making certain calls every other week to maintain efficiency.

### Enhancing Hashgraph Developer Engagement

The team discussed strategies to increase engagement with Hashgraph developers in the Hiero community. Sophie suggested that developers could present trainings or libraries they've built, which could be recorded and shared on YouTube. Antonio emphasized the goal of making Hashgraph more attractive to Hiero members and new contributors. The team also considered having Hashgraph developers join Hiero calls to share insights and best practices. They noted that not all repos are currently open to new contributors, which presents a challenge.

### Hashgraph Collaboration and Development

Hendrik expressed a preference for weekly, shorter meetings over biweekly ones to enable more dynamic responses to topics. Sophie clarified that the request for insights on Hashgraph's use of Hiero was not hers but came from Tech Brian. Priyanshu explained the need for better communication and collaboration with Hashgraph to improve development processes and address issues like the missing Python SDK in Hedera's playground. Hendrik clarified the distinction between Hedera as a product and Hiero as an open-source project, emphasizing that while both are developed by Hashgraph, decisions about resource allocation are made by Hedera's board. He suggested discussing the potential for Python to be included in Hedera's supported languages and encouraged open communication with Hashgraph for collaborative improvements.

### Hedera and Hashgraph Collaboration

Hendrik discussed the relationship between Hedera and Hiero, noting that while Hashgraph is involved in both, there are complexities due to Hedera's status as a company with its own products and identity. He emphasized that while Hedera listens to Hashgraph's input, the final decisions rest with the Hedera Council, and the goal should be to work together positively to gain more resources and influence. Hendrik also addressed questions about the Hedera playground, explaining that it's not part of Hiero and discussions should occur in the appropriate Discord channels.

### Hedera-Hiero Blog Coordination Plan

The team discussed coordination between Hedera and Hiero blog posts, particularly regarding a new JSON RPC relay version. Hendrik agreed with Rob's suggestion to better align blog content between the two communities, noting that while Hedera pays for developer relations, there's no restriction on sharing Hiero-specific content. They acknowledged that Sophie had previously discussed this with the developer relations team, and agreed that they should have a follow-up conversation to establish a better coordination process between the two blogs.

### DevRel Roles and Contributor Recognition

The team discussed the role and responsibilities of the Developer Relations (DevRel) team and Developer Advocates (DAs), with Sophie noting that while DevRel focuses on community engagement and education, there's a lack of clear onboarding processes for developers moving from Hedera to Hadoop. Hendrik and Rob clarified that DevRel handles community outreach while DAs work with enterprises, though there's some overlap in their responsibilities. The team also reviewed a list of six community contributors who will receive financial recognition for their work, with Hendrik explaining that this initiative will be ongoing and aims to support a diverse range of contributors each month.
