## Details

### European ZKP Discussion Overview

The meeting began with Stephen introducing the November 24th AnonCreds Working Group Meeting of 2025, noting that it was being recorded and governed by Linux Foundation policies. Stephen mentioned he wanted to discuss European ZKP efforts and shared that he had been gathering information on the topic. The meeting was brief, with no significant decisions or action items taken, and participants agreed to keep the discussion informal.

### MDL and Zero-Knowledge Proof Systems

Stephen discussed the adoption of MDL (Mobile Driver's License) as a single credential type and the potential use of Longfellow's zero-knowledge proof system for it. Mark expressed interest in seeing a proof of device position and the possibility of bridging across curves, mentioning a vision paper by Anya Lehman. They discussed the implementation of bridging BBS signatures with ECDSA in the doc network crypto repo, which Mark had recently discovered. Stephen noted that Christian and his team found Longfellow's EK too complex for broader adoption beyond MDL.

### Credential Systems: MDL vs BBS

The group discussed the advantages of using BBS for a broader ecosystem of credentials compared to Longfellows, which is better suited for MDL. Stephen emphasized that MDL will be universally adopted due to its use in various jurisdictions, while other credential types require a more flexible system like BBS, which offers unlinkability and pseudonymous identifiers. Mark highlighted the importance of separating cryptography from credential formats, and the group discussed the potential of targeting SDJOT with their abstraction to achieve unlinkability. They also touched on the need for attribute-level signing to enable features like predicates, equality, and verifiable encryption.

### Cryptographic Technologies Interoperability Discussion

Stephen and Harold discussed the development and implementation of cryptographic technologies, including equality proofs, verifiable encryption, and auditing capabilities. They emphasized the importance of creating an interoperable system that would allow different users and entities to utilize these technologies effectively. Stephen outlined the various components involved, such as message signing, attribute signing, and encoding, and expressed a desire to bring together the different teams working on BBS to ensure the technology's broad applicability and usability.

### BPS Format Standards Divide Discussion

The group discussed the state of BPS work, noting that while Harold knew only Lavesh, Mike, and Dan Yamamoto in this area, there were additional efforts in Europe and by Digital Bazaar and W3C. Stephen highlighted a divide in the community over the choice between JSON Web Proofs and W3C formats, with Digital Bazaar and W3C favoring the latter. Mark expressed concerns about mixing formats without proper abstraction layers, which aligns with their ongoing work.

### Attribute Signing Implementation Discussion

The group discussed technical details around attribute signing and data integrity, with Stephen explaining how Digital Bazaar's and Dan Yamamoto's implementations differ in their approach to normalizing and signing data. They explored the tension between JSON-LD requirements and abstraction needs, with Mark noting their organization is currently focused on settling into their new structure and awaiting permission to open source their non-creds implementation work. The conversation concluded with updates about a vulnerability fix being released and the planned creation of a new GitHub organization called AnonCreds to house their repos.
