## Overview

<p>The team explored various aspects of the project, including mobile wallet limitations, revocation mechanisms, and presentation requests, while also addressing the need for better documentation and testing. The conversation ended with discussions about project timelines, open source considerations, and future planning, with the team agreeing to reconvene in September.</p>

## Details

### Personal Updates and AI Concerns

Stephen, Mark, and Harold discussed personal matters, including Stephen's new paintings of his cabin in the Yukon and a horse farm in Ontario. They briefly touched on hunting moose with a shotgun, with Mark and Stephen agreeing it wouldn't be advisable. The conversation then shifted to Mark's concerns about being recorded and his dislike of AI, but no specific action items or decisions were made.

### Project Progress With Intern Rong

Mark reported progress on a project with intern Rong Sui, who is a PhD student at University of Rochester, noting they have developed non-creds v1 working over AC2C and DNC with attributes, hidden attributes, secrets, and predicates. Mark expressed concerns about time zone challenges affecting project efficiency, though they are making good progress despite suboptimal time usage.

### Test Improvements and Code Review

Mark and Stephen discussed the need to improve tests, particularly for witness updates after revocation. They identified a previously fixed bug related to revoking the last credential. Mark mentioned a large block of commented-out test code and expressed interest in contributing to the project. Stephen agreed to look into the commented-out code and its history. They also discussed the differences between tails files and accumulators in different systems, noting that their current implementation does not use tails files.

### Mobile Wallet File Size Limitations

The team discussed the limitations of mobile wallet file sizes, noting that a 3,000 file of 750 KB is too large for mobile use. Mark explained that the current test setup hard-codes revocation status lists, which needs to be improved to allow dynamic updates from the wallet. They also discussed progress on Anoncreds 2 revocation working with Anoncreds 1 credentials, which Harold confirmed is in development alongside support for Doc Network crypto. Stephen inquired about the potential for synonymous identifiers with Anoncreds 1, but the conversation ended before this question was fully addressed.

### Non-Creds Prototype Development Discussion

Mark and Stephen discussed the development and sharing of a prototype related to non-creds. Mark expressed hope to deliver the prototype to their internal product team soon and mentioned the possibility of contributing to the non-creds repo rather than the anon-creds repo. They agreed to have informal discussions about the project's progress and challenges, with Stephen offering to participate in such conversations. Mark noted that further steps, including writing a paper and seeking management and legal approval, would be necessary.

### Road Trip Planning and Revocation

Mark, Stephen, and Harold discussed Stephen's upcoming road trip from Whitehorse to Tuktoyaktuk, a 3,000-kilometer journey involving rough roads and camping. They also talked about Harold's past driving experiences and Stephen's newfound interest in an Irish Celtic band. The conversation then shifted to a discussion about revocation intervals, which Mark located in the documentation after some confusion.

### Credential Verification and Issuance Structure

The team discussed the structure of presentation requests and credential verification, focusing on how non-revoked intervals can be specified at different levels (attribute, predicate, or globally) and the flexibility of revocation checks. They clarified that issuance by default (issuance_by_default=True) is the only commonly used setting, as it simplifies the process by having credentials unrevoked by default rather than requiring explicit unrevocation. Mark shared a personal story about losing custom orthotics in a shoe store to illustrate methodical problem-solving, and the team agreed this approach was helpful for explaining complex concepts.

### Demo Timing and Open Source Strategy

Mark and Stephen discussed the timing of a demo presentation, with Mark expressing reluctance to commit to a specific date due to the delicate stage of the project. They agreed that getting the product group to recognize the project's importance before requesting open source status was a better approach. Stephen mentioned he would have a non-public developer-to-developer chat, and Mark noted that the internship's end would be a significant milestone for discussing open source possibilities with management. They decided to cancel the next meeting and reconvene in September.
