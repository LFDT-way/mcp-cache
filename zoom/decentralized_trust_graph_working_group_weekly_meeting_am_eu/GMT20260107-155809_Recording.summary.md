## Details

### Trust Graph Initiative Kickoff

The Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group held its first meeting of the new year, welcoming new members and providing an opportunity for introductions. Drummond explained that the group is a joint initiative of the Linux Foundation's Trust IP project and the Decentralized Identity Foundation. He reminded attendees about the Linux Foundation's antitrust policy and encouraged new members to introduce themselves. The meeting focused on welcoming new participants and setting the stage for future discussions.

### First-Person Project and H2H Summit

Drummond welcomed Christian Saucier to the Trust Over IP Group and provided an overview of the First-Person project, explaining its relationship with the standards work being done. Drummond announced a new conference called the H2H Summit on Human Agency, which will feature implementations of the First-Person project and is open to Linux Foundation members. The group discussed two new documents prepared by the Credentials Task Force and planned to review objectives for the quarter.

### Risk and Credentials Task Force Updates

The meeting focused on updates from the Risk Assessment Task Force and the Credentials Task Force. Scott Perry and Sankarshan discussed the progress made in transforming initial work into a format compatible with Trust over IP users and refining goals and deliverables. Drummond mentioned the absence of some members and the need to consider adding a GitHub repository for the Risk Assessment Task Force. Brendan inquired about the GitHub presence, and Scott Perry suggested sending comments or questions directly to him. The meeting also covered the H2H Summit site, with Scott Perry reminding everyone about the early bird fee deadline. Drummond and Scott Perry agreed to add the subject of a repo to the agenda for the next call. Finally, Drummond presented two new documents related to the Credentials Task Force, including a DTG glossary and a model for creating verifiable trust communities.

### Verifiable Credentials Framework Discussion

Drummond and Jeff discussed the categorization of verifiable credentials, including annotation, edge, and invitation credentials, with Jeff presenting a visual aid to clarify their relationships. Drummond explained the importance of edge credentials in defining relationships in a decentralized trust graph and mentioned the need to distinguish between different types of credentials. Hadrian inquired about schemas for aligning his models with this framework, and Drummond confirmed that such schemas would be provided in the upcoming DTG credential specification. Jonathan asked about the personhood credential, which Drummond clarified is a specific type of verifiable membership credential. Drummond also addressed Jonathan's question about the relationship between endorsement and relationship credentials, confirming that they are interconnected.

### Verifiable Credentials Structure and Terminology

The group discussed the structure and terminology of verifiable credentials, focusing on the distinction between verifiable relationship credentials (VRCs) and verifiable member credentials. Drummond explained that VRCs can use either relationship DIDs or membership DIDs, with the latter being necessary for proof of personhood. Steve mentioned his work on a taxonomy for agent systems in SpecupT, and the group acknowledged the need to address bootstrapping and standardize protocols for obtaining and exchanging credentials in future work. Adam raised a question about the use of VRCs without membership DIDs, which Drummond explained is for peer-to-peer relationships independent of trust communities.

### Verifiable Trust Networks Explained

The meeting focused on defining and explaining key concepts related to verifiable trust networks (VTNs) and verifiable trust communities (VTCs). Drummond and Brendan discussed the importance of protocols for establishing trust relationships and introduced the concept of verifiable trust agents to encompass various types of agents, including user, AI, and server agents. They emphasized the need for a clear understanding of local and cloud agents. The group reviewed a document outlining four phases for bootstrapping a VTC, motivated by the Linux Kernel Maintainer Verification Project, which aims to replace the current GPG web of trust with DTG credentials. Drummond encouraged attendees to review the documents and provide comments, noting that the glossary may become a separate deliverable.

### Bootstrapping Trust in Decentralized Graphs

Drummond explained the structure of a document outlining phases for bootstrapping policies in a decentralized trust graph. He described how personal network managers and verifiable trust agents interact to issue credentials and establish trust relationships. Drummond emphasized that individuals can choose their own MDIDs and reuse them across different trust communities, while communities can set their own policies for inviting trust anchors.

### Verifiable Trust System Documentation Review

The meeting focused on reviewing documentation for a verifiable trust system, including community growth policies and identity verification processes. Drummond explained that once a community reaches 65 members, they can use commercial identity verification providers to grow to thousands of contributors, with new members requiring invitations, two verifiable relationships, and approved identity credentials. The team discussed open issues including revocation mechanisms, zero-knowledge proofs, and document storage, with action items assigned to review the documents and provide comments over the next week. Carly suggested creating user stories to demonstrate the system's functionality, and Brian offered to test the system with his local Linux user group, while Drummond confirmed plans to work with Carly on creating implementation stories similar to her previous ORCID presentation.
