## Overview

The Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group held a meeting to discuss progress across multiple task forces and introduce new initiatives. Nicky presented tools for risk and harms assessment in specifications, which will be uploaded to GitHub in the coming weeks. The Credentials Task Force discussed terminology around entities and nodes, with Drummond clarifying definitions and identifying the need for further discussion about legal responsibility. ## Details

### Digital Cooperatives and Trust Systems

Drummond shared information about Nathan Schneider's work on digital cooperatives and decentralized trust systems, recommending his book "Governable Spaces." Steve announced he had posted the first draft of his paper "Fair Group Ratings" in the DTG Discord channel, which explores mathematical approaches to trust from different perspectives. The meeting was set to review updates from multiple task forces and discuss a new repository suggested by Martina, with a presentation on DTG-ZKP scheduled for the following week.

### Risk Assessment Toolset Presentation

Nicky presented a new toolset for risk and harms assessment in specifications, including personas, requirements specified as scenarios, epics, and user stories, and an AI-powered evaluation system using Claude. The toolset will be uploaded to GitHub within two weeks, starting with basic documentation and HTML files, with potential for future development of an assurance test suite. The team discussed how this toolset could be applied to other specifications and potentially developed into a specification itself, with Nicky planning to align more closely with Martina's interaction flows work.

### DTG Framework Entity Terminology Discussion

Drummond discussed terminology around entities and nodes in the context of credentials and the DTG (Digital Trust Graph) framework. He clarified that in the existing 500-term glossary, an "entity" is defined broadly as "someone or something that is known to exist," and a DTG node represents an entity participating in trust relationships with other nodes. When asked by Nico about the classification of VLEIs (Verifiable Legal Entity Identifiers), Drummond explained that VLEIs would be considered verifiable identifiers rather than VTCs (Verifiable Trust Credentials).

### VLEI Legal Liability Discussion

Drummond explained that VLEI (Verifiable Trust Community) is an identifier for entities with legal liability, specifically designed for businesses and organizations rather than individuals. He clarified that while people and VTCs can have legal liability, devices and AI agents cannot hold such responsibility. The discussion concluded with Drummond requesting confirmation from Martina or Brendan regarding this understanding of VLEI and legal liability.

### Trust IP Definitions Discussion

The team discussed clarifications needed around trust over IP definitions and node types, with Brendan raising concerns about distinguishing where responsibility stops versus where devices begin. Martina agreed that further clarification is needed but expressed some disagreement with Brendan's proposed approach. The group decided to schedule a new discussion among the three of them to address these concerns, with the location to be determined by Martina and Brendan. Drummond also announced that Glenn's TrustAS protocols spec now has a homepage thanks to Martina's help resolving CLA issues, and introduced a new S-Cards discussion topic for specialized data sharing mechanisms.

### Decentralized Ross Graph Repository Update

Martina presented a new umbrella repository for the Decentralized Ross Graph working group, which includes organized categories for discussions and information. The repository serves as a central hub for task forces and labs, with structured templates for questions and discussions. Drummond praised the GitHub discussions feature and emphasized its usefulness for managing and categorizing conversations. The team also discussed the importance of zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) for proving personhood and membership in VTCs without revealing personal data, and Drummond mentioned the introduction to the Privacy Stewards of Ethereum (PSE) team within the Ethereum Foundation.

### Privacy Division Strategy Discussion

The discussion focused on the evolution of the Privacy Scaling and Exploration (PSE) division at the Ethereum Foundation, which has been consolidated into a general privacy division. Otto explained that the current team specializes in zero-knowledge identity and is working on implementing the OpenAC library as an alternative to existing ZK proof frameworks. Drummond shared insights from recent discussions with vendors working on biometric verification, noting that they have identified the need for modern ZKP solutions to meet EU requirements, particularly around privacy-respecting liveness proof verification. The group agreed that Zoe from Hong Kong will present on ZKPs and OpenAC at the next meeting, which will be the first 30 minutes of the session, followed by Q&A.

### Anonymous Credentials and Terminology Clarification

The meeting focused on clarifying terminology around anonymous credentials and zero-knowledge proofs, with Drummond explaining the evolution from non-creds to OpenAC implementations. Carly announced a new Bite Size Trust GitHub page containing educational content, which Drummond agreed to promote in the announcement section. The group discussed the use of Discord channels for task forces, with only Credentials and Risk and Harms Prevention currently having dedicated channels. Drummond mentioned that the next meeting would begin with a presentation from the CKID team, and he planned to connect with Jeff and Martina about a template.
