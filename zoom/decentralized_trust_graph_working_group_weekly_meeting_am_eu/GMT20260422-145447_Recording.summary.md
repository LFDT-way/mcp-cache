## Overview

The meeting focused on updates from the Risk Assessment and Harms Prevention Task Force and a detailed presentation about Keyring, a new open-source wallet application developed by Brendan Miller and his team from the Applied Social Media Lab at Harvard University. Geoff provided an update on OpenVTC development, highlighting progress on the CLI tool and verifiable trust infrastructure, including security features like trusted execution environments. The Keyring team presented their solution for building verified, private, and portable digital identity connections, demonstrating a mobile app that enables users to create and verify relationship credentials through device biometrics, QR code scanning, and community witness verification. The team showcased different trust levels including full trust exchanges with biometrics and community connection, and discussed potential applications for social media verification and content provenance. The presentation included a live demo of the app's functionality and discussed future possibilities like Bluetooth-based presence verification and enhanced trust signals.

## Details

### Conference Planning and Toolkit Updates

Nicky chaired the meeting in place of Drummond and Martina, who were unable to attend. The group discussed upcoming conferences, including IIW42 from April 28-30 and the Agentic Internet Workshop on May 1st. Nicky announced that risk assessment and harms prevention toolkit assets have been uploaded to GitHub, with contributions and reviews welcome from the group.

### OpenVTC Development Progress Update

Geoff provided an update on the development of OpenVTC under LF Decentralized Trust Labs, focusing on two main repositories: the OpenVTC CLI tool and the verifiable trust infrastructure (VTI). Key developments included security improvements, the implementation of a GitHub signing module using DID keys, and progress on the terminal user interface. Geoff also highlighted the deployment capability of VTAs in trusted execution environments for enhanced security. The team aims to have an alpha testing version ready for the open source community within the next month.

### Decentralized Trust Graph Credentials Presentation

Brendan Miller from the Applied Social Media Lab at Harvard presented their work on implementing decentralized trust graph credentials for social media, focusing on three layers of trust signals: device biometric binding, in-person connection verification, and witnessed context. The team, including Alberto Leone and Nicole Brennan, demonstrated their approach to building privacy-preserving, portable identity systems that allow users to own and control their digital identities. Nicole explained the design principles of usability, transparency, and empowerment that guided the app's development, emphasizing that the complexity should be carried by the developers rather than the users.

### Keyring Wallet Trust Connections Demo

The team demonstrated the Keyring Wallet app, which enables trusted connections and credential exchanges between users. Alberto and Brendan presented three levels of trust connections: full trust exchange with biometrics and community connection, partial trust with optional biometrics, and non-community connections. The demo showed how users can connect through QR codes and verify relationships, with plans to explore additional trust signals like Bluetooth connectivity. The team discussed potential applications including social media verification and privacy-preserving reporting, with Mitchell suggesting innovative approaches using sound and video recordings for enhanced presence verification. Nicky expressed interest in connecting the work with risks and harms prevention efforts, planning to join a future working group call to discuss potential integration opportunities.
