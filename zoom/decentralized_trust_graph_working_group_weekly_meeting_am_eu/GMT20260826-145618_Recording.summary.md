## Details

### Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group

Drummond opened the August 26th meeting of the Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group, noting that Martina might be absent due to scheduling conflicts and that Nikki was on sabbatical. The group discussed a packed agenda including four task force reports and four special topics, with Glenn scheduled to present on kernel VTC onboarding flow and ZKP proof proposals. ### Specifications Progress and Planning Updates

The team discussed progress on multiple specifications, with Geoff reporting that several issues need review in the CredSpec repo and new spec repositories will be created for agent names, VDS, and VTI (a new "glue" spec to show how different specs fit together). The group confirmed that the Working Graph spec is now rendering properly after Rye Jones fixed the previous rendering issue. Drummond announced that the Virtual Private Knowledge Working Group will start on September 14, and the team noted an upcoming Linux Plumber Summit and LF Open Source Summit EU event in early October.

### ZKP Development Progress Update

Mitchell updated the team on the ZKP (Zero-Knowledge Proof) development, confirming they will create a specific ZKP for the kernel project first, then use that learning to develop broader proof of liveliness requirements. The team agreed to establish a ZKP spec repository alongside the existing trust taskforce repo, though no specific timeline was set for delivery. The group discussed upcoming meetings, including the GDC meeting next week without a call and planned inclusion of SanGEM and HART teams for September 8th to get cryptographer input on progress. Drummond also mentioned a real-world use case from New Zealand involving a woman's shelter that requires private identity verification for government assistance, which will be discussed in more detail on the September 8th call.

### GDC First Person Project Meetup

Drummond announced a First Person Project Meetup at GDC on March 31st from 1:30 to 5:00 PM, featuring live testing of OpenVTC and discussions about DTG progress and go-to-market plans. The team discussed two key topics: whether Verifiable Trust Networks (VTNs) should be defined as a separate node type, with Drummond now convinced it makes sense for reasons including sovereignty and efficient routing. The meeting also touched on the need to rename DTG credential DIDs, though the discussion was cut off at the end of the transcript.

### RDID Complexity Simplification Proposal

Glenn explained that there was significant confusion around RDIDs, PDIDs, MDIDs, and CDIDs in the OpenVTC development process due to overlapping purposes and scopes. He described how the complexity grew over time, particularly when integrating with VTCs and applying risk and harm prevention results. Glenn proposed simplifying the approach by focusing on the shape of graphs with different types of relationships, including pairwise connections, community DIDs, hybrid connections, and public relationships.

### Cross-Community Identity Management Terminology

The team discussed terminology for cross-community identity management, with Drummond and Glenn explaining that "persona" was the preferred term over alternatives like "linked" or "cross-context," as it best captures the concept of intentionally sharing identity across different communities while maintaining privacy controls. Glenn presented a Linux kernel community onboarding flow template showing four phases: community setup, founder onboarding, trust anchor invitations, and verified member joining, though he noted ZKP implementation was not yet included in this design. The discussion highlighted technical challenges around DID correlation and privacy preservation, with Grace raising concerns about the complexity of managing multiple DIDs, which Glenn acknowledged as a key design challenge that ZKP solutions aim to address.
