## Agenda

Goal: To define a set of best practices, templates, and guidance for establishing and evolving contributor ladders across LF Decentralized Trust (LF/DT) projects. This work will support community growth, contributor onboarding, and sustainable project governance.

## Overview

The Contributor Ladder Task Force meeting focused on discussing updates to the contributor repository, maintaining guidelines, and addressing navigation issues on the contributors page. The team explored content placement and organization across the Linux Foundation's Contributor site and main website, including guidelines for events and projects. They also discussed the importance of establishing governance structures for projects, the role of labs in contributing to the Contributor site, and the need for better ecosystem mapping tools to help new contributors navigate the complex landscape.

### Linux Foundation Content Strategy Discussion

Jessica and Diane discussed placing content on the Linux Foundation's Contributor site and main website. They explored adding guidelines and best practices for events and projects, such as Hacktoberfest and Project TSE. Jessica suggested publishing these guidelines in a specific section of the Contributor site to avoid crowding existing content. They also considered linking to specific sections of the charter for TSE, but Jessica decided against it to avoid potential errors in the document's structure.

### Early Governance for Decentralized Projects

Diane and David discussed the importance of having governance structures in place before projects are incubated, with Diane emphasizing the need for projects to think about their governance early on, especially in decentralized environments. They agreed that the labs process could help projects create community and establish governance structures. David noted that while Paladin had some community activities, they could consider evaluating governance requirements as part of the project proposal process.

### Labs' Role in Contributor Site

The group discussed the role of labs in contributing to the Contributor site and their involvement in running regular calls. David explained that while some labs do run regular calls, this is not a requirement for all projects, and their approach is to strongly encourage projects to do so while keeping it more optional for labs. Diane emphasized the need for a practical and pragmatic Contributor site that would provide useful tips and guidance for contributors, maintainers, and end users, while also linking back to more formal governance content on the TAC and tech sites. Jessica acknowledged Diane's point of view and understood the distinction between the governance-focused main LFDT site and the more practical Contributor site.

### Lab Growth and Mapping Challenges

Diane and David discussed the rapid growth of labs, including the ZKP Snarks lab and the new Longfellow-Z project, and agreed that Diane would write a blog post highlighting these developments. They also talked about the challenges of navigating the ecosystem and the need for better mapping tools for contributors. Diane mentioned her work on creating a landscape similar to the CNCF landscape for Hedera, HIRO, and partners, but David expressed concerns about the effectiveness of such a tool for new contributors.

### Improving Project Information Management

Diane discussed the need for a better board to manage and update information on Hedera Foundation's open-source, closed-source, and grantee projects, emphasizing the importance of making projects discoverable. She expressed interest in using the CNCF landscape model for self-reporting, which would allow projects to add themselves and update their information, reducing confusion and improving clarity. David agreed on the value of ecosystem maps but noted that the current CNCF map might not meet all needs, particularly for new users. The conversation also touched on the challenges of loading and using the current ecosystem map due to its size and complexity.

### Maintainers' Pages Content Update

Diane requested David and Rye to update the content on the maintainers' pages, particularly the operations and security sections, by adding more verbose and welcoming text. Jessica agreed to help with this task and clarified her understanding of the vision for the project. David mentioned he had been busy setting up new projects but would prioritize updating the content. The team also discussed a checklist Ry had created, which David approved for now while noting room for future iterations.
