## Agenda

Goal: To define a set of best practices, templates, and guidance for establishing and evolving contributor ladders across LF Decentralized Trust (LF/DT) projects. This work will support community growth, contributor onboarding, and sustainable project governance.

## Overview

The team explored using Deep Wiki to generate content for the Contributor Wiki site, with discussions around FAQ creation and content export capabilities. Diane presented updates on various documentation sections including welcome pages, project pages, and contributor guidance, while seeking feedback on her approach to creating comprehensive resources for new contributors. The team addressed challenges with content maintenance and organization, particularly regarding project selection pages and documentation formatting, with plans to simplify and centralize information while making it more accessible for end users.

## Details

### Deep Wiki for FAQ Generation

The team discussed using Deep Wiki to generate content for the Contributor Wiki site, with Diane exploring its capabilities to create FAQ content and links. Ry demonstrated how to run an MCP to export content in Markdown format, which could be useful for maintaining FAQ pages. Tracy joined the meeting after some delay and the group briefly discussed the potential of using Deep Wiki for generating answers to common questions about project status changes.

### Contributor Site Content Development Updates

Diane presented updates on content development for the Contributor site, including work on the welcome page, LFD Projects page, and various sections such as Start Contributing to Open Source and About. She highlighted the need for a page tailored for newbies and mentioned reworking content from the CNCF site with added attribution. Diane also discussed placeholders for the maintain and mentorship program pages, noting the need to better organize and link disparate content across different repos and sites. She invited feedback on her work and emphasized the importance of creating a comprehensive resource for contributors.

### Enhancing User Contribution Pathways

Diane presented her work on improving the documentation and user contribution process, focusing on creating a clear path for end users to contribute as adopters. She discussed adding new tabs and content to guide users based on their personas and mentioned drawing inspiration from her experience with the CNCF Contributor site. Diane sought feedback on her approach and mentioned plans to add more content, including options for contributing to labs and top-level projects. Tracy expressed support for the overall goal but raised concerns about the current mechanisms for user contribution.

### LFDT Project Content Management Challenges

Tracy and Diane discussed the challenges of maintaining and updating project content, particularly regarding the LFDT project page. Tracy expressed concerns about the difficulty of keeping the content up-to-date and the potential for creating duplicate information. Diane explained her intention to create a more accessible and searchable list of graduated projects, acknowledging that her initial choices for the labs section were not accurate. They agreed that the page would require minimal updates, mainly when new projects are added or existing ones change status. Tracy raised a concern about the selection of projects listed, noting that some projects might be left out, which could lead to dissatisfaction among project teams.

### Project Selection Page Improvements

Diane and Tracy discussed issues with a project selection page, particularly concerns about its completeness and the representation of graduated projects. They agreed to remove the labs section as it was incomplete, and Tracy pointed out that some projects' babies were not listed, which would cause upset. Diane acknowledged the need for a more static page that explains how to choose and understand projects, as there is currently a lack of beginner-friendly resources on the LFDT site. They discussed making the "Choose a Project" page the only dynamically maintained page, with other content being static and linked to from there.

### Documentation Updates and Content Organization

Diane and Tracy discussed updates to documentation and content organization. Diane plans to complete the Contribute section over the next week, including adding content to make it navigable and nitpickable. They agreed to embed existing links into the writing of each section, similar to what Tracy did on the Maintain page. Diane expressed interest in making the Deep Wiki tool helpful for generating content and organizing links.

### Centralized Documentation System Development

Diane and Ry discussed the development of a documentation system, with Ry expressing support for having a centralized "pool of pools" for information rather than siloed systems. Diane committed to fleshing out the Contributor section and home page content over the next few weeks, with Tracy providing critiques and updates. They discussed keeping the documentation simple and accessible, with a focus on end-user contributions and providing clear starting points for new contributors. Ry noted that combining governance and documentation in one place was an improvement over their previous system.

### MakeDocs Formatting Challenges Resolved

Diane and Ry discussed challenges with formatting documents using MakeDocs and Markdown, particularly with nested numbered lists. Ry spent time formatting the election site and encountered issues with MakeDocs converting third-level increments into code blocks. Tracy suggested using four spaces for indentation instead of two to resolve formatting issues in MakeDocs.
