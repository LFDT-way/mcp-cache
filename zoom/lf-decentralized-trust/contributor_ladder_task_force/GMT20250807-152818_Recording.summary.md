## Agenda

Goal: To define a set of best practices, templates, and guidance for establishing and evolving contributor ladders across LF Decentralized Trust (LF/DT) projects. This work will support community growth, contributor onboarding, and sustainable project governance.

## Overview

The team discussed meeting logistics and documentation methods, including the use of Google Docs for task force documentation and the development of a comprehensive contributing guide for the CNCF. They explored strategies for community development, focusing on different approaches needed to support projects based on their bandwidth for community work and the importance of tracking adopters and involving them in project maintenance. The group agreed to create templates for getting started guides and a new repository for contributor documentation, while discussing the need for better project templates and documentation on GitHub.

## Details

### Task Force Documentation Planning Meeting

The group discussed meeting logistics and documentation methods, with Diane presenting a draft agenda in the Discord channel. They agreed to use Google Docs for task force documentation, with Diane planning to append meeting minutes and agendas to the same document. The team noted that Arun would be joining later, and Diane shared her initial thoughts on a GitHub page about their work, which she had discussed with Arun and Peter in Discord.

### LFD Contributor Development Strategy

The team discussed developing a contributor ladder and improving community development processes for the LFD project. Diane proposed reviewing existing resources and creating a single landing page to help people find and use contribution information more easily. Tracy highlighted the need to update maintainer guidelines and best practices for finding contributors, while Char emphasized the importance of tracking adopters and involving them in project maintenance. David suggested reviewing and consolidating contribution information across different platforms. The group agreed to focus on three main aspects: showing people how to become contributors, growing contributors into maintainers, and managing adopters.

### Community Support Tracks for Projects

David discussed the different approaches needed to support projects based on their bandwidth for community work. He suggested two tracks for projects: one for those with community members who can handle tasks, and another for projects without such bandwidth. Diane proposed creating a space for end users under the CNCF model, similar to how they develop and promote end user communities. She also suggested using end user seats on technical steering committees to drive adopter participation.

### Open Source Community Development Challenges

The group discussed challenges in community development and resource allocation for open source projects. Diane shared her experience with having an end-user seat on the TSC for Hadoop, which helped increase community engagement. Arun suggested creating a comprehensive landing page for contributors similar to CNCF's model, while acknowledging that not all projects have active maintainers but do have active consumers. The discussion highlighted the need to differentiate between larger projects with established user bases and smaller projects that are still in early stages, as well as the importance of finding ways to support projects that lack technical capabilities.

### User Acquisition Strategies for Projects

The team discussed strategies for helping under-resourced projects attract and engage users. David suggested staff could help identify users and create getting started kits for smaller projects. Diane proposed creating a hierarchy of projects to distinguish between those with existing communities and those that need user acquisition. Tracy emphasized the importance of creating accessible and welcoming resources for end users, contributors, and maintainers. The group agreed to develop templates for getting started guides focused on end users, contributors, and maintainers to help projects at different stages.

### Hyperledger Documentation Template Discussion

Tracy shared a Hyperledger Labs documentation template and suggested expanding the getting started section to improve discoverability of resources. Diane highlighted the need for a central location to find documentation, similar to the CNCF's contribute page. David agreed and suggested revisiting the website, while also noting that not all projects are equal in terms of capabilities. He provided an example of a newer project, Credible, which could benefit from a contributing guide template to enhance their onboarding experience.

### GitHub Project Templates Discussion

The team discussed the need for better project templates and documentation on GitHub, with Tracy suggesting the creation of a template repository with a README that outlines essential information for new projects. David and Tracy noted that while they previously had wiki pages with templates, these were replaced when they shifted focus to GitHub. Diane mentioned that the group would continue meeting to discuss these topics and determine specific tasks, with Peter expected to join after the current week.

### CNCF Contributing Guide Development

Diane discussed the need to develop a comprehensive contributing guide for the CNCF, similar to the CNCF Contributing Guide, that would cater to different personas such as end users, new contributors, and maintainers. She emphasized the importance of making the guide accessible and useful for end users, as current resources are not well-suited for this audience. Diane also mentioned the need to finalize the definition of doc adopters and bring it up for a vote at the next DAC meeting. She proposed creating a diagram to outline project personas and levels to identify missing content. Tracy agreed with Diane's approach and suggested bringing the updated proposal to the DAC for approval.

### Contributor Documentation Repository Planning

The team discussed creating a new repository for contributor documentation, with Arun volunteering to create a Labs proposal by the end of the day. Diane emphasized the importance of making existing resources discoverable and suggested incorporating examples like the Firefly graphic and videos similar to those used by CNCF. The group agreed to give limited access to the documentation to the Tac pro team and others for feedback while keeping it private. Char was asked to review existing LFD materials and add any relevant templates or best practices to the new documentation.

### Open Source Contributor Documentation Strategy

The team discussed the placement and organization of adopter definitions, with Tracy confirming they are under governance documents. They explored the idea of having a centralized location for all contributor and maintainer documentation, with Diane suggesting it could serve all three personas and include templates and outreach guidance. The group also discussed maintainer retention challenges, particularly for projects like Cacti losing IBM support, and Diane shared insights about paid open source models from her blockchain experience, suggesting potential funding models through end-user organizations. The conversation ended with plans to reconvene after the TSC meeting next week, with Tracy and Diane to prepare adopter documentation and Char tasked with researching existing resources.
