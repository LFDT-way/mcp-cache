## Details

### TAC Meeting: Reports and Updates

The TAC meeting began with Tracy welcoming members and reminding them about antitrust policy and code of conduct requirements. Several quarterly reports were discussed, including Q3 reports that needed review and overdue reports from Bevel and Trust Over IP. Diane mentioned that Rama would be ready to submit the Trust Over IP Q3 report next week, and Arun confirmed they would follow up on the Bevel report due August 7th. Jessica reported that she had worked with Hendrik on Hacktoberfest guidance and good first issues, which were now public in HIRA with associated labels across repos.

### Hacktoberfest: Encouraging Open Source Contributions

Hendrik explained the Hacktoberfest initiative, a month-long event organized by DigitalOcean and supported by GitHub and the Linux Foundation, which aims to encourage new contributions to open-source projects. He highlighted that HIRO has participated in the event for the past two years, creating "good first issues" that attract new contributors. Hendrik emphasized the importance of creating meaningful issues to retain contributors, noting that a few individuals who found HIRO through Hacktoberfest continue to contribute to the project. He also mentioned a new workflow for this year's participation and a blog post on the HIRO webpage detailing the initiative.

### Good First Issue Guidelines Update

Hendrik presented new guidelines for creating good first issues, including a template and structure requirements. He explained that maintainers and committers should label potential issues as "good first issue candidate" before a weekly Monday meeting where community members review and approve suitable issues. The initiative has already identified 79 potential good first issues, with the goal of making them more accessible and understandable for new contributors.

### Hectoberfest Issue Tracking Updates

Hendrik presented updates on preparations for the Hectoberfest, including the creation of a candidate issue with a description and solution, and the development of a website showcasing "good first issues" categorized by programming languages. He mentioned plans to add non-code labels and a feature to highlight contributions from random contributors. Peter commended the idea of linking to similar pull requests, which he found particularly useful for contributing. Tracy inquired about the different levels of good first issues implemented in the Cacti project, to which Peter expressed willingness to share details.

### Cacti Repository Issue Categorization

Peter explained how they categorize issues in the Cacti repository for Hacktoberfest, breaking them down into beginner, intermediate, advanced, and expert levels. He noted that they specifically label winter warning fixes as beginner issues to help new contributors build confidence. Hendrik suggested adding descriptions to these categories and ensuring all introductory issues are labeled as "good first issues" to make them easier to find. Tracy and Matthew discussed the effectiveness of this categorization method for onboarding new contributors, with Peter sharing that it has been successful for several years and provides a lasting pool of issues for new contributors.

### Issue Categorization and Expertise Matching

The team discussed strategies for categorizing and labeling issues based on complexity and expertise levels. Peter shared insights from the Cacti project, highlighting its high complexity due to multiple languages and ongoing modernization efforts. They explored the idea of creating "good first issues" and suggested using labels to indicate different levels of expertise required. Tracy and Hendrik contributed ideas on how to match issues with the skills of potential contributors, including language-specific challenges and non-code contributions like documentation and video tutorials.

### Open Source Contributor Incentives

The team discussed strategies to attract and reward new contributors to open source projects, including creating "getting started" videos, offering custom merchandise, and implementing a paid open source model. Hendrik shared his experience with a project that provides financial incentives for new contributors who solve their first issue, while Diane mentioned the Hedera Foundation's exploration of a paid open source module to support work on HIRO and related projects. The group agreed that financial compensation, in addition to merchandise incentives, could be an effective way to encourage participation and retain contributors.

### Contributor Guidelines and Project Updates

The team discussed progress on contributor guidelines and a potential aggregated "good first issue" frontend for LFDT projects, with Hendrik suggesting this as a future goal for Hectoberfest. Diane reported on updates to the contributor site, which now includes navigation and content for user, mentorship, and maintainer sections, and requested feedback through PRs before sharing with maintainers. Matthew provided an update on the Project Consistency Task Force, which is working on identifying areas of inconsistency and will set timelines for their proposal to the TAC. The team also discussed the need to reschedule a contributor ladder task force meeting due to scheduling conflicts.
