The Technical Advisory Council meeting covered two main announcements. The second announcement reminded participants about the upcoming November 13 voting deadline for the 2026 TAC election, which will be conducted through the Helio voting platform using GitHub credentials. ### TAC Quarterly Reports and Planning

The TAC discussed several key topics, including the status of quarterly reports and the need for better communication regarding project dormancy. Arun noted that while most reports were on track, there was a pending request from Fabric for additional maintainers, which the TAC agreed to address in next week's discussion. The group also reviewed the process for handling overdue reports, with Matthew highlighting the importance of clear communication when projects are considered for dormancy. Finally, the TAC began a year-end retrospective, discussing their goals for integrating standard projects like Trust over IP into the community, with plans to review progress, challenges, and areas for improvement in the coming discussions.

### Challenges in Standards Lifecycle Management

The group discussed challenges in measuring and managing the lifecycle of standards projects, particularly Trust Over IP (TOIP). Diane noted that standards work progresses more slowly than code contributions and suggested sending an emissary from the TAC to better understand standards operations. Ramakrishna shared insights from his experience with IETF standards, highlighting the importance of charters and the multi-year process of developing specifications. The discussion concluded with agreement that the TAC could play a role in connecting TOIP with the broader community, though Matthew noted this support might be more indirect and long-term rather than specific, tangible examples.

### Standards Development and Maintainer Support

The TAC meeting focused on two main topics: standards development processes and maintainer support. Ramakrishna explained the stages of standards development in the IETF, including the formation of Birds of a Feather groups, draft maturity, and the requirement for working code implementations. Diane emphasized the importance of tracking participation metrics in standards groups and suggested developing better tools for reporting community engagement. The group discussed challenges faced by new maintainers, particularly in complex projects, and agreed that more training resources, pair programming opportunities, and hackathons could help address these issues. They also noted that maintaining diversity in project participation remains a challenge for some projects.
