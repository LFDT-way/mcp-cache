## Overview

The LF Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting covered several administrative announcements including the start of new term nominations and upcoming workshops. The main focus of the meeting was a discussion about bringing Paladin, a privacy-focused blockchain project, into the LFDT incubation program, with detailed presentations about its features and potential paths for adoption and development. After addressing concerns about licensing and potential overlaps with existing projects, the council voted to accept Paladin/Zeto as an incubated project under LFDT.

## Details

### LF TAC Meeting: Announcements and Proposals

The LF Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting covered several announcements, including the start of the 2026 TAC term nomination period, an upcoming workshop on cross-network interoperability, and the opening of Maintainer Days registration. Tracy reminded attendees about the antitrust policy and code of conduct, and Arun was tasked with following up on overdue quarterly reports. The conversation ended with a discussion of a project proposal to move Paladin to incubation, which Matthew presented.

### Paladin-Zeto LFDT Merger Proposal

The meeting focused on a proposal to bring together two independent labs, Paladin and Zeto, under one Linux Foundation Digital Trust (LFDT) project banner. Matthew provided an overview of Paladin, highlighting its role as a programmable privacy framework for EVM blockchains, supporting both notarized and zero-knowledge tokens. Peter emphasized Paladin's focus on enterprise needs for privacy, explaining its modular approach to enable open ecosystems and future-proof solutions. Jim was poised to discuss Zeto further, but Tracy interjected, indicating she wanted to address something first.

### Paladin Blockchain Development Update

The meeting discussed the adoption and development of Paladin, a privacy-focused blockchain project. Peter explained that while there is significant independent adoption of Paladin, particularly among large financial institutions, the project is moving to an incubation stage to focus on hardening and scaling for enterprise use. He outlined several potential paths for adopters to become maintainers, including contributing new modules, improving packaging and deployment, or integrating with other chains. Peter also highlighted a collaboration with researchers from UC Berkeley and UNC Chapel Hill that implemented post-quantum encryption within a zero-knowledge proof circuit.

### Paladin Project Incubation Approval

The meeting focused on the discussion and acceptance of the Paladin project proposal for incubation status. Peter, Matt, and Andrew highlighted the need to enhance documentation for building privacy technologies on Paladin, emphasizing the importance of addressing enterprise and end-user needs. Tracy raised questions about potential overlaps with existing projects in the Ethereum ecosystem and the implications of using the LGPL-licensed CIRCOM library for LFDT incubation. Hart clarified that the CIRCOM code would not be included in LFDT, and the linking exception likely poses no issues. The team discussed key management and privacy features of Paladin, with Matthew outlining plans to expand the community and governance structure beyond Kaleido. The motion to accept Paladin/Zeto as an incubated project was passed with a majority vote.
