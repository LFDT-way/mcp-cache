## Overview

<p>The TAC reviewed various project updates including reporting deadlines, community engagement initiatives, and Trust Over IP developments, while also discussing the need for improved communication with project maintainers and better enforcement of project goals. The conversation ended with plans for future presentations and transition discussions for next year's TAC members, along with agreement to review and potentially relabel a draft document about DCO and CLAs.</p>

## Details

### Travel Plans and Housing Insights

Tracy and Ry discussed their current locations and travel experiences, including Tracy's upcoming move to Spain and her plans to sell her house after Christmas. They also talked about the housing market in different areas, with Ry sharing her experience of rapid housing development in her neighborhood. The conversation briefly touched on the quality of cell phone service in Europe compared to the US.

### LF TAC Leadership Nominations Due

The LF Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting began with Tracy explaining the antitrust policy notice and code of conduct requirements. Tracy announced that nominations for TAC chair and vice chair positions are due by December 12th, with elections to follow the next week. She also mentioned the developer newsletter and maintainer days event in January, noting that registration is open and includes free entry to the Member Summit.

### DOIP Reporting Format Discussion

Tracy clarified that project maintainers are eligible for free attendance at the Member Summit, while prospective maintainers can attend Maintainer Days. Arun reported that the Trust Over IP Q3 report is overdue due to confusion about the new reporting format, and Tracy suggested inviting DOIP members to a future TAC meeting to address these concerns. Diane offered to co-meet with Arun and the Trust Over IP team to help clarify reporting expectations and assist with the new template.

### Q4 Reporting and Goals Review

The team discussed upcoming reporting deadlines for Q4 reports from various projects. Tracy noted that an open PR from Matthew would be postponed until he returns. Hendrik requested time to present on HIRO, which Sophie estimated would need 15 minutes plus 5 minutes for discussion. Tracy mentioned she would like to conduct a retrospective using the retrospective tool before the next governing board meeting. The team then reviewed 2025 goals, particularly focusing on whether the lifecycle fits the needs of all project types for LFDT, with Tracy noting this remains an open item for the TAC to continue working on.

### GitHub Project Progress Review

The team discussed progress on a GitHub repository project, which has been moved from labs and is now ready for review with blank security and operations sections remaining. Diane plans to lead a workshop at maintainer days to gather feedback and will follow up with a social campaign to increase awareness. Arun mentioned improvements to the mentorship program, including allowing multiple mentees per mentor and accepting unpaid voluntary mentorships, though questions remain about compensation for mentees.

### Community Engagement and Onboarding Initiatives

The group discussed several initiatives to increase community engagement and onboarding, including compensation for mentees through conference participation and the adoption of "good first issues" processes under HIRO. They reviewed efforts to create entry points for new community members, with Peter highlighting a Discord channel initiative to help users overcome Git and GitHub challenges. The team also discussed the need to better connect Trust Over IP with the broader community, noting that while some collaboration had begun, there was still work to be done in sharing knowledge and understanding between projects.

### Trust Over IP Project Updates

The TAC discussed several Trust Over IP initiatives, including the need for project leads to present their annual and 6-month reviews to enhance engagement and knowledge sharing. They noted that while some projects are incorporating Trust Over IP work, there are no specific implementations available yet. The group also touched on cross-project collaboration efforts, particularly highlighting HIRO's work and the Indian Besu implementation, and identified the need to develop project onboarding guidelines for DCO and licensing. An open item for the next TAC will be exploring ways to gather end-user feedback for their projects.

### Project Management and Contributor Challenges

The team discussed several topics related to project management and community engagement. Arun suggested holding quarterly meetings for project maintainers to update adopters on project roadmaps, which Tracy found interesting but noted challenges in identifying adopters. They also discussed the need for consistent approaches to reporting problems and challenges to the TAC, with Tracy mentioning the creation of a backlog system for such reports. Peter raised concerns about the complexity of the DCO (Developer Certificate of Origin) process, noting that it remains a challenge for new contributors despite previous optimizations. The team agreed to bring the work on guidelines for determining whether code is a lab or part of an existing project to the TAC for broader application across projects.

### DCO and CLA Document Review

The TAC members discussed a draft document about DCO and CLAs that was previously written but not published, due to concerns about providing legal advice. David and Hart explained that they had sought legal review but encountered roadblocks, with lawyers unable to provide the desired guidance. The group agreed to review the existing draft internally and consider relabeling it as non-legal advice, with the aim of sharing it with the TAC. Tracy also announced that a retrospective discussion would be postponed to a future meeting, as time ran short for the planned agenda items.

### TAC Retrospective and Project Improvements

The TAC meeting focused on reviewing the retrospective tool and discussing what went well and what could be improved. Tracy highlighted the need for better communication with project maintainers and suggested assigning a TAC member to each project to ensure goal progress is being made. The group agreed to improve quarterly reviews and enforce project goals more strictly. Tracy announced plans for future meetings, including Sophie's presentation on maintainers and a transition session for next year's TAC members.
