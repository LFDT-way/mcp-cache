## Overview

The team discussed repository management challenges and personal updates before transitioning to the LF Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting, which covered various announcements including the start of 2026 TAC nominations and upcoming workshops. The council reviewed and approved several project reports, including moving Hyperledger Bevel to a dormant stage and discussing the Trust Over IP annual report with suggestions for improvement. The conversation ended with discussions about project evaluation criteria and the potential incubation of the Paladin Lab, along with guidance on proposal submissions and creating dedicated discussion channels.

## Details

### Repository Integration and Project Boundaries

The team discussed the complexity of managing multiple repositories, particularly regarding the integration of Minakawa and Night Stream projects. Ry expressed confusion about the unclear boundaries between the two projects and their respective repositories, noting ongoing discussions with Sebastian, the maintainer of Halo 3 or 4. Tracy and Ry also briefly touched on personal topics, including Tracy's upcoming move and Ry's past experiences with moving and decluttering. The conversation ended with greetings to new participants and a note about missing members.

### LF TAC Meeting: Announcements and Votes

The LF Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting covered several key announcements, including the start of the 2026 TAC nomination period on October 14th and a workshop on cross-network Interop with Hyperledger Cacti on November 12th at 8am PT/5pm CET. The meeting addressed the need for six reviewers for quarterly reports, with one report pending updates from maintainers. The council voted to move Hyperledger Bevel to a dormant stage after receiving a request from maintainers, with all voting members supporting the motion.

### Project Lifecycle and Report Updates

Tracy discussed the project lifecycle, noting that moving projects to dormant or end-of-life status is a normal part of the process and not a final decision. She mentioned that Rama requested an extension for the Hyperledger Cacti report, which was granted. Tracy also highlighted upcoming reports for Identists, Web3J, Hyperledger Indy, and Hyperledger non-creds. Diane and Rama presented the Trust Over IP annual report, which included strengths and areas to watch, and suggested developing a new template for standards initiatives under LFDT.

### Enhancing Transparency and Contribution Metrics

Diane reviewed the annual report of a diverse organization with a membership model, noting its strong foundational work and steady participation in working groups. She suggested adding more metrics, clear paths with target dates, and better task force outcomes to avoid fragmentation. Diane recommended increasing GitHub usage to make the project more transparent and open, addressing the current high contribution concentration among a few members. She also highlighted the need for better attribution of contributions to organizations and suggested moving white papers to GitHub for easier commenting and participation.

### Trust Over IP Project Review

The TAC reviewed the annual and quarterly reports for the Trust Over IP project, which is currently incubating. Diane and Ramakrishna noted the project's active working groups and expanding activities, while suggesting improvements such as better GitHub organization and navigation aids. Tracy clarified that the 2024 report should be renamed to reflect the 2025 Q1 review period. The group discussed the project's status and potential graduation criteria, with Diane mentioning the need for specification maturity. Tracy reminded the group of their goal to create a task force to address project lifecycle changes for non-code projects in LF Decentralized Trust.

### LF Decentralized Trust Project Graduation Criteria

The TAC discussed criteria for evaluating and graduating standards projects within LF Decentralized Trust, with Ramakrishna suggesting that a published standard with implementation could serve as a graduation criterion. Tracy highlighted the need for structured review processes and collaboration with the Trust Over IP technical steering committee, while Hart emphasized that projects should not be judged based on their least mature components. The group agreed that more work is needed to define healthy project characteristics and determine appropriate governance structures for different project stages, with Tracy noting that the current project lifecycle model may need revision.

### Trust Reports Approval Meeting

The team discussed and approved both the annual and quarterly trust over IP reports, with Diane making the motion and Matthew seconding. There was some discussion about the process for appending assessments to the reports, with Ramakrishna agreeing to make the necessary modifications after the reports are approved. The vote was unanimous among the present team members, with Peter and Wendy absent.

### Paladin Lab Incubation Proposal Discussion

Matthew presented a sneak preview of a proposal to bring the Paladin Lab into incubation, along with its related project Zeto. Tracy advised that a complete proposal template should be used, even though some information may be reusable from previous proposals. They discussed the timing of submitting the proposal, with Tracy suggesting that if submitted early next week, it might be best to hold off on discussion until the following TAC call. Tracy also mentioned creating Discord threads for discussions on project reviews and late project updates, with a two-month timeline suggested for projects to indicate readiness for dormancy.
