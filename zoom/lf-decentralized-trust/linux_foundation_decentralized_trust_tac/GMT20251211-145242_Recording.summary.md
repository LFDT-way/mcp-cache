### Python SDK's Hacktoberfest Success

Sophie presented on the Python SDK repository in Hiro, highlighting its growth and success during Hacktoberfest. She discussed how the project attracted 39 new contributors and saw a 660% increase in developer activity, largely due to implementing "good first issues" labels and providing clear, automated onboarding processes. Since Hacktoberfest, the team has automated initial maintainer tasks, tested Copilot for reviews, and created a training course, while also establishing a new triage role to help nurture new maintainers. Sophie concluded that good first issues have been effective in bringing new contributors to the project.

### Automated Onboarding System Success

Sophie presented on the success of their automated onboarding system for new contributors, which has reduced the need for manual curation to 10% of what it was in October. The system includes 6 merged bots and 6 pull request checks, with 85% of automation complete. The team has focused on creating good first issues through example changes rather than feature changes, as examples require less testing and are easier for new contributors to understand. Sophie emphasized that successful good first issues should be simple, well-documented, and not require testing, while avoiding tasks that pull in multiple LT-specific functionalities. The team plans to generalize their documentation and training processes for use across other LFDT projects, starting with Hiro and Digital Identity initiatives.
