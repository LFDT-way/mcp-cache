## Overview

The TAC meeting addressed concerns about project review processes, with Tracy emphasizing the importance of timely quarterly reports and the TAC voting to keep Lochness in incubation status while rejecting their request to skip reporting requirements. Hendrik presented Hiro's graduation request, highlighting improvements in maintainer documentation, OpenSSF scores, and contributor diversity, though questions remained about maintainer diversity for the core consensus node. The TAC identified challenges with OpenSSF scorecard reporting for projects with multiple repositories and agreed to further discuss these limitations next week, as Hiro is the first project to test the graduation criteria.

## Details

### TAC Report Review Challenges

Tracy expresses disappointment with the TAC's poor performance in reviewing quarterly reports, noting that many from June remain unreviewed despite the two-week review period requirement. She urges members to be more diligent and set up GitHub notifications to stay informed of submissions. Regarding the Lochness annual report, after discussion between Ramakrishna and Char, the TAC votes to approve it and keep the project in incubation status. When discussing a request from Lochness to skip a quarterly report, Tracy and Diane argue against allowing this as quarterly reports are important for tracking project progress and identifying problems early.

### Project Reporting and Graduation Updates

Tracy explains that project updates are used for governing board meetings, event updates, and to inform people about project accomplishments. The TAC agrees that projects should not be allowed to bypass quarterly reports, emphasizing that reports don't need to be perfect and can be completed quickly. Ramakrishna will communicate to Dennis that both Q2 and upcoming Q3 reports are expected. Diane raises concerns about Trust over IP's overdue reports since March, and Wenjing explains they've been working on adapting the reporting format. Tracy suggests Trust over IP present to the TAC in September to discuss their progress. Hendrik then presents Hiro's graduation request, explaining they've addressed previous feedback on maintainer documentation and OpenSSF scorecards, and have included diversity metrics from Bitergia.

### Contributor Diversity and Onboarding Progress

Hendrik reports that Hiro has successfully onboarded new companies as contributors, including maintainers from Lime Chain, 8beef, and Hashgraph Online, with plans to further increase contributor diversity. The project has achieved OpenSSF scores of 7+ for code repositories, and the Hiro codebase is now used by multiple networks beyond Hedera, including Hash Sphere which creates private networks for business customers. Tracy raises concerns about maintainer diversity for the core consensus node, to which Hendrik and Diane explain that while most consensus node work is still done by Hashgraph Inc., they are actively working to train and onboard more diverse contributors, with Diane emphasizing Hedera's strong commitment to funding engineering resources for the core components.

### Identos Network Identity Graduation Challenges

Hendrik discusses ongoing discussions with Identos about running identity on a Hyro-based network, which may lead to contributions to the consensus node, though he cannot guarantee this. Tracy raises concerns about the project graduation requirements, specifically the Open SSF scorecard details that need to be addressed before moving forward. Jessica and Hendrik explain that they face challenges with Open SSF reporting as there isn't a way to merge scorecards for multiple sub-projects or report at a granular level. The TAC agrees that Jessica will provide an overview next week of what reporting is possible and what limitations exist, as this is the first project to test these graduation criteria.
