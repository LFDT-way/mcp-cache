## Overview

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council (TAC) held a meeting on May 7th to discuss various project-related topics. The meeting covered announcements including a developer newsletter and a new project proposal from Angelo, as well as updates on annual reports and lab proposal reviews. A significant portion of the discussion focused on the Digital Content Ownership (DCO) sign-off process, where participants shared feedback and suggestions for improving documentation and troubleshooting guidance. The TAC also reviewed the adopters file concept, discussing challenges with maintaining up-to-date information and exploring alternatives such as linking to a centralized use case tracker maintained by LFDT staff. The conversation ended with a brief review of the Paladin project report, with general approval but feedback requested for future reports to include more detailed content.

### Linux Foundation Technical Advisory Council

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting was held on May 7th. Arun announced a developer newsletter opportunity and a new project proposal from Angelo, requesting council members to review it. Annual reports were discussed, with most received except for Minokawa and some pending from other projects. David flagged that OptiMesh had changed its name to Veltora and requested the council to review the proposal, noting it had been pending for about 4 weeks.

### RISC-V EVM Replacement Proposal

Arun discussed a project proposal for replacing EVMs with RISC-V architecture, and confirmed that team members had reviewed the proposal. The main discussion centered on the TCO sign-off process, where David explained that the legal team had provided clarity on why the sign-off is required and shared documentation to address common questions from contributors. Angelina clarified that the DCO issues were primarily raised by new contributors having trouble with GPG workflows and cherry-picking commits, leading to the request for better documentation and troubleshooting guidance.

### DCO Implementation and Updates

The team discussed DCO (Developer Certificate of Origin) implementation and feedback. Ry explained ongoing work to remove the original DCO app and replace it with DCO2, which better handles merge queues. Hendrik suggested making DCO failure messages more visible to new contributors by adding comments in PRs rather than just in GitHub status checks. The team agreed to ask project teams to include DCO information in their contributing guidelines and to follow up with the Peso team for additional feedback. Regarding mid-year reports, the team decided to approve the current proposal and address format and expectations for future reports later, after implementing the two reports per year requirement.

### Adopters Database Management Discussion

The team discussed challenges with maintaining adopters files in project repositories, where some projects expressed difficulty in keeping information current and accurate. David suggested linking to an existing use case tracker maintained by staff, which contains over 300 recorded use cases across LFDT projects. Hendrik proposed creating a centralized adopters database managed at the LFDT level, with a user-friendly form for organizations to self-report their usage. The group agreed to invite Tomasz, who maintains the use case tracker, to a future meeting to discuss technical capabilities and implementation options. Regarding project reports, Diane provided positive feedback on the Paladin report, noting it was well-supplemented and contained good forward-looking information, though she suggested requesting more detail and OpenSSF badging for future reports.
