## Overview

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council (TAC) meeting focused on project updates, lab proposals, and guidelines. Arun announced upcoming changes to reporting cadence, moving from quarterly to biannual reports, and requested TAC member feedback on two pending lab proposals, including RET5. Tomaz presented the adopters file tracking system, explaining how use cases are collected and managed in Airtable, with 315 use cases currently documented. The team discussed challenges with DCO (Developer Certificate of Origin) sign-offs, particularly regarding friction in browser-based contributions and IDE environments. Matthew raised concerns about the mechanics of DCO implementation and its impact on development workflows. The conversation ended with a discussion of AI contribution guidelines, where Matthew suggested separating mandatory requirements from best practices recommendations, with plans to continue this discussion in the next meeting.

## Details

### Project Updates and Future Planning

The meeting began with welcoming participants and announcements. Arun announced the developer newsletter opportunity for project updates and the Hyperledger Token SDK proposal under review. Sean shared information about an upcoming Linux Project workshop on ZK roll-up stacks. Arun also mentioned a request for anonymous feedback on what's working well and improvements for the rest of the year, with a link shared on retrotool.io and Discord. The discussion then moved to potential topics for future meetings, including annual reports and updates on the Solang team's grant status and new lab proposal.

### TAC Reporting and Lab Proposals

The TAC discussed changes to reporting cadence, moving from quarterly to biannual reports with mid-year reviews. Two lab proposals were reviewed, with the TAC clarifying that lab proposals generally follow a lower barrier for approval compared to full projects, requiring only review rather than voting. The group agreed that TAC members should review the pending lab proposals offline and provide feedback, with a suggestion to document the lab approval process more clearly in governance documents. The conversation ended with plans to discuss the adopters file next, followed by project report reviews.

### Airtable Use Case Tracker Process

Tomaz explained the process for updating the Airtable use case tracker, which contains 315 use cases from various projects. He described how new use cases are added through a public Google form, with data sourced from case studies, webinars, news releases, and company announcements. Tomaz noted that while the tracker allows for filtered views and CSV exports, maintaining current data is challenging due to projects changing status or shutting down without notification. Diane confirmed that a recent Hashgraph case study about Australian payment plans had already been added to the tracker.

### Hedera Network Adopters Review Process

Diane and Tomaz discussed the process for reviewing Hedera network adopters and updating the adopters.md file. Tomaz agreed to check the file and add relevant use cases, with Diane offering help from Jessica. They also discussed the addition of Acacia to the tracker, which had received approvals from both the App and Reserve Bank of Australia. Diane explained that adopters.md entries require a GitHub pull request from authorized representatives of the organization. Arun mentioned upcoming discussion points regarding organizations willing to disclose their use cases but not willing to share details.

### DCO and AI Guidelines Discussion

The team discussed challenges with the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) implementation, particularly around practical issues with browser-based sign-offs and IDE limitations. David suggested documenting common mechanical issues and their resolutions, which could be added to the legal team's recently created DCO guidance page. The conversation then shifted to AI contribution guidelines, where Matthew and others raised concerns about the relationship between "assisted by" and "signed off by" in DCO context, and the need to clearly distinguish between mandatory requirements and best practices in the guidelines document. The team agreed to separate the guidelines into two documents - one for mandatory requirements and another for suggestions, to be reviewed in next week's meeting.
