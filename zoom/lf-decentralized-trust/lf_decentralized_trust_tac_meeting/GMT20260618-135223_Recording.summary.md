## Overview

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council (TAC) held a meeting on June 18, 2026, where members discussed several project reviews and reports. The meeting began with attendance checks, revealing quorum challenges with only 5 out of 11 members present. The TAC reviewed the Trust Over IP (TYP) annual report, with Diane expressing concerns about the lack of detailed links and evidence supporting the claims made in the report. ## Details

### Meeting Announcements and Updates

The meeting began with participants checking attendance and noting that quorum would not be reached due to several regrets and absences. Arun opened the meeting and provided announcements about the antitrust policy and the weekly newsletter opportunity for project updates. The group then discussed upcoming annual reports and the Trustor IP, though Hendrik and Marcus were absent, limiting discussion on those items.

### PR Review and Reporting Process

The team discussed voting on a PR that required additional review, with concerns raised about addressing Marcus's comments and code quality issues. Arun noted that while Henrik approved the report and the project team had responded to comments, Enrique pointed out that several previous concerns about code quality and readmes remained unaddressed. The meeting then shifted to discussing the TYP report, where Arun reported that the TSC team explained they don't require written reports from working groups but instead collect highlights in a shared document. The TSC team indicated they would consider implementing a Google form process for working group updates, though there appeared to be a disconnect between TAC's expectations and the TSC's current approach.

### Trust Over IP Report Concerns

Diane expressed concerns about the current annual report from the Trust Over IP standards group, noting that it lacks sufficient links and references to support the claims made, making it difficult for reviewers to verify the information. The group discussed that while the report is not negative, it is too vague and lacks measurable outcomes, with Diane suggesting that previous reports included much more detailed documentation and links. The team agreed that the standards group should retroactively add links to their detailed planning documents and bibliography to make the report more comprehensive for future reviews.

### Report Review Timeline Discussion

The team agreed to delay review of the report for another week to allow Diane and Yoav time to examine additional documentation, including a Google document link that Arun will add as a comment. They established that future reports from TYP should include meeting notes links and updates from all-member meetings in the bibliography section. The discussion revealed that TYP had previously conducted a two-day workshop and forum in November where working groups presented their work, though it's unclear if this information will be incorporated into future reports.

### Project Updates and Feedback Discussion

The team discussed sharing feedback with the project team and Diane mentioned she would create a mock-up of embedded references to provide additional detail and evidence. David updated the group that the project was renamed to IPRAM and had received its second review, with approval pending. The conversation ended with Arun seeking feedback on the Waterfall Network project, which had been resubmitted after previous maintenance work, and noting licensing concerns raised by the project team.

### GPL Licensing Concerns Discussion

Enrique explained concerns about a project proposal involving GPL and Apache 2 licensed code, noting that while the team has split codebases, some dependencies still require GPL components. The team agreed to request clearer documentation of licensing details and potential roadmap for replacing GPL dependencies in the future proposal. The group also decided to align mid-year reports with Q3 report timelines and use the existing quarterly report format, while Matthew will follow up with the Besu team regarding DCO friction points.
