## Overview

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council (TAC) meeting focused on reviewing project reports and discussing AI contribution guidelines. The council approved the Credible project's annual report, with Matthew and Diane providing positive feedback and recommending it remain in incubation status. The group also reviewed the Indy project's report, with Matthew noting the need to prioritize OpenSSF scorecard improvements. A significant portion of the meeting was dedicated to discussing Rafael's pull request on AI contribution guidelines, with participants raising concerns about clarity, implementation, and potential security issues. The TAC also briefly discussed Matthew's proposal to transition from quarterly reviews to mid-year reviews, though Diane requested more time to review the details.

## Details

### Project Updates and Reports Meeting

The meeting began with participants joining and waiting for quorum. Arun welcomed everyone and provided reminders about antitrust policies and community participation. He announced that project teams can share updates in the standard developer weekly newsletter by leaving comments on the wiki page. The group discussed pending annual reports, including updates on the Solang maintainers waiting for confirmation from the Stellar Foundation and the TYP team needing assistance with report preparation. Arun also mentioned receiving several lab proposals over the past two weeks, including AP3, and invited comments from the team on these proposals.

### Silence Laboratories Project Proposal Review

The team discussed a project proposal from Silence Laboratories, which has backing from organizations including Google. Arun confirmed the project team is open to feedback and suggestions for improvement. Diane expressed support for moving forward with the project. The group also noted that there are several other pending project proposals that TAC members should review. The conversation ended with a brief mention of pending annual reports that need review.

### Project Incubation Status Review

The team reviewed Arun's credible report on a project that has been incubated for about 1.5 years and is widely adopted in public-facing deployments across several countries. Arun noted concerns about the project's heavy dependence on a single organization for contributions, though the project team has been proactive in engaging through workshops and mentorship programs. The team agreed to keep the project in incubating status, with Matthew suggesting it could be considered for graduated status in the next year due to its production use. Diane suggested the project team should reach out to Hendrik regarding HIRO ledger support, though this was noted as an action item for the project maintenance team.

### Indy Annual Report Approval

The team discussed and approved the Indy annual report, with Matthew confirming it was in good shape and only requiring minor updates to the OpenSSF scorecard. Arun inquired about the multi-letter support for Indy Besu, to which Matthew responded that there had been releases and activity across multiple repositories in 2025, though the project remains in a stable maintenance mode. The team agreed that improving OpenSSF metrics should be a top priority given the project's stable status, and Matthew committed to updating his review PR and merging it separately following the approval.

### AI Guidelines and Review Processes

The TAC discussed two main agenda items: AI contribution guidelines and project review processes. Regarding AI guidelines, Matthew provided initial feedback on Rafael's pull request, noting the need to clarify whether the guidelines apply to LFDT activities and projects, and whether they should be mandatory or optional. Diane raised concerns about properly tracking AI-assisted contributions and suggested adding labels in addition to commit messages. The team discussed potential security risks and misuse of AI tools, with Hendrik sharing examples from other projects. The group agreed to review the pull request further and continue discussions in upcoming meetings. On the second item, Matthew presented a proposal to replace quarterly project reviews with mid-year reviews to reduce overhead while maintaining governance quality. The team agreed to take the proposal out of draft status and review it further, with Diane requesting additional time to provide comments.
