## Overview

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council (TAC) held a meeting to discuss the Linear project proposal and review project report processes. Julien and Simon from the Linear team presented their Layer 2 blockchain solution, which extends Besu to implement zero-knowledge proofs and direct transaction finalization. The team addressed questions about dependencies, licensing, and roadmap plans, including their transition to RISC-V and plans for real-time proving. The TAC discussed changing the project report review process, with members suggesting moving from quarterly reviews to fewer, more comprehensive annual reviews to better balance workload and allow more time for other TAC activities. The conversation ended with a reminder about reviewing a new lab proposal that had been submitted.

### Linear Stack Project Presentation

Julien presented the Linear Stack project, a layer 2 ZK EVM solution designed for enterprise use with high throughput and low transaction costs. The project includes several key components: execution layers, a consensus layer implementing QBFT algorithm, and a finalization stack for generating zero-knowledge proofs on Ethereum layer 1. The team has 88 contributors working on the codebase and follows strict security audit processes. The project excludes Linear mainnet and Sepolia networks from the donation, focusing instead on distribution software and specific bridge UI components. Communication will primarily occur through LFTT Discord and GitHub, with monthly calls including distributors and partners. The roadmap will be published soon, with key focus areas including the pivot to Risk 5 and reducing transaction proof costs.

### NARC Crypto Libraries Dependency Discussion

The team discussed the upstream dependencies of NARC crypto libraries and their impact on the project. Julien explained that the libraries are maintained by the linear stack team and are used for cryptographic primitives in the finalization stack. He confirmed that the project is 100% EVM compatible and follows the EVM spec with some customizations. Hendrik inquired about the connection between the project and Buzu, to which Julien explained that multiple components are deployed, including modified Buzu nodes, and some custom plugins are implemented as part of the monorepo. Arun raised questions about long-term dependency strategies and abstraction, to which Julien responded that while there isn't significant abstraction, the team has control over the NARC libraries and could easily replace or extend them if needed.

### Linear Stack EVM Compatibility Discussion

Julien confirmed that the Linear stack will maintain 100% EVM compatibility as a key selling point, allowing applications to deploy seamlessly without different compilers or toolboxes. He explained that while custom pre-compiles are possible, RISC-V implementation will make it easier to prove correctness, particularly for enterprise networks. Regarding project governance, Julien noted that contributions have primarily come from single organizations, though Status and the Bezu team have been active contributors, with Status planning to launch their mainnet soon. The discussion concluded with questions about dual licensing and resource tool continuation, though these topics were not fully addressed in the provided transcript segment.

### Dual Licensing Model Discussion

The team discussed adopting a dual licensing model, specifically combining Apache 2 and MIT licenses for the core protocol layer. Hart confirmed that this approach is feasible under the LF Decentralized Trust Charter and poses low risk, though contributions would need to comply with both licenses. The discussion also covered concerns about audit requirements and consortium involvement, with Julien clarifying that Linear Consortium and Status are the current project consumers, and that the project team would transition to managing audits independently.

### Linea Project Proposal Discussion

The meeting focused on discussing a proposal for the Linea project. Julien clarified that they would use LFDT's Discord channel instead of their current one, primarily for technical discussions on the linear stack. He also confirmed that QBFT would be used as the consensus mechanism, with plans to improve its efficiency. Sean outlined the process for voting on the proposal, with Matthew Whitehead putting it forward and Diane seconding. The attending TAC members were asked to vote on the proposal in GitHub, and staff would reach out to absent members for their votes.

### Project Review Process Changes

The TAC meeting focused on project report reviews and their cadence. Matthew submitted a review for the Loch Ness project but noted it was one week early. The team discussed moving away from quarterly reviews to a more scalable approach, with suggestions to reduce frequency and instead conduct annual reviews or use automated metrics for ongoing monitoring. David highlighted that CNCF had dropped their quarterly report system due to growth, and the group agreed to further discuss alternative review strategies in their next meeting, including potentially replacing some reviews with project presentations and formal action item tracking. David also reminded the team about an unreviewed lab proposal that needed attention.
