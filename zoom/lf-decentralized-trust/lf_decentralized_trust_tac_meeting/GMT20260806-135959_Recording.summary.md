### Linux Foundation Trust Advisory Meeting

Arun opened the Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting on August 6, 2026, and noted that while some participants including Hendrik, Rama, and Osama could not attend, the meeting would still proceed with six people. Arun reminded participants about antitrust policies and encouraged respectful discussion. He also announced the availability of the standard developer newsletter as a communication channel for project updates and requests for contributions.

### OWF Wind Down Announcement

Sean announced that the Open Wallet Foundation Board of Governors has voted to wind down OWF, with the Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust acquiring OWF's brand and assets. Sean reported that he has reached out to maintainers of key projects and has not received any negative feedback about the transition, though the process of informing all projects and labs is ongoing. The public announcement is scheduled for September 1st at the Global Digital Collaboration event in Geneva, where Sean, Daniela, and Jim Zemlin will make the announcement on the main stage.

### Open Wallet Foundation Migration Planning

The TAC discussed the transition of Open Wallet Foundation projects to LFDT, with plans to continue monthly meetings through December to keep stakeholders informed about migration progress. The team reviewed project reports from various teams, including Cacti and Fabric, and identified the need for follow-up on outstanding reports. The main discussion focused on GenAI guidelines, specifically addressing the definition of AI-generated code and content in repositories, with an example of a potential malicious incident in XC Utility being used to illustrate the importance of clear guidelines.

### AI Development Guidelines Enforceability Challenges

The TAC discussed challenges with AI-assisted development guidelines, particularly focusing on enforceability issues. Arun shared insights about a recent supply chain attack in the MPM registry package where malicious code was hidden in test files, highlighting the need for better attribution of AI-generated content. Enrique and Diane agreed that many proposed guidelines are unenforceable due to the difficulty in detecting AI-assisted code and relying on contributor honesty, suggesting the guidelines should focus more on best practices rather than strict requirements. The team decided to review the pull request comments and revisit the discussion in September with more TAC members present to address the enforceability concerns.
