## Details

### Linux Foundation TAC Meeting Updates

The meeting began with attendance being taken and welcoming participants to the Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting. Arun announced upcoming TAC elections in October or early November, reminding members to nominate community representatives. The group discussed changes to the lab proposal review process, moving from pull requests to a simpler GitHub form submission system, with TAC members expected to review GitHub issues and vote on new lab proposals.

### Proposal Review Process Feedback

The team discussed feedback on the new proposal review process, with Diane and Matthew noting limitations compared to the previous collaborative approach, particularly around making specific edits and reviewing changes. David suggested waiting to evaluate potential process changes after reviewing the current batch of four pending proposals. Arun highlighted the need for TAC members to review pending lab proposals, including a new application privacy proposal, while Matthew raised concerns about the increased review burden on TAC members due to fewer active lab stewards.

### Lab Steward Recruitment and AnonCreds

The group discussed lab steward recruitment processes, with David explaining that it currently operates on a volunteer basis without a formal voting process. Arun brought up pending media reports and online proposals requiring review, suggesting ways to potentially share learnings among TAC members to speed up the process. The discussion then shifted to reviewing a mid-year report for AnonCreds, where Diane and Stephen debated whether to move the project to graduation status due to its mature and stable nature, though resource constraints led to parking the decision for now.

### AnonCreds V2 Status Update

The group discussed the status of AnonCreds V2, with Stephen explaining that while it represents the most complete implementation in the BBS space for ZKP credentials, it has not gained adoption due to market fragmentation and focus shifting to ITF approval rather than practical implementation. Stephen clarified that V2 builds on the same data structures as V1 but adds flexibility and more powerful capabilities like equality claims and range proofs. The conversation ended with Diane making a motion to accept a report, which was seconded by Matthew and approved by all present members.

### OWF and SMUD Project Updates

The TAC discussed two main topics: the OWF project migration plan and the status of the SMUD project. Stephen explained that OWF projects, including Akapai and Credo, are planning to combine into a single LFDT project to reduce administrative overhead, with a proposal expected within the next two weeks. The team also addressed the SMUD project, which has been experiencing issues with vulnerability discoverability and resource access. Matthew proposed moving SMUD to a lab status, suggesting this could be voted on at next week's meeting to allow the project additional time to address concerns while ensuring it doesn't continue to distract from TAC meetings.
