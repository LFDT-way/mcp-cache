The January 22nd LFT Centralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting focused on reviewing quarterly and annual project reports, with Diane providing detailed feedback on the Trust Over IP report and suggesting improvements for future submissions. The TAC discussed project growth and health, knowledge sharing initiatives, community engagement, and governance transparency, with members sharing ideas on improving cross-project collaboration, creating training materials, and leveraging AI tools. ## Details

The meeting began with Jessica learning how to pause the recording and confirming a quorum of seven TAC members was present. Arun then welcomed everyone and provided two important announcements: an opportunity for developers to connect with a large community through a comment link, and a reminder about the upcoming maintainer days next week. Arun also proposed that the next meeting on January 29th would be canceled due to the LFG member summit, with all due reports getting an automatic extension until February 5th, and requested feedback from TAC members.

Diane explained that she would not attend the in-person summit but approved the Firefly report, pending a pull request fix. She discussed the Trust Over IP report, noting its sustained momentum and suggesting improvements for future reports, such as clearer progress indicators and easier consumption for TAC members. Diane encouraged others to review the report and provide feedback, which could be conveyed to representatives at the upcoming event.

### TAC Reports and Project Updates

Arun welcomed Diane's detailed report feedback and discussed bringing it up to the project team during maintenance and member summit. Arun confirmed that annual reports are proceeding, with TAC members aware of their project evaluations, and requested Ramakrishna to submit the CACTI team report soon. Arun noted that while there were no immediate lab space issues requiring TAC attention, there were pending proposals related to Hyperaja Fabric Flutter client that needed action. The conversation ended with a warm welcome to new TAC member Kanchan, who was invited to share her vision and contributions for the group.

### GitHub Voting and 2026 Goals

The meeting covered two main topics: GitHub asynchronous voting mechanisms and 2026 goal setting. Arun explained the new GitHub voting system, which allows for issue or pull request-based voting through comments and emojis, with results to be posted as final tallies. The team then discussed project growth and health, with Kanchan presenting themes derived from TAC member nomination statements and past retrospectives. Kevin was invited to share his experiences with project proposal challenges, though no specific issues were raised during the meeting.

### Cross-Project Collaboration and Ecosystem Mapping

The team discussed cross-project collaboration and ecosystem mapping, with David and Marcus suggesting quarterly reviews and maintainer days to create dependency maps and identify connections between projects. They also explored the idea of strengthening project maturity through successful examples and graduation processes. The group agreed to prioritize knowledge sharing and technical leadership initiatives, including publishing white papers, creating cross-project learning programs, and providing guidance for technical decision-making in blockchain protocols.

### Documentation and Project Improvement Strategies

The team discussed several key areas for improvement in 2026. Diane highlighted the need for more training materials and documentation, particularly for new contributors. Arun suggested creating generic knowledge articles that could be shared across projects. Marcus raised concerns about maintaining documentation when project maintainers are scarce. The group discussed strategies for translating documentation into multiple languages, with David mentioning past successful efforts. Diane and Arun agreed to explore leveraging mentorship programs for documentation translation. The team also discussed the need to improve project evaluation criteria, with Ramakrishna suggesting that projects should set OpenServe badges as a basic criterion. Diane emphasized the importance of collaborating with the LFX Insights team to improve project metrics and connectivity. The conversation ended with a discussion on how to identify and qualify AI contributions versus human contributions, with Diane suggesting engagement with the Trust over IP group.
