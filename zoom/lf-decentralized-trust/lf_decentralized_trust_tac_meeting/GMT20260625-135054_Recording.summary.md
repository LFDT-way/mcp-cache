## Overview

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council (TAC) meeting on June 25, 2024, focused on reviewing the Waterfall project proposal and discussing various governance topics. Due to multiple cancellations including Enrique and Rama, the meeting did not reach quorum, but the TAC members present discussed the Waterfall proposal and agreed to vote on it through GitHub. Diane made a motion to accept the new twice-yearly cadence for project reports, which was seconded by Osama, and Jessica agreed to help set up the voting process. The TAC also discussed the AI contribution guidelines, with Attila's comments suggesting the need for standardized gen AI tooling in code generation and project maintenance activities. Other topics included the status of adopters.md files, which Diane suggested should remain optional rather than mandatory, and the need for clearer communication processes when implementing new guidelines or policies. The conversation ended with plans to follow up on the Waterfall proposal vote and various pending action items in subsequent weeks when quorum could be achieved.

## Details

### Waterfall Proposal Voting Process

The meeting began with attendance being taken, noting that both Enrique and Rama had canceled, leaving 7 out of 11 expected attendees present. Sean and Ry discussed the importance of conducting a roll call vote on the waterfall proposal rather than a voice vote due to its significance. The group waited for additional attendees, including Hendrik, Kanchan, and Kevin, while addressing concerns about their absence and the process for voting on the waterfall proposal.

### Meeting Guidelines and Opportunities

The meeting began with a discussion about quorum, as key members Hendrik, Yoav, and Matthew were absent. Arun then welcomed attendees and provided meeting guidelines, including antitrust policies and expectations for respectful participation. Arun announced upcoming opportunities, including a developer newsletter and a Linux lab deep dive session scheduled for July 8th. The conversation ended with plans to discuss pending annual report review comments and to hear from Sergio from the Waterfall team regarding their project improvements and licensing suggestions.

### Project Proposal Incubation Discussion

The team discussed a project proposal that was moving forward to incubation, with Diane expressing approval despite the lack of a quorum present. The discussion addressed questions about legal review requirements, with Hart confirming that legal review is not mandatory for this type of proposal. Arun raised concerns about project dependencies on non-LFDT components, which Sergii addressed by adding clarification to the proposal. Diane indicated she would make a motion to approve the proposal in GitHub, with Sean confirming they had a second from Osama.

### Project Reporting Cadence Agreement

The team discussed and agreed to move forward with a twice-yearly cadence for project reports, with Diane making the motion and Osama providing a second. Arun requested all tech members to follow up on a full request and register their votes through pull request reviews. Jessica agreed to help set up the voting process after the call, and the team decided to continue the discussion offline regarding the project proposal.

### AI Guidelines and Standardization Discussion

The team discussed AI guidelines and comments from Attila, who proposed standardizing GenAI tools usage in code generation and project maintenance activities. Sean agreed to reach out to Attila to potentially join the next TAC meeting to discuss these proposals further. The group also briefly touched on the adopters file, noting that it is optional and not mandatory, though they acknowledged needing to follow up with Thomas regarding progress on this item.

### Adoption Metrics and Governance Changes

The team discussed changing adoption metrics to be tracked only through marketing channels and debated whether adopters.md files should be mandatory or optional. Diane suggested making it a "should" rather than a "must" in governance, but recommended waiting a week to get more input before voting on the change. The discussion also touched on DCO friction points related to GitHub's web interface, where lawyers have determined no exceptions can be made to the sign-off requirements, though feedback from base maintainers is still pending.

### Lab Policies and Lifecycle Management

Arun discussed documenting lab policies and creating a charter document for labs, but Diane indicated she had not been involved in those discussions and suggested postponing them until next week when there would be better attendance. The team also discussed a pending action item regarding how to treat Fabric X and Fabric as different lifecycle stages, noting that while they currently treat them as sub-projects at the same stage, special cases like AROHA require different treatment when frameworks change between versions. Arun requested staff members to review and provide feedback on how to handle sub-projects with different lifecycle stages.

### TAC Project Maintenance Discussion

The TAC discussed two main topics: project maintenance and communication processes. Regarding project maintenance, Yoav explained that both Fabric and Fabric Apps are maintained projects with different development levels, and Arun suggested starting a discussion thread in the governance repository to address how to report and maintain different life cycles. Ry raised a question about potentially moving Fabric Labs repositories from different organizations into the main Hyperledger organization for better statistics tracking, which Yoav agreed to bring up to the team for consideration. The group also discussed communication processes for guideline changes, with Hart suggesting that TAC members should post directly to mailing lists or Discord channels rather than going through staff intermediaries, and Diane noting that the previous process of tagging maintainers had been successful for gathering feedback.
