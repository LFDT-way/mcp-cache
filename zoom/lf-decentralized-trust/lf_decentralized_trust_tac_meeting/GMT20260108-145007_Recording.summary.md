## Details

### Linux Foundation TAC Meeting Updates

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council (TAC) held its first meeting of 2026, welcoming new members including Osama and discussing two key announcements. The first was a reminder about the developer newsletter, encouraging TAC members to submit project updates and requests for comments to reach the large developer community. The second announcement concerned Maintainer Days registration, which TAC members were urged to complete promptly. The conversation ended with a discussion about reviewing pending reports, with Arun explaining that TAC members would review reports asynchronously through pull requests, with a two-week timeframe for approvals.

### Project Report Review Process

The TAC members discussed the process of reviewing project reports, with Arun, Matthew, Marcus, and Ramakrishna sharing their experiences and insights. They emphasized the importance of understanding project purposes, community engagement, and project health indicators. Marcus and Ramakrishna recommended new TAC members review governing documents and project lifecycle guidelines. Kevin asked about grading and consequences for reports, which Arun explained involve recommendations for improvement or potential lifecycle changes for projects underperforming. The conversation ended with a brief discussion on improving project report processes, particularly for specification projects like DOIP.

### TOIP Reporting Requirements Clarity

The discussion focused on TOIP maintainers' reporting requirements, where they sought clarity on whether to submit quarterly reports for a specifications project. Rama suggested allowing them to submit a late Q4 report while skipping the overdue Q3 report, which Arun supported. Ry noted that the TAC still needs to provide clear guidance on differentiating between projects that produce specifications, code, or both, as this issue remained unresolved from the previous year.

### Q4 and Annual Reports Review

The team discussed the submission of Q4 and annual reports, with Arun confirming they would follow up with the project team and POIP for further clarity. They reviewed the 2026 project update calendars and requested TAC members to review and comment on the pull request. Arun mentioned he would replicate Tracy's previous process of mapping TAC members to projects for annual reviews. The conversation ended with a discussion on meeting times, considering the challenges of coordinating across different time zones, with Diane suggesting a 5 AM start time as a potential solution.

### TAC Meeting Time Zone Challenges

The TAC meeting discussed challenges with meeting times across different time zones, with Arun noting that current timing (10 PM UTC) likely excludes Australian participation. Arun requested TAC members to provide alternative suggestions for meeting times to accommodate majority participation. The group also discussed transitioning to asynchronous communication methods, with Arun suggesting the use of GitHub repositories, Discord channels, and email communications. Finally, Arun proposed standardizing the use of GitHub issues in the governance repository for tracking meeting topics and agenda items.

### GitHub Issues and Lab Proposals

The TAC meeting discussed two main topics: GitHub issue templates for agenda items and lab proposal reviews. Arun proposed creating formal GitHub issues for agenda items, but Marcus suggested maintaining the informal Discord approach while keeping the GitHub option available. The group agreed to improve tracking of lab proposals by advertising them in TAC meetings when reviews are slow, with Marcus offering to add agenda items for proposals requiring additional discussion. Sophie shared positive feedback about their junior committer role in Hyru, which has helped spread review tasks and increase motivation. The conversation ended with plans to discuss project retrospection items and set goals for 2026 in the next meeting.
