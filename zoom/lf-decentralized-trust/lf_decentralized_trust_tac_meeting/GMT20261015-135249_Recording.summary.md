### IBC Protocol LFDT Proposal

Barry and Matthew from the Interchain Foundation presented their proposal to contribute IBC (Inter-Blockchain Communication Protocol) to the LFDT. They explained that IBC is a ledger interoperability solution that has been widely adopted in the Cosmos ecosystem, connecting 150+ blockchains and securing over $50 billion in transaction flows. The foundation believes contributing IBC to the Linux Foundation will provide a more neutral home for the protocol, allowing it to attract contributors and users beyond the Cosmos ecosystem. Barry highlighted that IBC is open source, standards-based, and battle-tested, making it a good fit for the LFDT.

### IBC Project Supporter Discussion

Barry explained that many organizations listed as supporters are either current users, contributors, or potential contributors to the IBC project, with representatives from DataChain, OpenZeppelin, and Cosmos Labs set to become initial maintainers upon acceptance. Ramakrishna raised concerns about the existing UE lab project, which Barry addressed by stating they would collaborate with its maintainers and consider integrating relevant work into IBC. Matthew clarified the relationship between the Interchain Foundation and Cosmos Labs, emphasizing their continued collaboration post-migration. Arun inquired about governance and funding aspects, which the discussion did not fully address due to the transcript ending mid-question.

### IBC Practices and Future Plans

The discussion focused on IBC's current practices and future plans. Barry explained that while IBC operates in a fully open-source environment, they have implemented private vulnerability disclosure systems to ensure safe remediation before public patches are available. The team discussed funding, noting that the Interchain Foundation currently provides significant grant funding but plans to make IBC more self-sustaining through additional sources including other foundations and companies. Barry also addressed the integration of IBC with the Qbft consensus algorithm, explaining that while it currently exists in the IBC contracts repository, they hope to work more closely with the Besu team for better integration and day-one support.

The group agreed to have the AI working group review and provide feedback on the lingering AI guidelines topic, with Hendrik planning to organize a second meeting to move the discussion forward.
