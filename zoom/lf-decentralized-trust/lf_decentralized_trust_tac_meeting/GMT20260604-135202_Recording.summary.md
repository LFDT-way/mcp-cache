## Overview

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council (TAC) meeting focused on project updates and governance discussions. The council voted to move the Solang project to a lab status rather than maintaining it as an active incubation project, with Salah explaining that funding would now come from Stellar on a quarterly basis rather than annual reports. David presented a draft charter proposal for lab projects to establish clear governance frameworks, though the TAC decided to gather feedback from current lab maintainers before making final decisions. The council discussed improving communication processes for TAC proposals by giving project maintainers two weeks to review and provide feedback before voting, with Matthew volunteering to draft guidelines for this process. Diane raised concerns about the quality of the DYP project's annual report, noting it lacked sufficient detail and reference links compared to previous versions, while the council also received feedback from the Hyperledger TSC regarding AI guidelines, where the disclosure section received significant pushback from developers who felt it would not be effective in tracking AI usage.

## Details

### Note-Taker Bot Privacy Discussion

Sean and Ryan discussed their approach to handling a note-taker bot from the Linux Foundation joining meetings, agreeing to remove it due to concerns about data collection and privacy. They noted that proper communication about the bot's presence and data usage had not been made to staff. The meeting then began with Sean taking attendance and Diane joining early, with the agenda being shared in the chat.

### Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Meeting

The meeting began with participants joining a Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting on June 4th, 2026. Arun welcomed attendees and noted that 7 out of 11 members were present. He reminded participants about antitrust policies and the importance of respectful interaction during the meeting. The discussion briefly touched on a recent incident that was mentioned on a Discord channel, with Ry being invited to speak about it.

### Community Management and Updates

Ry discussed the authority to ban problematic members from the Discord server and emphasized the importance of maintaining a healthy environment. Arun announced the developer newsletter process, the upcoming Hyro Community Day on July 4th, and requested feedback on the tech team's progress through an anonymous retro tool. Sean shared information about an upcoming Lineth platform workshop, and Arun mentioned a new project proposal that TAC members should review. The conversation ended with a reminder about pending annual report submissions from the Solar team and Minnekova.

### Solang Project Status Transition

The TAC discussed moving the Solang project from incubation to a lab project status to reduce reporting pressure while maintaining its connection to LFDT. Salah explained that Solang's funding from Stellar would now be quarterly rather than annual, and the team agreed to this transition through a voice vote. David then introduced a proposal for lab projects to have their own charters, similar to individual projects, to provide clear governance information for code hosted in labs, though this discussion was postponed for further review at a later meeting.

### Lab Governance Proposal Discussion

The team discussed a governance proposal for lab management, with David explaining that the proposal would make existing implicit responsibilities more explicit rather than adding new ones. Diane suggested sharing the proposal with all current labs for feedback, which the team agreed to do as a next step. Arun raised questions about oversight and monitoring requirements, which David addressed by explaining that general reference guidelines could be created for staff and stewards to implement. The conversation ended with Matthew bringing up a separate topic about communicating TAC proposals more widely to project maintainers for feedback before voting, particularly in cases where changes might affect project teams.

### Project Communication and Guidelines Updates

The team discussed improving communication with project maintainers regarding TAC proposals and guidelines. They agreed to implement a two-week feedback period and use multiple communication channels including Discord, GitHub, and direct repository mentions to reach maintainers. Regarding the DYP project report, Diane and Yoav expressed concerns about the high-level format lacking sufficient evidence links and metrics, though Arun explained the team had agreed to this format after discussions about managing multiple working groups. The conversation ended with Diane reporting that the AI guidelines proposal received negative feedback on the disclosure section from the Hiro-TSC, with participants suggesting it should be removed as it wasn't trackable or useful.
