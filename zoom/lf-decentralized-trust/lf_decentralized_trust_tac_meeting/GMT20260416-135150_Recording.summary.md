## Details

### AI Guidelines for Open Source

The Technical Advisory Council meeting focused on discussing AI usage guidelines for open source projects. Rafael presented a proposal for AI guidelines based on his work with Hyperloglog Cacti, outlining core principles including human responsibility, disclosure requirements, and legal considerations around licensing. The discussion highlighted the need for consistent guidelines across Linux Foundation projects, with Diane suggesting that TAC should develop a comprehensive risk model and governing principles rather than relying on project-specific approaches. Key concerns included ensuring proper code review processes, managing PR sizes, and addressing legal implications of AI-generated content. The group acknowledged the need for more detailed guidance beyond basic disclosure requirements, particularly around maintaining code quality and reviewer bandwidth.

### AI Policy Approaches Discussion

The group discussed AI policy approaches across different projects, with Hart confirming that the Linux Foundation currently allows individual communities to set their own AI policies in the short term, similar to what the kernel project has implemented. Diane suggested tracking AI-assisted contributions through metadata labels rather than just pull request comments to make it easier for analytics tools to measure AI engagement. Arun noted a contradiction in Kubernetes's approach of prohibiting AI attribution in commits while requiring it in pull requests, and suggested that Rafael could lead the development of a more comprehensive guideline for all projects by proposing it in the governance repository.

### AI Tool Policy Implementation Discussion

The team discussed implementing AI tool usage policies across projects, with Rafael proposing to submit a pull request for discussion. Yoav raised concerns about disclosing specific AI tools due to potential business engagement leaks and privacy issues, suggesting a general description of AI assistance might be sufficient. The group agreed that AI tooling is already being used across projects in various forms, though current measurement capabilities are limited without proper policies in place. Arun requested TAC members to review pending lab proposals, including new repositories against the semantic framework and a diagnostic lab proposal that was approved.

### Team Approval Process Updates

The team discussed three main items requiring approval. For the locked list proposal, Matthew and Hendrik confirmed readiness and the team agreed to conduct voting on GitHub's pull request due to insufficient quorum in the call. Regarding the Paladin report, Diane indicated she needed more time to review it with Rama, requesting until next week for feedback. The team also addressed the Indy report, which had been previously accepted but still had an open pull request, with Diane making a motion to accept it through GitHub review.

### Project Reports Status Review

The team discussed pending project reports, with the TYP project team requesting assistance in preparing their report and planning to meet with Arun on Friday. Arun noted that most other project teams had not responded to requests for reports, and he encouraged TAC members to follow up with maintainers from these projects. The group also briefly touched on SLSA adoption guidelines and planned to continue this discussion in the next meeting, along with a review of fabric versus Fabric X graduation status.
