## Overview

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council (TAC) meeting on October 8, 2026, focused on reviewing the ThunderID project proposal for incubation. Geethika from WSO2 presented the ThunderID project, a 100% open source identity and access management platform that was originally built in Java but has been rewritten in Golang. The project team explained that ThunderID serves as both a VC issuer and VC verifier using OpenID standards, and has already gained adoption from organizations like MOSIP and Lanka Software Foundation. The TAC discussed the project's current maintenance structure primarily led by WSO2 and MOSIP, with plans to attract additional contributors and maintainers. Due to lack of quorum, Diane made a motion to conduct an asynchronous vote on the project's incubation status, which will be handled through GitHub pull request voting. Hendrik then presented recommendations for Cyber Resilience Act compliance, particularly regarding Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) requirements and support policies, suggesting enhanced standards beyond current LFDT guidelines.

### ThunderID Project Introduction Meeting

Geethika introduced the ThunderID team and provided background on the project, explaining that WSO2 initiated ThunderID in May 2025 as a modernization effort for identity and access management, particularly focusing on securing human and agent identities. She detailed WSO2's history and open source commitment, noting their transition from Java to Golang technology stack and their decision to seek neutral governance through the Linux Foundation. The team is now seeking to contribute the ThunderID codebase to LFDT after previously contributing other projects to CNCF and OpenWorld Foundation.

### ThunderID Identity Product Presentation

Darshana presented ThunderID, a new identity product designed with core capabilities including flexible login and registration journeys, agent-native functionality, and lightweight scalability. The product architecture consists of a management plane for configuration and a gateway for handling runtime traffic, with the team releasing an active open-source project called Sunlight that has gained over 500 GitHub stars and 90 contributors.

### WSO2 Thunder ID Open Source

Geethika explained WSO2's plans to release Thunder ID as an open source codebase built on Java, with Identity Server 8 being developed on top to focus on agent ID and API access management. She mentioned that WSO2 has already contributed to the codebase and wants to build a vibrant community with other organizations using and contributing back to it. Geethika noted that organizations like Mossip and Lanka Software Foundation are already using the ThunderID codebase. The discussion ended with Arun asking for more details on how Thunder ID integrates with existing authentication systems and translates them for use with DIDs and wallets.

### ThunderID Incubation Discussion Meeting

The meeting focused on discussing ThunderID as a potential incubated project within LFDT. Geethika and Darshana explained ThunderID's capabilities as an end-to-end authentication platform that can issue and verify verifiable credentials, including frequent flyer credentials for airline systems. The team demonstrated their involvement in European UDI wallet projects and discussed their plans to attract diverse maintainers beyond WSO2 and MOSIP. The TAC agreed to conduct an asynchronous vote on the project's incubation status, with voting details to be handled through GitHub. Separately, Hendrik presented on Cyber Resilience Act compliance requirements, particularly focusing on SBOM (Software Bill of Materials) standards and support policies, suggesting specific requirements for LFDT projects to meet European regulations by the following year.
