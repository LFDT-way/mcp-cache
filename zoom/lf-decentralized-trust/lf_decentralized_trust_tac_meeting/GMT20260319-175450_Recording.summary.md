## Details

### Linux Foundation TAC Meeting Updates

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting on March 19, 2026, began with Arun emphasizing the importance of following antitrust policies and respecting diverse participation. Arun announced a weekly newsletter for developers and requested TAC review committee members to evaluate mentorship proposals. He also reminded attendees to review LFX Insights team responses and shared a TAC member assignment working sheet for project reviews. Arun announced an upcoming meetup on April 1st at noon, featuring teams from CPQD and DSR discussing Indian-based implementation. He invited Christian Taylor to speak about a new proposal on open source frontiers and encouraged TAC members to provide feedback.

### Web3 Open Source Sustainability Lab

Christian presented a proposal for a lab focused on developing architectural frameworks for open source sustainability in Web3 ecosystems. He explained that the lab would provide neutral ground for L1s to collaborate on approaches to open source sustainability, building on existing models like Cardano's maintainer-retainer program. Christian outlined the Open Maintenance Framework, which includes concepts like funding instruments, portfolio stewardship, and decentralized open source program offices. He emphasized that the framework aims to be portable and adaptable to different ecosystems' needs, without disrupting existing governance mechanisms.

### Cardano Open Source Sustainability Model

Christian presented a paid open source model for the Cardano ecosystem, which includes retainers for project founders and community maintainers, a code for us bug bounty program, and a tooling sustainability budget. Diane expressed interest in sponsoring this work to provide reference materials for discussing open source opportunities with organizations. Christian suggested that the project could serve as both a collection of best practices and reference architectures, as well as a foundation for more formalized approaches if demand arises.

### Lab Sponsorship and Review Process

Christian discussed his work on a white paper and sought sponsors for his lab, with Arun and Diane offering to co-sponsor. They clarified that sponsorship is optional and helps communicate the project's value to others. Christian mentioned he would get more sponsors and port his white paper into the project, as well as include a Cardano example for reference architecture. Ry explained the next steps for implementing a lab, which involve filing a request in the LFTT Labs repo and getting two TAC members to approve it. Arun suggested formalizing the review process for project reports by having primary and secondary reviewers post comments on Discord or mailing lists and make recommendations for acceptance.

### TAC Meeting Participation Concerns

The meeting focused on concerns about low participation in TAC meetings, with only two members present, and six overdue annual reports. Arun suggested reviewing meeting times and reaching out to TAC members individually to improve attendance. The group agreed to analyze meeting participation data and discuss governance mechanisms for graduated projects like Fabric, as well as strategies for encouraging adoption of best practices, in future sessions.
