### Project Report Cadence Discussion

The Technical Advisory Council meeting began with Arun welcoming participants and outlining meeting protocols, including following trust policies and respecting diverse global participants. Arun announced the developer newsletter opportunity for project teams to share updates and discussed the topic of project report cadence, specifically addressing the current requirement of one annual report and three quarterly reports per project. The discussion focused on whether to modify this cadence as the number of projects scales up, with Arun suggesting inspiration from other Linux Foundation communities that spread annual reports over longer periods.

### Project Review Process Transition

The team discussed transitioning from quarterly project reviews to a twice-yearly review process, with Matthew suggesting this would reduce reviewer workload while allowing more thorough assessments. Diane proposed adding flexibility to request additional reviews for struggling projects, while Arun outlined a plan to move from asynchronous to more active review processes, including direct project team presentations during TAC meetings and regular health checks using LFX Insights. The group agreed that this approach would maintain accountability without creating excessive overhead for project teams.

### TAC Reporting Process Improvements

The TAC discussed improving their reporting process, with Diane suggesting September as a potential timeline for end-of-year reports due to review time requirements. Enrique proposed exploring AI-powered tools for continuous monitoring across multiple platforms like LFX, GitHub, and Discord to provide ongoing alerts and metrics, though he acknowledged bandwidth constraints. The group agreed to start a GitHub discussion thread to propose changes to their governance documents, which would need to be updated in multiple places including project intake processes and lifecycle management documents. Matthew suggested aiming for implementation after current annual reports are completed, given it's April.

### Project Report Review Process Updates

The team discussed implementing a new process for reviewing project reports, with Matthew proposing to trial it after the middle of the year and review governance documents to identify where this is laid out. Enrique emphasized the need for stronger reporting requirements and more structured reviews, suggesting two key components: optimizing review time and improving report quality. The group also addressed overdue reports, with Hendrik suggesting inviting project maintainers to TAC meetings to discuss delays and provide support.

### Project Updates and Reports Review

The team discussed strategies for contacting external projects, deciding to create a public issue with an invitation to the next meeting and email details. They reviewed and approved the identis annual report, with a merge conflict noted that would need to be addressed after approval. The Web3J report was postponed to the next meeting at Marcus's request, and the Loch Ness project report was presented by Hendrik, noting that the project wants to remain in incubation and plans to implement OpenSSF scorecards in the future.

### Loch Ness Project Review Meeting

The TAC discussed several project reports, focusing primarily on the Loch Ness project. Arun highlighted the strategic importance of Loch Ness for LFTT and suggested setting up community calls and workshops. Enrique raised concerns about the project's compliance with incubation requirements, noting that Loch Ness currently has only one organization as a maintainer instead of the required two. The team agreed to review past recommendations and data points before making a final decision on the Loch Ness report next week. The group also reviewed the Hyrule and Firefly project reports, with Enrique noting that Hyrule appears to be in good shape and Ramakrishna planning to provide a detailed analysis of Aloha by next week. The TAC approved the Firefly report with no major concerns raised.

### SLSA Adoption and Compliance Challenges

The team discussed challenges with SLSA adoption and project compliance with governance frameworks. Enrique shared that as a Firefly maintainer, he is working on creating an adopters file but faces difficulties in obtaining proper consent from companies to list them. Diane suggested allowing companies to use their own GitHub IDs to add their names to the adopter file as a better approach. The group agreed that SLSA best practices should be treated as mandatory requirements rather than optional guidelines, particularly in light of recent security concerns. Arun committed to bringing this conversation back in the next meeting and following up on all action items through the Discord channel.
