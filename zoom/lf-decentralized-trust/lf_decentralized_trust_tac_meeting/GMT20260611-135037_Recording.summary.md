## Overview

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council (TAC) meeting on June 11, 2026 focused on reviewing the Waterfall Network project proposal and discussing pending governance matters. Sergii and Oleksandr from the Waterfall team presented their updated proposal, addressing previous licensing conflicts by switching to Apache 2 license and separating their code from Ethereum native components. TAC members raised concerns about licensing issues, governance structure, and the distinction between the infrastructure technology and potential future governance foundation. The team agreed to provide additional documentation on licensing and governance details before the next review meeting. The meeting also covered the proposed LFDT Labs charter document, with David explaining the need for explicit governance documentation for all labs, though TAC members noted confusion around terminology between "projects" and "labs." Additional topics included pending annual reports for Smooth and DYP projects, with feedback requested on including source information, and discussion of AI contributor guidelines requiring further input from project maintainers.

## Details

### Linux Foundation TAC Meeting Updates

Arun opened the Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting and reminded participants about antitrust policies and respectful meeting conduct. Sean announced that the Linith Lab team had presented use cases in a workshop the previous day, with a follow-up technical deep dive scheduled for July 8th. Arun mentioned pending annual reports for Smooth and DYP projects and encouraged TAC members to review a Labs proposal, before introducing Sergil who was scheduled to discuss the waterfall network project proposal.

### Waterfall Proposal Update Presentation

Sergii presented a waterfall proposal with minor changes that was originally submitted approximately one and a half years ago. The proposal was previously delayed due to a conflict of licenses between Apache 2 and Ethereum native components, which has now been addressed to 95% resolution. Diane inquired about multiple deployments and adopters, to which Sergii responded that they are exploring three main directions for deployment in regulated markets.

### Waterfall Platform Performance Discussion

Diane inquired about scalability numbers for Waterfall and requested references to support the performance claims. Sergii confirmed that the code underwent third-party security audits and performance verification, mentioning that mainnet.waterfall.network, which uses the same codebase, is performing well. Matthew sought clarification on whether Waterfall is a blockchain or a development environment, and Sergii explained that it is a decentralized, highly scalable smart contract platform and infrastructure for building dApps. Enrique asked about the tokenomics and governance structure, particularly regarding proof of stake and the presence of a foundation similar to Ethereum's.

### Code Licensing Discussion Meeting

Enrique and Sergii discussed licensing concerns regarding the forked code from GoEthereum and Prism, which still carries GPL licensing. Sergii explained that the team addressed these concerns over one and a half years ago through email correspondence and documentation. Oleksandr clarified that while the original forks had LGPL3 and GPL3 licenses, the team created separate repositories with Apache2 licensing and connected GoEthereum as a plugin to resolve licensing issues.

### Waterfall Project Implementation Discussion

The team discussed the technical implementation and governance structure of the Waterfall project. Oleksandr clarified that their code is not being moved to the Linux Foundation repository and remains their own implementation using Go With You as the execution layer with potential future replacement options like Besu. Hart recommended that Alexander share licensing details with the TAC for review. Diane raised questions about current code governance, to which David responded by sharing a charter document link, noting that the project name may still change. The discussion concluded with Arun asking about the current ecosystem and stakeholders, to which Sergii responded that Digital Clover Solutions and other companies are potential contributors to the project.

### Project Proposal and Licensing Review

The team discussed a project proposal, with Diane requesting to review the licensing summary before moving it to review next week. Enrique and Sergii expressed support for the proposal but emphasized the importance of understanding licensing and governance aspects. Arun outlined next steps for the proposal review process, including sharing details via email and seeking TAC member approvals. The meeting also addressed project charters for LFTT Labs, with David explaining the need for explicit governance documentation for the labs, similar to what was done for individual projects.

### Labs Governance Documentation Review

The team discussed governance documentation for labs, with David explaining that while labs will follow a standard charter initially, they can transition to an incubation phase with customized governance if needed. Matthew raised concerns about terminology clarity, particularly around the use of "project" in the document, which David acknowledged as a terminology issue between Hyperledger/LFDT and other Linux Foundation parts. The team also reviewed pending project reports, with Diane requesting that the TYP team include source information and bibliography in their reports, and discussed the need to establish a schedule for biannual reports and review processes.
