## Overview

The Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council (TAC) meeting focused on reviewing annual reports and project status updates for several projects. The team discussed and approved the Web3J project report, noting concerns about maintainer diversity but deciding to maintain its incubation status. Salah presented an update on the Solang project, explaining that its continued development depends on quarterly funding from the Stellar Foundation, with a decision expected within two weeks. The TAC voted to accept the Loch Ness project's report and continue its incubation status. Enrique provided a positive review of the Hiro project report, recommending graduation status, with suggestions to better clarify the relationship between Hedera, Hashgraph, and Hashgraph Online. Ramakrishna presented the iROHA project report, highlighting its adoption for CBDC use cases and progress in splitting into iROHA 2 and iROHA 3, while noting concerns about maintenance diversity and project organization. The TAC voted to approve iROHA's graduated status with monitoring of maintenance diversity issues.

## Details

### Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory

The meeting began with Arun welcoming participants to the Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council Meeting on April 23, 2026. Arun outlined two note-keeping policies: adherence to antitrust laws and respect for diverse global participants. He announced the availability of the standard developer newsletter for project teams to communicate with the larger ecosystem, and the conversation ended with Arun preparing to discuss pending annual reports, starting with an update on Web3J from Marcus.

### Web3J and Solang Project Updates

The team reviewed and approved the Web3J project report, which noted active community engagement and development despite lower contributor numbers and concerns about maintainer diversity. Marcus suggested moving forward with the report approval despite minor formatting issues. The discussion then shifted to the Solang project, where Salaheldin explained that the current maintenance agreement with the Stellar Foundation ended in December and a new proposal is being discussed for potential production deployment. The project's future status depends on whether the Stellar Foundation provides quarterly funding, with plans to potentially add new maintainers if the proposal is approved. Diane expressed concerns about the quarterly funding requirement being burdensome for the project maintainers.

### Solang Funding and Development Status

The group discussed Solang's funding and development status. Saleh explained that Solang requires proposal-based funding from the Stellar Foundation rather than being pre-listed for grants. He noted that while usage metrics are challenging to track, the Solang Playground website provides some analytics data. Saleh clarified that previous attempts to secure funding from Solana and Polkadot were unsuccessful as these platforms showed limited product-market fit for Solang. Arun raised concerns about maintenance guarantees, asking whether security vulnerabilities and updates would be addressed regardless of funding status, though this question was not fully addressed in the discussion.

### Project Funding Status Discussion

The TAC discussed the funding status of a project, with Salah estimating that 60-70% of contributions are tied to Stellar funding while 30% comes from mentorships. The group agreed to wait for an update on the project's grant proposal in approximately 2-3 weeks, with Salah committing to provide an update before May 21st. If the grant is not approved, the team will need to discuss the project's future, including potentially moving it to a lab setting.

### Project Incubation Phase Acceptance

The team discussed accepting a project into the incubation phase, with Hendrik presenting the report and highlighting the need for high-priority implementation of OpenSSF scorecards. Enrique shared feedback from a recent meeting with Loch Ness/DFNS company in Paris, noting their positive response to the feedback and suggestions for future improvements in the report. The motion to accept the project was made by Enrique and seconded by Marcus, with all participants voting in favor, resulting in the motion passing.

### Hiro Project Review and Feedback

The team reviewed a project report for Hiro's work on Hedera, with Enrique providing positive feedback and recommending graduation. Enrique suggested adding more context about the relationship between Hashgraph, Hashgraph Online, and Hedera, as well as highlighting specific use cases built on Hedera technology. Hendrik acknowledged these points and agreed to address them in future reports, particularly regarding the relationship between Hedera and Hiro features. The discussion also touched on the potential for enterprise adoption of Hiro technology and the possibility of universities setting up their own networks.

### iROHA Project Status Review

The TAC meeting discussed the iROHA project's status and maintenance report. Ramakrishna presented a comprehensive report highlighting the project's progress, including its adoption by multiple institutions and the split between iROHA 2 (for private networks) and iROHA 3 (for public networks). The team approved the project's graduated status with caveats, particularly regarding the lack of diversity in maintenance and the need for better community engagement. Arun raised a question about how to treat the project given the separation between iROHA 2 and 3, which Ramakrishna explained as being partly an evolution and partly a variation with a common base layer.
