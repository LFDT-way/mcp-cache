## Overview

The Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting focused on project report compliance, governance updates, and the upcoming migration of Open Wallet Foundation projects. The council discussed the lack of responsiveness from the Minokawa and Smoot project teams regarding overdue annual reports, with some members advocating for a formal process to address non-compliance, such as moving projects to a dormant state. The group decided to give the projects a two-week notice period before taking action. The meeting also covered the need to update governance documents to align with the EU Cyber Resilience Act, with Hart recommending that members take a training course on the subject. Ace from the Open Wallet Foundation inquired about the migration timeline and the eligibility of OWF contributions for TAC elections. Sean informed the council that a migration plan was being finalized and would be shared soon. Other topics included the review of project reports and a reminder about the TAC election nominations.

### Linux Foundation TAC Updates

Arun opened the Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Council meeting and announced two key items: the developer newsletter as a communication forum for project updates, and upcoming TAC elections for the 26-27 term with nominations opening in October. Arun also addressed concerns about annual report responses from project teams, specifically mentioning challenges with Minopova and another project, and noted that per governance policies, TAC has the option to request project teams address graduation requirements if standards are not met. The conversation ended with Matthew being asked for thoughts on the two projects, though his response was not captured in the transcript.

### TAC Project Report Delay Process

Enrique and Hendrik discussed the need to formalize a process for handling delayed project reports in TAC projects. They agreed that a clear, documented process should be established before taking action on specific cases. Enrique suggested implementing a "three-strike" model with a comprehensive paper trail of all communication attempts with maintainers, while Hendrik proposed moving problematic projects to the LFDT reps stage instead of returning them to incubation. Both participants emphasized that defining the process should be prioritized over addressing current specific cases.

### Project Dormancy Criteria Discussion

The team discussed the process for determining when a project should be moved to a dormant state. Marcus and Ramakrishna examined the current criteria, which are based on inactivity and lack of response to TAC members, rather than a formal checklist. Ramakrishna noted that while Minokawa appears active, it has not responded to TAC members or submitted required reports, raising questions about the maintainers' awareness of their responsibilities. The discussion concluded with Enrique suggesting they should follow the existing policy criteria to determine whether projects like Smoot and Minokawa should be moved to dormant status.

### Project Responsiveness and Dormancy Protocol

The team discussed concerns about project responsiveness, particularly regarding Smoot where there hasn't been activity from the maintainer on Discord for a year. David confirmed they had reached out to Smoot's maintainer via email and scheduled a call, which had not yet taken place. Enrique suggested implementing a precursor step to formally mark projects as "dormant" in project reports before moving them to the lab, which would help avoid repeatedly discussing the same inactive projects in future TAC meetings.

### Public Comment Period Proposal Discussion

Arun proposed opening a two-week public comment phase for two non-responsive projects to allow community members to engage, with plans to revisit the projects afterward. Enrique strongly advocated for moving one project to dormant status due to it being 5 months overdue on its annual review, citing the project's failure to submit required updates despite ongoing development activity. Hendrik agreed that action was needed but requested more documentation and formal process before voting, expressing concern about the delay in addressing the issue over multiple weeks.

### Minokawa Annual Report Voting Discussion

The team discussed voting on the Minokawa annual report, with Hendrik suggesting they wait one week to allow for asynchronous feedback before voting at the next TAC meeting. Ramakrishna raised concerns about lack of communication with maintainers, but Daniela confirmed they had scheduled calls with both the maintainers and executive team. The group agreed to issue a public communication giving projects two weeks to respond and acknowledge requests, with voting on the Minokawa report postponed until the next TAC meeting.

### TAC Project and Compliance Updates

The TAC discussed several key topics including project report reviews, the EU Cyber Resilience Act compliance, and the migration of Open Wallet Foundation (OWF) projects to LFDT. Arun requested TAC members to review pending project reports and reach quorum for acceptance. Hendrik volunteered to lead efforts on updating governance documents to address Cyber Resilience Act requirements, with plans to complete a training course before drafting proposals. Sean announced that a migration plan for OWF projects to LFDT is being developed, with reviews expected to begin around September 14th, and Ace inquired about eligibility for TAC elections, which Arun indicated would be discussed further.
