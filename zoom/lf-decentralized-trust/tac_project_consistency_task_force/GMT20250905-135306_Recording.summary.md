## Overview

Matthew and Ry discussed the importance of consistency in project documentation and governance, focusing on clear communication over strict formatting requirements and considering various project stakeholders. They examined specific project structures and tracking approaches, including GitHub usage and the handling of security vulnerabilities across Hyperledger projects. The conversation concluded with discussions about bug submission processes and the need for standardized documentation practices across their projects.

## Details

### Consistent Project Documentation Approach

Matthew and Ry discussed the need for consistency in project documentation, focusing on how information is presented rather than dictating specific formatting. Matthew proposed a "meta consistency" approach, where projects would consistently guide users to find essential information like project overview, usage instructions, and resources. Ry agreed with this direction, emphasizing the importance of clear communication over enforcing uniformity in project management practices. They also touched on the need to consider the perspectives of current and potential project users, maintainers, and contributors in their discussions.

### Firefly Repository Structure Review

Ry and Matthew discussed the Firefly repository and its project structure. They noticed that the project hasn't been updated in a while and suggested adding links to projects for better visibility. Ry proposed creating a public release planning board to make the roadmap more accessible. They also looked at the Cacti project, which had only one board, and discussed the structure of release tabs in Firefly.

### GitHub Project Tracking Discussion

Ry and Matthew discussed project tracking approaches, with Ry expressing a preference for using GitHub issues and projects, as long as they are publicly accessible without additional authentication. They agreed that while GitHub is preferred, it should not be made mandatory, and Ry acknowledged that some external project tracking is acceptable as long as it remains public. Matthew proposed adding a key section at the top of their document to organize proposals more effectively, moving towards more structured discussions in future meetings.

### Project Documentation Consistency Discussion

Matthew and Ry discussed the need for consistency in project documentation and governance. They agreed that updates to existing governance and guideline documents would be more beneficial than creating a new consistency document. Ry emphasized the importance of having an umbrella document that explains the "why" behind project decisions. They also discussed the need for consistent READMEs across projects, with Ry stating that while content should be similar, the presentation can vary. Matthew suggested having a standardized list of links and information that new contributors can expect to find on project landing pages. The conversation concluded with Matthew expressing a desire to discuss CVS consistency further, as there is currently no uniform experience across projects.

### Hyperledger Bug Handling Process

Matthew and Ry discussed the process of handling CVEs and bug submissions for Hyperledger projects. They clarified that the list of published CVEs on the Besu GitHub page is not comprehensive, as it only includes a subset of all known CVEs. Ry explained that most bug submissions come from external contributors through GitHub, and these issues are tracked in private repositories. They also discussed the challenges of providing bug bounties, noting that the budget for bug rewards is significantly lower compared to other projects like Bayzoo, which pays in Ethereum.

### CVE Handling and Advisory Discussion

Matthew and Ry discussed the handling of CVEs and advisories in their system. They explored how releases could be tagged with CVE IDs and how this information could be communicated to users. They agreed that while every security bug doesn't need a CVE, having a low-severity option would be reasonable. Matthew raised questions about the timing of publishing advisories, suggesting they might be published soon after a patch version is released. They decided to have a further discussion on this topic in their next meeting.
