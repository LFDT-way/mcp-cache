## Overview

The meeting focused on reorganizing governance documentation and establishing consistency guidelines across projects in the Linux Foundation ecosystem. The team discussed merging project consistency requirements with existing guidelines while balancing the needs of different project stakeholders, including users and maintainers. They concluded by addressing document updates, participation expansion, and the importance of clear communication and security practices across projects.

## Details

### TAC Guidelines Reorganization Discussion

Matthew and Jessica discussed the reorganization of a governance document, moving it from "tac project governance" to "tac guidelines" as per the TSC's recommendation. Matthew provided a brief recap of previous meetings, noting that the document would serve as a guideline rather than a formal governance document, and mentioned that he had added some new areas for discussion. They agreed to focus on consistency in projects as the main topic for the current meeting.

### Project Consistency Guidelines Merge Discussion

The team discussed the relationship between the project consistency document and existing guidelines, with Matthew proposing to potentially merge the consistency document into the existing guidelines to avoid overwhelming project leads with too many rules. Jessica and Enrique agreed that the consistency areas largely overlap with existing guidelines, and Matthew emphasized the importance of ensuring new projects in the ecosystem can be easily understood and consumed consistently. The discussion highlighted the need to balance strict consistency requirements for core practices like version publishing and installation methods, while allowing flexibility for project-specific practices such as maintainer management.

### Linux Foundation Project Consistency Initiative

Matthew and Jessica discussed the need for consistency across projects in the Linux Foundation ecosystem. They explored the idea of creating a centralized dashboard to track project goals, issues, and roadmaps. The conversation touched on the importance of having a clear and consistent roadmap for projects to help users, maintainers, and consumers understand the project's direction. They also considered the need to enforce minimum levels of consistency, possibly through criteria like incubation, to ensure that projects meet certain standards.

### Project Management Practices and Needs

Matthew discussed the varying needs and perspectives of project users and maintainers regarding project management practices. He emphasized that while users may care about the roadmap and release planning, maintainers should have more freedom in their working methods. Matthew suggested that consistency across projects is less important for maintainers, but could potentially help attract new maintainers to projects that lack them.

### Project Release Guidelines Discussion

Matthew and Jessica discussed guidelines for project releases and communication. They agreed that while version numbers and release methods can vary, there should be consistency in how releases are communicated. Matthew suggested creating guidelines for roadmaps and CV reporting, as these areas could benefit from more structure. They also touched on the importance of security practices, including the use of templates for reporting vulnerabilities.

### Task Force Document and Participation

The task force discussed document updates and participation. Matthew proposed adding a section to document areas of inconsistency and planned to update the document after the call. Jessica suggested including more people in the task force, particularly core maintainers from different projects. They agreed to publicize the task force in project maintaining channels and to increase participation in the next meeting in September. Matthew committed to posting a summary in the Discord channel and mentioned that Rye, who has experience with most LFTT projects, would be invited to join future discussions.
