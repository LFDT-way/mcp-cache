## Agenda

An open call for people interested in the Virtual Private Knowledge Working Group (VPK WG). 

## Overview

The meeting focused on the Virtual Private Knowledge (VPK) project and its role in enabling confidential search for AI applications. Reza introduced Andrew from the Open Mind Foundation and other participants, including Darren, Neil, Scott, and Mariam. Reza explained that VPK aims to allow users to upload scrambled data to a service provider, such as a vector database, so that the provider can perform searches without seeing the original content. He described the use of partially homomorphic encryption and local embedders to keep data and its structure secret from the service provider. The group discussed the distinction between renting infrastructure and renting a service, noting that VPK could reduce liability for service providers by ensuring data is secured at the user's end. Darren shared that he had started a specification document using a template from the Trust Over IP workgroup. Neil mentioned his experience with SpecUpT as a tool for building vocabularies and specifications.

## Details

### Open Mind Foundation Introduction Meeting

The meeting began with introductions, where Andrew introduced himself as the founder of Open Mind Foundation, which focuses on privacy-enhanced technologies and AI auditing. Reza provided an overview of the VPK Protocol Task Force under the Linux Foundation, explaining its mission to create a "Linux of AI" to address the monopolization of foundational AI technology. The meeting included introductions from other participants including Darren Wanner, Neil Thompson, Scott Bly, Mariam Terani, and John Vaccaro, followed by a brief discussion of the organization's structure and white paper.

### VPK Cybersecurity System Presentation

Reza presented a new cybersecurity approach that introduces "Sam" as a character representing service providers with misaligned business interests, contrasting with traditional cybersecurity focus on identity theft. He explained the VPK (Virtual Private Knowledge) system that uses homomorphic encryption to allow secure data searching while maintaining privacy, with the system returning only indices rather than complete search results. Reza announced the formation of two task forces: one led by Darren focusing on VPK protocol and another led by Mitch Travers focusing on personal embedders, with plans to later launch a call for proposals task force.

### VPK Protocol Development Discussion

Reza explained that the mission of Kwai, a nonprofit, is to deconcentrate power over AI through three work streams: research on making AI safer, faster, and greener; development of a Decentralized Physical Infrastructure (DPIN); and policy work. The discussion focused on developing a VPK (Vector Private Query) protocol that would allow confidential RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation) searches on vector databases without modifying existing services, as long as they support search by index. Reza emphasized that this would help protect intellectual property while allowing companies like Quadrant to rent vector database services without liability for data breaches.

### VBK Data Access Controls Discussion

The group discussed data access and privacy controls in a VBK (Verifiable Business Knowledge) context, with Darren explaining how different trust relationships could provide varying levels of access to data while maintaining privacy controls. Reza mentioned ongoing work with the Linux Foundation to properly set up their Confluence page structure and access to meeting recordings for minute-taking purposes. Darren also updated the group on creating a specification document using a template from the TOIP workgroup, which Neil confirmed was based on SpecUpT, a vocabulary and specification language that Neil had previous experience with.
