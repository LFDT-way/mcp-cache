01:02:15	Carly Huitema (University of Guelph):	I was trying to introduce this risk Reducing Metadata Leakage in Credentials When we talk about privacy in digital credentials, we usually focus on the data itself—what’s inside the credential. But there’s another risk that’s easy to overlook: metadata. Even if the contents are private, the timing of when a credential is created or updated can still leak information. If a credential is issued or changed right when something happens in the real world, an observer can start to connect those dots. So how do we break that link?
01:02:41	Carly Huitema (University of Guelph):	One approach is to group changes together. Instead of issuing or updating credentials one by one as events happen, multiple changes are collected and processed in a single batch. From the outside, all you see is a group of updates happening at once, not a direct connection between a specific event and a specific timestamp. The second approach goes even further: creating credentials in advance. Credentials can be generated early and held in reserve, long before they’re actually needed. Later, when real-world events occur, those pre-created credentials are used or completed. That way, the original creation date no longer reveals anything about when the underlying event happened. Together, these two techniques—batching changes and pre-issuing credentials—help reduce what can be inferred just by watching the system. They don’t change the cryptography. They don’t make credentials harder to forge. But they do make it much harder to learn something sensitive just from timing.
