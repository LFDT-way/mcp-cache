### Joint Issuance in Dossier Spec

Daniel and Samuel explained the concept of joint issuance in the dossier spec, which allows multiple parties to endorse actions through separate ACDCs rather than requiring tight coordination like multisig. They described how this approach enables governance structures where parties don't need to share the same key event log, providing a cryptographically verifiable way to model real-world governance scenarios. Daniel noted that while joint issuance is a major feature of the dossier spec, it could potentially be separated from dossiers and implemented in other contexts.

### ACDC Specification Complexity Discussion

Daniel and Samuel discussed the complexity of specifications, particularly regarding joint issuance and dossiers in the ACDC spec. Samuel explained that while the IETF might split specifications into smaller parts for better manageability, adding joint issues and dossiers could make the system appear more complex than necessary. Daniel defended the approach by emphasizing that modeling real-world situations requires appropriate complexity, comparing it to Einstein's principle of making things as simple as possible but no simpler. Daniel also mentioned starting a draft PR on joint issuance operators and intended to contribute a feature alongside the specification. The conversation ended with Daniel raising a concern about a potential circularity problem in joint issuance, which Samuel had not fully addressed.

### Dossier Specification and Feedback Loops

The team discussed the dossier specification and feedback loops in discrete time systems. Daniel explained how dossiers are issued in snapshots, with meta ACDCs representing pending decisions that get replaced with concrete endorsements as decisions accumulate. Samuel clarified the concept using discrete time state evolution examples. Kent then reviewed the DataWebS portion, noting that no work was done since the last meeting and outlining seven action items including six corrections and one open question from Sam's review comments. The conversation ended with Kent preparing to review the seven items from Sam's feedback on the DIDWebS spec.

### DID WebS Specification Updates

The team discussed several updates needed for the DID WebS specification, focusing on AID forms and multisig threshold support. They agreed to update the BNF form to explicitly include different AID types and to add support for complex nested fractionally weighted thresholds in the specification. The group decided to handle complex examples in an appendix rather than in the main normative sections to reduce complexity for users not familiar with Carri. Action items were assigned including addressing Samuel's review comments on version 0.10.13, adding a VC version 2 example, performing spec language reconciliation with Python reference implementation, and reporting on recommended DID methods.
