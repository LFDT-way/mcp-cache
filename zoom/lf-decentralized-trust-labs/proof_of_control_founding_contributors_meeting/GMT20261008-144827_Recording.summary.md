## Overview

This was the first Founding Contributors Meeting for the Proof of Control Standard, hosted by the Advanced AI Society (Alliance for Verifiable AI). Tricia and Ken presented the organization's mission to create open, publicly definable verification standards for Agentic AI systems, moving from trust-based approaches to evidence-based verification. The meeting covered recent progress including the release of version 1.0 of the standard, successful webinars with 1,500+ viewers, and recent press coverage. Four tracks were outlined for participation: Founding Contributors (Track 1), Working Groups (Track 2), Demo Light contributions (Track 3), and Demo Plus research contributions (Track 4). Jim Schwobel presented research from the Open Verification Lab, including experiments on proof of control standards and plans for sandbox development. Multiple working groups were discussed including Legal (led by Kristin Flynn Goodwin), Insurance, and Health, with several participants expressing interest in joining specific industry-focused groups. The conversation ended with discussions about creating a Signal group for sensitive communications and plans for future webinars and research calls.

### Proof of Control Standard Update

Tricia welcomed attendees to the First Founding Contributor Meeting for the Proof of Control Standard, outlining the goals to introduce the Advanced AI Society's mission and specific tracks for participation. She provided updates on recent developments, including the successful move of the Proof of Control standard to LF Decentralized Trust, the launch of version 1.0, and a recent webinar that attracted over 1,500 viewers. Tricia also announced new leadership team members, a new Open Verification Lab Director, and a Director of Operations, and mentioned upcoming events and opportunities for involvement in the standard's development.

### Proof of Control Standard Progress

The meeting focused on the progress and next steps for the proof of control standard, with Ken explaining the plan to release version 1.0 on October 30th and transition to version 2.0 afterward. Ken emphasized that the majority of work would be asynchronous, with meetings reserved for discussing points of disagreement. Tricia highlighted the evolution of the standard, noting that the number of domains expanded from five to six and the verifiability tiers increased from three to four, with significant debate among founding contributors about these changes.

### Proof of Control Initiative Tracks

Tricia outlined four tracks for participation in the proof of control initiative: Founding Contributors (Track 1) who review and contribute, working groups (Track 2) focused on specific industry questions, demo contributions (Track 3), and research contributions (Track 4). She announced three working groups being formed in Legal, Insurance, and Health sectors, with each needing both industry and vendor co-chairs. Tricia also described communication options including Slack, email, and LinkedIn, and called for submissions of use cases and other contributions for Track 3.

### AI Threat Coverage and Governance

Tricia reported that 20 of 29 threats have use cases coverage, with 9 threats still needing use cases, and outlined two submission options: Google Doc or GitHub. She explained Track 4's in-kind and financial sponsorship opportunities and introduced the governance structure, including co-chairs, leadership team, and working groups. Andrew shared his background in auditing and evidence control systems, expressing interest in making AI evidence independently verifiable and accessible through proof of control. Tricia mentioned that Mustafa from UNICC would be collaborating with Andrew on the CISO practitioner guide, and introduced Jim who was set to discuss the Open Verification Lab's efforts to integrate AI safety research into enterprise AI infrastructure.

### Infrastructure Standards Research Roadmap

Jim presented a research roadmap focused on building infrastructure standards for auditable and usable systems across the technology stack. He highlighted his work on a standards paper and experiments in TEE environments, emphasizing the need for formal definitions of proof of control and open verification. Jim encouraged the team to review the paper and contribute to developing a version one archive paper, with plans to present at upcoming conferences.

### Sandboxes Priority Area Discussion

Jim and Tricia discussed the emerging priority area of sandboxes, particularly focusing on agent jail and anomaly detection across the entire technology stack. They outlined next steps including monthly webinars, research calls, and opportunities for community members to contribute through demos, use cases, and research reviews. Ken will lead the work asynchronously starting October 30th, with the community taking a holistic approach beyond just creating standards to include actual research and open source tools driven by industry and builders.

### Research Data Activation Opportunities

Jim offered to meet with anyone interested in getting activated on research papers and discussed the opportunity to use real industry data instead of synthetic data for model validation. Tricia mentioned their collaboration with FinTech Sandbox for accessing free data sets and emphasized the importance of documenting pilots through the alliance. The discussion covered various communication platforms and calendars used for organizing meetings and discussions, with Jim inviting questions and promising to share the presentation deck with relevant links.

### Healthcare Use Cases for Proof

The meeting focused on discussing healthcare use cases for proof of control technology, particularly around Medicaid re-enrollment in 40 states where the re-enrollment cadence has changed from 2 years to 6 months. Brigette highlighted Oregon as an ideal place to test citizen-centric records that incorporate proof of control for Medicaid populations. The group also discussed upcoming presentations at the EU AI Act meeting in Bologna, where members will present their exit reports from the first verifiable AI pilots. Tricia announced plans for an in-person event on November 6th in the Bay Area focused on workshop emerging standards and questions around agentic identity. The conversation ended with discussions about forming working groups for finance and legal areas, and creating a Signal group for more sensitive research conversations.
