The biweekly Standards Working Group has been defining a set of ontologies that enables the description of anthropogenic impact following W3C semantic web guidelines. 
The public release of four interrelated ontologies in OWL, JSON-LD and TTL format is planned for the third quarter of 2025. 
We foresee the use of the ontologies in data models, MRV systems, and AI-assisted applications.

## Details

### Net Zero Digital Twin Integration

Jeff discussed his two-hour phone call with Panovia from the IEEE group about integrating the net zero digital twin architecture into their product. He explained how the digital twin system shows current emissions and configuration options, while AI assists with identifying project steps and economics for reaching net zero goals. Jeff also mentioned a potential collaboration with Digital Passport and noted that AI systems have demonstrated surprising capabilities in understanding and providing information about digital twin architecture.

### Digital Twin Greenhouse Gas Tracking

Jeff explained that the digital twin project focuses on tracking greenhouse gases beyond CO2, specifically highlighting sulfur hexafluoride used in electrical equipment which has 23,000 times the greenhouse gas potential of CO2. The digital twin architecture includes 120 slides covering technical details, implementation, and business cases for meeting global standards on emissions, with AI serving as an assistant for tasks like data integration and gap analysis. The team discussed whether to include work processes like supply chain reconfiguration in the ontology, with Alex confirming general process clauses exist and Christiaan suggesting that AI agents should be modeled as a subclass of agents with appropriate controls and prompts.

### AI for Climate Accounting

Jeff discussed the complexity of climate accounting, particularly around Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions, and explored how AI could help solve these challenges through digital twins and ontologies. He shared his experience using AI to generate Python code for a carbon accounting prototype, which saved him significant development time. Christiaan suggested focusing the group's next session on how AI agents are modeled in the ontology, as this would be applicable across different applications and provide a concrete way to move the discussion forward.

### Agents and Accountability Discussion

The meeting participants discussed the topic of agents and accountability in work representation, with Alex referencing previous working group sessions from 2024 on this topic. Christiaan and Alex searched through a working group archive for relevant materials on representation, specifically looking for content about acting on behalf of others. The discussion was paused as they identified existing documentation on this topic, with Christiaan noting they would return to ponder the matter further after reviewing the materials.

### Accountable Impact Commons Website Demo

Christiaan demonstrated the Accountable Impact Commons website built using Yugo, a Go templating engine, which currently contains two blog posts about becoming a lab and an IEEE Climate Change Global Hackathon introduction. The website structure was explained, showing how content is organized under About, Ontologies, and Posts sections with simple Markdown formatting. Jeff asked about using ontologies to build data models for digital twins, which led to a discussion about their methodology and indicator ontologies built using the IO ontology suite as a basis.

### IO Ontology Suite Implementation Discussion

The team discussed the implementation and extension of ontologies, particularly the Methodology Ontology and Indicator Ontology, as part of the IO Ontology Suite. Alex explained how ontologies can be used to prime AI systems and tag data, while Christiaan and Jeff shared experiences using these ontologies in their work, including handling concepts like boundaries and equity in emissions accounting. The group agreed on a process for incorporating new ontologies into the suite, which involves internal review and presentation to the working group before making pull requests. They also noted the need to reach out to Efan's group at Pinovia to discuss potential collaboration on using these ontologies.
