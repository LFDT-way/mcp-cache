## Agenda

The biweekly Standards Working Group has been defining a set of ontologies that enables the description of anthropogenic impact following W3C semantic web guidelines. 
The public release of four interrelated ontologies in OWL, JSON-LD and TTL format is planned for the third quarter of 2025. 
We foresee the use of the ontologies in data models, MRV systems, and AI-assisted applications.
Anyone is welcome to join.
The call will take place every other Tuesday at 8 AM Pacific Time.
More about the Standards group is at: https://wiki.hyperledger.org/display/CASIG/Standards+WG

## Overview

The meeting focused on finalizing the project logo and discussing various operational updates for the Accountable Impact Commons project. The team reviewed multiple logo options and overwhelmingly chose option one, with participants agreeing on its simplicity and versatility across different backgrounds. ## Details

### Logo Finalization Meeting Discussion

The group discussed weather and home heating/cooling systems before transitioning to business matters. Christiaan apologized for missing the previous meeting due to a family commitment in Pretoria and announced they would focus on finalizing the logo at this meeting.

### Logo Design Review Meeting

The team discussed logo design options, with Alex expressing preference for designs without gray and yellow combinations due to visibility issues. Christiaan and Alfonso also supported option one, with Kit providing technical insights about the logo's versatility across different backgrounds and its legibility at small sizes. Alfonso committed to providing alternative yellow color options for consideration, while the group noted the current design's similarity to Google Chrome's color scheme.

### GitHub Display Name Update

The team discussed updating their GitHub display name to "Accountable Input Commons" under LF Labs, which Alex confirmed has been implemented. Christiaan approved of the change and suggested offering to help Rye with the URL update to make the process more palatable. Kit suggested waiting to approach Rye about the URL change until after the logo is implemented and the page appears more polished, though Alex noted they are restricted in what changes they can make to the GitHub page due to permissions limitations.

### Linux Foundation Branding Challenges

The team discussed branding challenges with Linux Foundation lab repositories, particularly regarding logo usage. They determined that while they must maintain the official Linux Foundation branding at the lab level, they could potentially customize branding for individual GitHub repositories. Alex agreed to continue the discussion with Roy about implementing custom branding for their specific repositories. The conversation also touched on a potential ISO interaction related to blockchain taxonomy work, though this was not fully explored in the meeting.

### AYA ISO Review Discussion

Alfonso shared that Diana Barrero, head of research at GBBC, offered to connect them with ISO officials to review AYA, expressing strong interest in the initiative. Christiaan suggested undergoing the ISO review process as a valuable opportunity to assess AYA's maturity and receive feedback for improvement, despite potential challenges. Alex mentioned potential connections to ISO 14060 greenhouse gas standards and suggested exploring how these standards could relate to AYA. The group discussed their original approach of focusing on the underlying structure of standards rather than specific standards themselves.

### Semantic Tool Positioning Strategy

The team discussed positioning their semantic structure tool as a knowledge graph for traversing standards across different environments, not as a standard itself. Christiaan suggested reviewing their existing materials and creating a focused presentation or introduction document to better explain the tool's usability and provide orientation for potential users. The group agreed to work on developing this presentation material over the next month, with Christiaan recommending they approach potential reviewers with respect and value for their time.

### Responsible Generative AI Framework

Alfonso shared his work with the Linux Foundation AI and Data, where he is contributing to the Responsible Generative AI framework (ERGAP) by proposing an Accountable Impact Framework to guide AI usage for environmental sustainability. The framework aims to address the challenge of people being unaware of AI's environmental consequences by incorporating an accountable impact framework. Christiaan mentioned their work with Flurry, a semantic graph database that combines graph and vector databases with native AI integration, as a potential implementation approach for similar concepts.

### LFX Crowd Platform Discussion

Alfonso discussed exploring LFX Crowd as an alternative to GitHub sponsorships, noting that LFVT had allowed them to use this platform and that the senior manager of mentorship at LFVT confirmed they don't use the funding mechanism. The team agreed that pursuing LFX Crowd might be preferable to GitHub sponsorships since it's part of a larger Linux Foundation platform and would avoid putting pressure on Ryan's team. Alfonso mentioned that mentees would be filling out forms this week, including an executive summary and letter of intention for their project.

### LFX Trout Funding Planning

The team discussed moving forward with LFX Trout Funding after getting LFDT approval. Alfonso explained that LFX crowdfunding works as a marketplace where projects are displayed and contributors can fund them through LFX, with the project requiring deliverables and a managed budget. Christiaan suggested developing a clear presentation and elevator pitch for maximum impact, while also considering the roadmap and potential resource allocation for ongoing development. The team agreed to follow up on the roadmap discussion and schedule Jeff's presentation on the digital twin demo in the future.
