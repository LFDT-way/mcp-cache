## Agenda

The biweekly Standards Working Group has been defining a set of ontologies that enables the description of anthropogenic impact following W3C semantic web guidelines. 
The public release of four interrelated ontologies in OWL, JSON-LD and TTL format is planned for the third quarter of 2025. 
We foresee the use of the ontologies in data models, MRV systems, and AI-assisted applications.
Anyone is welcome to join.
The call will take place every other Tuesday at 8 AM Pacific Time.
More about the Standards group is at: https://wiki.hyperledger.org/display/CASIG/Standards+WG

## Overview

This meeting was an introductory session between the Climate Action and Accounting Special Interest Group and representatives from Nature Services/Regenera.earth, a Peruvian ecosystem management company. Frank and Enrique from Nature Services explained their work connecting companies managing environmental footprints with land managers, particularly in the Amazon and Andes regions of Peru. They discussed their interest in semantic frameworks and ontologies, particularly the AI Ontology Suite developed by the group. Alex and Alfonso explained the origins and purpose of the AI Ontology Suite, clarifying the differences between Linux Foundation and W3C approaches to standards and ontologies. The group discussed how the ontology could support Nature Services' work in standardizing communication across different cultural contexts and land management practices. The conversation ended with an agreement to schedule a follow-up session where Nature Services would share examples of their work to explore potential applications of the ontology.

## Details

### Natural Capital Language Standardization Collaboration

The meeting began with introductions from participants, including Frank and Enrique from Nature Services/Regenera.earth, who explained their work in ecosystem management and natural capital registry in Peru and the UK. They discussed their interest in semantics and language standardization for different communities and sectors. Kit shared her background in software development focused on climate change solutions and involvement with the ontology group. The group expressed interest in exploring potential collaborations, particularly around language standardization and Web3 applications in natural capital accounting.

### Carbon Accounting Standardization Project

Jeff discussed his role as chairperson for the carbon accounting and supply chain special interest groups, focusing on standardizing data for a global supply chain project involving 40 partners. He explained their use of AI and IoT technology to model data, particularly for tracking Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions, with a focus on avoiding double counting in Scope 3 emissions tracking. Jeff also mentioned his work on a Python model for data integration and standardization, which stores information in blockchain or a world database, and noted his collaboration with Dr. Frank and Enrique from the University of California, Berkeley. The discussion ended with a question about the economic incentives driving their stewardship model, though this was not addressed in the transcript.

### Impact Accounting Framework Development

Frank clarified that their focus is on services rather than offsets or credits, operating at the unit level (such as tons of CO2) regardless of how they are packaged. Alex explained their team's interest in developing a framework for impact accounting that can consistently describe outcomes across different domains and standards, emphasizing the need to account for various crises beyond climate change. The discussion highlighted their decision to create a descriptive rather than prescriptive language for impact measurement, allowing for consistent description of impacts across different standards and practices.

### Shared Ontology for Crisis Solutions

The group discussed the development of a shared language and ontology to address multiple interconnected crises, including climate, biodiversity, and water issues. Frank and Alfonso emphasized the importance of creating a unified approach that transcends silos and can be applied across different domains. They also discussed the potential for this ontology to be machine-readable and usable in Web2, Web3, and AI contexts. Alfonso explained the relationship between the Linux Foundation and W3C, describing them as complementary entities, with Linux promoting participation and W3C focusing on standardization.

### AI Ontology Registry Integration Plans

The team discussed the registration of their AI ontology suite with W3C and its integration with Linux Foundation's Climate Action and Accounting Special Interest Group. Alex explained that the ontology suite is now hosted on W3C's registry with a W3ID.org URL, allowing it to be publicly accessible and reusable across industries. The group also discussed plans to create a dedicated "Semantic Frameworks" lab under Linux Foundation's Decentralized Trust division. Frank and Enrique, who are from a forestry engineering background, expressed interest in potentially using the ontology suite but requested to review the structure first before sharing specific data examples. The team agreed to schedule a follow-up meeting next week to discuss how the ontology could be applied to forestry engineering use cases.
