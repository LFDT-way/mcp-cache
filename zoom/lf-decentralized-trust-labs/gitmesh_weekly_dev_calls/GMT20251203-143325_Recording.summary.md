### Git Contributor Pathway Tiers

The meeting focused on the Git mesh contributor pathway and its governance structure. Ryan explained that contributors would receive badges for their contributions, starting with newbie contributors who have their first PR merged. Active contributors, who have made more than 5 contributions over 3 months, may be promoted to active contributor status. Core contributors, who have more significant contributions and the right to review others' code, are the highest tier in the pathway.

### Git Mesh Contributor Tiers Explained

Ryan explained the contributor tiers for Git Mesh, starting with core contributors who fully own and can review code for a subsystem after making significant contributions. He described principal contributors as those who have contributed for more than six months and have made a significant impact on the project, allowing them to work directly with maintainers and influence the roadmap.

### Git Mesh Contributor Governance Structure

Ryan discussed a governance structure for Git mesh contributors, outlining roles from contributor to principal maintainer with a 6-month progression timeline. He explained that promotion and demotion votes would be handled by roles higher in the hierarchy, with badges tracking contributor status and inactivity leading to demotion. Ryan also mentioned that security reporting would be handled by maintainers, with the entire governance system stored in a contributors.yml file.

### GitMesh Editions and Tech Updates

Ryan and Parv discussed the structure of GitMesh, highlighting the separation into Community Edition (CE) and Enterprise Edition (EE) with distinct security reporting processes. Ryan explained the licensing details, noting that the CE operates under Apache 2.0 while certain modules are restricted to Enterprise use. Parv detailed the technical stack changes, including a shift to VueGenz for frontend and a monorepo architecture with microservices for backend, along with the introduction of new libraries and tools.
