## Details

### Governance Framework Discussion Session

Art, the managing director of the Varna Foundation, led a session focused on governance from Bhutan, where he was attending a convergence forum organized by Yeezy Labs (Binance's investment arm). The session aimed to gather feedback on governance approaches with the goal of releasing the governance piece of a standard for public comment the following week. Art mentioned that he would present the governance framework and seek input from participants like Drummond and Karen.

### PDP Governance Structure Draft

Art presented a draft governance structure for the PDP standard, outlining a three-part framework covering governance, membership, and conformance. The proposed governance structure includes a board with an elected chair and technical committee, with the board responsible for setting conformance criteria and managing membership while the technical committee handles technical reviews and specifications. Art explained that this structure aims to ensure long-term adoption and maintenance of the PDP standard, with plans to launch a public comment period at GDC in September and close it in October.

### LFDT Lab Governance Structure Planning

Art discussed plans for establishing a governance structure for the LFDT lab, emphasizing that initial arrangements would be voluntary and interim, with maintainers handling functions until formal governance is established. Karen raised important questions about financial support, contract authorization, and legal personality, while Drummond clarified that governance projects at LF typically require separate organizational structures rather than using open source or JDF project types. Art acknowledged the need to move quickly on membership levels and governance structure, with a one-year timeline to determine the legal personality and relationship to LFDT and Linux, and mentioned that the Vana Foundation would administer initial sign-ups until formal governance is ready.

### AI Standard Adoption Strategy

Art explained that while they have relationships with potential adopters through the Varner and Open Data Labs network, they haven't yet sought formal support for the standard as they want the community to be satisfied with it first. The protocol is currently being assessed by the EU Sandbox for AI, and Justin noted that the US is moving toward data autarky, creating an opportunity for global standards like this one. Both regulatory adoption and private sector relationships were identified as potential fast tracks for standard adoption, with European regulatory bodies showing particular interest due to concerns about US dominance in AI systems.

### Regulatory Acceptance Strategy Discussion

The group discussed concerns about regulatory acceptance of their proposed structure, with Lisa highlighting that regulators might not view it as sufficiently open due to closed memberships and lack of government participation mechanisms. Art and Lisa agreed to ask Professor Rotolo about government buy-in strategies during an upcoming panel discussion next week. The conversation also touched on different approaches to generating demand, with Art referencing Gavin Starks' experience with the UK Open Banking Standard, and Justin suggesting that both bottom-up and top-down approaches could be useful simultaneously.

### Global Data Governance Challenges

Drummond raised questions about the scope of governance for personal data management, highlighting the challenges of designing a system that can scale globally like DNS or ICANN, which took years to establish. Art acknowledged the need for rapid action but noted the tension between speed and scalability, suggesting the need for interim arrangements to address immediate concerns. The discussion touched on the potential benefits of a supporter tier for organizations, particularly in addressing AI's public relations challenges related to personal data security.

### Data Portability Standards Adoption Challenges

The group discussed challenges with adopting data portability standards, with Lisa highlighting that DTI has struggled to attract members and get them to implement coding practices due to organizational barriers within large companies. Justin suggested that having both small entities joining at lower standards and large entities joining at higher standards could create social pressure for adoption, while warning against large companies joining at lower levels and setting incorrect standards. Art referenced governance design considerations around board elections and organizational voting rights, while Lisa shared a relevant example from the IETF's experience with Jabber/XMPP standards, noting how bringing work to IETF helped build industry awareness before transitioning back to private governance.

### AI Governance Standards Discussion

Karen discussed IEEE's standards process and open source community support, noting challenges in developing AI governance models due to sovereignty issues among different stakeholders. She advised focusing on common pain points rather than trying to represent all approaches, emphasizing the importance of interoperability through personal data management. Art presented the PDP system's three-actor framework (source, accessor, and verified operator) and explained how conformance requirements could serve as both carrots and sticks for companies like OpenAI, particularly regarding data connector development and official source verification. The discussion concluded with a question about the effectiveness of compliance "sticks" in the data access space.

### Data Portability Challenges Discussion

The discussion focused on data portability challenges and opportunities, with Lisa explaining how traditional companies typically resist third-party data access through various defensive measures. Art and Karen discussed the potential for an open source community to develop faster solutions and addressed the need for user education about data handling practices. The conversation covered different verification levels for data access (sources, accessors, and operators) and identified smaller companies as key potential adopters of data portability solutions, particularly those seeking to compete with larger platforms.

### Compliance Measures Discussion

Art and Karen discussed the need for compliance measures in their structure, with Karen suggesting annual compliance reviews and attestation requirements based on EU practices. Art planned to present their draft at GDC and seek public feedback, with recommendations to also seek input from governments like Switzerland or Brazil, particularly from developing nations. Art committed to sending out a recap with additional questions and gathering written feedback through GitHub or direct contact.
