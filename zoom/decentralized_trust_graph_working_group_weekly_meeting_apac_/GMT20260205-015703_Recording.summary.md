## Details

### VTA Development and Governance

The meeting focused on the development and governance of verifiable trust agents (VTAs) and their associated infrastructure. Drummond explained the outcomes of a recent workshop, which clarified the distinction between personal and community VTAs and outlined the architecture needed to support both. He emphasized that the First Person Cooperative is working on open-source code modules to implement this architecture and will steward the code to ensure it meets governance requirements. Sankarshan raised concerns about the potential for consolidation in the VTA ecosystem and the need to consider consumer-grade adoption. The group discussed the upcoming H2H Summit and Linux Foundation Member Summit, with Joe considering attending the latter. Drummond offered to arrange a comp ticket for Joe and mentioned a new topic he plans to introduce at the next meeting, which involves naming and discovery in the decentralized trust graph.

### Human-Friendly DID Naming Proposal

Drummond shared a proposal for human-friendly naming for DIDs, which he plans to present next week. The proposal, called "agent names," aims to layer human-readable names that resolve to verifiable trust agents. Drummond explained the background of this concept, which has been in development for about two years. He emphasized that while DIDs are decentralized and secure, they are not human-friendly, and this proposal addresses that limitation.

### Agent Name Protocol Development Discussion

Drummond and Sankarshan discussed the development and implementation of a new protocol for agent names, inspired by existing systems like email addresses and used in platforms such as Twitter, Slack, and Signal. Drummond explained that the protocol allows for agent names to be globally recognized and verifiable, resolving to a DID (Decentralized Identifier) that can be used for agent-agent communications. Joe raised a question about managing multiple personas across different domains, to which Drummond confirmed that agent names can be registered to any domain and shared globally, while also emphasizing the importance of verifying agent name control through a protocol.

### Agent Names for Decentralized Identifiers

Drummond explained the concept of agent names as a simpler way to find DID (Decentralized Identifiers) and emphasized the importance of verifiable credentials in enhancing security against phishing. He highlighted the flexibility of agent names, allowing them to resolve to the same or different DIDs, and discussed the potential for controlling multiple personas with separate connections and proofs of personhood. Drummond also previewed the integration of this capability into the DTG spec, noting its potential application in social media as a universal DM system, and addressed concerns about platform-specific rules.

Sankarshan explained that PERDA is a concept involving unservable handles that link back to an identity. Drummond mentioned he would introduce a specification next week, and Sankarshan agreed to review it once it's publicly shared. Joe mentioned he was too busy reading papers to read novels. The conversation ended with plans to follow up via Signal.
