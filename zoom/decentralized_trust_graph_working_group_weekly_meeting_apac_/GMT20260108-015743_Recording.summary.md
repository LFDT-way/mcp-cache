## Overview

The meeting introduced Geun-Hyung to the Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group, which aims to build global internet-wide trust models and holds regular calls across different time zones. ### Global Trust Models Working Group

The Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group, co-chaired by Drummond and others, aims to build internet-wide trust models globally, with calls scheduled for different time zones including Americas, Europe, and APAC. Geun-Hyung explained their interest in the group's focus on decentralized trust and participation in the APAC call. Drummond mentioned the group's intention to have more representation from the APAC region and invited interested participants to join as co-chairs. The meeting began with a review of the antitrust policy notice and an introduction to the meeting page, which includes links to the Confluence wiki.

### Decentralized Trust Graph Updates

The meeting focused on updates and announcements related to the Decentralized Trust Graph working group. Drummond introduced a new event, the Summit on Human Agency, scheduled for February 23rd, which will precede the Linux Foundation Member Summit and showcase demonstrations of Decentralized Trust Graph credentials. The group discussed their mission to create a cryptographically verifiable web of trust relationships using W3C standards, with a focus on privacy-preserving zero-knowledge proofs for proof of personhood. Two task forces were highlighted: the Risk Assessment and Harms Prevention group, which meets weekly, and the Credential Task Force, which is defining requirements for personal and verifiable relationship credentials.

### Decentralized Trust Credentials Overview

Drummond presented a glossary and a document on bootstrapping verifiable trust communities, explaining the six types of DTG credentials: edge credentials, annotation credentials, and invitation credentials. He described how these credentials define relationships and memberships in a decentralized trust graph, and mentioned that the specifications and discussions are hosted on GitHub. Geun-Hyung expressed understanding but requested a visual representation of the credential relationships. Drummond acknowledged this and mentioned that a separate diagram would be needed to illustrate the relations between the credentials.

### Linux Trust Graph Security Updates

Drummond presented updates on the development of a Decentralized Trust Graph system, explaining its four phases and its application to the Linux Kernel Maintainer Verification Project for the Linux Foundation. He emphasized that the system aims to address security concerns in open-source projects, following a request from Jim Zemlin, executive director of the Linux Foundation, after a malware attack. Drummond also mentioned that a new version of the glossary and related documents would be released the following week to incorporate recent updates.

### Decentralized Trust Graph Development

Drummond explained their work on decentralized trust graphs, which are similar to TrustRP's model and aim to create a more decentralized trust layer for the internet. He described how these graphs can be created independently and connected into verifiable trust networks, with the goal of having first specifications ready this quarter. Drummond also mentioned his focus on the First Person Project, which aims to build a verifiable trust network for proof of personhood.

### Joint Working Group Welcome Meeting

Drummond welcomed Geun-Hyung to the joint working group, explaining that it includes members from both the Decentralized Foundation (DIF) and TrustRIP. He mentioned that the group holds weekly calls and collaborates asynchronously on GitHub. Drummond highlighted the involvement of universities, particularly the Applied Social Media Team at Harvard, as potential early adopters of their work. He encouraged Geun-Hyung to actively participate and investigate the group's activities.
