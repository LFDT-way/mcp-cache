### Technology Stack Working Group Meeting

### Transparent Protocol Update Plans

Wenjing announced plans to update the transparent protocol from revision 2 to revision 3 within two quarters, focusing on cleaning up language, addressing inconsistencies, and completing informational sections. Drummond shared positive feedback from recent conference presentations about decentralized trust graphs, noting strong audience reception at the Future and Review Conference in San Diego and the GOSIM conference.

### Agent-Driven Development Insights

Drummond shared insights from recent sessions at GoSim, where experts advised against learning programming languages like Rust directly, instead focusing on managing agents. Wenjing agreed that while learning these languages might be useful for debugging, it's generally better to let agents handle the coding. They discussed the shift toward agent-driven development and the importance of managing agents rather than writing code manually. The conversation concluded with Drummond mentioning ongoing work on the technology architecture spec, which remains incomplete pending additional resources.

### TRQP Specification Approval Completion

The Trust Registry Task Force completed the approval of TRQP (Trust Research Query Protocol) as a formal specification, now moving to work on TRQP Core. Darrell explained that next steps include identifying areas for additional work, such as discovery capabilities and implementation guidebooks, with plans to have a detailed plan by next week. The group discussed how TRQP aligns with the needs of the Creator Assertion Working Group, which includes organizations like RIAA and IPTC focused on content creator identity verification.

### TSP Implementation Evolution Discussion

Wenjing discussed the evolution of TSP implementations, noting four known implementations including Carrie's, Open Wallet Foundation's, one submitted to AFDT, and a fourth in a telco-based language. Wenjing reported on performance testing results showing TSP's improved efficiency with larger message sizes, and mentioned plans to work on interoperability and formalizing the specification after revisions are complete. The discussion was occasionally disrupted by technical connection issues affecting Wenjing's participation.

### DID Methods and Trust Development

Drummond provided updates on two DID methods being developed at TrustRIP: DID WebVH and DID SCID. He noted that DID WebVH may become location-independent, similar to DID SCID, which leverages other SCID-based methods. Drummond also discussed the Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group, which is building on the trustworthy stack to define credentials and trust operations at Layer 3 and Layer 4, with the OpenVTC project recently being contributed to LF Decentralized Trust Labs.

### Trust Protocol Standardization Discussion

Drummond reported that OpenVTC has defined over 100 atomic-level trust tasks at TrustHas.org, which is driving progress toward replacing Linux kernel's PGP web of trust with a decentralized trust graph. He shared that the EU is shifting focus to business wallets and needs to standardize a wallet-to-wallet messaging protocol, currently considering MLS from IETF rather than TSP. Wenjing clarified that TSP could support multi-point messaging through existing protocols like Matrix, which has already been ported to TSP, rather than requiring new development. The group discussed the importance of getting Trust Over IP protocols to formal standardization status and agreed to follow up on the EU dialogue through the Trust Review Task Force meeting scheduled for the 18th.
