The quarterly Technical Stack Working Group plenary meeting focused on updates from various task forces and working groups. ### Product Spec Revision Updates

Drummond explained that Alex Duedale has returned after taking a break due to burnout and has joined a new SSI company as Director of Product or VP Product. Alex is working on a revised version of the spec that incorporates feedback from telephony and verified caller ID experts, particularly from Daniel Hartman. The new spec focuses on machine-verifiable assertions across multiple identifier systems like X509 certificates and LEI, providing enhanced assurance through cross-referencing of different identification methods.

### Certificate and DID Systems Discussion

Drummond and Eric discussed the differences between X509 certificates and DID/VC systems, explaining that X509 contains both public key material and credential subject information, while DID contains only public key material with separate credential information. They noted that the new draft specification has been moved to GitHub and will be reviewed by Alex, with plans to address outstanding issues before finalization. Drummond mentioned that TRQP 2.0 is scheduled for a steering committee vote next week, and discussed the progress of various specifications including TSP-enabled Agents and DID methods, noting delays in the recommendation process due to requirements for multiple implementations and concerns about including blockchain methods.

The group discussed revisiting and updating the Trust over IP design principles, with Makki sharing a draft document that expanded the principles from 17 to 21, dividing them into "dry" and "wet" code categories. The team agreed to review the document before the summer working group meeting, where they plan to further discuss how to operationalize the principles through a risk assessment tool. Drummond also mentioned plans to present updates on three mature specifications (TSP, TRP, and SCID) at an upcoming members meeting, though the meeting format had to be extended due to an overflow of presentation materials.
