## Overview

The Trust Over IP steering committee meeting covered several key agenda items including updates to the antitrust policy notice, a press release announcement about DID methods from DIF, and the approval of a new working group proposal from Kwai focused on homomorphic encryption for privacy-preserving AI. The committee discussed DIF's AI agent contribution policy and its implications for Trust Over IP, with Grace from DIF explaining the rationale behind requiring human review of AI-generated contributions due to IP protection concerns. Balazs presented the Agent Community application seeking endorsement for the .agent top-level domain, highlighting their 28,000+ members and 8,000+ company supporters, though concerns were raised about governance challenges and the need for trust mechanisms. The steering committee approved the new Kwai working group proposal by consensus and discussed the twice-yearly reporting requirements to the Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Committee.

## Details

### Trust Policy and DID Updates

The meeting began with Carly explaining changes to the trust over IP policy regarding note takers, and Drummond noting two agenda items related to AI trust. Daniela mentioned receiving an answer from her trademark lawyer and confirmed the new antitrust policy language had been distributed via email to chairs. The group discussed an upcoming press release from DIF about the first two DID methods (DidWebVH and DidWebPlus) that have passed approval, with Grace expected to provide more details about timing. Nicholas raised questions about the approval process for DidWebS, noting it has been under consideration alongside DidWebPlus for an extended period.

### DibWebS Review Status Discussion

Grace explained that DibWebS did not meet the criteria during its 60-day review period and is currently on hold, not formally rejected. She emphasized that putting it on hold sets a poor precedent and expressed willingness to remove it from hold once it meets the criteria, potentially with an expedited review process. Nicholas requested that DibWebS be acknowledged as still in the pipeline, and Drummond suggested that Nicholas and Grace discuss and reach a decision on how to handle the communication about this status.

### Steering Committee Updates and Approvals

The steering committee discussed and approved a quote for a press release that Grace will publish on a blog tomorrow. They reviewed minor updates to the JDC operating agreement but determined no action was needed. Daniela reported that trademark updates for "Trust Over IP" are being handled proactively, covering both US and EU markets, with no further action required from the committee.

### Linux Foundation Reporting Requirements

The steering committee discussed requirements for reporting to the Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Technical Advisory Committee (TAC). Daniela suggested creating a short form for working group chairs to report on activities and seek TAC assistance twice yearly, rather than using a burdensome paperwork process. The committee agreed to implement this form-based approach, with Carly noting they would include a requirement to submit something even if there's no reportable activity. The meeting then transitioned to reviewing a new working group proposal from Kauai, though the details were not completed in the transcript.

### Privacy-Preserving Technology Proposal

Reza presented a proposal for a new work group focused on privacy-preserving technology using homomorphic encryption. He explained their research on "Virtual Private Knowledge" (VPK), which aims to enable encrypted data processing by AI while maintaining searchability. The discussion centered on addressing the threat of data leakage to SaaS providers rather than external eavesdroppers, proposing a new protocol called Secure Model Context Protocol (SMCP) to secure data transactions between users and service providers.

### NIST Competition for Encryption Algorithms

Reza proposed creating a NIST-like competition to develop more secure and practical reference encryption algorithms, explaining that Quai has grown to over 1,000 volunteers working on AI safety over three years. The steering committee members expressed support for the proposal, with Drummond recommending it fit well under the TrustRP umbrella and Scott asking about momentum and alignment with existing working groups. The discussion highlighted potential synergies with other efforts, particularly around verifiable data loyalty and fiduciary responsibilities, with Nicholas and Reza finding alignment in their approaches to data privacy and mathematical solutions.

### Neutral Domain Technology Working Group

The steering committee approved the formation of a new working group focused on neutral domain technology, with Reza as the proposed chair. The group aims to prevent duplication of efforts across existing working groups and leverage open source collaboration. Daniela will work with Reza and others to complete the formal charter process and set up working group calls, while Solomon shared a link to a published paper about the reference implementation. The new working group will be the first since the Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group a year ago.

### AI Agent Contribution Policies Review

The steering committee discussed AI agent contribution policies, with Grace explaining that the Digital Identity Foundation (DIFF) adopted restrictions after experiencing issues with AI agents contributing to mailing lists. The main concern was the inability to verify IP rights for content generated by AI agents, leading to a policy requiring human review and submission of all contributions. The group agreed to review and provide feedback on the Linux Foundation's Trust and Security Technical Advisory (TAC) guidelines rather than developing their own policy from scratch.

### Agent Community ICANN Application Discussion

Balazs presented the Agent Community initiative, which aims to secure the .agent top-level domain through ICANN's community priority process. The community has grown to over 28,000 members with 8,000 companies providing endorsement letters, and the application deadline is August 12th. The discussion focused on governance challenges around trusted agents and potential concerns about spam and security, with participants questioning whether existing trust frameworks like Trust over IP could provide a better foundation. The steering committee will follow up with an email discussion and vote on whether to endorse the Agent Community application.
