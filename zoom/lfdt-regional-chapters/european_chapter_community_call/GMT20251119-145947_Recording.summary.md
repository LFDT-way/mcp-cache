### SPL Projects and Digital Euro Updates

Dominik shared updates on ongoing projects at SPL, including a logistics product for tracking agricultural food production in Spain, which is moving into its second phase after completing a prototype. He mentioned that the product will be described in papers next year and that they are inviting new experts from the blockchain space, including Gazi, to their meetings. Stoyan discussed the development of a digital euro, noting that while the European Central Bank's digital euro initiative is not progressing, a Plan B involving a consortium of major European banks led by Jan Sel, former CEO of Coinbase Europe, is being explored. Stoyan also mentioned that the European Banking Authority has proposed restrictions on CASPs holding and transferring e-money tokens, which he criticized as unnecessary.

### EBA's Crypto Tokens Stance Concern

Stoyan expressed concerns about the European Banking Authority's (EBA) stance on Imani tokens, which has created a legal limbo for crypto businesses. He noted that the EBA's position goes against the policies of the European Commission and ECB, and he expected it to be retracted. Stoyan mentioned meeting with Jan three weeks ago about a project related to digital crypto euros, which Jan later informed him was not moving forward. The group discussed inviting Jan to share more information about the digital crypto euros at an open discussion.

### Ethereum Privacy Mainnet Update

Ghazi provided an update on Zama's focus on the mainnet launch on Ethereum, which is a priority and will be announced in a few weeks. He explained that Zama is a fully homomorphic encryption company that has integrated a privacy side chain with two EVMs, starting with Sepolia and now adding Ethereum. The company aims to add remaining EVMs in Q2 and Q3, enabling confidential token transfers where encrypted data can be computed and verified without disclosing actual data. Ghazi also mentioned that they have an ERC-7984 pushed to the Ethereum foundation and are building the ecosystem with support from various companies. Ivan shared his work on decentralized identity for objects and cultural artifacts, which he is in the process of patenting through Sophia University.

### Project Collaboration and Outreach Update

### RWA Tokenization: Challenges and Solutions

Ghazi and Stoyan discussed the state of compliance and real-world asset (RWA) tokenization in the blockchain space. Ghazi highlighted the existence of several ERCs focusing on compliance, such as ERC-3643, and noted the maturity of compliance thinking for institutional-grade custody but lack of widespread adoption. Stoyan and Ghazi agreed that while there are multiple token standards for RWAs, such as ERC-3643 and CMTA, the current solutions are overly complex and not necessarily needed at the protocol level, often resembling monolithic systems that contradict the essence of blockchain technology.

### Blockchain Compliance and Exchange Challenges

The group discussed compliance challenges in the blockchain industry, with Stoyan sharing insights about failed projects like Fireblocks and Cooper that violated fundamental blockchain principles. They explored the difficulties of listing compliant tokens on major exchanges, noting that most exchanges don't support MICA-compliant tokens, limiting liquidity options for issuers. The conversation concluded with a discussion about upcoming December events, including AWS executive dinners in Berlin and a major event by the European Crypto Initiative, though Przemyslaw noted that Poland doesn't have any planned events for December.

### Bitvava's European Expansion Strategy

Stoyan discussed Bitvava, a Dutch cryptocurrency exchange with 2 million registered customers, noting that this number is misleading as only about 50,000 actively trade with meaningful amounts. He explained that Bitvava is expanding into Germany, France, and Spain, and has a partnership with Keyrock, a major market maker based in Belgium, which gives them access to 60-70% of global liquidity. The conversation ended with Ivan mentioning he would be in Poland next week, and Przemyslaw noting they would be in Poznan and Wroclaw, with no immediate plans to meet.
