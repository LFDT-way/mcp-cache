## Details

### Firefly Migration Strategy Discussion

The meeting focused on the migration of the Firefly organization from its current URL to Hyperledger-Firefly as part of a Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust requirement. Dan presented two issues related to the migration, primarily addressing how to minimize impact on downstream consumers of Firefly packages, particularly for Go and npm. The discussion covered strategies for handling GitHub redirects, updating package references, and managing Docker images and registry, with specific attention to NPM package scopes and documentation redirects. The team also briefly touched on the impact on Homebrew formulas and agreed to test the migration process to ensure minimal disruption to end users.

### Repository Migration Planning Meeting

The team discussed the impact of moving repositories from Hyperledger to their own organization. They addressed concerns about URL changes for GitHub libraries and confirmed that forks and local repositories would be unaffected. The group decided to rename some repositories and create new teams with appropriate access controls in the new organization. They planned to release version 1.5 after the migration to minimize disruption. Simon also mentioned a stable version release of the Pallass library, which would improve stability but not include the desired TX3 features. The team aimed to complete the migration by early June and agreed to time the release with broader updates to minimize breaking changes.

### Web3 Signer Integration Discussion

Kwanjoon discussed integrating a Web3 signer with Firefly's EVM connector, specifically addressing challenges with nonce management and transaction control. Enrique suggested two potential approaches: either integrating the Web3 signer with Firefly's existing system or extending Firefly Signer to support HSM systems. Kwanjoon agreed to provide more details on Discord after discussing with their team, focusing initially on EVM-compatible chains.
