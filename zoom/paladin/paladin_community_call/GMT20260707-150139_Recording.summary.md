## Agenda

An open call for the Paladin project, a platform for building programmable privacy preserving tokens on EVM, with integrated client and wallet. Anyone interested in Paladin is welcome to join. More about Paladin is at:
https://github.com/LFDT-Paladin/

## Overview

This community call focused on the recent release of Paladin V1 and updates on ongoing development work. Matthew announced that Paladin V1 was released approximately 10 days prior, featuring significant improvements in stability, robustness, and new distributed sequencing components, along with updated Noto token versions and standardization work around lockable semantics. The team discussed progress on the Java SDK mentorship program with Divyam making good progress on adding Java SDK support alongside existing Go and TypeScript SDKs. Anna reported on performance optimization work focusing on minting Noto tokens, identifying areas for improvement including logging, protocol formats, and assembly processes. Sankalp Sharma shared his company's interest in contributing a custom private domain with ZK circuits and multi-tenant issuance capabilities to Paladin, leading to a discussion about the process for integrating custom domains into the existing framework.

## Details

### Paladin V1 Release Update

Matthew announced the release of Paladin V1 about 10 days ago, which included significant work on stability testing, robustness, and new features like distributed sequencing. He reported that Divyam is making good progress on the Java SDK mentorship program, with PRs being merged into a non-main branch. Anna discussed her performance work focusing on minting Noto tokens, which has identified several areas for improvement including logging and assembly methods. Matthew also mentioned plans to migrate the community wiki from its current format to a source-controlled system like GitHub Pages to improve tracking and scalability.

### Paladin Integration Planning Discussion

Sharma discussed their organization's current use of Paladin for internal deployments and testing, mentioning their attendance at the recent LiveDD event where they presented on privacy topics. Sharma expressed interest in integrating their private domain with unique ZK circuits and contracts into Paladin, noting that Peter Broadhurst had indicated Railgun is pursuing a similar integration approach with customized circuits.

### Private Domain Contribution Discussion

Sharma expressed interest in contributing a private domain with multi-tenant issuance and auditing capabilities to the Paladin open-source ZK circuit project. Matthew suggested connecting Sharma with Jim Zhang, who has been working on a similar custom domain implementation, and proposed starting with a prototype in a separate repository while discussing the best approach for integration. They agreed to coordinate next steps through email and Discord, with Matthew planning to send an introductory email connecting Sharma with Jim and the broader team.
