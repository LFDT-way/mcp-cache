## Overview

The meeting focused on discussing the development progress and features of Paladin, an Ethereum client, towards its V1 release. Matthew and Anna provided updates on key features, including a new transaction sequencing engine, reassembly of transactions based on dependencies, and a common reusable state machine package. Stefano, a research lead at Nevermind, was introduced to Paladin's architecture and asked questions about transaction sequencing and consensus mechanisms. The team explained Paladin's distributed sequencer design, which allows any eligible node to coordinate transactions for privacy groups, while maintaining that the base ledger remains the source of truth. Anna and Matthew also highlighted improvements in problem diagnosis and user experience for transaction coordination. The conversation ended with a brief discussion about wallets and interacting with Paladin, suggesting potential areas for further exploration.

## Details

### Community Calls Wiki Discussion

Matthew and Stefano greeted each other and discussed the logistics of starting the call. Matthew mentioned sharing the community calls wiki page and displaying the Linux Foundation Antitrust notice and LFDC Code of Conduct, which Stefano should be aware of as they proceed with the call.

### Paladin Development Progress Overview

Stefano introduced himself as the research lead at Nevermind, interested in privacy-preserving solutions for enterprises and exploring Paladin. Matthew provided an overview of Paladin's development progress, focusing on the V1 release, which includes a new transaction sequencing engine and bug fixes. Anna explained a PR that refactors state machines into a common reusable package, enhancing logging and debugging capabilities. The team discussed the importance of the new state machine package for future updates and improvements in Paladin.

### Paladin Distributed Sequencer Architecture

Matthew explained that in Paladin, the state machine is run by every instance in a privacy group, and he confirmed that the sequencing process is handled by a specific client within the system. He then shared a branch of the documentation that includes diagrams related to V1 development, which provides more details on the distributed sequencer architecture.

### Distributed Sequencer for Privacy Groups

Matthew explained the new distributed sequencer in V1, which allows any eligible node to coordinate transaction sequencing for privacy groups, with nodes 3 and 4 shown as examples. He described how the sequencer can handle coordinator failures and support future cases where not every party needs to endorse transactions. Matthew also clarified the differences between notarized tokens (where the notary coordinates all transactions) and privacy preserving tokens (where each node coordinates its own transactions).

### Paladin's Distributed Sequencing Approach

The discussion focused on distributed sequencing and consensus in Paladin, where Matthew explained that unlike proof-of-stake chains, Paladin works with collaborating parties who don't need an additional consensus layer on top of the base ledger. Stefano raised questions about privacy groups and concurrency issues, which Matthew addressed by clarifying that the source of truth remains the base ledger. Matthew also mentioned that a PR has been merged to improve problem diagnosis and user experience by making the transaction process more transparent between nodes.

### Paladin Wallet Functionality Discussion

The meeting focused on discussing the functionality of wallets and their interaction with Paladin, particularly in relation to Zito tokens. Matthew explained that while Paladin behaves like a wallet, it primarily provides proof of ownership rather than storing token balances directly. He noted that offline data is necessary to verify holdings, especially for privacy-preserving tokens. The group agreed to follow up on wallet-related questions in the Paladin Discord channels, with Jim Zhang identified as a potential resource for further discussion. The conversation ended with plans for another community call in a month, aiming to make progress on the V1 board items.
