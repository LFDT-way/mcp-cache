### Community Data Model for Verification

Nicky presented a data model document outlining community qualities for demo communities that already run web-of-trust systems, including volunteer administrators facing rising impersonation and AI fraud. She described four community types: neighborhood aid networks, junior football clubs, freelance interpreter communities, and travel-related communities, with the purpose of stress testing and risk assessment. Nicky noted that face-to-face verification will still be necessary for over 50% of transactions by 2030, positioning the proposed system as a "human first substrata" to support the digital transition in areas like KYC and criminal records checks. Drummond mentioned that the UK government also uses vouching as an input to identity proofing.

### Firstperson.dev Page Architecture Review

The team discussed the need to address different layers of expertise when reviewing the firstperson.dev page architecture. Glenn identified multiple target audiences including developers, consumers, businesses, and AI systems, noting that while developers are currently well-served with technical details, there's a gap in serving non-technical audiences. The discussion centered on finding appropriate language and naming conventions for consumer-facing applications that would demonstrate the technology's value without requiring understanding of underlying technical concepts like VTA and PNF. Margeigh emphasized the importance of creating a simple application demonstration for consumers and less formalized communities.

### VTA Product Terminology and UX

The team discussed terminology and user experience challenges for their VTA (Verifiable Trust Architecture) product suite. Glenn suggested focusing on community building as the primary concept rather than leading with VTA, as VTA functions more as a database tool within the broader ecosystem. The group reviewed a user interface flow and identified several clarity issues, including the need for better explanation of admin account creation and ticket management processes. They agreed that an installer would be needed to replace the current manual binary installation process, with packaging through system managers like Homebrew being a future goal. The team concluded by prioritizing the creation of a visual guide showing the relationships between VTA, VTC, VTN, and PNM components, which would help different audiences understand the architecture.
