### Human Trust Experience Explorer Update

Daniel provided an update on the Human Trust Experience Explorer platform, explaining that it is still under development and will include a scoring system to evaluate best practices, with plans for community input through voting. He mentioned that the platform aims to help identify and categorize good practices, though it currently contains many placeholder entries that need to be replaced with real examples. The meeting then transitioned to discussing Margit's prepared work, with Daniel offering to share screen control to allow Margit to lead the discussion on the next agenda items.

### Kubernetes Community User Journey Diagram

Margaret presented a user journey diagram for Rena's Kubernetes community creation process and shared it with the team, including Drummond, Jeff Turk, and Erica Bunet. She explained the key concepts of the diagram, which include one VTA for every node in the graph and verifiable relationship credentials between nodes. Margaret sought feedback from the team to ensure understanding of the diagram and its components.

### App Persona and VMC Creation

The team discussed the app's process for creating personal DIDs and VTAs, with Margaret explaining how users can create profiles containing one or more personas. Drummond clarified that persona creation is optional and up to individual user preference, emphasizing the system doesn't force multiple personas on users. The discussion then focused on trust community membership, where Drummond corrected Margaret's terminology, explaining that community verification results in a Verifiable Membership Credential (VMC) rather than a relationship credential.

### VTA Wallet Concept Discussion

Margaret and Drummond discussed the VTA wallet concept, clarifying that it holds credentials on behalf of users across all their community activities rather than maintaining separate wallets for each community. They explored the process of creating community DIDs and VTAs, with Margaret introducing the concept of a community profile with associated policies managed by the community VTA. Drummond identified an action item to discuss the formal definition and necessity of the "profile" concept in tomorrow's call, while Daniel raised concerns about the potential confusion between profiles and personas, questioning how to ensure a single human identity remains the foundation for multiple personas.

### Personas and R Cards Integration

Drummond and Daniel discussed the relationship between personas and R cards in their system, with Drummond explaining that R cards serve as human-readable data shared with specific personas. They explored how personas and R cards could work together without needing a separate profile concept, allowing for multiple personas and R cards that can share common information like phone numbers or emails. Daniel raised important legal considerations about the EU AI Act and potential issues with misleading actions when creating different personas, particularly regarding the verification of credentials belonging to the same human person across different contexts.

### Digital Identity Profile Discussion

The team discussed the concept of personal data profiles for creating digital identity cards (R cards), with Margaret explaining her previous work on a community network manager interface that required a comprehensive profile as a data repository from which users could selectively share information. Drummond expressed concerns about using the term "profile" due to its association with public sharing, suggesting instead the use of a "private data store" concept. The discussion also covered the importance of different verification levels for data attributes, with Drummond referencing Mike Jones' concept of self-issued attributes that only the individual can verify. Technical issues with screen sharing interrupted the meeting, but the team continued discussing the flow of creating community profiles and inviting trusted anchors to join.

### Community Membership and Invitation Process

The team discussed community membership processes, focusing on the anchor invitation pattern where trusted community members can invite new members. Drummond clarified that anchors are designated roles in a community with permission to invite others, similar to the web of trust concept in PGP. Kevin mentioned working on a context for LLM analysis and simulations, highlighting the complexity of human-in-the-loop mechanisms. Drummond shared that a real-world application opportunity exists with the Maori people in New Zealand who want to implement a VTN, and Kevin offered to connect Drummond with Susan Bowles from Indigenous Commons for potential collaboration.

### Anchor Pattern Diagram Discussion

The team discussed a diagram representing the anchor pattern and verifiable invitation credentials. Drummond clarified that verifiable invitation credentials can be used in various organizational contexts beyond just the anchor pattern, providing examples like corporate HR systems. Margaret proposed validating the diagram with the DTG working group and updating it to include a separate flow for non-anchor join processes. The team agreed to review the journey map and fill in Runa's process steps after validating the diagram. Drummond suggested sharing the diagram in tomorrow's reporting to help others understand the HTX team's work.
