## Details

### Distributed Trust System Discussion

Kevin discussed with Carly his work on stress testing the distributed trust graph and requested an offline discussion about concerns regarding its effectiveness in preventing bad actors. Carly mentioned working with Scott on comparing X509 versus DIDS ecosystems, noting challenges in scripting the differences between the two systems. The meeting appeared to be a bite-sized trust session, but several key participants including Daniel and Margie were absent, leading Carly to decide to postpone her planned GitHub documentation introduction.

### Verifiable Trust Community Setup Guide

Carly presented a high-level guide for setting up a Verifiable Trust Community, emphasizing the importance of governance structure before technology selection. Drummond explained that Verifiable Trust Agents (VTAs) exist in multiple platforms including OpenVTC and the peer-to-peer trust stack, with Keyring being a nascent VTA that may evolve into a full-featured VTA or interface to VTA. The discussion focused on how to handle hybrid scenarios where mobile apps like Keyring work with cloud VTAs, though this remains an open question for future exploration.

### VTA and VTC Infrastructure Discussion

Carly and Drummond discussed the technical aspects of VTA (Verifiable Trust Anchor) and VTC (Verifiable Trust Container) infrastructure, including the need for DIDs and the distinction between community and personal VTAs. Drummond explained that VTAs handle all work whether for individuals or communities, and addressed Kevin's question about individual community presence by introducing the concept of "community of one." The discussion concluded with Drummond describing challenges around multi-device support and hybrid models, using iMessage as an example of how different devices handle end-to-end encryption and key management.

### Message Encryption Architecture Discussion

Drummond explained the technical architecture of message encryption, describing how multiple devices can handle separately encrypted messages on behalf of a single user or community. The discussion focused on distinguishing between personal network managers (PNM) and community network managers (CNM), with Drummond confirming these terms were established during a previous Seattle workshop. The conversation explored whether community administration would require a separate app or could be handled through role escalation within existing apps like Keyring.

### Privacy-Preserving Credential Management

Drummond and Carly discussed the management of group credentials and roles, with Carly using GitHub as an analogy to explain how different permission levels work within groups. They explored the challenges of revoking credentials privacy-preservingly, with Drummond noting that while revocation is necessary, current privacy-preserving methods are not scalable and new solutions are still being developed. The discussion highlighted the complexity of implementing a comprehensive credential management system that includes privacy-preserving revocation mechanisms.

### Verifiable Trust Communities Discussion

The team discussed the components and terminology of verifiable trust communities, focusing on policy engines, trust registries, and different types of DIDs (Decentralized Identifiers). Drummond explained that OpenVTC uses the Rego policy engine and outlined the concept of VTSPs (Verifiable Trust Service Providers) as being similar to ISPs for the trust layer. The group reviewed Glenn's proposal to rename current DID acronyms (RDID, MDID, PDID, CDID) to more intuitive terms like pairwise, community, public, and linked/persona, though they agreed the "linked" terminology needs refinement. The discussion concluded with acknowledgment that implementation experiences from projects like OpenVTC and the P2P Trust Stack are helping identify gaps in the specifications that need to be addressed.
