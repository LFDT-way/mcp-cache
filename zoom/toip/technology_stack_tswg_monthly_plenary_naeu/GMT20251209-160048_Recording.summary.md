## Overview

The Tagne Stack Working Group meeting covered updates on various task forces and protocols, including the Trust Rosie Query Protocol and Trust Fighting Protocol Task Force, along with discussions about document updates and upcoming events like the Q4 All Members Meeting. The group reviewed developments in DID Skid implementation and introduced concepts like red teaming for DID methods, while also discussing a new DID method called DID:CEL. The conversation ended with plans to reassess the working group's future meeting schedule, potentially moving from monthly to quarterly meetings in 2026.

## Details

### Tagne Stack Working Group Updates

The December meeting of the Tagne Stack Working Group focused on task force reports and updates. Drummond announced that he would be working on updating several documents, including the Technology Architecture Task Force document, over the holiday break. The group discussed the upcoming Q4 All Members Meeting and Virtual Holiday Party, which would include a recap of the symposium. Carly and Scott were tasked with preparing a deck for the meeting, though Drummond noted he had not given them much bandwidth due to his busy schedule.

### Trust Rosie Protocol Enters Review

The Trust Rosie Query Protocol is in or about to enter public review, with significant adoption already seen on the IRA side and interest from the Swiss EID team. The protocol is simpler than its first version, and a blog post is expected soon. The Trust Fighting Protocol Task Force, co-chaired by Wenjing, has been stable since implementing changes dependent on CSER 2.0, with Wenjing giving talks about using MCP and A2A over TSP. The Caesar spec is now finalized and under review by the steering committee, paving the way for 1.0 release in Q1.

### AEIM Deliverables and Task Force Review

The meeting discussed the reassessment of deliverables for AEIM through 2026, with a focus on identifying areas not covered by existing trust interaction work. Drummond and Scott noted that the High Assurance Vids Task Force had stalled due to lack of interest and leadership, with Alex Tweedale previously identified as a potential driver but now unavailable. The group decided to put the task force in suspension mode, acknowledging that its priority might rise again in the future.

### DID Skid Implementation and Formats

Drummond reported on several developments related to DID Skid, including its implementation by Affinity in Rust and its potential for peer-to-peer usage without requiring a web server. He mentioned that Daryl and he would discuss the evolution of DID Skid formats at the Victoria Digital Trust Squad Meetup. Drummond also noted that DID Skid uses formats from other SCID-based methods, and since these methods are going through the DIF DID Methods Working Group's review process, DID Skid's approval should follow more easily.

### Red Teaming and DID Method Updates

Carly introduced the concept of red teaming for DID methods, suggesting that a group could take on the persona of a hacker to creatively explore vulnerabilities and potential improvements. Drummond explained that a new DID method called DID:CEL (Cryptographic Event Log) was recently announced by Mandy Sporty on the W3C mailing list, which is essentially an SCID-based method that standardizes the history format. Drummond added this topic to the agenda for the upcoming Victoria Digital Trust Squad meeting and expressed interest in the red teaming idea, noting that it aligns with ongoing work on risk assessment and harms prevention in the decentralized trust graph working group.

### Blog Post Announcement Plans

Drummond and Darrell discussed plans to announce a blog post for public review, emphasizing the importance of broader input from the community. They agreed to share the announcement at the upcoming all-member meeting and coordinate with Carlin Scott for presentation slides. The conversation ended with a welcome to Eric and confirmation that task force reports were covered, with no further business to address.

### Working Group Meeting Transition Plan

Drummond suggested discussing the future of the working group, noting that most work is now done in task forces and questioning the need for monthly meetings. He proposed meeting in January to decide whether to switch to quarterly meetings in 2026. The group agreed to this plan, and Drummond ended the conversation.
