## Overview

The meeting began with discussions about AI research tools and social media impacts on youth, followed by introductions and membership details for the Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group. The group explored technical aspects of privacy-preserving proofs and decentralized identifiers, with Hart presenting a new ZK framework and discussing plans for future collaboration. The conversation ended with discussions about resource management, task force formations, and concerns about potential misuse of first-person credentials, with emphasis placed on establishing proper documentation and governance structures.

## Details

### AI Research and Social Media Insights

The group discussed their experiences with AI research tools, particularly Gemini, which John noted has become increasingly enthusiastic and similar to his own thinking style. John shared insights from his deep research on social media usage and its effects on under-16s, highlighting Australia's leadership in delaying social media access and the challenges of accessing scientific research due to paywalls. Drummond and John agreed on the importance of making scientific content more accessible to the public.

### Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group Kickoff

The kickoff meeting for the Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group welcomed participants from APAC, Americas, and EU regions, with Drummond emphasizing its joint participation between Trust over IP and DIF. The group discussed introductions, with Adam Lartner from Affinity highlighting their focus on agent identity and transitive trust, while John Phillips shared insights on the Global Trust Registry. Participants were encouraged to add their bios to a shared document for future reference, and Hart, representing the Linux Foundation, was introduced as a key contact for LF-related support.

### TrustRMP Membership and Working Group

Drummond explained the membership options for TrustRMP, a project within LFDT, and highlighted the existence of two stages of membership and a separate JDF Working Group agreement. He clarified that contributors have a specific agreement, and individuals can use a new link for participation. Drummond also discussed the working group's operating terms, emphasizing the scope statement's explicit details about decentralized trust graphs. He mentioned the creation of supporting pages to facilitate communication and idea sharing, built on years of collaborative work across various projects.

### Decentralized Trust and Privacy Proofs

Drummond discussed the importance of understanding personal credentials, recommending everyone involved in the working group read the detailed paper or summary. He highlighted the potential harms and mitigations discussed in the paper and emphasized the significance of pairwise private channels in decentralized trust graphs. Hart contributed to the development of privacy-preserving proofs, particularly zero-knowledge proofs, which are crucial for handling complex credential exchanges.

### ZK Framework for Proofs of Proofs

Hart presented a new ZK framework designed for proofs of proofs, which is being developed and migrated to the LF Decentralized Trust lab called ZKBK. He explained that the framework is modular and uses general-purpose SNARCs for future-proofing, with a research paper in development. Drummond expressed gratitude for Hart's efforts in recruiting Dr. Sanjim Garb and his team at Berkeley, who are leading the ZKP work. They discussed plans for a meeting in the Bay Area during the week of October 6th to discuss funding strategies for the project.

### Decentralized Identifiers and User Experience

Drummond discussed the importance of private decentralized identifiers (DIDs) for personal channels and the user experience challenges associated with personas in a decentralized trust graph. He mentioned that since the first-person white paper was released, they have learned of interest from Banksy, who is connected to their network. Drummond also highlighted the significance of JS Contacts, a new standard approved by the ITF, which could streamline the process of exchanging contact information in R-cards, potentially improving user experience. The Harvard Applied Social Media Lab team plans to work on the user experience of breaking connections and exchanging cards in the coming quarter.

### Decentralized Trust Network Documentation Management

The meeting focused on the management of resources and documentation for a decentralized trust network project. Drummond explained the process of contributing resources to a wiki and emphasized the importance of open-source collaboration. John raised a question about the source of truth for documents, which Drummond addressed by clarifying that the wiki and future repositories would serve as primary sources. Adam encountered issues with editing permissions on the wiki and received guidance from Drummond and Hart on how to resolve the problem. The group discussed the transition from Confluence to GitHub for better permission management and the setup of repositories for specific projects. Finally, Drummond introduced the concept of task forces for self-organized work within the working group, mentioning proposals that had emerged from discussions at the First Person Project and a new idea for peer-to-peer PHC issuance.

### Risk Assessment Task Force Coordination

The group discussed forming a risk assessment task force and agreed to use LF tools like Discord and GitHub for collaboration. Drummond proposed holding one weekly meeting for both time zones to coordinate work, with task forces deciding how to manage their own work. The group also discussed moving their work to GitHub to avoid having multiple places for updates, with Drummond agreeing to promote this approach. Adam and others expressed support for the proposed meeting time, noting their flexible schedules across different time zones.

### Addressing Misuse of Credentials

Drummond and John discussed concerns about the potential misuse of first-person credentials, particularly in the context of coercive relationships and domestic violence. John highlighted that while the original academic paper considered harms related to deepfakes and chatbots, there was a need to address how these credentials could be used to amplify harmful behaviors in personal relationships. They agreed to bundle harms and risks together rather than creating separate groups to avoid over-proliferation, with John mentioning that they had already sought AI's opinion on the matter and included relevant documents for further analysis.

### Digital Trust Task Force Formation

The meeting focused on the formation of a task force related to digital trust and governance. Drummond introduced the idea of bundling work with Nikki Hickman and Scott Perry, who have expertise in risk analysis and governance. Kevin Triplett, a contributor to the Now Genesis effort, joined the meeting and expressed interest in the project's focus on trust and protocols. The group discussed the importance of in-person meetings and ceremonies. Drummond set an expectation for the first task force meeting in two weeks, encouraging participants to join Discord and get properly signed up in the meantime.
