### OpenVTC Project Development Update

Christian requested examples of verifiable trust communities (VTCs) to build a demo, and Drummond explained that the OpenVTC project is an ongoing software initiative currently in the Linux Foundation Labs stage, with plans to graduate to a full project in the coming months. Drummond highlighted that the code for setting up VTCs, particularly for open source projects like the Linux kernel, is being actively developed. The discussion covered the three types of nodes in the decentralized trust graph: persons, AI agents, and VTCs, with Drummond clarifying that AI agents can represent various entities and can either be verifiable trust agents themselves or work with associated VTAs for trust interactions.

### Verifiable Trust Agents Architecture Discussion

The meeting focused on discussing the architecture and control relationships of Verifiable Trust Agents (VTAs) and the broader trust graph system. Drummond and Martina presented different views on how devices and personal network managers interact with VTAs, including questions about whether device VTAs should store keys and credentials at the edge or through a central personal VTA. Mitchell demonstrated a practical application by performing a "sun ceremony" that created a behavioral proof through a knowledge graph, showing how such ceremonies could establish trust between different communities while maintaining privacy. The group agreed to continue discussions next week to resolve questions about the relationship between personal and device VTAs, with Drummond and Martina committing to start discussions with relevant diagrams and links in Discord.
