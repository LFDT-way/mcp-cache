## Details

### First Regular Weekly Meeting

Drummond welcomed attendees to the first regular weekly meeting, noting he was in a Delta Lounge at SeaTac Airport and would keep his voice down. The meeting began at 4:01, and participants included Lisa, Rob, Marcus, Daniel, Brendan, Jeff, and Michael. Drummond requested a moment for everyone to join before proceeding with the agenda.

### Trust Graph Working Group Updates

The Trust Graph Working Group meeting covered several updates and announcements. Mitchell shared details about an upcoming Blockchain Governance Initiative Network conference in Washington DC, where he will experiment with a multi-agentic framework. Reza introduced the Loyal Agents workgroup, initiated during the UN General Assembly, which aims to develop a tech stack ensuring AI agents remain loyal to users. Scott mentioned his upcoming speaking engagement at the Utah Valley University Digital Identity Summit, where he will discuss open standards and protocols. The group reviewed two completed action items from the previous week and discussed potential deliverables for the working group, with plans to form task forces to address these deliverables.

### Risk Assessment and Harm Prevention

The team discussed proposed deliverables for their project, focusing on risk assessment and harm prevention. Scott and Nikki clarified that the risk assessment matrix would be based on the Trust Over IP Risk Assessment Companion Guide and template, while the harm prevention business requirements would be presented as a recommendation. The group also reviewed other potential deliverables, including credential specifications and a sovereign wallet guide. Reza expressed interest in participating in the risk assessment framework, sharing his experience from the streaming media industry. Drummond emphasized the importance of an iterative process between task forces and working groups, starting with use cases and requirements before moving to specifications and implementation guides.

### Risk Assessment for Trust Ecosystem

Scott explained the importance of risk assessment in the context of trust over IP and the proliferation of verifiable credentials within an ecosystem. He introduced a risk assessment process and template that can be used to mitigate risks, drawing from ISO 27000 and NIST standards. Scott emphasized the need for asynchronous sessions to discuss key aspects of the risk assessment process, including defining the ecosystem and identifying potential threats and harms.

### Risk Assessment Process Overview

Scott explained the risk assessment process, emphasizing the need to identify, analyze, and mitigate risks based on their likelihood and impact. He highlighted that not all risks can be eliminated and that the focus is on mitigation, which will inform specifications and requirements. Scott also mentioned the importance of residual risk assessment to ensure completeness and transparent communication with relying parties.

### Dynamic Risk Assessment for Trust Graphs

Scott presented a risk assessment template for decentralized trust graphs, which categorizes risks by trust layers and impact. Reza suggested making the risk assessment matrix dynamic and self-scoring, similar to the NIST framework, to maintain its relevance over time. The group discussed that this template would serve as an input for credential management ecosystems, rather than being used directly for operations. Drummond confirmed this was an input for other ecosystems to extend for their specific needs.

### HARMS Framework for Digital Trust

Nicky presented the HARMS framework, which aims to provide concrete guidance to product managers and developers for assessing human harms in digital trust ecosystems. She explained that the framework considers different contexts, such as credential exchange, and includes strategies for mitigating risks. Nicky emphasized the complexity of assessing harms in decentralized and new relational systems, highlighting the need to recognize emergent properties and enable early identification of potential issues.

### Business Requirements and Risk Assessment

The task force aims to define business requirements, with Nicky expressing excitement about working with Scott's rigorous process to assess risks and outcomes. Scott proposed using part of next week's time to discuss the context and definition of credentials, which Drummond supported, emphasizing the need to start with risk assessment and harms analysis. Drummond will prepare a document describing the ecosystem, and Scott will facilitate the risk assessment process, ensuring everyone reads the harms document and related materials. The group agreed to use the Discord channel for interim communication and will reconvene next week, noting the following week's Internet Identity Workshop as an off week for meetings.
