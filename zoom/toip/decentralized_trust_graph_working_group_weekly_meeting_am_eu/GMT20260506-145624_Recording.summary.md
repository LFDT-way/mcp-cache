Drummond provided an update on the OpenBTC project, highlighting significant development work led by Glenn Gore and other contributors, with a focus on the Verifiable Trust Infrastructure Repo. The team discussed a toolkit developed by the task force, with Nicky emphasizing that it was a collaborative effort involving contributions from multiple team members. The toolkit is designed to help identify vulnerabilities and provide guidance on preventing harms, though it currently exists as spreadsheets and documents rather than a fully executable system. Drummond suggested the possibility of turning the toolkit into a skill for agents to learn and apply these practices, which Nicky confirmed could be possible while emphasizing the importance of human curation in risk assessment. The discussion concluded with an update on the Credentials Task Force's trustworthy specification template, which Martina and Jeff had provided comments on, with Drummond indicating he would review these comments the following day.

### Specification Template Progress Review

The team discussed progress on the specification template structure, which was largely complete with only minor adjustments needed. Drummond committed to reviewing the template with Jeff and others by Friday to finalize it for use as a basis for the DTG credential spec. Meanwhile, Jeff has been working on wording for the V03 draft, focusing on specific sections rather than boilerplate content. The team engaged in a detailed discussion about what happens when someone refuses a VLC, which evolved into a rich conversation about social anthropology and human exchange patterns, with Martina synthesizing the discussion into a visual summary that combined technical, governance, and social aspects.

### Trust Protocols and Credentials Development

The meeting focused on discussing recent developments and initiatives related to trust protocols and verifiable credentials. Drummond highlighted the progress made asynchronously through GitHub discussions, particularly regarding verifiable witness credentials and exchange processes. The group reviewed recent presentations and demos from events like IAW and VRM Day, including discussions on agent names, decentralized trust graphs, and Keyring wallet functionality. A significant portion of the discussion centered on the introduction of trusttask.org, a new registry created by Glenn Gore to formalize trust task protocols, similar to the DIDCOM protocol registry. The group agreed on the importance of codifying trust tasks and leveraging existing ARIES RFCs as a foundation for formal specifications.
