## Details

### Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group

Drummond opened the March 25th meeting of the Decentralized Trust Graph working group, a joint effort between TrustRIP and the Decentralized Identity Foundation. He provided a brief tour of a media lab at the University of Colorado Boulder before reviewing the agenda, which included task force reports, Martina's update on interaction architecture, and discussions on opt-in reporting to trust communities and linking cards and personas. No new members introduced themselves, and no additional announcements were made before moving on to the agenda items.

### Bayesian Trust Network Planning Meeting

Steve announced his plans to work on a new paper titled "Bayesian Trust Network," which will explore using Bayesian analysis to construct mathematical trust and determine how transitive trust works. Drummond suggested posting the paper in the Discord for feedback and mentioned the upcoming IW conference in Mountain View. Nicky reported progress on the Credentials Task Force and requested a one-on-one meeting to review the work, as she encountered issues uploading documents to GitHub. Drummond offered to assist with the GitHub permissions issue and highlighted the team's progress on the template, which is now following the ITF format and will be further developed using SpecMT.

### Onboarding Flow for Community Creation

Martina provided a recap of a previous presentation on an onboarding flow for a persona named Daniel Wright who wants to create a community/VTC in the Decentralized Trust Graph. She explained the interaction architecture involving personal and community network managers, with corresponding cloud-based agents, and highlighted open questions about node addressing and the potential consolidation of PNM and VTC creation processes into a single app. Martina also noted a discrepancy in GitHub where membership credentials are depicted as dots rather than edges, inviting further discussion on the appropriate representation.

### Membership Credentials in Trust Graph

The group discussed the structure and function of membership credentials within the trust graph framework. Brendan clarified that membership credentials should function as edges rather than nodes, establishing a relationship where having a membership credential makes someone a member of a trust community. Reza expressed concerns about potential misuse of community-based trust levels, emphasizing that a decentralized trust graph should remain local, subjective, and subject to trust decay over time. The discussion highlighted the need to carefully evaluate new credential innovations against these guiding principles of local, subjective, and time-decaying trust information.

### Trust Credentials and Data Sharing

The team discussed two main topics related to trust credentials and data sharing. First, Brendan presented ideas for implementing reporting credentials (reporting DIDs) to allow members to share information with trust communities while maintaining privacy. The group agreed that these should be separate from membership credentials and allow users to control what data is shared and with whom. Second, Drummond raised a question about the relationship between persona DIDs and R-cards, suggesting they could be paired but not necessarily required to be linked. The discussion explored how these credentials could work together to enable privacy-preserving data sharing within trust communities. The team agreed to continue these discussions in GitHub repositories to reach consensus on these topics.
