### Zero-Knowledge Proofs and Post-Quantum Schemes

Mitchell explained the importance of zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) in the transition to post-quantum signature schemes, highlighting their role as a stopgap through crypto agility. Vikas inquired about future discussions on aligning with these developments. Drummond discussed updates on the credential spec, particularly focusing on simplifying DID correlation scopes, and praised the editorial team for their work on the revisions. No specific decisions or action items were mentioned in the transcript.

### HDX Impact Simplification Discussion

The team discussed simplifying the HDX (Human Trust Experience) impact, with Glenn explaining the approach to the task force. Eric asked about how persona declaration works, and Glenn clarified that VTC's governance structure specifies the correlation scope without using ZKPs, though there's a risk if governance isn't properly enforced. The discussion then moved to three new proposed credentials, including delegation and authorization credentials originally proposed by Glenn.

### Verifiable Data Credential Specifications

The meeting focused on discussing new credentials in the Verifiable Data Credential (VDC) specification, including the Verifiable Authorization Credential (VAC) and a proposed Verifiable Statement Credential. Glenn explained that VDCs enable both public and private delegation of authority, with the latter requiring zero-knowledge proofs, while VACs serve as portable permission slips for accessing data rooms and other systems. The discussion highlighted how these credentials create different types of edges in the decentralized trust graph and emphasized that each VTC controls what information it shares in the graph. The team also discussed challenges around representing semantic richness in RDF statements, particularly regarding predicate collapse, and noted that Bruce Schneier has joined the VPK working group as a founder member with a kickoff meeting scheduled for Monday the 14th.
