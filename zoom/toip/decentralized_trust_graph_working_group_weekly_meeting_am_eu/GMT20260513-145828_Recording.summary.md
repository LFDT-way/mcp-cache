### Trust Task Credential Requirements

Brendan opened a discussion about credential requirements for trust tasks, specifically focusing on ceremonies for multi-party credential exchange. He explained the concept of trust tasks as defined in Trust over IP, using the witness protocol as an example to illustrate the need for verifiable completion credentials. Brendan proposed creating a verifiable completion credential or verifiable trust task credential to link subsidiary credentials to overall ceremonies, allowing third parties to verify when trust tasks are complete. He invited the group to reflect on what should be included in the credential and protocol specifications to support various trust tasks.

### Trust Tasks and Credentials Framework

The team discussed the distinction between trust tasks and credentials, with Nicky proposing that governance should focus on the composition of trust tasks rather than individual tasks. Nicky introduced a relational design framework that considers trustworthy relationships between different entities, including humans, devices, and the natural world. The group agreed that there needs to be some form of binding between subsidiary credentials and an overarching credential for trust tasks, with Brendan suggesting this could be implemented in a simple way. The discussion concluded with an agreement to continue the conversation on GitHub, with questions raised about the appropriate home for this ongoing discussion, though no final decision was made.

### Credential Formats and Trust Discussion

The team discussed credential formats and trust tasks. Brendan presented proposals for canonical formats, including W3C VC 2.0, and sought feedback on whether to start with a single format or support multiple formats from the beginning. Tim raised concerns about mandatory dependencies and potential issues with certain cryptographic schemes. Juan, speaking as a European, warned about the complexity of adding SDJOT to the stack and recommended against it. The group agreed to continue the discussion in the GitHub repository linked in the meeting. Mitchell also shared his ongoing performance art experiment using AI agents for trust tasks on Telegram.
