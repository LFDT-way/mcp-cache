## Overview

The meeting covered updates from various task forces within the Trust Over IP and Decentralized Identity community, focusing on specification development, risk assessment tools, and user experience improvements. Sankarshan presented progress on the risk assessment and harms prevention toolkit, highlighting its modularity and stability. Geoff and Brendan discussed the Credentials Task Force's recent work, including the merging of statement credentials and the adoption of semantic versioning. ## Details

### Meeting Updates and Agenda Review

Nicky opened the meeting and noted that Drummond and Martina were unable to attend due to being at the ProHuman conference and client engagement respectively. She reminded attendees about the antitrust policy and asked for any new members to introduce themselves, but none did. The meeting followed the agenda, which included general announcements, updates from task forces (except VDS and agent names), and a hoped-for update from Daniel.

### Risk Assessment Toolkit Updates

Nicky introduced a special topic on risk assessment and harms prevention, with Sankashan presenting updates on transforming the toolkit into a more modular and portable set of tools. Sankashan explained that the current tool focuses on checking specifications across the DTG portfolio and has been stable since the Cypress release, with weekly updates available for download. Nicky proposed holding task force meetings directly before the Trust Over IP calls and received agreement from the group to proceed with this schedule.

### CredSpec Task Force Updates

Geoff announced that the statement credential was merged after discussion in the CredSpec Task Force call, which will create a public repository for predicates in a namespace still being determined by Drummond's discussions with LFTT. The team has adopted semantic versioning across spec repositories thanks to Brendan's work, and Geoff encouraged participants to review and provide feedback on the ongoing issues and pull requests.

### Plumbers' Conference Specifications Update

The team discussed the status of specifications for the Plumbers' Conference, with Geoff reporting that while the specification is moving forward and developing well, it won't be in its final state but will be ready as an implementer's draft. Brendan clarified that the CRED spec and trust test spec remain narrowly scoped while a new VTI spec addresses cross-linkage issues. Glen provided updates on Trust House, noting its continued growth with support for additional programming languages and the implementation of post-quantum cryptography capabilities, including a shift from Trust Spanning Protocol Revision 2 to Revision 3.

### ZKP Specifications Progress Update

The ZKP Task Force discussed progress on ZKP specifications, with Mitchell presenting a machine-checked spec of construction records containing 16 proofs, including Glenn's ADR001 as the first composed one. The team confirmed their ownership of the construction common control primitive and established a working model for reviewing adjacent specs asynchronously. New collaborators including Leif from the Cyros Foundation will join on the 29th, and the team is working toward showcasing ZKP capabilities at the upcoming Plumbers event, though there may be some short-term deviation from the specifications to meet the Linux Foundation's requirements.

### Human Trust Experience Task Force

Daniel reported on the Human Trust Experience Task Force's work, including efforts to collect best practices and create explainer videos, though these activities have been temporarily paused due to unstable underlying concepts. The team discussed personas and attributes, exploring ways to help users define these attributes through automated systems while maintaining privacy. Nicky mentioned input for the VTI work related to the Plumbers Conference, which was shared in the chat.

### Tadal Decision Layer Concept

Daniel presented a concept for a "Tadal" decision layer to address the privacy paradox and data oversharing issues in digital wallets and trust infrastructure. He explained how this layer could help users make better decisions about data sharing through game theory models and intervention strategies, positioning it within the VTA (Verifiable Trust Agent) system. The discussion included questions from Steve about the game theory framework and from Brendan about IEEE 7012 standards, while Reza shared information about a new VPK Working Group focused on confidential vector search using homomorphic encryption. The group agreed to revisit the topic after the Plumbers Conference to explore potential integration with decentralized trust graph and Trust over IP initiatives.
