## Overview

The meeting focused on reviewing a new proposal for enhancing the decentralized trust graph (DTG) credential types, which included discussions on refining terminology and defining new credential types. Drummond presented updates on the proposed verifiable trust community (VTC), verifiable trust network (VTN), and verifiable membership credential (VMC) concepts, emphasizing the need for precision in terminology. The conversation ended with an update on the R-card discussion and a reminder for participants to join the ZKP mailing list for further collaboration.

## Details

### TrustGraph Working Group Final Meeting

The meeting began with Drummond introducing the agenda for the final decentralized TrustGraph Working Group meeting of the year. He noted the absence of co-chairs Martina and Daniel due to holiday breaks, with Nikki providing notes support. The group discussed new reactions added to their platform and Drummond shared a background photo of Zurich, taken during the formation of the Ira Association. Drummond introduced Georg Link, a new observer from Beturgia, and confirmed the agenda items: updates from the Risk Assessment Harms Prevention Task Force, a new enhancement proposal for DTG credentials, and progress on ZKP work.

### Decentralized Credentials and Risk Analysis

The group discussed a document analyzing decentralized verifiable relationship credentials and their potential misuse in coercive control situations. Nicky explained that the GDPR right to be forgotten doesn't apply in peer-to-peer contexts, and the team identified several risks and harms associated with these credentials. Scott emphasized the need to capture these risks while moving forward with specifications, and Drummond mentioned that the credentials Task Force, which meets on Tuesdays at 7 AM Pacific, will help ground which credential types could be subject to certain risks or cause harms.

### Credential Proposal Simplification Review

The meeting focused on reviewing a refined proposal for credential types, specifically the V.02 DTG credential proposal. Drummond explained that the proposal had been simplified by collapsing two credential types into one, addressing concerns about the complexity of the trust graph. He introduced a new term, "verifiable trust community" (VTC), to clarify the concept of decentralized trust networks. The group discussed the implications of this change and agreed to review the updated proposal, which will be the focus of their next meeting in January.

### Trust Network Terminology Clarification

The group discussed terminology related to trust networks and credentials, focusing on three key concepts: verifiable trust circles (VTCs), verifiable trust networks (VTNs), and verifiable community credentials (VCCs). Drummond explained that VTNs could be structured as hierarchies or federations, with federation being explicitly supported but not emphasized due to potential confusion with centralized systems. Michael suggested that VTCs could serve as the common denominator for higher-level business constructs, while Steve proposed using "federated" to describe a group of groups but was convinced otherwise by Drummond's explanation about decentralization. The discussion concluded with Drummond clarifying that VCCs represent membership relationships, distinct from VRCs which represent association relationships.

### Trust Credentials and Personhood Definitions

The meeting focused on discussing and refining definitions for various trust community and relationship credentials, including VTCs, VRCs, and VPCs. Drummond presented a proposal for five core credential types and outlined how these could be used to prove personhood and relationships within trust networks. The group discussed the potential for using zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) to create privacy-preserving proofs of personhood. Drummond also mentioned ongoing discussions about whether R-cards should be considered verifiable credentials. The conversation ended with an update on the importance of ZKPs in the context of personhood credentials and a reminder for participants to engage in further discussions on GitHub and the Discord channel before the next meeting on January 7th.
