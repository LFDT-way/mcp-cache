## Details

### Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group

Drummond opened the July 15th meeting of the Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group, a joint working group of TrustRP and DIF. He welcomed attendees and announced a full agenda including the introduction of several new co-chairs and a discussion about agent names. Drummond began the meeting by reviewing the antitrust policy and inviting new members to introduce themselves through various methods, including the DTG bio page.

### Cybersecurity Task Force Meeting Updates

Jez Gladstone, with 20 years of enterprise cybersecurity experience focused on customer authentication and fraud prevention, introduced himself as a new member and expressed interest in applying his background to public good initiatives. The meeting reviewed the agenda which included task force reports, ZKP and UX updates, and planned sessions from Marcus about web camp lessons learned and agent work. Drummond announced that First Person and Trust Over IP have two proposed sessions at the upcoming Global Digital Collaborative (GDC) event in Geneva, which will host approximately 2,000 attendees with 140 proposed sessions across four days, and invited interested participants to contact him for invitation details.

### AGI Conference and Task Forces

Reza announced upcoming events including the AGI conference in San Francisco from July 27-30, which will feature a free documentary screening about AI and consciousness. Drummond then discussed the seven task forces being developed, noting that the Risk Assessment Task Force has completed its work and is now meeting asynchronously, while the Credentials Task Force has published Working Draft 01, with Jeff Turk and Brendan contributing to the setup of the repository.

### Credential Specification Working Draft Status

Brendan and Drummond discussed the current status of the credential specification, which is now in the working draft stage as specified in the Joint Development Process. They explained the process for reviewing and providing feedback through issues and pull requests in the dedicated DTG-CRED repo. Drummond outlined the stages of the development process and emphasized the importance of updating the working draft number with significant changes. The team also mentioned the separation of card-related content into the VDS spec and the integration of specifications through related sections.

### VDS Task Force Specification Discussion

Drummond discussed the need to define trust task specifications and allocate between core tasks versus extensions, noting that verifiable data structures will be handled in a separate specification rather than as a credential type. The team decided to rename the current task force to the VDS task force to reflect its broader scope. Marco was scheduled to present on agent names as the special topic for the meeting.

### ZKP Task Force Updates

Scott and Mitchell provided updates on the ZKP Task Force, including the establishment of infrastructure and the publication of requirements. They discussed key conclusions about cryptography and privacy, as well as identified dependencies and gaps across task forces. The team outlined next steps, including creating a predicate and assurance boundary decision document, engaging cryptographers, and scheduling weekly calls. Mitchell emphasized the importance of considering privacy, adversary models, and the longevity of ZKPs in the specifications.

### Human-Centered Trust and Agent Naming

The meeting covered two main topics: human-centered trust experience and agent naming. Daniel reported on the first discussion about treating solutions as socio-technical systems and emphasized the importance of delivering a good human trust experience for adoption. Nicky mentioned that some participants were unable to join and requested another round of discussions next week, particularly for the APAC region. Marcus presented a proposal for agent names, which are human-readable identifiers that resolve to deeds, and demonstrated a CLI tool and API for creating and managing these agent names using a generic DID registration interface. The group discussed the potential integration of agent names with verifiable trust agents (VTAs) for contact purposes, with Drummond explaining that contact agents could help individuals control their contact policies independently.
