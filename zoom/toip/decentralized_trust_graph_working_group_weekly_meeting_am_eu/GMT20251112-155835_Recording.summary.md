## Details

### SIROS Project Updates and Symposia

The meeting began with Drummond apologizing for the previous week's cancellation due to a conflict with the Affinity workshop in Berlin. He introduced the agenda, which included updates on the working group's co-chairs, presentations on risk assessment and harms prevention by Scott Perry and Nick Hickman, and a detailed discussion on verifiable relationship credentials by Brendan Miller and Alberto Leon from the Harvard Applied Social Media Lab. Drummond also announced the upcoming symposium on the 19th and 20th and the Kauai holiday party in Los Angeles on December 18. He concluded by mentioning his recent meeting with Leif Johansen, who will become CTO of the SIROS project, and the creation of a new ITF mailing list for ZKP-related work.

### Trust Over IP Governance Framework

The team discussed a Miro board diagram representing the scope of their standards development organization, Trust Over IP, which includes components for personal decentralized trust graphs and shared components for governance frameworks. Scott explained that while the First-Person Project would develop its own governance framework within the yellow box, the working group focuses on creating reusable toolkit components in the purple and green boxes that other ecosystems like GliaNet could derive from. Reza and Drummond raised questions about the placement of personal data and policies in the ecosystem, leading to a decision to add a new box for personal policies, and Drummond noted the need to consider community network managers alongside personal network managers.

### Privacy and Risk Assessment Integration

The group discussed the integration of privacy-preserving data sharing and credential storage into the ecosystem, with Reza emphasizing the importance of keeping personal data secure and decentralized. They agreed to postpone defining AI agents due to the lack of a clear taxonomy, with Stephen working on a classification system. Nicky suggested focusing on logical and physical entities connected to trustworthy digital supply chains. The team planned to review risk assessments from existing frameworks to identify new risks and focus areas for the purple box in the risk assessment.

### Verifiable Relationship Credentials Development

Brendan and Drummond discussed the development of verifiable relationship credentials (VRCs), emphasizing the importance of decomposing credentials for user choice and the inclusion of witnessed credentials to enhance trust models. Brendan outlined the structure of VRCs, including core fields like subject ID, pairwise DID, personas, and session information, and explained that endorsements would be separate from core relationship credentials. Drummond suggested using GitHub discussions for further collaboration and agreed to initiate discussions on specific questions later in the week. The team plans to build on these specifications in the fourth quarter, with Brendan and Alberto focusing on a social media lens while considering broader applications.

### Decentralized Credentials Specification Discussion

The group discussed the draft specification for decentralized credentials, with Markus raising questions about the credential subject and its relationship to dates or identifiers. Drummond suggested that Markus start a discussion about the VRC credential subject, as there are different viewpoints on the matter. Reza inquired about the stability of the protocol, to which Brendan responded that it is still fluid and being formalized by the group. Alberto mentioned that the team is working on implementing the specification within a bi-fold from the Open Walid Foundation, aiming to test its performance and gather feedback from the community.

### Credential Development Straw Man Discussion

The group discussed the development of a straw man proposal for credentials, with Drummond emphasizing the need for further attention before the Christmas holidays to reach a consensus. Michael suggested that relationship credentials could be categorized as another type of credential, and Drummond agreed to initiate a discussion on this topic. Brian raised the possibility of nesting credentials, which Brendan supported by explaining the need for separate linking credentials to allow for their presentation and witnessing. The group also briefly touched on endorsement credentials and their potential for machine-readable claims.

### VRC Updates and Collaboration Initiatives

Brendan presented updates on VRCs and relationship cards, emphasizing the importance of leveraging existing standards to avoid recreating the wheel. He introduced a flow specification for session-based VRC exchanges, highlighting the role of witnesses, which could be humans or bots, in verifying credentials. Drummond encouraged everyone to engage asynchronously through the GitHub repo and Discord channel, noting that the next meeting would be skipped due to the Trust RP Forum Symposium. He expressed gratitude for the progress made and the collaborative effort, particularly highlighting Nikki's contribution to ensuring continuity of meetings.
