## Overview

The Decentralized Trust Graph working group met to discuss several key topics, including the Risk Assessment Harms Prevention Task Force's revised approach to deliverables and the introduction of personas to better understand user experiences. The group reviewed upcoming events, including the Human Agency Summit and Linux Foundation Member Summit, where two sessions focused on decentralized trust graph technology. A significant portion of the meeting covered the introduction of agent names as a new naming system that resolves to DIDs, with Drummond Reed and Markus presenting the concept and discussing its potential applications. The conversation ended with Mitchell demonstrating a governance ceremony system that combines knowledge graphs, promise graphs, and trust graphs to create verifiable trust communities.

## Details

### Linux Foundation Summit Preparation Meeting

The meeting began with Drummond welcoming attendees and reminding them of an upcoming APAC meeting. He introduced the agenda, which included updates from the Risk Assessment Harms Prevention Task Force, progress on credentials, preparation for the Linux Foundation Member Summit, and a discussion on the agent names proposal. Drummond also announced upcoming events: the H2H Summit on Human Agency and the Linux Foundation Member Summit, both taking place next week at the Silverado Resort in Napa, California.

### Qui Summit and Blockchain Updates

The meeting discussed the upcoming Qui Summit co-located with the Southern California Linux Expo on March 5th, where Drummond Reed will speak alongside notable speakers like Bruce Schneier and Ben Goertzel. Reza shared details about the summit's agenda and ticket information, highlighting a 50% discount for attendees. Mitchell presented an update on his work involving trust graph formation for a blockchain governance event, which combines knowledge, promise, and trust graphs. Drummond agreed to add Mitchell's presentation to the agenda and encouraged attendees to review the link shared in the chat.

### Risk Assessment Strategy Document Update

The task force, led by Nicky, Sankarshan, Kevin, Martina, and others, decided to revise their meeting schedule to bi-weekly, starting the week after next, and to adjust the time to be more convenient for West Coast participants. They agreed to shift focus from creating a comprehensive risk assessment spreadsheet to developing a shorter guidance document by the end of March, incorporating insights from various analyses and using the harms paper checklist. The new document will include guidance, personas, scenarios, and practical steps to ensure a risk-awareness and harms-prevention mindset in specifications.

### Verifiable Trust Communities Paper Planning

The group discussed developing a paper on verifiable trust communities, with Drummond proposing it as the next first-person project to explain the concept, its importance, and potential risks. Reza suggested creating a role-playing model similar to the traditional Bob-Alice-Eve security model to understand the roles and interactions within trust graph communities. Nicky shared her approach of using personas to design socio-technical systems, emphasizing the importance of human-centered design and storytelling. The group agreed to focus on the VRC spec initially and to contribute to ongoing work by documenting discussions in GitHub.

### Blockchain and AI Persona Development

The group discussed persona development for blockchain and AI applications, with Mitchell presenting on blockchain incident response and harms communication. Nicky proposed using Claude agents as personas for questions and context engineering. Christian shared insights from the DIF Travel and Hospitality Working Group, where he will document an AI travel agent scenario involving decentralized trust graphs. Grace raised concerns about bot armies potentially exploiting trust graphs, which Drummond acknowledged as a key threat vector. The conversation ended with plans to adjust future meetings to Tuesdays and Nicky agreeing to set up a GitHub discussion on personas.

### Transitioning to GitHub and Trust Graphs

The team discussed transitioning from Google Docs to GitHub for documentation and addressed the importance of defending against automated astroturfing. They reviewed progress on the credentials specification template, which received positive feedback from Jory Burson, and planned to finalize it after upcoming events. Two sessions on decentralized trust graph were accepted for the Linux Foundation Member Summit, focusing on the Linux Foundation Maintainer Verification Project (LKMV) and the need for decentralized trust between humans and agents. A workshop was also planned to discuss integrating global trust communities into the Linux workflow, with a special session featuring Jim Zemlin and LFX platform representatives.

### DTG VTCs and Agent Names Proposal

The meeting focused on two main topics: a comparison of DTG VTCs versus traditional PGP web of trust, and a new proposal for agent names. Geoff presented a detailed comparison table highlighting the advantages of DTG VTCs, particularly identity continuity and typed credentials. Drummond shared that there is ongoing education needed for kernel developers to understand these new concepts. The group then discussed a proposal for agent names, which would be human-readable URLs that resolve to DIDs. Drummond and Markus explained how this could be useful for privacy-preserving and empowering addresses. Mitchell demonstrated a ceremony for governance and knowledge sharing using AI and DIDs. The conversation ended with a brief discussion about the simplicity of DID names and where they should be specified in the DID specification.
