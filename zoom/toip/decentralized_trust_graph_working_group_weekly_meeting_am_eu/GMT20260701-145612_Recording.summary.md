## Overview

The Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group meeting focused on Nikki's presentation of a relational reference model for trust tasks, which she developed to provide a socio-technical framework for understanding human relationships within the trust graph system. Nikki outlined a model with six aspects including cross-pattern infrastructure, conduct definitions, graph definition, ceremonies, operational patterns, and lifecycle management, along with five types of trust tasks that depend on presence from layer two. The discussion included technical considerations about agent names, contact agents, and VTA (Verifiable Trust Agent) implementations, with Marcus offering to demonstrate his team's work on agent names. The group also discussed the potential for a broader human experience task force beyond just UX, with Mitchell volunteering to help create visualizations and expressing interest in geometric encoding of credentials for privacy.

### Socio-Technical Trust Graph Model

Nicky presented a comprehensive socio-technical model for trust graphs, outlining 39-40 data objects categorized across six aspects including cross-pattern infrastructure, conduct, graph definition, ceremonies, operational patterns, and lifecycle management. The model aligns with Trust Over IP layers and includes interaction patterns for onboarding, adoption, and governance frameworks. Steve raised questions about incorporating generative policy and fiduciary duties, to which Nicky responded that negotiation patterns could handle such cases, and Steve noted compatibility with his own fiduciary agent work. Mitchell suggested geometric encoding of credentials to improve privacy through coordinate-based sharing rather than credential types, while Drummond emphasized that the complexity is appropriate for a socio-technical system and suggested starting with a focused approach to contact agents before tackling full implementation.
