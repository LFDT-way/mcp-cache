## Overview

The Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group met to discuss the relationship between credentials and trust tasks, with Jeff Turk's post serving as the main topic of discussion. The group reviewed a proposal for simplifying credential types to focus on relationship credentials (VRCs) and membership credentials (VMCs) as the fundamental building blocks of the trust graph, while exploring how to keep credentials simple and avoid creating too many complex credential types. Key discussions centered around the distinction between cryptographic correctness and actor reliability, with participants agreeing that trust tasks should be modular and transport-agnostic, potentially implementable over DIDCOM, TSP, or Nostr protocols. The group also discussed the completion of a POP specification template that will allow current markdown documents to be converted into a formal trustworthy template format, and announced plans for a full demo of the OpenVTC project at the next meeting.

### DTG Credentials and Trust Boundaries

Geoff discussed the challenge of defining the boundary between DTG credentials and trust tasks, proposing the concept of relationship or membership credentials that create edges in the graph. He noted Glenn's work on trust tasks, which uses modular and composable components, and expressed interest in the flexible architecture. Brendan acknowledged the progress and suggested examining more complex tasks to validate the concept, while also raising concerns about the legibility of trust community actions for third parties.

### Trust and Audit Path Design

The group discussed the importance of creating negative paths for testing and keeping Prudential Formats simple. Tim shared insights about trust tasks and signatures, emphasizing the distinction between correctness of the system and reliability of actors. Drummond agreed on the significance of signed messages in creating an audit trail for verifiable trust agents.

### Trust Graphs and Reliability Assessment

The team discussed trust graphs and reliability assessment methods, with Steve proposing a Bayesian mathematics approach for social reliability assessment. Tim distinguished between AI reliability (a fiduciary issue) and correctness (a cryptographic issue), comparing it to trusting a car versus a driver. The group examined the current 7 credentials in the draft specification, with Drummond proposing to evaluate each one based on its universal necessity or broad usage across trust tasks. Mitchell suggested a layered approach to trust tasks that includes both cryptographic artifacts and human-readable elements for sharing relationships and invitations between communities.
