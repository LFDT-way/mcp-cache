## Overview

The meeting focused on AI travel agents and identity management systems, with Neil sharing insights about companies developing agent-to-agent commerce solutions and discussing how these technologies might transform traditional travel booking and loyalty programs. Participants explored challenges related to agent identity, authorization, and data management, including the need for structured profiles, micro-sharing of information, and mechanisms to prevent agents from exceeding their authority. The group also discussed potential solutions such as zero-knowledge proofs, multi-agent verification systems, time-limited authorizations, and comprehensive logging to ensure security and accountability in AI agent operations.

Neil describes a company developing an agent-to-agent system for commerce in the travel industry, which uses DIDComm and works with major airlines. The system operates through WhatsApp as a front end, where users can input travel requirements and the agent, using APIs rather than other agents, finds flights meeting those criteria and can negotiate based on parameters like price. Neil plans to have further conversations with this company in early September and suggests inviting them to present to the group, as they are pursuing an open standard approach and could be potential customers for trust spanning protocol.

### Agent Identity in C2PA Ecosystem

Eric and Wenjing discuss the issue of agent identity in the C2PA ecosystem, with Eric noting the current focus on transactional rather than lasting identity, while Wenjing explains her work on long-term identity solutions. Neil shares insights from a presentation about travel industry applications, where agents operate on session-based identities with micro-sharing of information. The group discusses how AI-powered travel booking agents might transform loyalty programs, with Neil reporting that industry experts believe traditional loyalty programs may collapse as agent-to-agent negotiations optimize for travelers' interests rather than airlines' profits.

### ZKP for Dynamic Profile Management

Neil discusses how ZKP (Zero Knowledge Proofs) can be used for authorized information sharing without revealing entire credentials. Makki emphasizes the importance of dynamic data management, suggesting that agents can help with periodic profile updates based on changing user conditions. Neil agrees and explains that travel profiles will likely have subsets (personal, business) and may vary based on activities like skiing versus scuba diving. He notes that service providers expect to work with continually updated profiles, with information shared on a "micro sharing" basis, meaning only the necessary information is requested for each specific experience.

### AI Transparency and Profile Challenges

Makki shares an update about the California AI Transparency Act (SB 942), which will launch in January 2026 and require declaration of AI-generated versus human-created content, with fines of $5,000 per day for violations. Neil discusses a challenge regarding how traveler preferences detected by AI can be verified and incorporated into structured profiles, noting that industry professionals at a meeting acknowledged this issue remains unresolved. Neil also mentions asking ChatGPT about structured profiles versus AI agent interpretation, with the AI responding that relying solely on AI agents for interpretation is risky due to potential misinterpretation, suggesting structured, human-readable data is necessary.

### AI Structure Maintenance Challenges

Neil discusses the challenges of maintaining structure when working with AI like ChatGPT, noting that it often changes agreed-upon structures despite instructions to the contrary. Wenjing begins to comment on this being a common problem before getting disconnected. While waiting for Wenjing to return, Erran mentions he's just there to listen, and Makki provides an update about a charter being prepared for an AI Agents task force under their working group.

### AI Agent Authorization Safeguards

Neil suggests using multiple AI agents with different technologies to cross-check each other, similar to the space shuttle's redundant computer system, to prevent misunderstandings and drift. Eric raises concerns about agents overrunning their authority, such as creating unauthorized content in someone's name, while Makki adds that agents might continue working under someone's name even after they leave an organization. Neil proposes a solution involving micro-authorization with limited reuse tokens that grant access for specific purposes within time windows, though Eric questions how to limit frequency of use. Wenjing believes a new approach beyond the OAuth model is needed to address these scope and intent issues.

### OAuth Delegation Security Concerns

Eric expresses concern about the potential for abuse in delegation systems like OAuth, noting that time-based permissions could allow an agent to produce excessive content without restrictions. Wenjing and Eric discuss the lack of restraints within permission periods, comparing it to travel permits in Casablanca. Neil suggests that business rules and specifications could help, but Wenjing questions whether vague policies can effectively express or enforce frequency limitations. Eric proposes blockchain-based credit systems as a potential solution, while also suggesting human-in-the-loop verification as another mitigation strategy. Neil mentions that extensive logging could be essential for governance and monitoring agent behavior.

### AI Agent Security and Accountability

The group discusses the importance of logging and signatures for security in AI agent systems, with Neil emphasizing how extensive logging has been standard in industrial settings for accident prevention and accountability. Makki suggests implementing periodic reports and central account management for agent activities to increase accountability, especially in large organizations where employees might deploy multiple agents. The conversation shifts to how delegated authority should be balanced with central control, with Neil noting that AI assistance for travel is not just about booking but providing continuous support throughout the journey as users make decisions in unfamiliar situations.
