### Scalable Human Verification Solutions

Scott Jones from Realize presented on human verification at scale, sharing insights from processing over 100 billion authentication calls. He explained how Realize's computer vision technology, originally developed for ad testing, evolved into identity verification solutions. Scott demonstrated their progressive verification approach, which offers lightweight, frictionless checks that can run in the background while maintaining security, contrasting with traditional heavy-weight ID verification methods that create significant friction for users. The presentation included a live demo showing how their system can verify liveness, uniqueness, and demographic information without storing actual face images, making it GDPR compliant.

### Biometric Verification Edge Architecture

Scott Jones discussed Verify's approach to biometric verification, emphasizing their community-scoped model that avoids centralized global biometric databases. He explained their technology provides confidence levels for age verification and other use cases, with models tuned for specific requirements including demographic fairness across skin tones, age, and gender. The company is working on edge-first architecture with client-side SDKs for iOS and Android, expected by end of quarter, along with micro-embeddings and zero-knowledge proofs tied to biometrics. Scott also presented a case study of their ongoing integration with WWallet for personhood credentials and outlined several open questions around holder binding, credential refresh intervals, and privacy preserving revocation.

### Privacy-Preserving Biometrics Discussion

Alexander from TNO discussed privacy-preserving user binding and biometrics with Scott Jones, confirming that no information leaves the customer's device when processing biometrics in the client-side modality. Alexander expressed interest in continuing the conversation and mentioned his colleagues' expertise in developing zero-knowledge proofs. Scott Perry, who joined late, shared his 25-year experience in identity verification and expressed alignment with Realize's approach to transitioning beyond physical documents for verification.

### Deepfake Detection and Verification

Scott Jones discussed his work on deepfake detection and mentioned exploring temporal and image-based artifacts to identify deepfakes. He also touched on the concept of continuous verification in the gig economy using telemetry data from devices. Radar suggested exploring alternative identification methods beyond faces, such as hand patterns or freckles, to enhance security against deepfake attacks. Scott acknowledged the potential of these ideas and mentioned the possibility of detecting operating system breaches and synthetic cameras as part of future developments.

### Biometric Trust and Technology Discussion

Drummond expressed enthusiasm about Scott's work with Seros and First Person Project, and they agreed to follow up next week to discuss building trust infrastructure together. Jorge raised concerns about potential pushback from users regarding biometric technology, particularly in the US context, to which Scott responded that while there are challenges, the technology is becoming more normalized globally and can be effectively positioned as privacy-centric. The discussion also covered technical aspects of embeddings and authentication methods, with Scott explaining that their system uses embedding-to-embedding matching with irreversible hashed data points.
