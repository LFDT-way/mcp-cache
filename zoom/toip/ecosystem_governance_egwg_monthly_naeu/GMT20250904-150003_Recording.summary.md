The team discussed plans for an introductory Trust Over IP presentation, agreeing it should be 30 minutes long and include data and graphics from the Web of Trust team showing the digital trust community's growth since 2020. Drummond suggested adding teasers about Linux Foundation's planned implementations to build momentum and encourage social media amplification. Carly proposed using a video featuring AI-mined quotes from previous presentations to promote the symposium, but Eric advised against releasing it too early, suggesting it could be used closer to the event date.

The team discussed concerns about video clips used in a presentation, with Steven raising questions about the source and permissions of clips that might not be from Trust Over IP presentations. Carly confirmed that most clips were from Trust Over IP, but Eric noted a few exceptions. ### Global Event Planning Strategy

Eric and Drummond discussed scheduling a 12-hour event with content for different time zones. They considered starting early to accommodate Asia-Pacific and European teams, with breaks in between 3-4 hour blocks. They agreed on a September 19th deadline for submissions, giving participants two months to prepare content. Drummond suggested creating a sense of urgency by making submissions first-come, first-served.

### Symposium Curation and Planning Meeting

The group discussed the curation process for an upcoming event, deciding that the steering committee would curate the participants rather than using a first-come, first-served approach, as suggested by Darrell to avoid favoring smaller organizations. Carly confirmed she had created a Google Drive folder for the symposium and had archived a previous trust case use case. Drummond outlined two action items: coordinating a call with Tomas to brief him on event preparation and drafting a blog post to announce the event, with Eric volunteering to help with both tasks.

### Digital Sovereignty and Identity Control

The group discussed digital sovereignty, with Eric noting its prominence in Europe and Asia as a reaction to U.S. big tech, while Drummond observed that the term is increasingly used by both nations and individuals to assert control over digital trust relationships. Jim raised concerns about the direction of digital sovereignty, suggesting it may not be the best approach, and highlighted the success of universal digital identity concepts. Drummond clarified that self-sovereign identity was intended to empower individuals with control over their data, aligning with the Trust Over IP architecture's goals.

### Symposium Planning and Submission Deadlines

The group discussed plans for a symposium, focusing on expanding Day 2 to include workshops and panel discussions. Carly suggested a forum topic about "quiet side beneficiaries" of Trust over IP, and Jim shared his experience implementing Trust over IP concepts in Rhode Island. The group agreed to include moderators in panel proposals and to review submissions in the next meeting. Eric was tasked with reaching out to Tomas about scheduling the promotion and communication of the symposium. The deadline for submissions for volunteer panels and presentations was set for two weeks, after which they would decide whether to push harder for submissions or reach out to additional parties.
