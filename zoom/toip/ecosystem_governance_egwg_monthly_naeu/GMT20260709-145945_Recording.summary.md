## Overview

The Ecosystem and Governance Working Group met to discuss their next project focus, specifically whether to update the governance metamodel to version 2. Carly, who is stepping down as co-chair, facilitated the discussion about two potential proposals: Mackie's working group realignment proposal and an update to the governance metamodel. Drummond expressed interest in governance work for verifiable trust communities and AI agents, noting that First Person Cooperative has strong interest in this area, though he lacks bandwidth to lead the effort without sufficient funding. The group identified that they need stakeholders with real implementation experience to drive the metamodel update, rather than abstract proposals, with Karla offering her expertise from implementing the current VLEI ecosystem governance framework. The discussion revealed that while the current metamodel works for basic governance needs, new use cases around AI agents, smart buildings, and decentralized trust communities require updates to address gaps in terminology, glossary definitions, and specific governance requirements for verifiable trust communities.

### Governance Metamodel Feedback Discussion

Drummond provided feedback on two proposals, expressing that the second version of the governance metamodel has been on the docket for some time but lacks strong support due to limited bandwidth and competing priorities. He noted that interest in governance may resurgence as the decentralized trust graph working group evolves, particularly regarding policies enforced by agents. Carly suggested that rather than creating a separate task force, the governance of AI agents could be addressed within the existing Ecosystem and Governance Working Group with proper advertising to relevant groups.

### Governance Meta-Model Revision Discussion

The group discussed revising the governance meta-model to address new challenges in AI, agents, and digital trust, particularly focusing on trusted execution environments and verifiable trust communities. Drummond noted that while funding prospects are improving, specific resource allocation cannot be guaranteed until funding is secured. Neil highlighted the importance of trust execution environments in addressing concerns about AI agents and personal data, suggesting that only transaction outputs should be shared outside trusted sandboxes. The team agreed to take a fresh look at the governance meta-model over the next six weeks, with specific focus areas to be determined during that time.

### Governance Model for Trust Graph

The group discussed developing a governance model for the centralized trust graph, with Steven suggesting they should identify specific questions and concerns rather than simply creating a V2 based on V1. Neil proposed engaging the insurance industry, particularly cyber insurance firms, to provide insights on risk and personal information collection. Carly noted that successful group outcomes typically come from participants who have specific problems to solve and understand the relevant workflows.

### Governance Metamodel Update Discussion

The group discussed updating the governance metamodel, with Carly and Karla agreeing to lead the review effort. Karla shared her experience implementing the current metamodel in the VLEI ecosystem governance framework, which serves as a practical application that could inform version 2 updates. The team identified several potential improvements including adding a glossary and updating terminology to reflect changes in the technology stack, though no major structural changes to document types were needed at this time. Drummond agreed to locate previous notes on proposed updates and share them with the group, while the team decided to continue meeting every two weeks to prepare for a more focused review in September when key stakeholders return from vacation.
