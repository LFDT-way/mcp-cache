## Overview

The Trust Over IP Ecosystem and Governance Working Group meeting focused on a presentation by Nick Lambert from DocLabs and Glyn Pover from Telefonica Tech about their Trusted Caller Identity proof of concept (POC). The presenters demonstrated a solution that uses SIM card-based authentication and verifiable credentials to securely authenticate callers to call centers, reducing authentication time from up to 5 minutes to under 60 seconds. The POC, sponsored by GSMA, successfully processed over 500 calls with 70 demos at Mobile World Congress, but the presenters identified several challenges including the need for business cases, incentives for carriers to issue credentials, and consumer adoption of the technology. The discussion highlighted the broader industry goal of achieving cryptographically secure voice and messaging communications, with Glyn noting that Telefonica has already started implementing similar capabilities through their RCS messaging service with Verified Sender features.

### Trusted Caller Identity Solution

Glyn Pover from Telefonica Tech presented on Trusted Caller Identity, a proof of concept developed in response to impersonation fraud targeting call centers of major brands. The solution leverages SIM card technology and real-time APIs to provide enhanced caller verification, addressing gaps in existing security measures like knowledge-based authentication and SMS OTPs. Glyn explained that the project originated from discussions with GSMA and a UK bank, and was later demonstrated at Mobile World Congress, where they partnered with DocLabs to create a robust ecosystem involving consumers and strengthening privacy protections.

### Call Center Authentication Solution Demo

Glyn and Nick presented a demonstration of a call center authentication solution under the GSMA's "United Against Scams" initiative. Glyn explained that the solution aims to address security issues in global interconnected carrier networks, including SIM swap fraud and CLI spoofing, by providing faster authentication, stronger security, and enhanced privacy through decentralized identity and verifiable credentials. Nick highlighted the growing problem of scams, noting that $1 trillion worth of scams are expected in 2024, with 25% of unknown calls being scams or fraud. The team demonstrated a video of their solution in action, showing a process where users receive a notification on their mobile device to confirm their identity through a TrueVira Wallet app.

### Wallet Credentials Call Center Integration

Nick presented a proof of concept (POC) for integrating wallet-based credentials into call center authentication, specifically demonstrating the implementation with O2 (a Telefonica brand in the UK). The system allows users to be onboarded with wallet addresses during a call center onboarding flow, where they can then receive and verify credentials through either DIDCOM messages or credential presentations. Nick highlighted the potential for mass adoption, noting that approximately 70-75% of O2's monthly subscribers regularly use their mobile app, reaching about 15 million customers, and discussed various use cases including partner onboarding and delegated authority. The demonstration utilized W3C credentials, JSON-LD, multiple DID methods, DIPCOM messaging protocol, and OpenID Connect as part of the Truvera/DocLab technology stack.

### User Experience Design Approach Discussion

The team discussed the user experience design approach for their POC, where Nick explained they aimed for minimal friction by removing clicks and using DIDCOP messages instead of verifiable credentials. Glyn noted that initial feedback has been positive and similar to the open banking experience, though Eric raised concerns about the back-and-forth between apps creating confusion for users. The team acknowledged this is their first iteration and will continue gathering user feedback to improve the experience.

### Phone Call Verification System Implementation

The group discussed potential approaches to implementing a verification system for phone calls, including the possibility of embedding it directly into phone dialers through partnerships with major tech companies like Google or Apple. Glyn emphasized the importance of putting customers in control of their data, though he acknowledged this might create friction. Carly raised concerns about verifying the identity of companies or individuals requesting verification credentials, leading to a discussion about how the proposed system would provide multiple authentication factors and notifications during ongoing calls to address these security concerns.

### Cryptographic Voice Communication POC

Glyn and Nick presented on a proof of concept (POC) project aimed at implementing cryptographically secure voice and messaging communications between consumers and brands. The POC successfully demonstrated reduced authentication time from up to 5 minutes to under 60 seconds in over 500 calls, with 70 demos given at Mobile Congress in Barcelona. The team identified three key challenges moving forward: developing a commercial business case, analyzing different technical solutions including wallet and credential integration, and addressing privacy and security concerns while avoiding high implementation costs. Glyn noted that Telefonica has already started implementing verified sender capabilities in their RCS messaging service, and the team plans to explore making the technology appear as a seamless feature within carrier apps following Steve Jobs' principle that technology should be "beautiful or invisible."
