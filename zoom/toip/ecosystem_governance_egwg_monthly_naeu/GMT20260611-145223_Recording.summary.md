### Keyring Decentralized Identity App

Brendan presented the Keyring mobile app, developed at the Applied Social Media Lab at Harvard University, which aims to help users verify their identity and establish trust in a decentralized manner. The app uses three layers of trust signals: device biometrics, in-person connections, and witnessed context to create portable proof of identity without centralized storage. Brendan explained that the team is working on developing levels of assurance that are more flexible than traditional financial standards, drawing inspiration from models like the I-9 form, and they are actively contributing to the Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group's standards development.

### Keyring Wallet Application Presentation

Brendan and Alberto presented Keyring, an open-source wallet application that enables peer-to-peer relationship credential exchanges. They demonstrated how the app allows users to create and manage trusted connections, with optional biometric authentication and community witness verification. The presentation covered seven types of credentials including relationship credentials, witness credentials, and R-cards, with the core functionality built on pairwise private decentralized identifiers to ensure privacy. The team discussed future plans including strict co-locality proof implementation and modularizing the relationship credential component into an SDK.
