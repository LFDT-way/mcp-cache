## Overview

The meeting focused on discussing biometrics and digital trust, with Daniel providing expertise on the technical aspects of biometric systems, standards, and implementation challenges. The group explored topics including biometric authentication methods, error rates, liveness detection, demographic biases, and privacy considerations in both government and private sector contexts. Participants discussed practical challenges such as handling physical changes to biometric data and the ethical considerations around deduplication across different identity contexts. The conversation concluded with discussions about potential outputs from a future task force, including governance guidance for non-governmental use of biometrics and community-level identity management approaches.

### Biometrics in Digital Identity Verification

The meeting focused on discussing biometrics and trust in digital identity documents. Daniel explained how biometrics are used to verify the authorized holder of a document, particularly through the cryptographic signing of identity attributes and photographs as required by ICAO standards. He noted that while EU digital identity wallets initially excluded biometrics due to privacy concerns, they were later included to ensure non-repudiation. The discussion highlighted the importance of biometrics in establishing trust in digital identity verification processes.

### Biometric Authentication Standards Revision

Carly and Daniel discussed biometric authentication challenges, particularly Carly's issues with fingerprint recognition on her phone. Daniel explained that they are revising MDL standards to include on-device holder authentication, allowing wallet providers to use third-party biometric algorithms rather than relying solely on device manufacturers. The discussion touched on the advantages of this approach, including transparency in error rates and the ability to choose more reliable authentication methods.

### Biometric Authentication Liveness Detection Standards

Daniel explained the concepts of false match rates and liveness detection in biometric authentication standards, particularly focusing on ISO 30107-3. He described how liveness detection works at different levels, including the use of 3D dot projection technology and presentation attack detection methods used by companies like Apple. The discussion covered various techniques for verifying the authenticity of biometric presentations, including Flashmark technology and 3D depth analysis.

### Biometric Liveness Scores Implementation

Daniel and Carly discussed liveness scores and their implementation in biometric systems. Daniel explained that the ISO specification allows relying parties to set thresholds for match decisions, providing transparency on similarity scores. They also addressed concerns about demographic differentials and bias in biometric algorithms, with Daniel mentioning NIST's evaluations on error rates across different demographics and the impact of bias on legislation, such as Virginia's ban on automated facial recognition.

### Biometric Matching and Challenges

Daniel and Carly discussed biometric matching rates and challenges. Carly asked about handling physical changes in biometric credentials, such as after an injury affecting fingerprint or facial recognition. Daniel explained that while face and fingerprint biometrics are generally stable in adults, changes due to accidents or maturity could require re-enrollment for identity documents. He mentioned the concept of "best minutiae" in U.S. border systems, where multiple fingerprints are collected, and described the EU's entry-exit system requiring four fingerprints and face scans with a three-year retention policy.

### Biometric Data Collection Challenges

Daniel and Carly discussed challenges with biometric data collection, particularly for individuals with missing or impaired biometric features. Daniel explained that multiple biometrics are collected to address these issues, and metadata is used to note any limitations during collection. Ann raised questions about decision-making processes regarding biometric standards, exceptions, and data ownership, highlighting the need for clear governance and laws to ensure system resilience and robustness.

### Biometrics Implementation Privacy Challenges

Daniel discussed the challenges with biometrics implementation, particularly focusing on privacy and transparency issues. He explained that while some governments, including the US, have publicly available privacy impact assessments outlining what data is collected, how it's protected, and who it's shared with, private sector companies often have less transparency. Daniel highlighted an example of a company that misleadingly claimed they only performed one-to-one biometric matching, when they were actually conducting one-to-many searches for identity verification.

### Biometrics for Identity Deduplication

Daniel and Carly discussed the use of biometrics for deduplication in identity systems. They explored the distinction between foundational and functional identities, with Daniel explaining that foundational identity is typically a government function aimed at establishing uniqueness within a population, while functional identities are used in various contexts. Daniel mentioned examples like passports and driving licenses as proxies for foundational identity in the U.S., and referenced NIST 863A's focus on establishing uniqueness through identity proofing.

### Biometrics and Privacy-Preserving Solutions

The group discussed the use of biometrics and foundational legal identity, particularly focusing on privacy-preserving methods like zero-knowledge proofs for age verification and other purposes. Neil highlighted challenges in implementing privacy-preserving solutions, especially in travel and medical contexts where personal information sharing is necessary. The group considered forming a task force to develop governance guidance on biometric identification for non-government entities, with potential outputs including recommendations for responsible biometric use and community-level deduplication approaches. The discussion concluded with an agreement to continue the conversation in a Discord thread and potentially at the May 14th IIW follow-up meeting, where participants could propose specific task force outputs.
