### Enforceable Policy and Auditability

Christine presented on "The Making of an Enforceable Policy and How it Improves Auditability," explaining that enforceable policies consist of observable and measurable language. She outlined three key characteristics of observable rules: a responsible party, specific outcomes, and conditions (including when, where, and how the outcome must happen). Christine used an example of a sales policy requiring representatives to provide return policy copies with receipts to demonstrate how enforceable language identifies the necessary data collection points for observation and measurement.

### Enforceable Policies for Effective Governance

Christine discussed the importance of enforceability in policy creation, explaining that enforceable policies are observable and measurable, not indicators of implementation or effectiveness. She provided an example of an ambiguous policy rule and demonstrated how using definitive language like "must" and specific conditions can improve enforceability. Christine emphasized that enforceable policies help collect the right data for measurement, enabling proactive governance and improving audit readiness. She concluded by stating that enforceability serves as the foundational building block for auditability, enhancing readiness and sustainability over time.

### Enhancing AI Governance and Traceability

Christine presented on computational governance and auditability, emphasizing the importance of clear, concise language in policies to ensure traceability and accountability, particularly in AI agent decision-making processes. She explained that system-to-system governance is easier to implement than human-centric processes, as digital systems can automatically capture and collect the necessary data. Eric discussed the challenges of implementing policies for agentic AI, highlighting the need for real-time observability and transparency in decision-making processes. Christine concluded by noting that while no policy is perfect, having observable, measurable language allows for continuous improvement and adaptation to dynamic environments.

### Policy Implementation and Control Strategies

Christine and Eric discussed policy implementation and measurement structures, with Christine explaining how to build intended outcomes into policy sentences using a sentence structure approach. Scott provided additional insights from an auditing perspective, emphasizing the importance of preventative controls over detective controls, particularly in AI and machine operations contexts where speed of transactions requires proactive measures rather than reactive detection. The discussion highlighted the need for clearer corollaries and mitigation strategies when policies are not executed, with Christine suggesting that the proposed methodology could be applied across various governance domains and strategic planning documents.
