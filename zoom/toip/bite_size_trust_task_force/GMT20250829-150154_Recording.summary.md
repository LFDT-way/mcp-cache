## Details

### Passkeys vs. Passwords: Security Insights

Neil and Carly discussed the differences between traditional passwords and passkeys, with Carly proposing to create a video explaining how passwords work and contrasting them with passkeys. They explored the security aspects of passkeys, including the use of private and public keys, and the concept of DIDs (Decentralized Identifiers) for managing authentication. Neil emphasized the importance of making security measures expensive for attackers, which passkeys and Travel Profiles aim to achieve by making each individual attack more costly.

### YubiKey Authentication Limitations Discussion

Neil and Carly discussed the limitations and practicality of using YubiKeys for authentication. Neil shared his experience with different authenticator options, noting that YubiKeys were less useful due to their high cost and inconvenience when used with remote desktop setups. Carly expressed frustration with the lack of clear documentation on YubiKey features, such as the liveness test. Neil suggested that the YubiKey could be useful as a backup method but is not as versatile as other authenticator options. The conversation concluded with Carly questioning whether Neil had specific feedback on scripts or slide changes, as she was hesitant to make extensive modifications without clear direction.

### DID Document and Transaction Storage

Neil and Carly discussed the proper use of DID documents and transaction logs. They agreed that transaction logs should be stored separately from DID documents, with the latter serving as an authority for changes while the former contains the actual transaction data. Neil explained that ACDCs (Atomic Commitment Data Chains) can be used as ledgers for verifiable data, credentials, and access controls, with different levels of complexity depending on the use case. They also touched on the concept of micro-ledgers as a lightweight alternative for certain applications.

### Witness Requirements and Credential Security

Carly and Neil discussed the necessity of witnesses and notarization for various transactions, emphasizing that while some transactions require oversight, others do not. They also talked about the potential risks of hackers accessing emergency contact information stored in simple files. Carly mentioned an incident involving international child kidnapping and the challenges of jurisdictional legislation in guardianship cases. Finally, Carly shared information about the Credential Engine, an organization that has developed an ontology for academic credentials, but noted that it does not yet address the signatories of these credentials.

### International Academic Credential Security

Carly and Neil discussed the risks of hosting academic credential verification systems in a single country, highlighting the potential for political pressure and disruption during trade negotiations. They explored the possibility of using blockchain technology to distribute and secure credential data across multiple countries, ensuring international cooperation and reducing the risk of any single country gaining control over the system. Neil questioned the practicality of this approach, emphasizing the need for secure data storage and the limitations of physical location in protecting against legal jurisdiction issues.

### Decentralized Trust Graphs Working Group

Carly and Neil discussed a new working group focused on decentralized trust graphs, which will meet on the 17th and overlap with an existing trust registry meeting. Neil mentioned that the hospitality and travel industry is particularly desperate for SSI due to complex trust relationships between different legal entities. Carly plans to start publishing videos in September, which she has already reviewed with members of the steering committee.

### DIDCOM and Trust Spanning Protocol

Carly and Neil discussed the concept of DIDCOM, its relationship to Trust Spanning Protocol, and its potential applications in secure communication. Neil explained that DIDCOM enables individuals to set up communication channels leveraging security and signing abilities, using private and public keys. They also touched on the differences between centralized and decentralized identity systems, with OpenID Connect being mentioned as a centralized approach currently popular in Europe. Neil highlighted the trend of governments wanting a single identifier while individuals prefer pairwise DIDs for privacy reasons, using Bhutan as an example of a country allowing different departments to use central identifiers initially but switching to pairwise DIDS afterward.

### Authentication Methods: Passwords vs SSH

Carly and Neil discussed the challenges and benefits of different authentication methods, comparing password-based systems to SSH key authentication. They agreed that while passwords can be managed with tools like password managers, they still pose security risks, especially for less tech-savvy users. Neil mentioned he uses 1Password for managing his passwords, while Carly noted that her parents would struggle with such tools. The conversation concluded with Neil mentioning an upcoming deadline for delivering a schema by September 19th.

### Secure Data Sharing in Travel

Neil and Carly discussed the challenges and ethical considerations of personal data collection in the travel industry, focusing on the balance between customer privacy and personalized services. They explored how medical information and emergency contacts could be managed securely, emphasizing the need for a system that allows access to necessary information without compromising privacy. Neil explained the concept of using SSI (Self-Sovereign Identity) to enable secure sharing of information through certified agents, rather than directly disclosing sensitive data. They concluded that working with certified agents could be a promising approach for Bite Size Trust.
