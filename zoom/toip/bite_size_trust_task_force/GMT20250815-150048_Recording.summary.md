### Multi-Signature Authentication With DIDs

Carly and Neil discuss multi-signature authentication methods where access requires permission from multiple parties, similar to cryptocurrency systems. They explore how this approach can be used in organizational settings, with Neil mentioning Sam's "3 of 5" model where any three of five designated people must sign to authorize changes. Carly explains how DIDs (Decentralized Identifiers) work with passkeys, noting that Google supports passkeys in Android, while they debate the security implications of key rotation and account recovery methods across different DID implementations. Neil recommends watching a presentation by Datara on AI agent technology that he shared in the AI.M-TF Slack channel.

### Decentralized Travel Data Management System

Neil discusses a travel company's approach to using DIDs (decentralized identifiers) with limited lifetimes for specific purposes, where data sharing is restricted both by time and purpose, ending when parties agree they're finished. Carly mentions they're working on ODRL, a machine-readable format to express license rights, which could fit Neil's use case. Neil explains their travel profile system uses ENUS as the internal technical value with an enum-driven approach and translation lookups, with AI translation becoming good enough to interpret user input into structured data.

### AI Agents and Data Privacy

Neil and Carly discuss how technical information in user profiles, such as medical conditions, can be used by AI agents to provide appropriate support services without sharing sensitive details. They explore how decentralized identifiers (DIDs) and verifiable credentials work with delegation of authority, where users can delegate tasks to AI agents while maintaining control over their data. Neil advocates for a distributed wallet model with different classes of DIDs ranging from ephemeral (for single transactions) to permanent ones, citing Bhutan's approach as a positive example where transactional DIDs are used instead of a single trackable identifier.

### DID and FIDO Authentication Comparison

Carly and Neil discuss the mutual authentication capabilities of DIDs and FIDO, noting that DIDs allow mutual authentication through public keys while FIDO relies on TLS certificates. Neil explains that the Trust Spanning Protocol builds on these security mechanisms, creating secure communication channels regardless of underlying infrastructure, and mentions that the DIDComm community sees TSP as the next evolution of DIDComm. They also discuss how OpenID Connect is being used as a bridge to transition from OAuth to DID-based systems, with Neil sharing that even organizations like Visa Europe are initially supporting digital wallets with OpenID Connect while planning to transition to SSI and DIDComm internally.
