## Agenda

Quarterly meeting for Trust Over IP Contributor Members

## Overview

The Trust over IP semiannual All Members Meeting was hosted by Tomas and featured updates from various working groups and task forces. The meeting covered highlights from the Ecosystem and Governance Working Group, Technology Stack Working Group, Concepts and Terminology Working Group, Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group, AI and Human Trust Working Group, Creator Assertions Working Group, and Cary Suite Working Group. Key updates included the approval of the Trust Registry Protocol 2.0 as a Trust over IP approved deliverable, progress on the Trust Enabling AI Agent Protocols (TEA) specification, and the development of a JSON schema registry for schema discovery and AI consumption. The discussion portion covered upcoming events including the SETI Summit, Cary Conference, and IIW, with particular emphasis on the growing adoption of Cary-related technologies in telecom, state-issued digital identity, and healthcare sectors.

## Details

### Trust Over IP Anniversary Update

The meeting began with opening remarks from Drummond and Carly, who highlighted that Trust Over IP is approaching its sixth anniversary and has recently approved its first Trust Over IP approved deliverable. They emphasized the growing relevance of AI in the trust and safety market, which is providing momentum to the organization's efforts. Carly mentioned plans to refresh the foundational documents of Trust Over IP during the upcoming summer pause in the Ecosystem and Governance Working Group meetings. The meeting then transitioned to working group and task force highlights, with the Ecosystem and Governance Working Group scheduled to present next.

### Ecosystem Governance Strategy Updates

Makki presented updates on the Ecosystem Governance Working Group's strategy refresh, which includes five key pillars focusing on tech stack collaboration, roadmap establishment, and continuing trust-related initiatives. The group is working on converting governance principles into a tool to assess protocol interoperability before public releases, with feedback being collected from team members including Scott, Drummond, and Wenjing. Carly announced upcoming events including a special topics meeting on biometrics on April 16th and a presentation on cryptographic proof of process on April 28th, while also highlighting the availability of Bite Size Trust videos on the Trust Over IP webpage and a new learning pathway organization suggested by Sankarshan.

### Trust Protocol Progress Updates

Drummond reported significant progress in the technology staff working group, highlighting the approval of the Trust Registry Protocol 2.0 as a trustworthy approved deliverable. Wenjing discussed ongoing implementation work on the Trust Manning protocol, with Revision 3 expected by the second half of the year and potential version 1.0 release shortly after. The meeting also covered updates on the High Assurance Verifiable Identifiers Task Force, led by Alex Tweeddale, and the DIDSCID method, which is currently in the queue to become a DIF recommended DID method.

### Technology Stack and Working Groups

Drummond provided updates on the technology stack and concept terminology, highlighting the development of Specup, an open-source spec editing tool enhanced with external glossary capabilities. The Decentralized Trust Graph working group has grown to seven task forces, with ongoing work on credential types and implementations by Affinity and Harvard teams. Martina briefly mentioned her involvement with the First Person Cooperative, which is evolving to provide governance for the first-person network initiative.

### Trust Working Groups Progress Update

The meeting covered updates from two working groups. Drummond discussed the Ecosystem Governance Working Group and mentioned that their work would be presented at IDW. Martina provided information about personhood credentials and 3 labs related to verifiable trust communities, which Drummond agreed to include in the presentation materials. Wenjing presented on the AI and Human Trust Working Group's progress on their first specification called TSP-enabled AI Agent Protocols (TEA), which aims to define ways of constructing and managing AI agents with both authorization and accountability mechanisms. The working group meets weekly on Thursdays at 9am Pacific time and has an active Discord channel for collaboration.

### Creator Assertions Working Group Updates

Scott presented updates on the Creator Assertions Working Group (COG), which is a joint collaboration with DIF focused on allowing creators to make assertions about digital objects. He explained that COG works alongside CHPA, a standards organization under the Linux Foundation, to create hardbound manifests and handle claims about digital objects' creation and modifications. Scott outlined recent progress, including updates to the identity specification and the creation of task forces focusing on verifiable credentials, trust ecosystems, and consent mechanisms. He also mentioned plans to explore Carri technology integration and the establishment of advisory groups to gather feedback from end users, while emphasizing the need for careful management of identity assertions to prevent fraud.

### Travel Profile System Development Update

Wenjing announced the completion of a working group focused on self-addressing identifiers, identifying two potential improvements related to resolution and verification without prior schema knowledge. Neil presented a joint project with the hospitality and travel working group, discussing the development of a comprehensive travel profile system that could include up to 10,000 different points of information, being developed as a JSON schema to support personalized travel services.

### AI in Travel Planning Challenges

Neil discussed the challenges of using AI in travel planning, emphasizing the need for structured data over free-form text to ensure accurate interpretation and privacy protection. He highlighted survey findings showing traveler skepticism about AI booking, with only 8% expressing comfort using AI platforms. Tomaz then announced opportunities for deeper involvement through LF Decentralized Trust membership and mentioned upcoming events, transitioning to plans for presentations by the steering committee.

### Digital Identity Conference Planning

The group discussed upcoming conferences, including the SETI Summit and Cary Conference scheduled for April 20-22. Nicholas, who is on the steering committee, described the inaugural SETI Summit as a test run to gauge interest in the decentralized identity community. Samuel highlighted three key adoption vectors for digital identity technologies in 2026: telecom sector implementations, state-endorsed digital identity legislation, and healthcare data provenance initiatives.

### IAW and AIW Conference Planning

The group discussed upcoming events, including the IAW and AIW conferences in two weeks. Kaliya highlighted that these conferences are open space events where topics are co-created on the day, and she encouraged proactive participation and sponsorship opportunities. Drummond mentioned specific sessions planned, including topics on legal underpinnings of decentralized digital identity and ZKP work for the decentralized trust graph. The group also briefly touched on JSON schema work, with Lisa mentioning it in response to Drummond's inquiry.

### Schema Registry Initiative Presentation

Lisa presented a new schema registry initiative called schemas.pub, which aims to provide persistent URLs for schemas using JSON schema and is designed to support multiple appropriate schemas rather than enforcing a single standard like schemas.org. The system is being developed to help AI agents understand data by allowing them to look up explanations for field names, though it remains in pilot phase and is still being revised. Samuel mentioned that the ACDC spec already uses cryptographically verifiable immutable schemas, and two new task forces have been established: one for DIDWEB aspects and another for the dossier spec led by Daniel Hardman, which allows construction of evidence graphs from ACDCs for trust decisions.

### IEEE Trust in Digital Communication Program

Vikas Malhotra announced the formation of a new IEEE industry connections program focused on trust in digital communication, which will work over the next two years to define trustworthiness between entities. Drummond mentioned IEEE standard P7012 and VRM Day as upcoming events related to personally asserted privacy terms, with the latter featuring an Executive Director from MyData Global discussing European adoption of the standard. The meeting participants discussed plans for the Global Digital Collaboration event, where Trust Over IP communities can submit topics for curated open space sessions, with at least two organizers required per topic and LFDT coordinating participation from their group.
