### Trust Over IP Protocol Implementation

Steve discussed methods for implementing these duties in agents through training data, prompts, and workflow design, including the use of RAG for context-specific trust tasks.

### Trust Over IP Process Overview

Steve explained the importance of redundancy in the Trust Over IP process and outlined key steps including duty preference compatibility checks, template selection, and externality scans. He discussed the concept of fiduciarity, emphasizing cooperative negotiation and ensuring fair benefits for both parties, with contracts being renegotiable when they cease to benefit both parties. Steve also covered the process of externalities scanning, mutual attestation requirements, and runtime operation privileges, concluding with a discussion of how fiduciary preferences create a feedback loop through negotiation positions and execution feedback.

### Digital Twin Implementation Discussion

Steve explained the digital twin concept, describing how it contains multiple models including a compliance module and an unalterable binary version that monitors user modifications to ensure they don't deviate too far from baseline. Mitchell raised concerns about potential prompt injection attacks, which Steve addressed by emphasizing the benefits of model heterogeneity and redundancy. The discussion concluded with Steve outlining his approach to distributed agreement repositories and decentralized taxonomy, noting that while there will be convergence on central duties like loyalty and care, many specific agreements will remain decentralized and searchable.

### IPFS and TSP Protocol Discussion

Steve discussed IPFS (Interplanetary File System) and reviewed Appendix B of a document related to arbitration precedent repositories and distributed storage. He highlighted the relevance of the TSP (Trust Spanning Protocol) paper by Wenjing, which maps well to fiduciary parties and relationship formations. Steve emphasized the importance of the routed mode in the TSP protocol for privacy and discussed how it addresses edge cases related to artificial intelligence. The group considered how these concepts could feed into the main document being developed by Wenjing regarding T-agents (trust-enabled agents).

### Fiduciary AI Agents Discussion

The group discussed Steve's paper on fiduciary AI agents and digital twins. Nicky suggested mapping duties to corresponding rights using frameworks like FRIA, while Neil highlighted the importance of guardian-like controls as stress test cases. The discussion covered implementation details including how to handle multiple digital twins, separate private and public data spaces, and the role of fiduciary duties for both AI agents and providers. The team also explored the relationship between Steve's trust spanning protocol (TSP) and fiduciary duties, with Mitchell clarifying that TSP serves as a trusted communication channel between different system layers without needing to understand the specific details of what's being communicated.
