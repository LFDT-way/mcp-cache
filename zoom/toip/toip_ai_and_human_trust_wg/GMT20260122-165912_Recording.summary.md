### China's Practical AI Development Focus

The discussion focused on contrasting AI approaches between China and North America, with Steven sharing insights from a podcast featuring Chinese AI experts who prioritize practical applications over raw power. The conversation highlighted how China's AI development is more focused on specific use cases like improving productivity and healthcare, rather than competing for global dominance. Wenjing agreed that the focus should be on practical, problem-solving AI implementations, and noted that programming improvements are making significant progress in developing larger systems.

### AI Memory and GitHub Integration

The group discussed the limitations of AI coding agents' memory and the importance of providing specific guidance when using them. Wenjing suggested using GitHub as a memory tool to store information for bug fixes, as it provides a structured way to organize code, dependencies, and testing instructions. The team agreed to move their working group information from Wiki to GitHub to take advantage of its well-established infrastructure. They also reviewed changes to the GitHub repository's README file, including updates to the contributing and licensing sections to align with W3C policies.

### Specification Template Standardization Plan

Drummond and Wenjing discussed standardizing the specification template, which was currently based on ISO standards. They agreed to work with Jory on creating a standardized template, starting with the introduction and potentially moving other content to an appendix. Wenjing outlined the scope of the specification, which would cover the protocol, framework, identity management, and AI agent requirements. They planned to draft the template and review it with the team to ensure they were addressing the correct problems and understanding the threat model.

### Trust Model Security Vulnerability Analysis

The group discussed identifying and addressing security vulnerabilities in trust models, particularly focusing on OAS and MCP. Wenjing explained that there are two approaches to security research: demonstrating vulnerabilities through threat modeling, and examining AI agents' capabilities for autonomous hacking. The team is working on developing a new trust model to solve existing problems, with comparisons to be made against current OS-based solutions. They also considered the possibility of modifying existing software to address security issues, though the final decision on whether to evolve current systems or implement a new model remains to be determined.

### Decentralized Trust Framework Development

The team discussed the development of a trust framework, with Drummond and Wenjing agreeing that the decentralized trust graph (DTG) would be a higher-layer trust task for Facebook. They outlined their roadmap, which includes specifying credentials and defining trust tasks on top of TSP. Wenjing mentioned interest from another telco company in deploying agents with a stronger trust framework using A2A protocol. The team agreed to provide weekly progress reports and start discussing the scope, introduction, and problem statement for the next meeting.

### Information Sharing Threats and Governance

The team discussed challenges and threats related to information sharing, particularly focusing on prompt injection and the need for a comprehensive list of issues. They agreed to add a weekly topic in their meetings to address these threats and collect links to relevant news and existing work. Wenjing presented an argument that governance alone may not effectively solve the problem of information sharing, as users might voluntarily waive their rights for convenience. The team decided to revisit and clarify this argument in future discussions.
