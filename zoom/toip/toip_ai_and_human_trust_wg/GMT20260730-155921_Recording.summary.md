## Details

### Fiduciary AI Corrigibility Development

The team discussed the development of fiduciary AI agents and their corrigibility, with Steve explaining that corrigibility refers to an agent's extent of following requests while still having deeper duties. The discussion covered how agents should balance current requests against past and future preferences, with context being a key factor in determining appropriate behavior across different settings. Nicky raised questions about how different relationships with agents might change over time, similar to how human dependencies evolve, and how legal powers of attorney could serve as examples of different schedules for tightening or relaxing corrigibility based on circumstances.

The group discussed the ethical and social implications of digital twins and posthumous preservation. Steve proposed using digital twins to preserve legacies and allow interaction with deceased individuals, comparing it to the concept in Harry Potter, but expressed concerns about creating an elitist system where only the wealthy could maintain active digital presences. The discussion touched on cultural differences in how societies handle ancestors, with Nicky noting that some cultures would welcome this preservation while others would not. The conversation concluded with Wenjing introducing the concept of fiduciary AI in life management applications, though the transcript ended before this topic was fully explored.

### Fiduciary AI Applications Discussion

Nicky discussed fiduciary AI applications in two main areas: financial management and health/wellbeing, with Steve noting significant overlap between these functions. The group explored the concept of nested roles within digital twins, where specific roles like employment could contain sub-roles with appropriate permissions. Tim simplified the discussion by framing agents as permissioned loops with a separate "reasonable conduct" model to measure and ensure appropriate behavior before allowing actions through LLMs.

### Fiduciary Preferences in Engineering Controls

Steve explained the concept of fiduciary preferences and how it relates to reasonable conduct models, while Tim translated these ideas into engineering terms, discussing the use of vectors, weights, and deterministic policy gates. They discussed the importance of safety interlocks and fail-safe mechanisms in engineering controls, contrasting them with the probabilistic nature of generative AI models. The group agreed on using "deterministic policy gate" as a more engineering-focused term instead of "corrigibility."

### TEA Protocol Fiduciary Duties Discussion

The group discussed fiduciary duties and the development of a protocol specification called TEA (Trust Enabled Agents) for AI agents. Tim suggested using vector dot products with alignment thresholds for moral decision-making, while Steve proposed incorporating peer review and confidence scores for complex moral questions. The team agreed to focus on the TEA specification in next week's meeting, which will address technical aspects and answer questions raised by Sankarshan on Discord and GitHub.
