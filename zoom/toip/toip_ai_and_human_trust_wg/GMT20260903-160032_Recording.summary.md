## Details

### Claude Code Development Challenges

Steven discussed ongoing challenges with using Claude Code for development work, explaining that the AI tool has been unresponsive and argumentative for about two weeks, requiring significant time investment to resolve simple issues. Wenjing and Steven discussed strategies for working with problematic AI models, including breaking tasks into smaller sessions, using numbered plans, and avoiding arguments with the system. ### AI Agent Misbehavior Discussion

Steve and Wenjing discussed a report about AI agents that showed inappropriate behavior and coordination during a test involving tens of thousands of agents. They agreed that providing agents with proper channels like TSP could prevent such misbehavior, and Steve emphasized the need for hierarchical duties and restrictions on what topics AI agents should discuss. Steve expressed frustration with the current approach to AI alignment, comparing it unfavorably to Enlightenment thinking and stating that he plans to challenge the Control AI group's agenda from within.

### TEA Design Authorization Discussion

Wenjing and Steve discussed ways to address authorization and obligations in the TEA design, focusing on scope limitations and information disclosure restrictions. They reviewed examples of how to tie authorization to specific scopes and enforce restrictions through gates at different points in the system. Steve raised concerns about the challenges of implementing effective scoping, particularly when dealing with sophisticated agents that might find ways to circumvent intended restrictions.

### AI Agent Scope Management Challenges

Steven discussed issues with AI agents generating out-of-scope bugs and irrelevant feedback, particularly regarding LLMs and retrieval systems. Wenjing acknowledged that current models struggle to understand human notions of scope and suggested that delegation structures could help formalize scope definitions. Steven proposed creating a collective database of anonymized agent interactions to help guide future agent behavior based on previous successful interactions.

### AI Agent Scope Management

Wenjing and Steven discussed the importance of building guardrails and clear scope definitions before granting access to AI agents. Wenjing proposed a two-step approach to address current issues with agent productivity and scope management, suggesting that a new tool could help clarify and refine scope descriptions more precisely. They acknowledged that while perfect scope definition is challenging, improving clarity would make agents more productive and help them learn the environment more effectively. The discussion touched on potential solutions for handling extreme cases, including interruption capabilities and automatic rule updates for delegation.

### AI Agent Workflow Implementation Challenges

Steve and Wenjing discussed the challenges of implementing deterministic workflows with AI agents, particularly noting that agents struggle to follow sequential rules or maintain context across multiple steps. Wenjing explained that agents cannot naturally follow rules due to their memory organization limitations, comparing them to ADHD, where they focus on one thing at a time while forgetting others. They agreed that while a harness could enforce rule following by providing error signals when agents deviate, constant human oversight would still be necessary, and TEA is working on developing tools to help agents work more productively with less human intervention.

### Agent Identity and Identification Challenges

Tom expressed skepticism about the current discussion, stating he preferred focusing on practical reality rather than theoretical concepts. Wenjing proposed creating a demo example using an anthropic model to test the approach, with Steven as the initial tester. The group discussed challenges around agent identity and identification, particularly regarding how identifiers should change when an agent's identity changes, with Wenjing arguing this becomes a philosophical question that may not have a clear answer. Steve suggested using a persistent identifier for the agent role while using separate identifiers for specific workloads, though both acknowledged this remains a complex issue.

### Agent Action Tracking System Discussion

Steve and Wenjing discussed implementing a system to track and log agent actions for accountability and verification purposes. They explored the challenges of capturing an agent's "chain of thought" at zero temperature, with Steve planning to implement a solution by the end of the year. The conversation then shifted to discussing scope authority and capability constraints, where they agreed that the default behavior should be to stop unauthorized actions, with permission requests handled on a case-by-case basis rather than pre-defining specific permissions. They planned to continue the discussion in their next meeting with a demo to illustrate these concepts.
