## Overview

The meeting focused on discussing privacy and data exchange challenges in travel and hospitality services, with Neil presenting ideas about using Trust Environments (TEA) to address the tension between personalized service requirements and privacy concerns. Neil explained how TEA environments could enable selective disclosure within sandboxes while ensuring that raw personal information cannot be extracted, similar to controlled access to classified information where only approved messages can be taken out of secure areas. The discussion included technical considerations about enforcement mechanisms, including the use of real-time auditors or "black box" recording systems to monitor communications, though specific implementation details were not fully resolved. Several participants noted that while the concept made sense for addressing privacy concerns in personal service scenarios like travel for individuals with special needs, questions remained about how to guarantee the security of digital environments and enforce these controls effectively.

### Travel Data Privacy Challenges

Neil discussed the challenges of balancing personalization in travel services with data privacy concerns. He explained the concept of selective disclosure within a TEA (Trusted Execution Environment) framework, which allows for sharing personal information in a controlled sandbox while ensuring that raw data cannot be taken out of the session. Neil noted that this approach addresses the issue of obtaining personalized services without compromising privacy, as evidenced by a recent EU survey showing 62% of people were extremely anxious about sharing personal data with AI tools for travel.

### Digital Security Enforcement Scenario

Tim asked Neil about how a described security scenario would be enforced digitally beyond zero-knowledge proofs. Neil explained that a third party could serve as a traveler in the sandbox with their own tools and agents, but could only transmit information through a secured observable channel that anyone could monitor. Neil clarified that real-time auditors would watch for any unauthorized actions or communications from the agents.

### Selective Disclosure in Sandbox Environment

Neil discussed the concept of selective disclosure in a sandbox environment, comparing it to aircraft black box recorders that capture detailed information but with restricted access. He explained that while ZKPs focus on anonymity, certain situations require sharing personal information, such as medical needs for travel, which operates under legal privacy bindings.
