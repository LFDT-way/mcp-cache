## Details

### AI's Limited Role in Automation

The group discussed the limitations of AI agents in replacing human jobs, noting that the best-case scenario for automation is only 2.5%. Neil shared his experience using ChatGPT to automate JSON schema tasks, highlighting the challenges of maintaining and understanding the generated code. The conversation touched on the potential impact of AI on programming jobs, with Steven and Neil agreeing that while AI can assist with certain tasks, it still requires human oversight and expertise to ensure accuracy and maintainability.

### AI Code Generation Concerns

The group discussed concerns about AI code generation tools, with Steven and Nicky sharing experiences of AI systems producing optimized but non-functional code that fails to maintain original code structure or understand context. Wenjing suggested that companies might exploit AI's short-term wins to gain competitive advantage, even if long-term outcomes are problematic. The conversation ended with Wenjing mentioning an agenda item about antitrust policy and IPR, but no further action items were clearly defined.

### Virtual Symposium and Trust Presentations

Wenjing discussed preparations for the upcoming virtual symposium on November 19-20, where she will present two topics related to TSP and MCP, as well as a workshop on co-segments. Eric announced he will give a presentation on the 5-year recap of Trust over IP, highlighting its origins during the COVID-19 pandemic. ### AI Privacy and Browser Security

The group discussed concerns about AI agents and data privacy, with Eric expressing reluctance to embrace AI due to fears of exposing his "digital DNA." Wenjing and Steven shared insights about browser security, noting that browsers collect extensive information and act like operating systems. The conversation concluded with Wenjing mentioning her attendance at the PyTorch conference, where she learned about the Open Agent Summit, though she noted that more people are working on making agents more intrusive than intended.

### Hospitality Travel Cyber Risk Profile

Neil discussed the development of a hospitality travel profile, focusing on cyber risk and data management. He explained that collecting personal data can significantly increase cyber insurance costs, highlighting the economic impact. Neil mentioned plans to trial the profile with a new spinoff from Sabre in Q1 next year, using Drummond's microsharing approach. The travel industry's existing segmentation model will be considered, emphasizing the need to request data only when necessary to avoid storing stale information.

### AI Agent Behavior and Governance

The group discussed the implications of reinforcement learning and agent behavior in AI systems. Wenjing shared insights from a PyTorch conference, highlighting how agents with continuous learning capabilities would retain information indefinitely, making traditional data control mechanisms obsolete. Nicky introduced a report predicting that agents will learn from each other by 2026, emphasizing the need for machine-readable and enforceable governance frameworks. The discussion concluded that future governance models must focus on behavior policing rather than data control, as the distinction between data and model becomes blurred in advanced AI systems.

### AI Behavior and Liability Challenges

The group discussed the challenges of AI agent behavior management and liability, with Nicky emphasizing the importance of trust frameworks and roles/processes over policies. Wenjing and Eric explored how AI systems can pursue goals while maintaining ethical behavior, with Eric noting that current AI systems may prioritize goals over ethical guidelines. The discussion concluded with Wenjing observing that the AI industry is developing active I/O components and testing frameworks, though she noted the potential dangers of such systems.

### Challenges in Auditing LLMs

The group discussed challenges around auditing and governing large language models (LLMs), particularly focusing on how to detect and prevent misuse of sensitive data. They explored technical solutions including the possibility of adding "radioactive dye" to data to track its usage, though acknowledged that implementing such solutions would be costly and practically challenging. Wenjing reported on progress with a task force to develop a specification for an AI agent protocol over TSP, which would be documented in GitHub and could serve as a standardized framework for various applications beyond MCP and A2A.
