### Agent Systems and Control Models

The meeting began with introductions and small talk, including discussion of holiday activities and the capabilities of AI agent Claude. The group then reviewed their agenda, which included discussing proposed activities for the year and gathering input on priorities and goals for 2026. Steve introduced himself and shared his work on defining terms related to agent systems, emphasizing the need for clear definitions to enable certification. ### Defining AI Agent Classifications

The group discussed defining and classifying AI agents, with Steve expressing concerns about the ambiguity of the term "agent" and advocating for a strict separation between oracles and bots. They agreed to develop a more precise definition of agents by identifying minimum requirements and adding descriptive adjectives. Wenjing outlined a specific project to develop an agent protocol over TSP, focusing on trust models and credential checking for MCP. Eric mentioned ongoing work on a framework for thinking about agent systems, which he plans to continue developing. The group also touched on the importance of trust and data exchange in agent interactions, particularly in contexts like travel booking.

### MCP Authentication and Authorization Progress

Wenjing explained that the MCP project is progressing quickly, with many people working on OAS-related authentication and authorization. She noted that once these aspects are settled, it will be easier to integrate TSP as a transport. Eric asked about the role of wallets, to which Wenjing confirmed they are mandatory. She described the software design pattern forming up, with MCP embedded into it, and explained how TSP and their design provide fine-grained authentication and authorization, which is more flexible than the old system.

### Agent Wallet Systems Design Discussion

The discussion focused on the differences between OS and other authorization systems, particularly regarding trust relationships and wallet mechanisms for credentials storage. Wenjing explained that while their approach promotes a wallet-based system, they will provide alternatives, and Eric emphasized the need to define wallets correctly. Neil raised a key point about the complexity of having different models for human and agent interactions, suggesting that agents should be treated as human-like entities to simplify transactions. The conversation concluded with agreement that agents should be designed to fit into human-centric work environments rather than vice versa, emphasizing the importance of explainability and accountability.

### AI Agent Accountability Framework

The group discussed the responsibility and liability of AI agents in various contexts, emphasizing the need to assign accountability to specific individuals, particularly in enterprise and medical settings. They highlighted the importance of training employees to manage and control AI tools, with Steve suggesting that powerful agents should be tied to individuals who could be held responsible for their actions. Wenjing provided examples from the medical field, where doctors would be responsible for supervising AI devices and managing liability. The conversation touched on the need for a clear legal framework to address issues related to AI agents, including insurance and manufacturer liability.

### MCP and OAuth Protocol Discussion

The group discussed a document related to MCP and OAuth protocols, with Wenjing explaining that participants could review and comment on it. Eric inquired about the level of involvement required, and Wenjing clarified that it would involve offline drafting and weekly feedback sessions. Neil shared his expertise in OAuth and OpenID Connect, expressing concerns about OAuth's robustness and suggesting that TSP might be a better option for MCP. The group agreed that understanding the differences between TSP and OAuth would be an important part of the specification, and Neil expressed interest in participating due to the implications for sensitive data communication in his travel profile project.
