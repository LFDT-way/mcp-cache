### AI Agents: Trust and Sustainability

Nicky expressed concerns about focusing on AI agents, questioning if they are a sustainable focus given that most commercial implementations rely on systems rather than agents. Wenjing and Eric discussed the benefits of focusing on agents, with Wenjing highlighting the need for a trust framework due to increased autonomy and complex interactions, while Eric emphasized the human resonance of agents and their applicability to future AI developments.

### Travel Agent Systems Integration Discussion

Neil discussed the role of travel agents as intermediaries between complex travel systems and users, emphasizing their importance in managing corporate travel preferences and bookings. Wenjing highlighted the potential for standardized protocols to enable the composition of complex systems, including those with agent-based components, which could provide more influence over system architecture. Nicky appreciated the inclusive design approach and the stack's applicability in handling complex, autonomous systems.

### Security and Privacy Protocol Draft

Nicky emphasized the importance of not trying to get ahead of the fast-moving market and thanked the team for their previous discussions. Wenjing presented a draft specification, outlining a problem statement and motivation, and highlighted the need for new protocols in security and privacy, especially in relation to the web model. She discussed three key issues: endpoint identity, delegate authorization, and message authentication, explaining the risks and limitations of the current authorization model.

### Metadata Privacy and TSP Protocols

Wenjing presented an outline for a technical specification document focusing on metadata privacy and interoperability issues. She explained that current methods of solving these problems are not satisfactory and proposed using TSP to provide a systematic approach. The document will cover running agent protocols over TSP, including specific protocols like MCP and A2A, and will include a section on credential issuance and presentation. Eric inquired about the focus of the credential issuance section, and Wenjing clarified that it involves protocols for verifying and presenting credentials between software components and humans.

### Protocol Delegation Workflow Design

Wenjing and Eric discussed the structure and implementation of delegation and authorization workflows within a protocol specification. They clarified that while the specification provides a recommended workflow, implementations can vary, and the workflows are intended to serve as examples rather than mandatory templates. Wenjing emphasized that the specification aims to offer practical guidance for implementing policies, particularly around human-in-the-loop requirements and messaging designs. Steven suggested the inclusion of "As Is" and "To Be" diagrams to better illustrate the workflow changes.

### Mitigating Protocol Failure Solutions

The team discussed the need to address points of failure in current protocols and demonstrate how proposed solutions, such as using specific identifiers like DID:WebVH, can mitigate these issues. Steven emphasized the importance of providing concrete examples to ensure the solution's effectiveness, suggesting that if a proposed solution cannot be shown to work, it is merely an academic exercise. Wenjing acknowledged this challenge and proposed describing a few examples in the specification, while also recognizing the need to balance specificity with flexibility in identifier choices.

### DID Web as Primary Identifier

The group discussed starting with DID Web as the primary identifier specification, noting its flexibility and growing momentum, while considering adding CAR as an alternative in the future. They agreed to focus on a straightforward implementation of DID Web to enable quick publication, with the possibility of incorporating other identifier options later. Eric raised questions about current identifier practices and adoption paths, to which Wenjing explained that existing email-based identifiers could be used with TSP, suggesting a gradual transition to new identifiers while acknowledging user comfort with familiar methods.

They also briefly touched on an upcoming symposium and a conference in New York in January. Wenjing mentioned she would be traveling to Hangzhou for a conference in September, where she would be presenting about AMP.
