## Overview

The meeting began with introductions and updates on recent developments in AI protocols and working groups, including discussions about trust frameworks and human-agent interactions. The team explored various approaches to improving trust between humans and AI agents, including the development of a meta-framework for trust governance and the creation of a decentralized trust graph. The group concluded by addressing the need to integrate human-centric aspects into protocol design and agreed to review related work while planning upcoming symposium presentations and workshops.

## Details

### Welcome and Policy Overview Meeting

The meeting began with introductions, including a welcome to Geun-Hyung, a new participant from Dongyi University in South Korea, who expressed interest in sub-sovereign identities and AI agent cooperation. Wenjing provided a brief overview of antitrust policy and IP rights before the meeting commenced.

### Agentic AI Initiatives and Roadmap

The group discussed recent developments, including a presentation by Wenjing to the Wallet Interability Special Interest Group and Google's announcement of an Agent Payment Protocol initiative. They reviewed upcoming events, including the Internet Architecture Workshop (IAW) and the Agentic Internet Workshop, with Drummond expressing excitement about the growing interest in agentic AI. The conversation ended with plans to consolidate the working group's goals and create a roadmap for ongoing projects, though Eric was absent from the discussion.

### AI Trust Framework Development Discussion

The meeting focused on discussing the Trust of IP and DIF working groups' current projects and their differentiation from other AI-related initiatives. Wenjing explained that their work involves developing a layered architecture for AI trust, with protocols like TSP playing a foundational role, and emphasized their focus on AI agents due to their urgent needs and the increasing importance of protocols. The group discussed their approach of complementing existing AI agent protocols rather than designing new ones, and Wenjing highlighted their framework for defining and reasoning about trust as a significant contribution. The conversation ended with a question about the scheduling of a Trust Framework Task Force meeting, which Neil and Steven indicated had not yet been set up.

### Human-Agent Trust Framework Development

The team discussed a conceptual framework for improving trust between humans and agents, with Eric having developed a draft document outlining patterns of pre-agent engagement evaluation. Drummond characterized this as a meta-framework for general policies and best practices in trust governance among AI agents, with Nicky agreeing it operates at a principles level and connects to both technical and governance requirements. The framework aims to apply the Trust of IPs approach to the problem of enhancing human-agent trust, with Eric's work focusing on unique patterns of pre-agent engagement evaluation.

### AI Trust Collaboration Planning Discussion

The team discussed potential overlap between their work and the Trusted AI Agents Working Group, particularly regarding travel use cases and trust-related patterns. Nicky presented a workflow visualization comparing A2A without TSP to A2A with TSP, while Wenjing suggested scheduling a combined meeting with BNC to discuss how their work relates to decentralized trust and AI agent protocols. Drummond humorously suggested a collaborative "hot tub" approach to working together, though the exact nature of their collaboration remains to be determined.

### Decentralized Trust Graph for AI

Drummond explained the concept of a decentralized trust graph that enables authenticated delegation to AI agents and provides a way to verify trust levels. He described the trust graph as a digital utility for users to develop trust in their relationships with AI agents. Wenjing asked about the DIFSA working group call, and Drummond confirmed he would check the timing. Wenjing mentioned a workshop on IEW and discussed their current work on writing a specification and producing a prototype implementation for TMCP and TA2A. They plan to have a demo ready for their November symposium.

### Human Experience in AI Interactions

Nicky raised concerns about the loss of human elements in the working group's focus, emphasizing the need to explicitly address both machine-to-machine and human-to-machine interactions in their charter. She proposed exploring ways to integrate human experience into their work, potentially through open sessions at the symposium. Wenjing agreed and suggested adding human experience as a regular agenda item for future discussions. Nicky expressed a desire to add value by addressing trust over IP in relation to human experience and suggested exploring how to link AI and technical views with human experience to mitigate potential harms.

### Governance and Trust Framework Integration

The group discussed integrating governance and trust frameworks into protocols, with Steven suggesting the need for a governance "hook" that would ensure DIDs cannot exist without governance structures. Neil shared insights from the DIF Hospitality and Travel Working Group, highlighting the importance of micro-sharing with callback for trusted data exchange and the need for structured data to ensure clear communication between AI agents and users. Mitchell presented an interface for a governance forum meeting that incorporates Trust Over IP concepts, including first-person credentials for access and a trust network system for managing contributions and reputation.

### Human-Centric AI Development Strategy

The group discussed the need for a structured agenda focused on human experience and agency in future meetings. They agreed to include human-centric aspects in protocol design and to review related work by other groups. Wenjing confirmed submissions for a symposium presentation and workshop scheduled for November 19-20, with plans to discuss content in the coming weeks. The group also addressed the need for mapping various initiatives to identify gaps and opportunities for integrating human elements into AI and software development processes.
