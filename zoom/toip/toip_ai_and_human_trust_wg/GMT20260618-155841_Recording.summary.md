## Overview

The meeting focused on updates to the TSP-enabled agent specification framework and discussion of a proposal for Trust Over IP. Wenjing presented progress on the authenticated exchange protocol, covering capability-based authorization, delegation mechanisms, and duty frameworks that allow for both formal and informal accountability structures. ## Details

### TSP Agent Specification Framework Updates

Wenjing provided updates on the TSP-enabled agent specification framework, noting progress in defining requirements, message serialization, and payload signing. She explained the integration of capabilities, limitations, and duties, emphasizing the append-only nature of limitations and the distinction between duties and verifiable constraints. The team discussed the significance of signed payloads and the potential use of VTA for signing responsibilities. Steve raised questions about terminology and the role of VTA, which were addressed by Wenjing. The group also briefly touched on recent developments in AI regulation and post-quantum cryptography, expressing concerns about government policies and potential impacts on their work.

### Multi-format Duty Expression System Design

Wenjing and Steve discussed a system design that allows expressing duties in multiple formats including layman, legal, and machine-readable terms. Nicky raised questions about the relationship between duties and capabilities rights, and expressed concerns about incorporating dynamic governance elements into the encoding. Wenjing clarified that the design aims to accommodate diverse governance approaches rather than dictate specific governance structures, and explained that the system assumes intelligent agents capable of interpreting natural language rather than requiring strict machine-readable formats.

### Authenticated Exchange Protocol Overview

Wenjing explained the authenticated exchange protocol, focusing on authorization capabilities that allow actions to be taken on behalf of others without sharing secrets. The protocol uses a data structure called capabilities to express policies and handle delegation, working similarly to a three-party credential system. Wenjing clarified that while the protocol provides the framework, applications can determine their own governance formats and vocabularies for resources and abilities.

### Data Handling in SSI Environments

Neil discussed insights from Jamie Smith's Substack columns about data in SSI environments and planned to write a separate document on the topic. Steve informed Neil about the VTA Project within the distributed trust working group and recommended he review the presentation from the previous day. Neil explained his perspective on handling personal data in travel contexts using a sandbox approach with audit capabilities. Eric raised a question about authority modeling as a capability, specifically asking who extends that authority in an agent-based system.

### Authority Delegation System Structure

Wenjing explained two examples of authority delegation: web services where a service provider issues credentials to users, and public authorities like driver's licenses. The discussion clarified that authority can be independent or dependent on other factors, and while someone may claim authority, verification and recognition by the counterparty still require governance and negotiation. Wenjing outlined the structure of their system, which includes capabilities that can be formally enforced and open policy extensions for non-structured information, concluding with a recommendation to review the detailed examples and design rationales provided.

### Trust Over IP Proposal Presentation

Steve presented a proposal for Trust Over IP focused on creating a verifiable agent relationship architecture. He outlined plans to integrate various Trust Over IP components into an end-to-end system that enables long-term fiduciary AI agents to manage complex relationships with both other agents and people. Steve noted he would be unavailable next week due to a presentation to the Gleanet Alliance, and requested the team continue work on the MCP section during his absence.

### TSP Agents Documentation Review

The team reviewed documentation about TSP-enabled agents and trust components, with Wenjing providing feedback on terminology and clarifications about VTA being a subsystem of TEA rather than a separate component. They agreed to swap responsibilities for the next session, with Wenjing taking over the MCP portion and Steve handling another agent-focused session in two weeks. Steve committed to posting meeting notes and documentation in the Discord channel for team review.
