## Overview

The meeting focused on discussing the development of a TEA (Trust, Authorization, and Accountability) protocol for AI agents, with Wenjing presenting progress on the specification and seeking feedback from the group. Wenjing explained the protocol's approach as a "meta-protocol" that would allow communication of authorization and accountability between agents using TSP (Trust over IP) as the underlying packaging layer, rather than modifying existing protocols like MCP. The group discussed how this approach would be more flexible and easier to adopt than traditional protocol specifications. Tim shared his experiences experimenting with AI agents and raised questions about reliability versus trust, while Nicky contributed insights about multi-party negotiations and the importance of considering broader ecosystem interests including community and environmental impacts. The discussion concluded with agreement that the protocol should be designed to integrate with existing systems rather than requiring complete system overhauls, and plans were made for future sessions to continue developing the specification.

### Open-Source AI Models Landscape

Wenjing discussed the current landscape of open-source AI models, highlighting that while some Chinese models are "open parameter" (meaning they share model weights but not training data or code), European groups are working on fully open datasets and code. She explained that while NVIDIA's dominance creates significant barriers to running open-source AI on alternative hardware, some companies like Amazon are working to diversify their chip usage. Wenjing noted that most smaller companies focus on applying existing LLM models rather than developing new architectures, as current models are considered sufficient for many applications, though performance improvements continue to be important for staying competitive.

### AI Agent Reliability Discussion

Tim shared his experience experimenting with AI agents, noting concerns about reliability and token costs, and discussed the distinction between agents acting on behalf of users versus "claws" that operate independently. Tim emphasized moving away from the concept of "trust" to "reliability" when working with these models, and highlighted ongoing debates about whether to use centrally hosted LLMs versus locally hosted and fine-tuned models. The discussion concluded with Tim questioning the group's central focus and seeking clarification on the key objectives they are trying to address.

### TEA Protocol Explanation Meeting

Wenjing explained the TEA protocol, which aims to facilitate authorization and accountability through a flexible, meta-protocol approach rather than a traditional, specific protocol. The protocol focuses on providing communication mechanisms and trust-building frameworks, leaving semantic details to higher layers and intelligent agents. Wenjing clarified that while the protocol involves technical specifications, it assumes smart agents can handle complex semantics, reducing the need for extensive pre-defined details. The discussion also touched on the relationship between the working group and the Decentralized Identity Foundation (DIF), noting their close collaboration despite remaining separate entities.

### Trust Signals Packaging Approach Discussion

Wenjing discussed a proposed approach for packaging trust signals using ACDC as the container layer, allowing for backward compatibility with existing protocols like W3C DIGCOM. She explained that most of the work is already completed, with only a few specifications needed, and that she is writing the code in Rust for better security. Wenjing also compared their approach with other groups working on similar initiatives, highlighting differences in implementation methods but noting commonalities in goals.

### MCP Integration Approaches Discussion

Wenjing and Steve discussed the differences between two approaches to integrating MCP (MCP1) with DIDs and verifiable credentials. Wenjing explained that their approach involves creating a separate protocol layer that can be used by multiple protocols including MCP, while the other group's approach modifies the MCP protocol directly. Wenjing emphasized that their method allows for easier integration and makes TSP a general facility that can be used beyond just MCP, including for A2A protocols.

### TSP Implementation and Integration Planning

Wenjing and Steve discussed the implementation of TSP (Trust Service Provider) and its integration with MCP and A2A systems. They highlighted that TSP offers benefits like accountability, security, and privacy without significant barriers to adoption, allowing selective implementation across different features. Wenjing outlined a plan to document the protocol, including sections on ACDC usage, a virtualized messaging pattern, and running TSMCP over existing systems, with a focus on addressing potential ambiguities and ensuring interoperability between current running codes.

### Fiduciary Duties Contract Template Discussion

The group discussed a contract template for expressing fiduciary duties and data privacy requirements. Wenjing and Steve explored how to implement these duties in practice through a natural language approach rather than a code-like format. Nicky expressed support for the meta-pattern concept and shared thoughts on developing a decentralized trust graph, particularly focusing on exchange functions and potential mediation mechanisms for multi-party negotiations. The discussion concluded with Steve mentioning pendulum arbitration as a relevant approach.

### Pendulum Arbitration Decision-Making Approaches

The group discussed a pendulum-based arbitration approach where parties present solutions to arbiters who can only choose one option, incentivizing reasonable solutions that incorporate both parties' viewpoints. Wenjing explored extending this to multi-party systems and integrating with existing bidding systems without requiring changes. Nicky described her work on quadrilateral decision-making that includes representation of commonweal and environmental interests alongside party A and B, while Steve explained the concept of duty of care versus duty of loyalty in fiduciary agents.

### Multi-Party Communication System Expansion

The team discussed expanding beyond point-to-point communication patterns to include multi-party interactions, with Nicky suggesting this approach would be more applicable to complex systems like digital supply chains. Wenjing proposed incorporating parties as players in existing consensus systems or messaging platforms to make the pattern more versatile. The group also explored the importance of human intervention in governance requirements and discussed the concept of stable system strategy, with Steve offering to share knowledge about game theory and Nash equilibria in future sessions.
