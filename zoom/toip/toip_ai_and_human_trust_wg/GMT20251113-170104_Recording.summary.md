### Trust and Data Sharing Challenges

The group discussed the limitations of micro-sharing and zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) in addressing trust issues with personal data and AI agents. Drummond emphasized that while data sharing is often necessary for value, the focus should be on establishing trust relationships and governance agreements over data use. Wenjing noted the need for antitrust and IPR notices, while Nicky highlighted the concept of contextual permissioning as a potential solution. The discussion concluded with a call for decentralized models and privacy-enhancing technologies to allow data to be used without being shared centrally.

### Data Sharing and Privacy Governance

The group discussed data sharing and consent mechanisms, particularly focusing on selective disclosure and permission lists. They agreed that while data minimization is important, it alone won't be sufficient to address privacy concerns, as there will inevitably be significant data sharing in many interactions. Wenjing mentioned an FDA news report about potentially regulating AI chatbots as medical devices when providing psychological counseling, suggesting that governance measures may be needed beyond technical solutions.

### AI Healthcare Regulation Challenges

The group discussed the regulatory implications of AI in healthcare, particularly focusing on the FDA's approach to AI as medical devices. Jim shared insights from a previous AI risks document in healthcare and highlighted the FDA's challenges in keeping up with new AI technologies like generative AI. They also touched on the potential benefits of chatbot-driven models in counseling and psychology, including their ability to provide immediate support, capture data for longitudinal analysis, and offer neutral, problem-solving responses. The conversation concluded with Drummond emphasizing the clarity and complexity of healthcare problems as a testing ground for AI applications.

### AI Agent Privacy Regulation Framework

Drummond and Wenjing discussed privacy and data control issues related to AI agents, considering a model that aligns with the FDA's approach to regulating AI as medical devices. Wenjing suggested identifying the agents and the humans behind them to develop a governance and technical solution. Drummond agreed and shared insights from a recent workshop in Berlin, emphasizing the importance of verifiable identities and credentials for AI agents, similar to regulations in the healthcare system. They discussed the need to document and regulate AI interactions as if they were practitioners rather than medical devices.

### Healthcare Data Consent Framework

The group discussed a framework for data sharing and consent in healthcare, focusing on a layered approach that builds upon existing legal agreements and patient-doctor relationships. Jim highlighted the complexity of granular consent, noting that while it's an important concept, it can be challenging for patients to manage, especially for vulnerable populations. Wenjing and Drummond agreed that while the framework may not be perfect, it provides a coherent structure for addressing consent and data sharing issues, and they decided to explore this approach further while also considering other potential solutions. Drummond raised a question about how this framework aligns with the work of the DIF Trusted Agents Working Group, noting the potential for collaboration between the two efforts.

### Data Access Control Standards Discussion

The group discussed the importance of incorporating data access control and policy enforcement into technical standards, with Nicky emphasizing the need for machine-readable and machine-enforceable governance. Drummond acknowledged the significance of including policy terms in data sharing processes, comparing it to a "handshake and terms of engagement." The conversation also touched on the IEEE standard IEEE 7012, which Drummond referred to as "My Terms," and its potential application in healthcare scenarios, though he noted it might require adaptation for such contexts.

### Guardian Agents: AI Decision Support

The group discussed the concept of "guardian agents" as specialized AI entities that could support decision-making and provide trust metrics, with Nicky emphasizing the importance of decision support systems in various sectors. They agreed to reconvene after the upcoming symposium and before Thanksgiving, with plans to share diagrams and further develop their ideas.
