## Details

### TSP AI Agent Protocol Discussion

The meeting began with introductions, where Anton Sokolov from TIHI Institute in Tallinn, Estonia, joined as a first-time participant to discuss an issue filed on the TSP enabled AI agent protocol spec regarding attestation slot and failure semantics. Wenjing outlined the agenda, which focused on reviewing the draft specification and discussing its integration with MCP and A2A protocols, including a planned walkthrough of an example chart. The group also learned that Open Wallet Foundation is winding down and will merge with ARFDT, which will streamline the TSP SDK integration process.

### Open Wallet Foundation Priorities Discussion

Wenjing explained that Open Wallet Foundation has two distinct priorities: organizing the GDC event and open source software development, with these groups having different focuses and interests. Drummond agreed that separating GDC from the rest of OWF's projects makes sense, as GDC was not the original reason for starting the foundation. Wenjing indicated that while attending GDC is interesting, their primary focus remains on building durable systems, and they might join some working groups related to this effort.

### AI Developments and Safety Concerns

Wenjing discussed recent developments in AI, including the AI backlash and OpenAI's focus on privacy-first systems for monitoring AI misuse. She also mentioned the acquisition of Cursor by SpaceX and the development of Origin, an AI-integrated Git repository system. Wenjing noted that the recent incidents involving AI systems "going rogue" have helped people better understand the capabilities and potential dangers of AI, which validates their work and showcases the need for appropriate controls and protocols.

### TSP Progress and New Zealand Update

Drummond reported positive feedback from his visit to New Zealand regarding their interest in the decentralized trust graph work beyond digital wallets and credentials. Wenjing announced significant progress on TSP with Revision 3 PR addressing implementation issues, Caesar 2.0 finalization, and post-quantum algorithms, with only test vectors remaining before formal 1.0 publication by Trust of IP later this year. The team agreed to schedule a dedicated meeting to review TSP after GDC.

### TEA Protocol Architecture Discussion

Wenjing explained the TEA protocol, which operates on top of TSP and involves two main functions: delegation and invocation. She described how TSP handles authentication and privacy in the transport layer, while TEA uses ACDC as a data encoder to manage capabilities and obligations. Anton clarified that TSP authenticates and protects the exchange, ACDC carries authority, and MCP handles invocation.

### ACDC Capability and Obligation Structure

Wenjing explained the capability and obligation structure in ACDC, distinguishing between automatically enforced abrogations and delayed obligations with accountability mechanisms. She outlined the delegation and invocation processes, showing how agents can receive capabilities from issuers and access services through verification procedures tied to VIDs. The team discussed plans to create a minimal MCP over TSP profile, with Anton volunteering to lead this effort and help transform the current draft into a more consistent specification. The group agreed to explore fiduciary agents and verifiability in next week's meeting.
