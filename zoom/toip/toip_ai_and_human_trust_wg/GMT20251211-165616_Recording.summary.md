## Details

### AI Privacy System Development Discussion

Neil discussed a new AI disclosure system using Trust Over IP that would allow for layered and controlled sharing of personal information, including emergency contacts and medical data. Nick, who previously worked on Trust Over IP and recently completed a multi-million dollar project, joined the meeting to share his expertise on privacy-preserving agent registration protocols. Wenjing mentioned that attendees would need to review and agree to antitrust notices and IPR policies to fully participate in the working group, with individual membership being free.

### Team Planning and Project Updates

The team discussed upcoming meetings focused on planning and work prioritization, with Wenjing sharing that she prepared a spreadsheet to guide discussions on focusing efforts for the coming year. Nick introduced himself, highlighting his experience with the Atala project, trust over IP deployment in Africa, and work with Cardano, as well as his current role as chief product officer at ASI, part of SingularityNet. Wenjing also shared news about the Linux Foundation forming a new open-source foundation, Agendic AI, which will house projects like MCP and Agent MD, with contributions from OpenAI. The team expressed hope that this initiative would enhance transparency and facilitate progress on their work items related to MCP and TSP.

### MCP-I Integration and Authentication Planning

Neil discussed the interest in MCP-I (MCP plus Identity) following a presentation to the hospitality and travel special interest group. He mentioned that DIF is actively trying to get those groups to present on MCP-I in the new year and is working on setting up a single meeting to brief everyone on AI across different IP. Wenjing noted that MCP's current product uses SSI, contrary to the demo's use of ID and passwords. They briefly touched on MCP's ongoing work to integrate authentication into the platform and agreed to focus on planning for next year, with Wenjing preparing a spreadsheet to prioritize their efforts.

### AI Agent Protocol Development Proposal

Wenjing presented a proposal for two main projects related to AI agent protocols and Trust over IP (ToIP) stack. The first project involves developing a specification and implementation for running AI agent protocols over TSP, with plans to create a new specification and potentially implement it in Rust. The second project focuses on using the ToIP infrastructure for credential checking, authorization, and delegation protocols, with two interns already working on preliminary examples. Wenjing invited volunteers to contribute to the specification review, testing, and development of these projects.

### Aligning Work with DIF Group

The team discussed the differences between their work and the DIF group's approach, noting that while they are not using MCP or TSP, they are working on achieving similar effects through DECOM. Nicky emphasized the importance of completing their current work as a priority and suggested aligning their efforts with the DIF group's work to explore where TSP and DIDCOM might be complementary. Neil shared insights from the DIF group's use cases and discussions about authorization and authentication mechanisms, highlighting the potential role of agents in selective disclosure and cross-platform communication. The team recognized the need to consider patterns and services that could align across different ecosystems, with Neil expressing interest in exploring these concepts further.

### AI Initiatives Alignment Discussion

The group discussed the alignment of different AI initiatives, particularly focusing on trust, use cases, and patterns. Neil noted that while different groups may approach the problem from different angles, there is general agreement on key areas like agent-to-agent communication and delegation. Nicky highlighted the distinction between single-agent and multi-agent systems, particularly in Project Nanda. Wenjing explained their bottom-up approach to designing TSP, aiming to make it usable within the MCP community, and emphasized the complementary nature of different use cases and patterns. Neil shared insights from the hospitality and travel industry, noting that AI will likely emulate the current human chain and that SSI could provide a solution to the fragmented identity and credentialing issues in the industry.

### AI Hospitality and Security Insights

Neil and Wenjing discussed the interplay between hospitality and travel, noting its potential to drive patterns in AI agent interactions and delegation. Neil suggested monitoring the DIF working group's work to learn from their use cases and patterns. Nick shared his experience managing a military network, emphasizing the importance of security in multi-agent environments and the need for programmatically enforceable rules. He highlighted the potential for misuse of open-source decentralized agentic systems and suggested focusing on security in multi-agent environments to address these issues.

### Framework Integration and Security Challenges

Wenjing and Nick discussed the challenges of integrating a new framework into an existing environment, emphasizing the need for a simplified, hardened, and secure alternative. They highlighted the importance of incorporating trust registries and an AI notion for self-governance, as well as addressing issues related to human versus machine ownership. Wenjing proposed completing most of the specification by the first quarter and invited feedback to improve the current documentation. Nick suggested reviewing the specification to identify potential improvements and offered to assist as a reviewer.

### AI Trust and Governance Frameworks

The group discussed trust and governance in AI systems, focusing on the need to move governance policies from human-readable formats to machine-readable forms. Nicky emphasized that trust is primarily a human issue (80%) rather than technical (20%), and proposed exploring machine-readable governance policies alongside data catalogues and trust registries. Neil suggested involving Carly Heidema's work on data provenance and suggested exploring data disclosure mechanisms, while Makki highlighted the importance of understanding AI reasoning codes for agent interactions. The group agreed to continue their discussion in the next meeting to finalize the structure and content of their work, with a focus on relationships and trust frameworks.
