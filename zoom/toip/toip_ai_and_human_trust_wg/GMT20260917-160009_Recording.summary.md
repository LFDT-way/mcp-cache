## Details

### TRUST Policy and IPR Discussion

Wenjing led a meeting to discuss the TRUST (formerly TSP) policy and IPR policies, emphasizing the goal of making specifications and publications freely available without restrictions. The team reviewed a draft proposal and discussed concrete examples to validate the current specification approach. Wenjing sought feedback on the proposed framework and planned to continue refining the details for publication.

### Trust-Enabled Agents Evolution Presentation

Wenjing presented the evolution of trust-enabled agents (TEA) from TSP-enabled agents, explaining how TEA integrates TSP concepts with additional features like delegation, constraints, and obligations. She outlined a practical example of booking an airline ticket using a credit card with specific limitations, demonstrating how multiple authorities can delegate concurrent permissions to an agent. The team discussed comparing TEA's policy expression capabilities with existing policy languages like ODIL, with the goal of validating that TEA can express the same policies as these established languages.

### ODL to TEA Translation Process

Wenjing explained how policies expressed in ODL can be translated into TEA format for more verifiable implementation, inviting volunteers to test this translation process. Steve discussed the practical applications of policy packets within the VTA (Verifiable Trust Agent) framework, clarifying that VTA serves as a higher-layer application that can use TEA for delegation and policy enforcement. The discussion established that TEA-based delegation requires specific message formatting rather than direct API calls, creating a standardized way for applications to delegate authority to agents with attached constraints.

### TEA Application Integration Discussion

Wenjing explained the conceptual relationship between TEA and various applications like VTA, SEDA, and ODL, emphasizing how these entities can leverage TEA to express policies. The discussion covered examples such as terms and conditions, "my terms," and verifiable logs, with a focus on determining what should be logged and whether certain logging practices should be mandated. Wenjing noted that while TEA should serve diverse applications with varying logging needs, the specification could provide best practice guidance on accountability and logging methodologies.

### TEA System Logging Implementation

Wenjing and the team discussed the implementation of logging and accountability mechanisms in TEA systems. They clarified the distinction between liability and accountability, with accountability focused on provenance and error detection through verifiable logs that cannot be tampered with after creation. The discussion covered how agents can generate logs for both debugging purposes and potential dispute resolution, with logs being either local or shared depending on the use case. The team also addressed the difference between witnesses and attesters, with attesters being more reliable for proving actions like data deletions, though witnesses still serve a useful purpose in simpler verification scenarios.

### TEA Specification Verifiable Logs Discussion

Wenjing explained the capabilities of verifiable logs in the TEA specification, including the ability to add witnesses, testers, and corrections without deleting original entries. Steve raised a question about redaction capabilities, which Wenjing acknowledged as a requirement that needs further research to determine if it can be supported natively in the log system or if it requires additional mechanisms at higher levels. The discussion concluded with agreement that while the TEA specification provides guidance on log production and best practices, specific governance decisions about data sharing and privacy would need to be determined at the application level rather than in the specification itself.

### Care Protocols and Terminology Standards

The team discussed drawing lines and establishing protocols for care, with Wenjing and Steve noting similarities to existing work by Mitch. Sally raised questions about terminology standardization and requested access to documentation to better understand and contribute to the definitions. The group agreed to review the draft spec and discuss terminology in more detail during the next meeting, with Sally needing to sign the member agreement to access the Trust over IP wiki.
