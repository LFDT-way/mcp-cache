### Traveler Data Management Solutions

Neil discussed the challenges and potential solutions for managing traveler data in the travel industry. He highlighted the issue of stale data and proposed a system where travelers share only the necessary information on a short-term basis, using tokens for future access. Neil also introduced the concept of micro-sharing, where travelers can share specific details like emergency contacts or medical information without disclosing their entire profile. He noted that this approach reduces exposure to personal identifiable information (PII) and provides accurate, up-to-date information to service providers. Neil mentioned that some travel insurance companies, like Chubb, have indicated that storing large amounts of PII could lead to higher cybersecurity insurance premiums.

### Data Sharing and Privacy Strategies

Neil and Wenjing discussed data sharing practices, particularly in the travel industry. They explored the concept of micro-sharing, where only necessary data is shared, and the use of federated queries to access specific information without transferring entire databases. Neil explained that moving personal data and models to client-side storage can improve privacy and security. They also discussed the challenges of balancing data privacy with the need for personalized services, and the use of depersonalized queries to conduct market surveys.

### Data Privacy and Relationship Management

The group discussed data management and AI agent relationships, with Neil explaining how data can be accessed in real-time through federated queries without being stored locally, and emphasizing encryption options for proprietary information. Nicky proposed adding constraints, consent, and relationship management to their scope, suggesting a model similar to the theory of change that includes goals, behavior, and outcomes. The discussion highlighted tensions between data sharing for relationship management versus privacy concerns, with Drummond emphasizing the importance of trust in how data is used by relationship providers. Jim noted the rapid pace of Trusted AI Agent discussions and raised concerns about policy-based access controls potentially creating audit logs of personal information sharing.

### AI for Policy Management Challenges

The group discussed challenges with policy control and management in decentralized ecosystems, particularly the limitations of machine-readable policies and their execution. Nicky shared insights from academic papers on using AI and large language models to support policy coherence and semantic interoperability, suggesting opportunities for AI to help manage complex policies. The discussion explored how AI could be used to enhance trust and governance in technical systems, with Nicky mentioning potential collaboration with compliance platforms like Inclusive. Drummond suggested exploring the concept of Guardian Agents, AI advisors that help enforce and advise on policies, as a topic for a future call.

### Browser AI Security Vulnerabilities

The group discussed potential security vulnerabilities in browser-based AI agents, particularly focusing on a technique demonstrated by LayerX that allows malicious code injection into ChatGPT through browser memory. Neil suggested using a middleman AI to manage selective disclosures and minimize exposure, while Wenjing highlighted that digital IDs alone are insufficient to protect against these types of attacks. The discussion concluded with an acknowledgment that this is a long-term known problem, with the WCC having a working group on similar topics, and the meeting was scheduled to continue next week with participants encouraged to share references in the Discord channel.

### AI Security and Memory Protection

The group discussed security concerns around AI agents and memory modification, with Wenjing and Steven exploring how malicious actors could potentially compromise an agent's memory even in distributed systems. They agreed that while trusted execution environments offer some protection, they cannot currently handle the entire agent execution, and Jim noted the need to carefully manage communication between agents and trusted environments. The group decided to continue exploring these topics in next week's meeting, with Wenjing planning to add notes and welcome others to help edit them.
