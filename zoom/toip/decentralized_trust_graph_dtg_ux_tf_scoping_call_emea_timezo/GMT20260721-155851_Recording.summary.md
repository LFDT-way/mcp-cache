### Human Trust Experience Framework

Daniel presented a framework for human trust experience (HTX) organized into six areas: trust formation, representation, explainability, control, evolution, and interaction. He mapped these vertical domains against horizontal axes including functional and ceremonial aspects, identifying potential patterns and best practices for trust-related tasks and ceremonies. The discussion concluded with potential deliverables including a user ceremony library, developer experience guidelines, and a Trust Signal API, with emphasis on creating reusable building blocks for different stakeholders.

### Decentralized Trust Graph Implementation Strategies

The group discussed approaches for understanding and implementing the Decentralized Trust Graph (DTG), focusing on practical use cases to inform development. Carly suggested starting with a real-world example, such as the Linux Foundation's key signing initiative, to better understand implementation challenges and user experience. The team agreed to explore user journeys for key use cases, including the Linux Foundation's key ceremony, a fictional content creation platform, and potentially travel/hospitality scenarios. They decided to reach out to experts like Jeff Turk and the Harvard Applied Social Media Lab team for insights and to gather best practices, particularly around accessibility and UI design. The group also planned to use a shared repository to document findings, user journeys, and outputs, with a focus on iterating through examples to refine the DTG specification.
