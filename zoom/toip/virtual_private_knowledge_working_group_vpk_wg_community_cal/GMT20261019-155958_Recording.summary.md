## Agenda

An open call for people interested in the Virtual Private Knowledge Working Group (VPK WG). 

## Overview

The team held a Monday morning meeting to discuss updates on the VPK (Vector Privacy Kit) protocol development, with Reza leading the discussion. Darren reported on his work with LibP2P bindings for Trust Tasks, noting potential crossover with the VPK protocol structure, and agreed to connect with Wenjing about the Trust Spanning Protocol (TSP) implementation. Sulimon provided an update on optimizing CKKS homomorphic encryption algorithm, explaining that through new optimizations they reduced the performance impact from 6,000 times slower to only 16-17 times slower than regular processing, making it a viable cryptographic solution for their RAG implementation. The team also discussed flipping the narrative from user-focused security to vendor-focused liability protection, where service providers could require customers to use VPK encryption to indemnify themselves from data liability.

## Details

### LibP2P and CKKS Updates

Reza discussed updates on two main topics: LibP2P bindings for Truster and Sulimon's latest research. Regarding LibP2P bindings, Darren will be learning about the protocol structure to apply similar principles to the VPK protocol implementation. Reza also mentioned that Sulimon would present findings on the optimization of CKKS, one of the key components, at a later time.

### Homomorphic Encryption and TSP Discussion

The team discussed homomorphic encryption algorithms and the Trust Spanning Protocol (TSP). Steve mentioned that Wenjing planned to reach out to Darren to discuss TSP implementation versus the paper itself. Darren agreed to join the Trusted RIP Discord server and suggested starting communication via email. Darren explained his research into libp2p bindings for trust tasks and expressed interest in potentially utilizing TSP for VPK implementation, though he needed to clarify the current state of TSP's transport layer capabilities.

### Protocol Task Force Progress Update

Reza provided an update on protocol progress, noting that two out of three planned task forces have been initiated. The third task force, focused on call for proposals for algorithms inside the VPK, is pending. Reza mentioned previous work on dimensional scrambling and noise algorithms, as well as two new algorithms from a workshop with the Society of Industrial Applied Mathematics, and highlighted CKKS as a potential cryptographic library option.

### CKKS Algorithm Implementation Update

Solomon provided an update on the implementation of CKKS algorithm for homomorphic encryption in RAG, explaining that after optimization, it is now only 16-17 times slower than regular queries rather than 6,000 times slower initially. The team has made the repository public and is working on benchmarking different algorithms and parameters. Solomon also mentioned measuring the security benefits of noise injection using the Vec2Text attack, which will be reported in more detail next week. Steve asked if this solution remains overkill for average users, though this question was not fully addressed in the transcript.

### System Security and Performance Discussion

Steve and Reza discussed the security and performance considerations of their system, with Steve expressing concerns about the cost of compute resources versus the value of security. Reza explained that embedding takes between three and six microseconds per vector and could be further optimized using SIMD architecture. The discussion touched on the balance between academic requirements for publication and practical consumer product needs, with Reza suggesting they could compromise on security for an embedded system implementation while focusing on getting the paper published first.

### Data Liability Strategy Discussion

Reza discussed a strategy to help companies indemnify themselves from liability by requiring data scrambling and using VPK with no keys provided, which Scott Bly described as flipping the narrative. The team agreed to limit future calls to 30 minutes if all topics are covered, with specific action items assigned including Darren speaking with Wenjin about protocols and potentially Glenn Gore regarding libP2P bindings, Sulimon presenting CKKS optimization findings next week, and Reza working on developing the narrative flipping strategy for go-to-market purposes.
