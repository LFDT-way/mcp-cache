## Details

### Virtual Private Knowledge Work Group Kickoff

The meeting served as a kickoff for the Virtual Private Knowledge work group under the Linux Foundation Trust Over IP Foundation. Reza, the chair, led the meeting and outlined plans to develop founding documents, adopt bylaws, and establish task forces. The group discussed using templates from the Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust working group and planned to define three task forces with designated owners. Participants introduced themselves, and Reza mentioned the involvement of notable members like Bruce Schneier and Mitch Travis in the initiative.

### VPK Privacy Working Group Formation

Reza and Mitchell discussed the formation of a working group focused on developing a privacy solution called VPK (a firewall-like network component) that would protect personal and company data when engaging with third-party AI services. They clarified that the project should use "partial homomorphic encryption" rather than "homomorphic encryption" terminology, and emphasized they are not aiming to solve generalized confidential compute but rather focusing on protecting data privacy when using untrusted third-party services. The group identified key participants including Reza, Mitchell, Solomon from Kauai, and Tom Joy as co-chairs, with SingularityNet and Kauai as founding members.

### VPK Protocol Work Group Formation

Reza led a meeting to discuss the formation of a work group focused on developing the VPK protocol specification and related security measures. The group introduced themselves, including Steve Vidka as head of policy at Kwai, Scott Bly as a post-quantum cryptography consultant, and John Vaccaro as the infrastructure plumber at Kwai. Reza explained that the VPK protocol addresses security concerns around knowledge bases by focusing on protecting data at rest rather than just securing communications between parties, particularly highlighting risks when sensitive data is outsourced to third-party service providers like SAM. The work group plans to establish three task forces: developing the VPK protocol specification, creating custom personal embedding methods, and potentially conducting a NIST-style call for proposals to identify suitable algorithms that meet the protocol's requirements.

### VPK Development and Security Approach

The team discussed the development of VPK (Vector Privacy for Knowledge), focusing on securing knowledge bases using partial homomorphic encryption algorithms rather than achieving perfect security. Reza explained that the approach aims to make attacks economically unfeasible by limiting data exposure and requiring significant collusion to compromise multiple systems. The group agreed to update their white paper, establish GitHub repositories under Linux Foundation governance, and develop automated tools for Slack and GitHub discussions. Mitchell presented a visualization of their approach using a torus-based geometry to represent knowledge pathways, which could help obfuscate data access patterns and enhance security.
