Darrell proposed presenting an IRA cards use case to demonstrate the Trust over IP stack's business, governance, and technical aspects. Darrell and Drummond discussed organizing a presentation on Trust over IP, deciding to split it into two parts: a 30-minute talk on the protocol's role and importance, followed by a separate presentation on the IRA implementation. Drummond suggested packaging the content for reuse over 6-12 months, emphasizing the need for diverse voices in the first presentation while Darrell would handle the IRA segment. They agreed to involve task force members and other participants in the first part, with Drummond offering to assist.

### Trust Registry Protocol Implementation Discussion

Darrell discussed the Trust Registry Query Protocol V2 implementation with Fabrice, who expressed interest but noted limited availability due to a busy schedule. Carly and Drummond offered to assist, with Carly focusing on creating educational content and Drummond emphasizing the importance of involving ecosystem and governance perspectives. Darrell highlighted the connection between the Trust Registry and governance, stating that it serves as the technical manifestation of governance in managing credential types within the ecosystem.

### TrustRVP Presentation Planning Discussion

Darrell and Drummond discussed plans for an upcoming presentation, agreeing to script it with 3-4 speakers and allocate time for Q&A. They considered including John Phillips from the UN Global Trust Registry Project to provide a different perspective. The group also talked about the need for one of the chairs to step down as they move to public review 2, and Drummond mentioned that a binding for TrustRVP was successfully created in just 2 hours by the Open Wallet Foundation development team.

### DID Authentication for Research Data

Carly and Drummond discussed the use of DIDs (Decentralized Identifiers) in a research data ecosystem. They explored how DIDs could be used for authentication and authorization, with Carly suggesting a federated login approach using OpenID or similar protocols. Drummond explained the importance of separating the DID from the authorization, using verifiable credentials (VCs) to prove access rights. They agreed that the most efficient way to handle access would be for the VC granting access to be signed by the dataset owner, with Drummond presenting the VC and signing it with his private key to prove his identity and access rights.

### Decentralized Identity Systems Discussion

Carly and Drummond discussed the advantages of decentralized identity systems, particularly how they enable more flexible authentication and authorization compared to traditional OAuth systems. Drummond shared a blog post by Tim Bauma that explains why OAuth is no longer supported in favor of the new decentralized identity paradigm. They also agreed to update the channel list for an upcoming symposium, with Carly suggesting they should gather feedback from participants to determine which channels are actually needed.
