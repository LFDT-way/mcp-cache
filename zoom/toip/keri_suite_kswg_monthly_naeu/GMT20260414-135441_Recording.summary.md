### KERI Development Progress Update

The team discussed the status of a draft document that Kent and Jonathan had completed, which is now ready for review by Samuel and Phil. They agreed to delay the technical vote until the next DIDWEBS meeting in May due to timing constraints around an upcoming conference. Regarding KERI development, significant progress was reported including completion of technical debt, implementation of read-the-docs functionality, and preliminary support for IndexDB in WASM environments, which allows running the KERI library without porting code to another language.

### CRAM Authentication Implementation Discussion

The team discussed the implementation of CRAM, a non-interactive replay attack authentication mechanism that provides protection at scale with better performance than interactive mechanisms. Samuel explained that CRAM will be integrated into the parser to handle authentication and filtering at the appropriate protocol layer. The team also covered recent updates to KERI and Signify TS, including a patch release (040) of KERI due to issues with LMDB upgrades, with plans for additional patch releases (041) and a new release of Signify TS (040).

### Private Keys vs Shared Secrets

The team discussed the limitations of shared secrets in authentication and the advantages of private keys with digital signatures, particularly in light of emerging AI security threats. Samuel and Phil explained how protocols like TLS, Signal, VPNs, and OAuth rely on shared secrets, making them vulnerable to attacks, especially with the advent of tools like Mythos. They emphasized that systems based on KERI and ACDC, which use append-only key event logs and anchor non-ephemeral issuances, offer more secure and privacy-preserving solutions compared to approaches relying on ephemeral identity or tokens. The group also touched on the importance of regulatory measures, like the duty of data loyalty in Utah, to ensure privacy and security. They noted that while privacy mechanisms like zero-knowledge proofs provide some unlinkability, correlation is inevitable, and the focus should be on minimizing exploitable correlation. The team agreed to revisit the topic of Cram in a future meeting.
