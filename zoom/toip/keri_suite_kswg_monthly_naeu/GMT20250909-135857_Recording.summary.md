## Details

### Keri Seat Working Group Meeting

The Keri Seat Working Group meeting welcomed George Chan from Hong Kong as a new member. Samuel, the chair, reminded attendees about the antitrust policy and membership agreements. The group discussed the status of the Keri urn application, which Samuel noted had not yet been sent out. Kevin agreed to create the meeting page on the new Confluence platform. The meeting began with introductions, with participants including Kent, Fergal, Daniel Hardman, and Henk, who is also the chairman of the Keri Foundation.

### Action Items and Project Updates

The meeting focused on reviewing action items and discussing updates on various tasks. Kevin confirmed that the previous action items were copied over to the new live document, and Samuel noted that Jonathan Ryback volunteered to review the ABF for the DID Web Spec but hasn't completed it yet. Kent agreed to follow up with Jonathan. The group discussed the status of the joint credential issuance spec, with Daniel indicating it was not a priority at this moment but would be revisited later. They also reviewed the progress on fixing T-refs and X-refs for Hank, and confirmed that the DID Web S link was added to the Keri repo. Fergal announced the completion and open-sourcing of a Java implementation of Signify, and Samuel reminded everyone about the upcoming IETF meeting in October.

### Keri Pi Updates and Features

The team discussed recent updates to Keri Pi, including the addition of working example unit tests and support for blindable state tell in classes. Samuel explained new features for ACDCs, such as the compactor class for generating most compact SEDs and support for graduated disclosure mechanisms. Daniel mentioned Provenance's interest in using the compaction feature in the rules layer. The team also discussed native Caesar support for all Keri and ACDc messages, offering more compact serialization options. Fergal reported on updates to OpenAPI specs for easier generation of types in TypeScript and Java, using Python type hints as input.

### DID Resolver and Method Updates

The team discussed updates on various projects, including the creation of an issue for type hints and the resolution of issues in the DID Web S resolver. Kent mentioned that the universal resolver is now working again, but there is one outstanding issue regarding the DID carrier representation in the also-known-as field. Samuel inquired about the progress of the DID method qualification for secure DID, and Kevin clarified that Jonathan, one of the chairs of the working group, is actively involved. The team also discussed an upcoming presentation by Switch Cord at the Keri Foundation on September 28th.

### Telecom Dossier Specification Discussion

The group discussed creating a specification for "dossiers," which are bags of evidence used in telecom verification. Daniel proposed writing a spec to formalize the technique and terminology, particularly around how to handle evidence that isn't in ACDC format. Samuel suggested calling it an implementation guideline rather than a spec, as it would fall between the broad ACDC specifications and the detailed ecosystem governance frameworks. Kent noted that this work aligns with interoperability efforts, particularly regarding DID methods and integrating non-ACDC evidence with ACDCs. The task force would be led by Daniel, with potential contributions from other community members.

### Task Force Formation and Spec Updates

The group discussed forming a task force to address recurring questions and agreed to tentatively approve it, with Samuel planning to send an email to the Keri Stack working group mailing list to announce a vote in October. They reviewed the progress on specs, noting that examples and glossaries have been updated, and set a target to finalize the specs as Trust over IP specs by the end of the year. Kent proposed dedicating part of the next DID Web S meeting to discuss the dossier task force, and Daniel confirmed his availability to attend.
