## Details

### Trust Registry Task Force Updates

John and Sankarshan discussed Sankarshan's recent writing about attribute selection in schemas and identity, which John found interesting. ### GDC September Deliverable Scope Discussion

Darrell discussed the scope of the GDC September deliverable, which focuses on government-issued documents from an issuer perspective and the verifier-relying party side. He emphasized that the work should not boil the ocean and should remain within specific boundaries. John noted that Steve Capel, despite being isolated from earlier discussions, has brought novel applications to the table within the bounds of the standard for trade.

### Supply Chain Verification Discussion

John and Darrell discussed the use of temporal queries and identity resolution in the context of supply chain verification, particularly focusing on how to verify credentials and data over time. They explored the distinction between registry and directory terminology, with John explaining that "directory" was chosen to avoid confusion with registrar terminology. The conversation touched on the challenges of implementing supply chain verification, including the need to trust data without revealing supplier information, and mentioned the involvement of companies like Apple, Microsoft, and Amazon in the Responsible Business Alliance.

### UNCP Implementation and Trust Registry

John and Darrell discussed the implementation of UNCP by the Responsible Business Alliance, with John explaining that major technology companies have created this alliance as a vehicle for sustainable development goals. Darrell shared his experience with the GDC Trust Registry Task Force, where he is advocating for broader scope beyond just relying party devices to include offline capabilities similar to earlier Trust Registry Protocol versions. The conversation also touched on market adoption vectors, particularly regarding age verification services like Yoti's success in France despite legal challenges in Spain.

### NATA's Role in Testing Infrastructure

John and Darrell discussed the role of organizations like NATA in Australia's testing and standards infrastructure, highlighting how NATA functions as a trust anchor for trade through its government recognition and Global ACI certification. They explored how these bodies operate independently of the UN, with authority deriving from government recognition and international bodies rather than UN sovereignty. The conversation emphasized that while the UN can be an effective solution for certain recognition functions, sovereign nations maintain the right to operate independent systems, as illustrated by the US maintaining its own passport list despite ICAO's global framework.

### UN Grid Directory Development

John discussed the development of a lightweight, self-sustaining directory called "grid" that would be run by the UN similar to ICAO PKD, costing participating countries around $20,000 USD annually. He explained that the grid would function as a metadata repository for trust registries rather than caching their data, allowing users to discover organizational registrars for specific countries while directing them to the source of truth for the actual registry information. John demonstrated the current sandbox version of the grid table which shows various registry data including UN Member State codes, registrar names, and public keys, with the ability to issue DIA (Digital Identity Anchor) verifiable credentials for each entry.

### Grid Pilot Project Progress Update

John discussed the progress of the Grid pilot project, announcing that Alina will be responsible for grid entry in Spain, with support from John and Marcus Allande who is working with Brazil, Colombia, and Uruguay on verifiable credentials implementation. The team discussed the addition of a query protocol to the Grid, with Darrell confirming that TRQP support would address this need. John reported that 5 of 7 UN stakeholders have approved moving the Grid to public review, with a July 21 deadline for final decisions, and the group explored potential collaboration opportunities with Global ACI and discussed concerns about platform-dependent credential aggregation versus independent verification systems.
