### OpenVTC Decentralized Identity Framework

The meeting focused on a demonstration of OpenVTC, a new decentralized identity and trust framework being developed as a potential replacement for current PGP systems. The presenters explained the concept of Verifiable Trust Networks (VTNs) and Verifiable Trust Communities (VTCs), which allow individuals and AI agents to maintain control over their digital identities through a graph-based system of relationships and credentials. They emphasized that this system is built on top of existing identity frameworks rather than replacing them, and highlighted key features including trust tasks, cryptographic task management, and policy-driven approval processes. The team has been rapidly developing the code alongside specifications over the past six months, with over 4,000 commits across 24 repositories, and will be presenting their work at the upcoming Linux Plumbers Conference in Prague.

### VTI System Overview Presentation

Drummond explained the VTI (Verifiable Trust Infrastructure) system, focusing on its core components including VTAs (Verifiable Trust Agents), trust contexts, and the verifiable trust community concept. He detailed how DIDs (Decentralized Identifiers) serve as unique identifiers, hold public key infrastructure, and define service endpoints, highlighting DID WebVH as the preferred method due to its durability and performance. Drummond also discussed the transport mechanisms supported by VTI, including DIDCOM, TSP, and REST, explaining the weighted matrix for protocol negotiation and the system's ability to work across different network environments through messaging relays.

### Linux Foundation Credential System Demo

Drummond demonstrated a browser plugin using native messaging protocols and discussed their work on modeling the Linux Foundation's credential system, which resulted in a 600-megabyte graph representing 8,300 entities and 18,000 credentials. He explained three community types: public, pairwise, and hybrid models, and outlined the current join ceremony process using OpenVTC, VTC, and VTA tools for community verification and membership credentials. The demonstration also covered Git integration for code signing and verification, device linking capabilities, and AI agent integration through MCP interfaces, with Drummond preparing to show a live demo of OpenVTC.

### Personal Network Manager Demonstration

Drummond demonstrated a new profile setup process using the Personal Network Manager tool to connect to a VTA running in Ireland, showing how ephemeral keys and trust contexts are created during the connection process. He then walked through joining a verifiable trust community (VTC), explaining how the system handles membership credentials and trust graphs without requiring pre-existing invitation credentials. The discussion concluded with an exploration of how trust graphs work in community membership, clarifying that while communities can choose to expose relationship information, this is under community control and not required by default.

### Vouching System Implementation Discussion

Drummond and Daniel discussed the implementation of a Vouching system using ZKPs and Linux Foundation PGP Web of Trust rules, focusing on how membership and community relationships are managed within the platform. Glenn demonstrated a live VTA (Verifiable Trust Agent) and VTC (Verifiable Trust Community) setup, showcasing features like creating DIDs, invitation credentials, and managing community access through a Kubernetes-based deployment. The team highlighted the ease of setting up a VTA using a Kubernetes pod and discussed the use of Hetzner and Cloudflare for infrastructure, with Terraform (or OpenTerraform) used for orchestration.

### VTA Cluster Setup Discussion

Drummond and Vincent discussed the setup process for their VTA cluster, which takes approximately 30 minutes including waiting for the computers to stabilize, with actual keyboard time being less than 5 minutes. The hosted VTA currently lacks a TEE (Trusted Execution Environment) but this is considered for future development. The cluster costs around $30-35 US per month and can comfortably handle a couple hundred VTAs, with each VTA costing approximately 30 cents per month. After the technical discussion, the meeting took a break for coffee, and later included a conversation about legal and fiduciary responsibilities in digital services, where a participant explained their work with the Gleanet Alliance on developing digital fiduciary standards.

### Privacy-Preserving Biometrics Technology Discussion

Drummond presented his work on privacy-preserving biometrics, explaining how his company developed technology for anonymous human authentication that was deployed by Meta for account recovery. The discussion revealed Meta's plans for Ray-Ban smart glasses using similar technology, though the public narrative around this has been negative due to privacy concerns. The conversation also covered personal backgrounds, with Drummond sharing his experience in product strategy and innovation leadership, while others discussed their roles in the company and recent travel experiences.

### Decentralized Trust Graph Project Update

Drummond provided an update on the Decentralized Trust Graph project, highlighting significant progress since its public kickoff at a First Person Project meetup a year ago. The project has grown from 6 initial participants to approximately 30 attendees meeting weekly, with 7 active task forces working on specifications and infrastructure. Two new trust stacks are being developed in parallel - an Open VTC stack and a P2P Trust stack led by Erica and Shannon, both former Wikipedia alumni, with plans to present at an upcoming IWW meeting in 2-3 weeks. The project has achieved working draft specifications for two deliverables and is working toward interoperability between the different trust stacks.

### VTN Project Progress Update

Drummond provided an update on the first-person cooperative and VTN (Verifiable Trust Network) project, highlighting recent progress including the discovery of a crowdfunding app that can be integrated into VTA. He explained that the project has evolved from planning a single cooperative to enabling multiple VTNs, with Colorado's Limited Cooperative Association (LCA) law chosen as the most suitable structure. The discussion revealed plans to instantiate the first VTNs in the coming year, with potential anchor tenants including the Linux Foundation and interest from New Zealand's Maori community for sovereign infrastructure.

### Verifiable Trust Networks Implementation Models

Drummond discussed various models for implementing Verifiable Trust Networks (VTNs), including using cities as starting points and the concept of founder circles where each founder recruits six others to build trust networks organically. He outlined upcoming milestones including an alternative AI stack conference, Green Build Conference, and several identity-related events in the next 90 days. The group clarified questions about trust registries and their relationship to trust networks, with Drummond explaining that trust registries serve as the "DNS for trust" and are essential for maintaining accurate credential information across networks.
