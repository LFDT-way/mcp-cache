## Details

### PKI vs DIDs: Key Differences

Carly and Daniel discussed the differences between traditional Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) and Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs). Daniel explained that PKI combines technical key infrastructure with identities, whereas DIDs separate identifiers from keys, requiring additional trust signals to associate identity with the identifier. Carly shared her developing script on comparing traditional PKI and certificate authorities to DIDs, aiming to understand and explain these differences.

### Trust Protocols and Certificate Authorities

Daniel and Carly discussed the development of trust protocols and the role of certificate authorities in securing web traffic. They explored how trust registries can be used to manage credentials issued by different entities, such as the Department of State, and the need for a verified issuer certification authority list for mobile driving licenses. Carly mentioned her work on multisig for keys, key pre-rotation, and decentralized identifiers (DIDs), emphasizing the importance of trust registries in verifying the authenticity of DIDs and the entities behind them.

### Digital Identity Verification Challenges

Daniel and Carly discussed the challenges of verifying identity through digital credentials and biometrics. Daniel highlighted that while digital credentials can be legitimate, they can also be presented by someone else unless there is a binding check to the presenter's identity, which biometrics traditionally provide. Carly acknowledged this limitation in her current system, which binds credentials to a governance authority but not directly to individuals. They also touched on the EU's preference for PINs over biometrics and the Estonian identity card's use of cryptographic machines and PINs for secure voting.

### Biometric Authentication: Challenges and Concerns

Daniel and Carly discussed the limitations of PIN-based authentication and the challenges with biometric systems, such as fingerprint and facial recognition, which can be unreliable or invasive. Carly expressed concerns about biometrics being used for mass surveillance, while Daniel noted that companies claim to store only templates and not personal data. They agreed that biometric binding might not be necessary for low-stakes activities like buying a drink but could be problematic in other contexts, especially with changes in government. Daniel also mentioned that, in the context of decentralized identifiers (DIDs), it is important to understand that you do not have a single DID.

### Digital Identity Security Explained

Daniel and Carly discussed the importance of privacy and security in digital identity systems, focusing on the use of different DIDs (Decentralized Identifiers) to prevent tracking and ensure that only the rightful owner can prove control over their digital identity. They explored how DIDCOM messages, which are cryptographically signed, help verify the sender's identity and prevent impersonation. Daniel explained that in systems like Fido, the first interaction involves registering a DID, and subsequent interactions use new DIDs generated with the private key, ensuring that only the rightful owner can create these DIDs.

### Differences Between Fido and DIDs

Carly explained the differences between Fido authentication and DIDs, highlighting that with Fido, users broadcast their password or hash over the internet, while with DIDs, users store their public key in a decentralized identifier (DID) and only share their DID with service providers. Carly noted that rotating keys in Fido systems requires re-registration, whereas with DIDs, users can update their public keys without needing to cancel and recreate their DID.

### Trust Spanning Protocol Encryption Discussion

Carly and Daniel discussed the concept of sending the same DID over and over again, as well as the idea of chaining and renegotiation. Carly explained that in the Trust Spanning Protocol, every message is encrypted with a new symmetric key to address concerns about post-quantum decryption. They also touched on the Bite Size Trust's other videos as a helpful resource.
