### Trust over IP Specification Clarification

Darrell led a discussion on Trust over IP specifications, focusing on clarifying the distinction between ecosystem governance frameworks and trust registries. Alex demonstrated a UI implementation that showed how queries are made against ecosystems rather than governance frameworks, which helped resolve concerns about the specification's abstraction levels. The group agreed to revise terminology from "controlling party" to "governing authority" and to simplify the specification by focusing on ecosystem-based queries rather than trust registry IDs.

### Trust Ecosystems and Protocol Design

Darrell and Scott discussed the complexities of trust registries and ecosystems, focusing on the relationship between governing authorities and their participants. Scott emphasized the need to clearly define the trust relationship between ecosystems, particularly in the context of machine communication and off-band negotiations. They agreed that the protocol should facilitate one-way trust transactions between ecosystems, with the query protocol serving as a mechanism for obtaining trust information.

### Trust Registry Protocol Discussion

Scott and Darrell discussed the process of trusting a trust registry and the need for a protocol to facilitate this trust. They emphasized that trust registries should be machine-readable and focused on the interaction with the trust registry rather than reviewing the trust program itself. Scott shared his work on creating a protocol for relying on industry-developed trust lists within the CTPA ecosystem. They also discussed the two types of queries to a trust registry: authorization to check if an entity is on the trust list, and recognition to establish trust in the registry itself. The main question they addressed was whether to use the trust registry ID or ecosystem ID when querying the endpoint, with Darrell suggesting that using the trust registry ID is the most straightforward approach.

### Trust Registries and Assertion IDs

Scott and Darrell discussed trust registries and assertion IDs. They clarified that trust registries represent separate systems of record, including timestamp authorities, CAs, and conforming products lists. Darrell explained that assertion IDs are strings used to query authorization questions, and suggested that ecosystems could define their own string formats. Scott proposed showing Darrell the contents of the trust registries to determine if any additional statements were needed. They agreed that the protocol they were developing would focus on the questions being asked, rather than how the answers are generated, with IRA members working on defining assertion ID values and namespaces.

### C2PA Trust Registry Protocol Discussion

Darrell and Scott discussed the C2PA Trust registry protocol, with Darrell emphasizing that the protocol can accommodate new marketplace elements and suggesting they could review it separately rather than during the current meeting. They agreed that the protocol should be implemented as a federated process rather than a hierarchical one, with Darrell explaining that it serves to bridge between systems of record and C2PA authorities. The discussion concluded with Darrell noting they had limited time remaining to address a major item raised by Alex in recent weeks.

### TRQP Documentation Clarity Discussion

Darrell and Alex discussed the need for clarity in the Trust Registry Query Protocol (TRQP) regarding the use of terms like Authority ID, Entity ID, and ecosystem ID to avoid confusion. They agreed that while the protocol defines the API structure, concrete examples and additional documentation are necessary to better explain its implementation. Darrell planned to meet with Alex to further discuss the protocol's scope and potential for a metadata protocol to handle rights and capabilities within ecosystems.
