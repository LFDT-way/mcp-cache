## Overview

The team discussed updates and changes to the TrustRAP project, focusing on competition-friendly policies and clarifying various identifiers and specifications. They reviewed procedural questions about document publication and addressed action items from the previous meeting, including discussions about park models and assertion IDs. The group agreed to simplify the trust registry model by removing certain parameters and focusing on ecosystem governing authorities, with plans to draft a new specification using the Park model for review at an upcoming symposium.

## Details

### TrustRAP Project Policy Updates

Darrell, Makki, Drummond, and Scott discussed updates on the TrustRAP project, focusing on competition-friendly policies and clarifying the distinction between trust registry IDs and ecosystem IDs. They addressed the issue of claim IDs and the addition of a locator object in the context object, with Alex agreeing to the changes. Darrell mentioned a pending PR that was nuked and plans to resubmit it. The group also discussed the need to deprecate the V1 TRP and move forward with the new specifications.

### Document Publication and Version Control

The task force discussed procedural questions about document publication, with Darrell and Drummond agreeing that final decisions should go to the working group while task forces prepare documents for review. They reviewed three action items from the previous meeting, including a discussion about park models and changes to assertion IDs, which Drummond and Oren had weighed in on. The group noted that one company was fixated on the current specifications, prompting the need to clarify that the new version supersedes previous versions.

### Trust Registry ID Replacement Proposal

Darrell proposed removing the Trust Registry ID and replacing it with ecosystem ID, action, and resource components to improve clarity and reduce confusion in the protocol design. He argued that the ecosystem context is sufficient for authorization questions and that the proposed changes would eliminate the need for ABNF concepts. Drummond agreed with the changes, and Darrell planned to update the documentation accordingly.

### Park Model Specification Revision Plan

The team discussed shifting to adopt the park model, which involves cleaning up assertion to focus on action and resource. Drummond and Darrell agreed to work on drafting a revised version of the specification, with the goal of having it ready to discuss at the upcoming IW conference. They plan to create a Google Doc for collaboration and review, with the help of Andor who was unable to attend the meeting. The team also decided to leverage existing documentation of the park model to simplify their own process.

### Ecosystem Trust Registry Framework Discussion

Darrell and Drummond discussed the role of ecosystem and trust registry identifiers in their framework. Darrell emphasized that the ecosystem ID should be used to connect directly to a trust registry, making the operational details opaque to the consumer. Drummond explained that the second diagram represented a use case where a trust registry could provide information about ecosystems it is authoritative for. They agreed that while Darrell's premise about ecosystem ID was valid, there was value in considering the multi-tenant aspect and profiles in their specifications.

### Trust Registry Query Protocol Discussion

Darrell and Drummond discussed the Trust Registry Query Protocol (TRQP), focusing on authorization and recognition aspects. They explored how metadata, lookups, entities, and ecosystems contribute to trust registry interactions, emphasizing the distinction between querying and discovery processes. Darrell highlighted the goal of the IRA project to quickly iterate and refine these processes, potentially leading to changes in the TRQP. They also discussed the need for more information about trust registries, such as supported actions, resources, and assurance levels. The conversation concluded with a question about how trust registries are discovered and connected to, emphasizing the importance of obtaining DIDs to initiate interactions.

### TRQP Protocol Authorization Integration

Darrell and Drummond discussed the integration of trust registry authorization and discovery capabilities within the TRQP protocol. They agreed that while the protocol should support basic authorization queries, complex traversal and discovery operations should be handled separately in Turkle. Drummond acknowledged that while the protocol could potentially include reverse lookup capabilities, they decided to simplify the specification by focusing primarily on authorization queries rather than comprehensive registry discovery.

### Trust Registry Authorization Simplification

Drummond and Darrell discussed simplifying the trust registry authorization diagram by treating it as a resource, potentially eliminating the need for a separate diagram. They agreed that the trust registry could be identified by a URL or a DID, with authority derived from the ecosystem ID or Authority ID. Makki suggested using "Authority ID" for better accountability, which Drummond and Darrell considered but had concerns about its potential to confuse implementers. Darrell expressed a need to further consider the implications of using "Authority ID" in the IRA protocol.

### Trust Registry Model Simplification

The group discussed simplifying the trust registry model by removing the Authority ID parameter and focusing on ecosystem governing authorities instead. They agreed to proceed with drafting a new specification using the Park model, which Drummond will work on today, and Darrell will convert into PR format. The updated spec will be reviewed by the TC for a week before being presented at the upcoming IW symposium on November 19th and 20th. The team acknowledged the need to reach closure on this long-standing issue and expressed frustration over the delay in finalizing the specification.
