## Details

### Trust Registries and Technical Governance

The group discussed trust registries and their importance in connecting technical trust with governance. Carly is working on bite-sized trust videos and mentioned plans to explore trust registries further. Darrell explained how trust registries can provide a technical anchoring to human trust and described the Trust Triangle concept. The discussion touched on the need for ecosystems to easily launch and trust mechanisms to decide who to trust. The conversation ended with a reminder about antitrust policies and some technical issues regarding LFX accounts.

### Repository Cleanup and Issue Review

Darrell led a discussion on closing old issues and PRs to clean up the repository before public review. They reviewed several tickets, including ones about authorization IDs, metadata, and signed responses, explaining the reasoning behind closing or backing off certain items. Darrell emphasized the importance of cleaning up dangling issues and standardizing terminology. The team agreed to close or backlog various tickets, with some items like metadata and signing responses left open for future consideration.

### Trust Registry Protocol Discussion

Darrell and Fabrice discussed the Trust Registry Query Protocol (TRQP) and the need to understand assertion IDs in different ecosystems. They agreed that while context information about ecosystems could be valuable, it should not be standardized in the core protocol. Fabrice highlighted their unique approach of indexing trust registries and visualizing ecosystems, which adds value beyond the protocol. Darrell suggested that context objects and metadata could be useful for providing ecosystem-specific information, with the possibility of some signals being promoted to the core protocol over time.

### Trust Registry Claim ID Discussion

Drummond and Darrell discussed the concept of claim IDs in a trust registry system. Darrell explained that the authority ID refers to the system of record, while the entity ID pertains to the government ID issuer. They debated the necessity of a claim ID, with Drummond questioning its purpose if the trust registry serves as the checked system. Darrell suggested that the claim ID might point to additional proof or context, though the exact meaning and usage of claim IDs remained unclear.

### Protocol Identifier Description Clarification

Drummond and Darrell discussed the need to clarify the descriptions of identifiers in the protocol specification, particularly the entity ID and assertion ID. They identified discrepancies between the descriptions in Section 5 and the information model, which may have caused confusion for implementers. Drummond suggested creating a ticket to resolve these issues, and both agreed to spend time reviewing and aligning the documentation. They also touched on the broader question of how assertion IDs are generated and used in the context of trust and credentials.

### Specification and Role-Based Access Control

Darrell and Drummond discussed the challenges of creating a specification that aligns with various approaches, including Fabrice's, EU Trusted List, and AMVA. They emphasized the need for an implementation guide to assist users in integrating the specification into their systems, which is currently out of scope but necessary. Carly raised the issue of defining roles in an ecosystem for delegation using DIDs, and Drummond explained that roles are a subset of assertion IDs, which are machine-readable and specific to the ecosystem. The discussion highlighted the complexity of role-based access control systems and the need to simplify processes for verifiers.

### Assertion IDs for Credential Verification

The group discussed assertion IDs and their role in credential verification, with Drummond explaining that assertion IDs are like atomic units that allow verifiers to check the authenticity of credentials without needing to understand the specific rules or permissions within the ecosystem. Darrell shared examples of how assertion IDs are used in professional registries to verify credentials and authority, while Drummond clarified that TRQP handles who is authorized to issue credentials but not the specific permissions within them. The discussion concluded with Drummond mentioning the newly formed Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group, which will begin weekly meetings the following Wednesday.

### Trust Surgery Task Force Expansion

Darrell discussed the need for additional members to join the Trust Surgery Task Force, particularly seeking someone from the APAC region to address time zone challenges. He mentioned that public review is expected by mid-month, and he encouraged participants to consider contributing to the task force. Mitchell inquired about the significance of assertion IDs, to which Darrell explained they are currently arbitrary strings but may evolve to include semantic guidelines similar to the A, B, and F format used in the IRA world. Drummond noted that Darrell would bring up Mitchell's question during a high-assurance bid call later that morning.
