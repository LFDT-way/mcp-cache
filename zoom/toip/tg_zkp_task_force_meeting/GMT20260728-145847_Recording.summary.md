## Details

### ZKPs Trust Graph Task Force

The Decentralized Trust Graph Zero Knowledge Proofs Task Force held its first working call with Scott Jones and Mitchell Travers as co-chairs. The participants introduced themselves, with Denys sharing his background as a former nuclear physicist who transitioned to IT and later focused on SSI and ZKPs at Affinity Group since 2019, and Mitchell explaining his work bridging the gap between zero-knowledge proof concepts and practical applications. The group discussed the need to establish clear parameters and understanding around ZKPs for the TrustGraph implementation, with Mitchell suggesting they would work to determine the most compressed or protective ZKPs suitable for the context.

### Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group

Drummond provided context about the personal credentials paper and explained how it led to the establishment of the Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group Task Force. He mentioned Hart Montgomery's involvement with the cryptography requirements, particularly regarding ZKPs, and noted that Hart had worked with Dr. Sanjam Garg and his Berkeley team on efficient ZKP solutions for mobile devices. Drummond suggested ensuring Hart's occasional participation in future meetings to involve the Berkeley team's expertise.

### Zero-Knowledge Proofs for Credentials

The group discussed a paper on zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) for credentials, with Mitchell explaining that it was developed early in discussions and serves as foundational documentation for future work. Denys raised questions about cryptographic decisions made in the document and whether those decisions had been formally committed to, which Drummond clarified had not been finalized and were still under consideration by the task force. Drummond also mentioned that privacy-preserving proof of biometric liveness was identified as an additional key class of ZKPs needed, with Scott and Andrew Hughes from FaceTech expressing interest in this area.

### Biometric Security and Quantum Resistance

Mitchell discussed his work with World Foundation on biometric proof of liveliness and ZKP technology, noting that they had addressed issues with closed-source proofs securing World token biometrics. Denys raised concerns about the post-quantum resistance of the current signature schemes, particularly regarding the potential need for migration when quantum computing becomes more advanced. Mitchell addressed these concerns by explaining that the drafting rules document includes a requirement for every privacy claim to name its horizon, with an estimated 2028 for EDCs to be broken, ensuring people are aware of the limitations of current signature schemes.

### ZKP Privacy and Assurance Boundaries

The team discussed cryptographic privacy and assurance boundaries for zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs), focusing on how to balance privacy with the ability to detect duplicate credentials. They reviewed a draft document from Sankar Sean that proposed splitting V1 into a simple minimum liveness profile and an extended personhood profile, with context-dependent unlinkability as the target approach. The group agreed to ratify several foundational decisions including the cybersecurity framing of privacy and assurance claims, and discussed the need for governance-bounded contexts to prevent issuer-verifier collusion while maintaining privacy. Drummond requested that key decisions be documented in GitHub threads for broader visibility within the Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group.
