### Record 023 Implementation Status Review

The team discussed the status of record 023, which is running end-to-end and has been reviewed by Dennis. Dennis confirmed that the current spec is close to the real implementation and highlighted that this solution is highly specialized and optimized for specific problems, though it requires additional implementation work. The discussion touched on the balance between highly specialized solutions and more generalized approaches, with Dennis noting that while specialized solutions may be most efficient for specific use cases, a more generalized approach might be needed to avoid creating multiple custom solutions.

### Zero-Knowledge Proofs for Digital Identity

Leif from Zeros Foundation presented an overview of their work on zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) for digital identity wallets, highlighting their open-source stack that supports various trust mechanisms and credential formats including Google Long Fellow and Microsoft Vega credentials. He discussed the trade-offs between different ZKP approaches, noting that while circuit-based methods like Longfellow and Vega work with unmodified issuers but have performance limitations, BBS-based solutions offer better performance but require issuer modifications. Leif also explained that their implementations are memory-intensive and currently require native code rather than WebAssembly, making server-side or edge computing more practical than client-side implementation.

### Hybrid Wallet Architecture Development

Leif explained the development of a hybrid wallet architecture with a back-end and front-end that can run on various infrastructures including laptops and cloud services. He described their work on a JSON-RPC-based protocol for wallet communication that incorporates multi-point communication with MLS as the cryptographic layer, which was requested by the commission team. The team is developing this protocol under the wallet messaging protocol (wmp.name) specification, and discussed challenges around zero-knowledge proofs and post-quantum security considerations.

### ZKP Mobile Credential Implementation Discussion

Leif explained that while ZKPs can work with MDLs, they are not feasible for mobile driver's licenses due to performance constraints. He suggested creating secondary credentials with specific claims like nationality and birth date for proof purposes, though this introduces new complexity with issuer requirements. The group discussed the need for cross-platform support of multiple ZKP systems rather than forcing adoption of a single solution, with Mitchell mentioning his work on a ZKP mage repository for circuit verification and Leif advocating for circuit catalogs that work across different proof systems.
