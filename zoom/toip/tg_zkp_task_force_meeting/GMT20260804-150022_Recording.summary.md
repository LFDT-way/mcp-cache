## Details

### Zero Knowledge Proofs Task Force

Scott led the second working call of the Zero Knowledge Proofs Task Force, noting that Drummond was traveling and would not be joining. The group discussed the status of context questions B1 and B2, with B1 being effectively settled and B2 remaining open regarding issuer-verifier collusion resistance. Scott mentioned a proposal to make issuer-verifier collusion resistance the target for the extended profile with Sankarshan's honesty clause, and Mitchell was tasked with exploring the practical feasibility of this target.

### Zero-Knowledge Proofs Task Force Introductions

The meeting focused on introductions for new participants in the CK Task Force and discussions about zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs). Kevin shared his interest in ZKProofs for proving geographical residency without revealing specific locations, while Martina introduced herself as a co-chair of the Decentralized Source Graph Working Group and expressed her interest in learning more about ZKPs. Mitchell discussed the importance of blockchain in ZKP implementations and explained the B2 collision target principle, which proposes that the issuer and verifier of credentials should be different entities, using biometrics as an example use case.

### ZKP Verifier-Issuer Collusion Concerns

The team discussed concerns about verifier and issuer collusion in zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) systems, with Denys initially unclear about the problem until Mitchell clarified that it involves situations where a verifier shares information about a credential holder back to the issuer. Martina explained that this could be problematic in certain contexts, such as government-issued IDs where authorities might not want information about where credentials are being used. The group concluded that while these attacks should be documented as potential risks, they may not necessarily need to be engineered into the specification, with Denys agreeing that separation between issuer and verifier should be the default system behavior.

### Issuer-Verifier Role Separation Discussion

The team discussed the role separation between issuers and verifiers, with Denys clarifying that an entity should not have privileges when playing both roles simultaneously. Mitchell explained that while nodes can be both issuer and verifier in a decentralized trust graph context, they cannot serve both roles in a specific transaction. The discussion then shifted to planning the next topic about building a circuit for the first proof, with Mitchell mentioning previous work he had done across three Trust Links Foundation repositories including credentials and Trust Tasks.

### ZKP Research Repository Presentation

Mitchell presented a research repository for the ZKP task force that contains experiments and runtimes for exploring zero-knowledge proofs. He demonstrated the structure of the repository, which includes different runtimes, circuit benchmarks, and a wiki format to track decisions and references. Mitchell explained that the goal is to equip task force members with tools that their AI agents can use to understand and participate in improving ZKP circuits, with a focus on enabling agentic-led contributions and creating a unified machine-readable record of research outcomes.

### ZK Proof Repository Discussion

The group discussed the current state of a local wiki page repository and ZK proof types, with Mitchell explaining that the repository primarily contains SNARK circuits rather than other proof types like Plonk. Denys raised concerns about the trusted setup requirements of ZK SNARKs and noted that while they are the most production-tested option, they are not post-quantum resistant and require trusted ceremonies. The team agreed to have members explore the repository weekly and provide feedback, with Mitchell suggesting this could help build agent trust over time. Denys also presented a proposal for probabilistic sampling to optimize trust verification in anonymous peer-to-peer systems, which the group decided to continue developing separately from the current V1 implementation.
