### Cryptographic Framework for Proof of Personhood

Mitchell presented on a cryptographic framework for proof of personhood, explaining how verifiable relationship credentials and personhood credentials are being integrated into zero-knowledge proof systems. He discussed various security challenges including the MDL passage incident, issues with WorldCoin's closed-source systems, and threats from nation-state actors using deepfakes and spear phishing attacks. The presentation emphasized that privacy cannot be retrofitted to existing systems and better cryptography alone is not sufficient, requiring a more comprehensive approach from the beginning.

### Privacy System Technical Components

Mitchell explained that privacy systems are most effective when implemented from the start, as adding privacy measures after the fact can be vulnerable to retroactive data capture. He outlined three core technical components of the paper: personhood credential issuer, Verifiable Relationship Credentials (VRCs), and zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) encapsulated in three theorems. The discussion included a note about the potential limitations of ECDSA in the face of quantum computing, though the paper itself doesn't address quantum security specifically.

### Personhood Oracles Protocol Implementation

Mitchell discussed a paper's content on personhood oracles and their implementation in the protocol, focusing on the separation between the verifier and issuer systems. He explained concepts like one-sided error, trusted setup, and toxic waste, referencing Zcash's ceremony as an example. The discussion also covered the impossibility of full unlinkability in certain scenarios, particularly when relationship attestation or context issuance is involved, and addressed the need for revocation and reissuance of credentials to maintain privacy.

### Proof of Personhood Credential Walkthrough

Mitchell presented a comprehensive walkthrough of a proof of personhood credential paper, explaining key concepts including FPOP (first person's proof of personhood), unlinkability, and nullifiers. He discussed the technical implementation details including the math constructions on pages 30-31 and the Groth proof from pages 47-49, while noting that revocation is handled as an example predicate rather than built into the native machinery. Mitchell highlighted the distinction between agent identifiers and agent identities, suggesting this needs further discussion in a separate thread about delegation, and mentioned connections to related work including the Decentralized Identity Foundation and TrustGraph working group.

### ZKP Research and Presentations

The team discussed Mitchell's work on zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) circuits and trusted setup ceremonies. Scott reported running a research cycle on the lab repo and found the results impressive, with real circuits and numbers anchored to decision documents. Mitchell explained the next steps involving a verification registry and proposed creating a website for group participation in trust tasks to generate entropy. The team also discussed upcoming presentations at GDC, including Mitchell's session on private authentication between agents and Vikas's sessions on cybersecurity for next-gen connectivity systems.
