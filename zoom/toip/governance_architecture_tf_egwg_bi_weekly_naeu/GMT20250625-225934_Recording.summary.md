### Global Trust Registry for UNCFACT

John discussed the concept of a global trust registry for UNCFACT, which would catalog and authenticate registries maintained by UN member countries. He emphasized that while the registry would focus on trade and commerce-related structures like business organizations and land records, it would not exclude other important registries like those for historical sites, though these would not be the primary focus. Makki suggested considering heritage and historical site registries, to which John agreed, noting that while UNCFACT's scope is trade-focused, these other registries could have commercial implications.

### Product Origin Verification Systems

John and Makki discussed the importance of verifying the authenticity and origin of products, particularly focusing on legal and commercial recognition. They explored how systems like barcodes and land registration play crucial roles in proving the origin and legality of goods, such as beef, for international trade. John emphasized that the entire supply chain, from production to manufacturing, must be considered to ensure compliance with legal requirements and environmental standards.

### Trust Registry and UN Directives

John discussed the complexities of recognizing countries and jurisdictions in the Global Trust Registry, particularly in light of United Nations decisions and international law. He highlighted the need to address how to update registry entries in response to UN directives, acknowledging the challenges of codifying existing practices into a unified system. Sankarshan pointed out the potential impact of these issues on government registries maintained by bodies like the UN, suggesting it could influence ongoing work at UNC. Neil raised questions about the expressiveness of the query language in handling these complexities and the link between trust registry entries and verifiable data supporting them. John explained that the UN Transparency Protocol is addressing the latter point, and he expressed hope that the Trust Registry Query Protocol could handle the simpler questions about authority recognition.

### Global Trust Registry Project Overview

John explained the Global Trust Registry Project, focusing on a supplier-buyer scenario where buyers need to verify suppliers' claims about products and credentials. He described two types of claims: product passports, which suppliers issue, and conformity credentials, which third parties provide. John emphasized the importance of digital identity anchors and authoritative registers to verify the legitimacy of suppliers and assessors. He concluded by highlighting the challenge of ensuring the trustworthiness of authoritative registers in the face of potential deepfake threats.

### Global Trust Registry Project Overview

John presented a diagram of the Global Trust Registry project, which aims to create a system for verifying claims across supply chains. He outlined key areas of focus, including understanding digital identity flows, establishing eligibility requirements for registries, and governing the process. Neil mentioned that ISO 18013 has been discussed in recent meetings and received support. John shared research findings on the implementation of ISO 18013 in various jurisdictions, highlighting the importance of legal and governance protections alongside technical measures. Neil also touched on the need to regularly verify authority chains in supply chains to prevent tampering.

### Global Trust Registry Overview

John and Neil discussed the Global Trust registry and its role in verifying business transactions and trustworthiness. John explained that the registry aims to make existing business information transparent rather than trying to solve all global trust issues. They agreed that while the registry won't prevent all fraud, it will help legitimate companies prove their compliance and credibility, potentially leading to a "race to the top" in global commerce.

### Supply Chain Verifiable Credentials Discussion

John and Neil discussed the concept of verifiable credentials and their role in supply chain transparency. John explained that UNTP aims to instrument supply chains with verifiable credentials to enable trust verification and transparency. They explored the complexity of international law and mutual recognition, with John sharing insights from an ongoing discussion between two lawyers. Neil suggested that a reputation mechanism could help validate actors in the supply chain, while John acknowledged that the current system, despite its flaws, generally functions effectively.
