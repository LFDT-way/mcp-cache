### Cross-Ecosystem Governance and Trust Models

Neil and John discussed the need to gather information on governance related to Verifier, Trust Registry, Holder, and Issuer, as well as the challenges of conducting cross-ecosystem governance audits. They emphasized the importance of creating logical models for trust registries and developing a portable data model for exchanging traveler profiles between different ecosystems. John noted the difference between supply chain trust registries and traveler profiles, highlighting the need for bridging mechanisms to facilitate data transfer between ecosystems.

### Global Trust Register Draft Discussion

John discussed his work with the Spanish registrar, focusing on creating a draft document for a Global Trust Register that does not exist yet. He explained that the document outlines legal and governance criteria for authoritative registers to be included in the trust registry. Scott suggested that the registry would need a governance framework and a conformance program, similar to what Ira would need to do. They discussed the differences between this registry and existing trust registries, emphasizing the need for ecosystems to meet specific requirements to be included.

### Trust Registry Governance Standards Review

John explained the project's focus on developing governance requirements and data structure standards for a trust registry, with a recommendation to be reviewed by UN Cfac in July 2026. He described a draft document outlining legal and institutional requirements for UN member states to establish recognized registries, which Scott suggested should be formatted using ISO-style "shall/must" language. John acknowledged the need to develop a principles framework before finalizing requirements, and noted that Alina, with her legal background, had drafted the initial text.

### UN Global Trust Registry Discussion

John and Scott discussed the Global Trust Registry project under the UN, emphasizing the need to recognize varying levels of digital maturity among UN member states without imposing strict entry criteria. John highlighted that while the project aims to establish minimum standards to prevent false representation, it should respect each country's unique digital governance frameworks. John also shared his experience using Gemini, a large language model AI, to generate a detailed research plan and report on trust registries, covering various authoritative frameworks for global supply chains and trade.

Scott and John discussed the challenges of implementing a trust list for verifiers in the context of cryptographic verification.
