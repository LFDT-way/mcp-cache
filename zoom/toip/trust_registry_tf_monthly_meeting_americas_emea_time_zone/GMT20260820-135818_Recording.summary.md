## Overview

The meeting focused on discussing the current state and future direction of Trust Registry Task Force work, particularly around the Trust Registry Query Protocol (TRQP) and broader trust registry initiatives. Darrell shared updates on the evolution of trust registries, noting that questions about trust registries have shifted from "what is it?" to "where are they running?" as more organizations adopt digital credentials. The group discussed various implementation examples including AMVA's work with US, Canada, and Australia/New Zealand on driver's license recognition, ICAO's public key directory, and private sector initiatives like IATA's potential role in air travel credentials. ## Details

### Digital Trust Registries Discussion

The group discussed trust registries and digital credentials, with Darrell explaining his role chairing non-technical architecture aspects including policy, onboarding, governance, and taxonomy for GDC. They discussed how major tech companies like Apple initially sought to control digital identity initiatives like mobile driver's licenses and digital passports, but now face challenges as countries and regions are requesting to be included in platforms without clear governance structures. The conversation touched on how trust registries are becoming more important as verifiable credentials gain adoption, and concluded with a reference to following the trust of IP governance template for handling sovereign issues.

### Digital Identity Trust Registries

The discussion focused on trust registries and levels of assurance in digital identity systems. Darrell and Daniel discussed how sovereign entities typically don't apply to be listed in registries but rather request to be included, and the challenges with assurance levels, using examples like AMVA's mobile driver system and ICAO's public key directory. They noted that ICAO's traveler registration system has issues with levels of assurance, allowing multiple people to use the same passport in e-gates due to insufficient verification standards. The conversation concluded with a comparison to IRA's simplified approach focusing on trust registries and person binding.

### Trust Registries and Authentication Processes

Darrell and Daniel discussed trust registries and technical processes, particularly focusing on holder device binding within ISO 18013-5 series and levels of assurance including biometric and PIN-based authentication. Darrell mentioned that credit unions face significant family fraud issues involving PIN-based attacks. The discussion centered around TRQP version two, which is now approved and being used in production by commercial members in the IRA space, with plans to share this progress at Trust Over IP.

### Lifecycle Management and Protocol Standards

Darrell and Sankarshan discussed a structured approach to identifying next steps in their work, focusing on lifecycle management, discovery needs, and transport protocols. They highlighted ongoing efforts with implementations like DIDCOM and TSP, as well as credential ecosystem interop challenges. Darrell noted that business questions around trust registries have evolved from last year's GDC conference, with current focus shifting to implementation locations and support requirements. Daniel explained how AMVA functions as a trust service umbrella for sovereign registries while maintaining individual entity autonomy, and discussed IATA's potential role in maintaining trust registries for the aviation industry as digital credentials become more widely used.

### Digital Identity Systems Expansion

Daniel and Darrell discussed the expansion of digital identity systems in both private and public sectors, including the standardization of digital birth certificates and vehicle registrations using ISO standards like MDL or MDOT. They explored how these systems cross domains, touching both driver's license and vital statistics (CRVS) realms. Darrell also shared insights about OpenVSX, a trust registry standard that has seen significant growth with the rise of AI, leading to the need for operational registries with service level agreements.

### LEI Credential and Trust Governance

Jeff discussed parallels between current identifier issues and challenges with the Global LEI Foundation's expansion plans, highlighting the need for a verifiable LEI credential dependent on a chain of trust. He expressed concerns about the proliferation of trust registries and the need for governance and accreditation to ensure legitimacy and prevent artificial or criminal registries. ### Registry Systems Implementation Discussion

Darrell and Jeff discussed the implementation of registries and handle systems for different business domains, with Darrell emphasizing that existing authorities in specific domains, like the recording industry, could effectively manage their own registries. Jeff compared this to CNRI's work with movie credits and highlighted the challenges in verifying identities and establishing credentials, particularly in trust and reputation systems. They also discussed the lack of a standard digital identifier beyond a URI in the protocol, with Darrell noting the flexibility to choose different types of URIs, including HTTP, Ethereum, or Solana anchored DIDs. The conversation touched on the potential for sock puppets and the development of a Registry of Registries in Canada focusing on identification and education issuers.

### Decentralized Trust in Digital Identity

Darrell and Jeff discussed the formation of trust in decentralized systems, focusing on how assurance levels and service costs impact trust in digital identifiers. They explored the challenges of verifying organizational trust versus individual trust, with Darrell emphasizing that while decentralized systems offer benefits, they cannot completely eliminate human deception. The conversation also touched on how different countries approach identity management and the role of centralized players in the digital ecosystem.

### Trust and Identifier Systems Discussion

Jeff and Darrell discussed the importance of trust and accuracy in identifier systems, particularly focusing on the role of registries and levels of assurance in establishing trust. They explored how different authorities like Open Corporates and Dun & Bradstreet contribute to identifier validation, and discussed how the Trust Registry protocol handles identifiers without taking a stance on their quality. The conversation highlighted that while registries can support multiple identifier types, they must specify which DID methods they support, and that higher assurance data may be more valuable but accuracy in identification remains fundamental.

Darrell explained that the Trust over IP (TRQP) initiative needs more traction and adoption, highlighting work being done with COG and IPTC to develop practical applications and guidebooks. He described ongoing efforts to help organizations like IPTC implement TRQP through business-focused approaches rather than technical protocols, with the goal of creating a guidebook based on these case studies. Darrell committed to actively working on this initiative on an IRA basis and sharing results, though he noted uncertainty about whether it would eventually become a Trust over IP product.
