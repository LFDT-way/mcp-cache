## Overview

The Trust Registry Task Force meeting focused on discussing next steps for Trust RGC Query Protocol (TRQP) 2.0 following its publication as a formal specification under the Trust Over IP framework. Darrell and Sankarshan presented work on discovery metadata capabilities, explaining how registries can expose information about themselves to enable connection to known ecosystems and facilitate entity lookups. Sankarshan outlined plans to develop lifecycle and revocation guidance, emphasizing the need for operational recommendations rather than changes to the core specification to avoid requiring a new version release. The team discussed how trust registries like Hand ID handle revocation through aliasing to other IDs or providing tombstone records, with Will explaining that Hand ID follows a policy of no deletions and permanent deprecation requests only. Drummond presented the "onion" model showing how verifiable trust agents, communities, and networks require increasing reliance on trust registries as systems evolve from basic credentials to complex trust networks. The discussion included analogies to DNS and explored how registry interop becomes more critical as systems move from individual verifiable credentials to larger trust communities and networks.

### TRQP 2.0 Next Steps Discussion

The meeting focused on discussing next steps for Trust RGC Query Protocol (TRQP) 2.0 following its publication as a formal specification. Darrell and Sankarshan had previously discussed key areas including lifecycle recommendations, discovery mechanisms, transport modes, and promotional materials to drive adoption. The discussion centered on discovery and metadata topics, with Darrell explaining two main use cases: connecting to a known ecosystem and finding new ecosystems. The current TRQP profile primarily addresses connecting to known ecosystems rather than discovering new ones, though there are plans to expand capabilities for registry traversal and cross-registry connections.

### Digital Trust Ecosystem Authorization Framework

Darrell discussed the need to identify authorizations, assurance levels, and DID methods in digital trust ecosystems, noting that the technical work of exposing these elements is straightforward, while the business and governance decisions are more complex. He explained that listing entities is optional and depends on business model and governance approach, using Hand ID as an example with its free discovery and paid service model. The discussion touched on metadata challenges, with Darrell acknowledging past "religious battles" over metadata standards and suggesting that the Trust Registry Task Force might explore this area further.

### Verifiable Credentials Revocation Discussion

The group discussed metadata and registry information for handling verifiable credentials, particularly focusing on how revocations are managed. Darrell explained that different types of registry information exist, including details about license status, revocation reasons, and payment issues, which could be valuable but aren't necessarily free. The discussion highlighted challenges around privacy-preserving revocation in verifiable credentials and questioned whether special registries are needed or if current approaches can handle revocation effectively.

### Hand ID Registry Policy Discussion

Will explained that the DOI policy allows no deletion of hand ID registry entries, only aliasing to another ID or creating a tombstone when requested for deprecation. Darrell and Will discussed how different registries maintain their own policies and authority over lifecycle and revocation processes, with the hand ID registry not adjudicating external organizations' decisions. Darrell emphasized the importance of maintaining registry autonomy and noted the benefits of digital processes over analog ones.

They emphasized the importance of trust registries as communities grow and need to interconnect. Darrell noted that while verifiable credentials signed by DIDs may not always require trust registries in closed ecosystems, more interconnected registries are needed to form larger networks. ### Trust Protocol Interoperability Discussion

The group discussed the analogy between the Trust Banning Protocol and DNS, focusing on how different communities maintain their own registries while needing interoperability across systems. Darrell explained that the protocol provides trust registry query capabilities, allowing members to verify credentials and establish trust networks. The discussion highlighted challenges around interoperability between different trust ecosystems and the need for external identifiers that can work across different communities, with examples like emergency response scenarios where out-of-state professionals need credential verification.
