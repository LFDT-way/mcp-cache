### Dynamic Content Addressability Solution

Nicholas presented a solution to address issues with content-addressed identifiers in dynamic workflows, introducing the concept of a "Smith tree" which allows for dynamic updates while maintaining content addressability. He explained how traditional Merkle trees require rebuilding when content changes, creating cascading updates that become problematic in interdependent environments. Nicholas demonstrated how Dynamic Autonomic Identifiers (DAIDs) can solve this by creating registries around documents that maintain credential state autonomically, enabling self-resolution without external resolvers. The discussion concluded with how this approach aligns with TRQP governance modes and establishes a trust framework for predictable content updates.

### Trust Registry Protocol Discussion

Darrell praised Nico's work on the Trust Registry Query Protocol, which provides resource identifiers and answers backed by specific levels of verification. He noted that while the protocol offers technical solutions, governance decisions about which trust registry to use remain necessary, and suggested that over time more "wet code" decisions might shift to technical implementations. The group discussed the protocol's potential to handle verification at different assurance levels, from simple methods to cryptographic proofs, with Nicholas planning to present this work at an upcoming meeting and Drummond expressing interest in further discussion via email due to technical issues with Signal.
