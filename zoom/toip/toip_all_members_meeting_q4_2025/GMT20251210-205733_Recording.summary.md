Carly presented a video discussing the impact of generative AI on trust infrastructure and identity weaponization, emphasizing the need for self-sovereign identity to empower individuals. She highlighted the progress of the Trust Over IP ecosystem, including the development of an identity and payment platform with strict ethical guidelines. Carly also announced upcoming speakers for the Ecosystem and Governance Working Group in 2025 and encouraged community members to contribute to "Bite Size Trust" videos to explain complex concepts related to Trust Over IP.

### Digital Identity Key Concepts

Carly presented a video explaining digital identity keys and pre-rotation, highlighting how private and public keys work together and the importance of key rotation to prevent identity theft. Drummond praised Carly's Bite Size Trust videos for their clear explanations of complex concepts, particularly pre-rotation, and suggested linking them to the trustworthy glossary to enhance understanding.

### Virtual Symposium Impact and Collaboration

Drummond and Carly discussed the recap of a recent virtual symposium, the largest event held in the past year. Drummond thanked Daniela and the LFDT staff, particularly Tomas, for their support in organizing the event. The symposium's content, including videos, slides, session abstracts, and speaker bios, is now available on a dedicated page. Reza, a speaker at the event, shared his experience, highlighting the profound impact it had on choir members who felt they had found their community in decentralized infrastructure. Reza expressed hope for continued collaboration in the decentralized trust graph workgroup and Trust Over IP initiatives.

### Linux Foundation's AI Governance Expansion

The meeting focused on the Linux Foundation's growing role in the AI space, highlighted by recent announcements of new AI protocols joining the foundation. Drummond and Daniela discussed the importance of neutral governance and collaboration within the Linux Foundation, citing examples like the A2A protocol and Agency's integration. They emphasized the foundation's ability to combine technology and standards, as well as its frameworks for open governance and global neutrality, which attracted major AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic.

### AI and TrustRP Progress Update

Drummond and Daniela discussed the recent announcements and the growing relevance of their work, particularly in AI and interoperability standards. Drummond highlighted the positive reactions to TrustRP and its potential to address trust issues, while Daniela emphasized the importance of maintaining focus on core work beyond AI. They agreed that the transition under LFDT has increased their exposure and that 2026 will likely be a significant year for their initiatives.

### AI Foundation Membership Fee Discussion

The meeting discussed the potential lowering of membership fees for the Agentic AI Foundation and LFX to make it more accessible for SMEs, with Makki raising this concern and Daniela agreeing to submit feedback. Drummond inquired about any public reactions from W3C or other standards bodies regarding the protocols, but Daniela reported no such reactions. The group reviewed presentations from the symposium, highlighting Carly's talk on the VOI ecosystem's progress and its annual review cycle using the trustworthy meta model, as well as Carly's presentation on applied trust over IP.

### DID Integration for ORCID Profiles

Carly presented a proposal to integrate DIDs into ORCID profiles, which would enable trust signals and support secure data transfer, authentication, and signing credentials. Drummond noted that this proposal aligns with the work of the Creator Assertion Working Group, co-hosted by TrustRIP and Diff, which focuses on how artists and authors can sign content credentials using standards like C2PA. Drummond mentioned that Adobe is interested in demonstrating how this proposal could work in practice.

### Linux Foundation Summit Educational Impact

Drummond and Carly discussed the progress and impact of the Linux Foundation Member Summit and the development of educational content for infrastructure building. Drummond highlighted the success of the DidSkid meta method, which was inspired by a Victoria Digital Trust Squad Meetup conversation, and noted its potential to simplify the process of self-certifying DIDs. Carly mentioned the availability of resources and tools related to DidSkid and other specifications, while Drummond shared insights from a recent talk on the growing trend of self-certifying identifiers in DIF-recommended DID methods.

### DID PLC Implementation and Workshops

The group discussed the DID PLC method, noting that Blue Sky uses it for all their accounts and is planning to transfer control of their DID PLC repository to a Swiss foundation. Carly requested a speaker from Blue Sky to present about their DID method implementation at an upcoming ecosystem event, and Drummond offered to make introductions. The conversation ended with a review of recent workshops, including SETI's progress in Utah and a panel discussion about the TrustRP stack focused on human agency, which will result in a paper draft expected in January.
