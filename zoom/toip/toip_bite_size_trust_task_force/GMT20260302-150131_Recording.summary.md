### Zero-Knowledge Proofs Explanation Meeting

### Cryptographic Proof Statistical Nature Discussion

Carly and Scott discussed the statistical nature of a cryptographic proof, emphasizing that while it is not 100% certain, the likelihood of a collision (two different inputs producing the same hash) is extremely low. Carly explained that the proof relies on mathematical heuristics rather than absolute facts, similar to the principles of cryptography, which involves the challenge of breaking the binding between private and public keys. Scott sought clarity on the level of detail needed for understanding zero-knowledge proofs, and Carly clarified that while the concept involves public-private keys and potential risks from quantum computers, the proof's uniqueness lies in its statistical nature, making it single-use and non-reusable.

### Zero-Knowledge Proofs for Privacy

Scott and Carly discussed the importance of zero-knowledge proofs in protecting Alice's privacy, emphasizing their ability to prove a fact without revealing additional personal information. They highlighted the need for a single-use proof that cannot be reused or stored, which aligns with privacy objectives. Scott also noted that zero-knowledge proofs are becoming a crucial tool in data minimization, allowing for the provision of only the necessary personal information for transactions while maintaining privacy.

### Anchored Signatures for Digital Credentials

Carly explained the concept of anchored signatures, which are used to address the risk of key compromise in digital credentials. She described how universities can maintain a tamper-evident log of all issued degrees, using distributed watchers and witnesses to monitor and sign the sequence of events. This creates a transparent, append-only history that can prevent the forgery of degrees with compromised keys, as any forged credentials would lack the necessary anchor proving their legitimate issuance. Carly also mentioned that anchored signatures have been revised multiple times to ensure fact-checking accuracy.

### X509 vs Decentralized Certificate Systems

Scott and Carly discussed the differences between X509 certificates and decentralized signing systems, particularly focusing on timestamp validation and key rotation. Scott emphasized that while centralized certificate systems require regular key rotation and validation periods, decentralized systems like the C2PA allow for timestamp-based verification without relying on a central authority. They agreed that the presentation should clearly distinguish between these approaches and highlight the importance of key rotation in decentralized systems to prevent exposure of signing keys over time.

### Signing Key Rotation Best Practices

Carly and Scott discussed the importance of rotating signing keys to ensure the trustworthiness of signers. They emphasized that best practices require rotating keys to keep signing actions secure and prevent attackers from creating fake degrees. Carly explained that universities regularly replace their signing keys to maintain security and trust. They also touched on the concept of anchored signatures and decentralized key rotation, though Scott clarified that anchored signatures do not exist in X509.
