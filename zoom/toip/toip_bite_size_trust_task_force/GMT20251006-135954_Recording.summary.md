### Digital Identity Key Pre-rotation

Carly and Scott discussed the concept of key pre-rotation in digital identity systems, focusing on how it prevents attackers from hijacking a DID by compromising a private key. They agreed that pre-rotation involves generating and committing to the next key in advance, making it tamper-resistant and securing DIDs against takeover. Scott suggested exploring the broader topic of trust, including both security risks and the potential for deception in digital identity systems. Carly mentioned she was working on a script about trust registries, which address the governance aspect of trust by establishing a structure around claimed identities. Scott recommended Carly research the C2PA as an example of a trust registry.

### C2PA Trust Program Overview

Scott explained the current state of the C2PA conformance program, noting that as of January 1st, 2024, the program will require use of the official trust list for validating content credentials. He detailed the governance structure, including the steering committee as the governing authority, the conformance task force as the administrative party, and the roles of certification authorities, generator products, and validator products in the trust ecosystem. Carly inquired about X509 certificates and the process of validating trust chains, to which Scott provided an explanation of how certificates are issued, endorsed, and validated through a chain of trust.

### CTBA Conformance and Certificate Management

Scott explained the CTBA conformance program and its implementation, including how products are registered and certified. He demonstrated how to access and decode X509 certificates from the public GitHub repository, showing the detailed information contained within each certificate. Carly asked questions to clarify the relationship between the conformance records and the actual certificates, which Scott addressed by explaining how the root certificates contain the necessary keys and information.

### X509 Certificate Structure and Verification

Scott explained the structure and requirements of X509 certificates, including their fields, public keys, and validity periods. He described how certificates are audited and verified against C2PA guidelines, including specific algorithms, policies, and object identifiers. Carly confirmed her understanding of the certificate information and its display in the CSR decoder.

### Trust List Security and Verification

Scott and Carly discussed the security of trust lists and certificate authorities in the context of the Google Pixel camera and content authenticity. They explored potential risks, such as hacking GitHub accounts or cloning repositories, and the challenges of verifying the authenticity of trust lists. Scott explained that while there are risks, measures like the conformance program aim to prevent malicious validators from being added to the trust list. They also compared the management of trusted roots in browsers and the C2PA system, noting that while browsers have roots embedded directly on devices, the C2PA system manages them in the cloud.

### Trust Registry Validation Processes

Scott and Carly discussed trust lists and validation processes, particularly in the context of X509 certificates and identity verification. Scott explained that trust lists are currently used to validate the legitimacy of organizations and certificates, and that there is an opportunity for trust registry technology to facilitate easier endorsements and build larger trust networks. They also touched on the upcoming EU trust list and the potential need to modify the Trust Registry Query Protocol to interlink different trust lists for interoperability. Scott mentioned that work is being done on a generic scheme for trust registry validation processes.

### C2PA Trust Protocol Adaptation

The group discussed the need for C2PA to adapt its structure to meet higher protocols, with Trust over IP requiring a query protocol mandate. Drummond suggested writing a paper comparing X509 infrastructure with decentralized trust registries, which Scott confirmed already exists. Carly presented educational scripts on digital identity, including topics on key storage and recovery methods, and received feedback from Drummond on the content and structure of her materials.
