### DIDs vs Traditional PKI

Scott and Carly discussed the differences between DIDs and traditional PKI, with Scott emphasizing that DIDs are not certificates but credentials that link to names, organizations, and internet elements, unlike PKI which often communicates with machine addresses. Neil contributed by comparing DIDs to OpenID Connect identity tokens, noting that DIDs are tokens that don't handle routing but are used for authentication and authorization purposes.

### Decentralized Identity Systems Overview

Scott and Carly discussed the differences between current email systems and decentralized identity systems (DIDs), emphasizing that DIDs enable direct communication without intermediaries. They also talked about SETI's move towards a decentralized model, mentioning their upcoming symposium and the use of Carrie, Caesar, and ACDCs in their ecosystem. Carly shared her experience reading markdown files about VLEI and Carrie, highlighting the training resources available for developers.

### DID Key Rotation Strategies

Carly and Scott discussed key rotation and pre-rotation in DID systems. Carly explained the process of key pre-rotation, where a new key is generated and stored securely while the current key remains active. Scott emphasized the importance of maintaining control over the pre-rotated key and the need for frequent key rotations. They agreed that pre-rotation makes key rotations predictable and tamper-resistant, securing DIDs against takeover.

### Secure Key Storage Strategies

Carly and Neil discussed strategies for secure key storage, including using air-gapped machines and physical devices like YubiKeys, while acknowledging the challenges of device loss. They explored the concept of hybrid storage combining edge and cloud storage, with social recovery as a backup method. Neil emphasized the importance of transient data storage and the need for services to make these security measures transparent to consumers. They also touched on multi-signature (multisig) technology in decentralized identifiers (DIDs) and the role of ACDC chains in defining authorized signers. Carly expressed the need to learn more about WebVH and ACDCs as she continues her research.
