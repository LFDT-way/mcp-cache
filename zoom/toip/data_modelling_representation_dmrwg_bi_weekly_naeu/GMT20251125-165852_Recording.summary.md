### Challenges in Digital Privacy Proofs

Neil Thompson discussed the limitations of zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) and batch-issued credentials in addressing data privacy concerns, particularly in the context of EU digital wallet requirements. He explained that while ZKPs can handle simple yes/no verifications, they struggle with complex personal information exchanges needed for services like medical procedures. Neil also highlighted the challenges with batch-issued credentials, noting that even one-time-use credentials could be traced across transactions if public keys are reused. He concluded that while digital credentials face similar issues as physical identifiers, the ability of third-party services to learn and track user behavior presents a significant privacy concern, especially in the hospitality and travel industries where personal information is often inferred from user behavior for marketing purposes.

### Personal Data Sharing Challenges

Neil and Steven discussed the challenges and limitations of sharing personal information, particularly in the context of travel and medical conditions. They explored how data becomes less relevant over time and the potential for misuse, except in cases like medical information where sharing might be necessary. Neil suggested mechanisms for limiting the sharing of personal information, such as focusing on needs rather than conditions, and proposed a system where doctors could assess a patient's fitness for travel based on a detailed form. They also discussed the complexities of verifying credentials in the hospitality and travel industry, highlighting the need for trust registries and better registration systems. Neil mentioned the use of batch-issued credentials and one-time signatures, but Steven questioned the benefit of generating multiple credentials when only one is needed at a time.

### Data Privacy Challenges and Solutions

Neil and Steven discussed the challenges of data privacy and the limitations of technical solutions in protecting personal information. They explored the concept of linkability in data sharing and the potential risks associated with micro-sharing. Neil highlighted the importance of governance, auditability, and reputation in managing data privacy, emphasizing that there is no simple technical fix. They also touched on the travel industry's approach to data collection and the evolving methods of payment processing to reduce data exposure. Neil mentioned his progress on a travel profile project and the complexities involved in software development using GitHub.
