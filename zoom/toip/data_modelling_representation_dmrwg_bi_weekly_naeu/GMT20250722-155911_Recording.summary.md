### Traveler Medical Data Management

### Traveler Medical Profiles Automation

Neil and Makki discussed the development of medical profiles for travelers with disabilities. They agreed that the profiles should include information about whether conditions are static or dynamic, as temporary conditions like a broken leg might require different accommodations than chronic conditions. Neil explained that airlines already have forms for medical assessments, and the goal is to automate some of this process by using traveler profiles to make recommendations about fitness for travel. They also discussed the importance of keeping medical profiles up to date to ensure accurate assessments.

### Travel Vaccinations and Fitness Requirements

Neil discussed the complexities of travel vaccinations, particularly for individuals with autoimmune diseases, noting that vaccines like yellow fever are live and can be problematic for such individuals. He also highlighted the importance of fitness certifications for extreme sports like skiing and scuba diving, emphasizing the potential liability travel services face when clients are unable to participate in activities due to misrepresentation of their fitness levels. Neil concluded by expressing the need to provide clear information about travel support requirements, including fitness certifications, to ensure a smooth travel experience.

### AI-Driven Travel Fitness Evaluations

Neil and Steven discussed the use of AI and medical profiles to automate or expedite travel fitness evaluations, focusing on the need to compartmentalize who can access health information. They explored the distinction between static and dynamic health conditions, with Steven suggesting the use of expiration dates for fit-to-travel certificates, which Neil confirmed aligns with current airline and regulatory practices. Neil shared personal anecdotes about medical emergencies during travel, emphasizing the importance of such systems in managing travel fitness assessments.

### Medical Data Privacy in Air Travel

Neil and Steven discussed the challenges and privacy concerns in digitizing medical information for air travel. Neil explained that current processes rely heavily on manual PDF forms and faxing due to data privacy concerns. They explored potential automation solutions for assessing medical needs and communicating travel accommodations securely. Neil highlighted various medical scenarios and the need for secure, efficient communication of travel requirements, suggesting existing protocols like S/MIME or DTAP could be adapted for this purpose.

### Accessibility Travel Management System

Neil discussed the need for a seamless system to manage travel services for individuals with accessibility needs, outlining four tiers of transportation assistance. He emphasized the importance of having a travel profile that can be shared with relevant parties while preserving privacy. Steven highlighted the potential benefits of storing this information in a digital wallet, which could be accessible to verified individuals in case of emergencies. The group also discussed the challenges of keeping medical information up to date and the potential for first responders to access critical information in urgent situations.

### Dynamic Profile System for Practitioners

Makki discussed the importance of having a dynamic profile system for health practitioners to ensure accurate and up-to-date information across cross-border, automated supply chains. They emphasized the need for timely notifications and corrections to prevent errors, highlighting the role of human involvement in the process. Makki also stressed the significance of providing quality customer experiences through intelligent bots, aiming to avoid repeating past mistakes in customer service.

### Structured Traveler Profiles in Hospitality

Neil explained the importance of structured traveler profiles in the hospitality and travel industry, emphasizing that AI alone cannot effectively handle unstructured data for business analytics. He highlighted that the profile serves as a presentation mechanism for exchanging information between parties, rather than a storage or query profile. Neil also discussed the limitations of AI in creating extendable schemas and the need for human oversight, while Steven suggested exploring OCA's layered approach for flexibility and privacy features. Neil agreed on the value of layers for extensions like translation and confidence levels but noted that skiing and other activities require distinct structures rather than simple layers.

### Data Privacy and Payment Evolution

Neil discussed a data privacy approach where sensitive information is held in memory temporarily and not persisted in storage, allowing for on-demand access and immediate deletion after use. He also shared insights about the travel industry's evolving payment systems, noting that by 2027-2028, credit card information will be processed through certificates rather than direct input, with neither merchants nor payment processors ever seeing the actual card details. Makki raised concerns about legacy systems and interoperability issues, emphasizing the importance of proper system design.
