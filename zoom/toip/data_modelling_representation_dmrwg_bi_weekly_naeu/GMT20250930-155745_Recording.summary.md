## Overview

The meeting focused on developing a comprehensive travel profile system that would manage personal data, including medical information, dietary restrictions, and preferences, while ensuring privacy and efficient data exchange. Discussions covered the technical challenges of implementing such a system, including schema development, data synchronization across devices, and the role of personal agents in managing information. ### Travel Food Profile System Challenges

Neil and Bo discussed the challenges of implementing a travel profile system for food preferences and dietary restrictions. They explored how to handle various aspects of food profiles, including diets, cuisines, and food catalogs. Neil emphasized the complexity of managing allergies and certifications, such as halal and kosher, and the need for a clear audit trail for food sourcing and preparation. They also touched on the vast amount of personal information involved in food profiles and the importance of ensuring accurate and reliable data.

### Privacy and Personal Information Sharing

Neil and Bo discussed the challenges of managing and sharing personal information, particularly in the context of food allergies and travel preferences. They explored the implications of selective disclosure, where individuals must decide what information to share with restaurants or hotels to meet their needs. Neil suggested that the current model of sharing detailed personal profiles and preferences might not be ideal, as it could lead to privacy concerns and inefficiencies. Instead, they considered a model where service providers, like restaurants and hotels, would share their profiles and offerings, allowing individuals to make informed decisions based on the available information.

### Data Privacy and Agent Evolution

Neil and Bo discussed the concept of personal data privacy and the evolving role of agents in managing and exchanging information. Neil explained that while some data is publicly available, much of it is personal and potentially identifying, and highlighted a shift from anonymous agents to dedicated agents that report to users and can handle heavy lifting tasks. They also discussed the functionality of a travel profile that extracts and presents data in a subset form, without exchanging raw stored data, and the need for translation capabilities when interacting with international entities. Neil emphasized the importance of having synchronized information across devices and a cloud wallet for offline access, while Bo mentioned considering preferences and credentials that should be manageable even when offline.

### Data Privacy in Travel Services

Neil and Bo discussed the challenges and potential solutions for data privacy in travel and hospitality services, focusing on the role of personal agents in managing and accessing information without compromising privacy. Neil emphasized the importance of having a service profile for personal agents to interact with public services efficiently and securely. They also touched on the need for medical records to support special requirements in travel and accommodations.
