### Travel Data Sovereignty Project

Neil discussed a digital personal profile project focused on hospitality and travel, which aims to create a standardized way to collect, manage, and share personal information across different services. He explained that the project uses SSI (Self-Sovereign Identity) to allow travelers to control their data and share it on an as-needed basis during the seven stages of travel, from exploration to post-travel. Neil emphasized that while data modeling focuses on the schema for exchanging information, the project acknowledges the need for an AI agent to help travelers manage their data and make informed decisions about sharing it.

### Travel Profile Structure Overview

Neil presented a comprehensive travel profile structure, outlining key components including personal identification, contact information, characteristics, health and accessibility details, and travel preferences. He explained how different aspects of the profile, such as allergies and dietary information, intersect across multiple categories. Neil noted that while the basic structure was developed, industry-specific requirements were still being considered for inclusion.

### Food Preferences and Dietary Modeling

Neil discussed the complexity of food preferences and dietary requirements, explaining how they can be captured in a JSON schema. He outlined the distinction between dietary restrictions, which are absolute, and food preferences, which are subjective. Neil also touched on the importance of allergies, both food-related and related to hotel environments, and how these are handled in the model.

### Traveler Profile Verification Systems

Neil discussed the complexities of traveler profiles, including various preferences, restrictions, and health considerations for activities like skiing and scuba diving. He emphasized the need for self-attested information and the potential integration of verifiable credentials, such as fitness certificates or medical notes, to support traveler data. Neil clarified that while traveler profiles would primarily contain self-attested information, verifiable credentials would be referenced separately and used to complement the profile.

### International Name Model Presentation

Neil presented a comprehensive model for handling international names, which includes prefixes, given names, family names, and suffixes, along with considerations for different languages, scripts, and cultural practices. Makki noted that the model aligns well with Western Asian and other global name structures, while Neil emphasized the need to accommodate various official names, including passport formats and local variations. Steven inquired about mapping between this model and passport formats to ease adoption, to which Neil confirmed that standard name formats for travel purposes would be included. Neil acknowledged that while the model is a good starting point, it will likely face feedback from experts and further refinement.

### Travel Communication Model Development

Neil discussed the development of a comprehensive communication model for travel services, focusing on creating a knowledge base that travelers can confirm and control. He emphasized the need to accommodate various communication channels, including voice, text, and video, across different devices and platforms. Neil also highlighted the importance of allowing travelers to specify their preferred contact methods and urgency levels for different types of communications, which could help improve the efficiency and effectiveness of travel services.

### Travel Profile System Development

Neil discussed the development of a travel profile system that would allow travelers to share specific information about their accessibility needs and medical conditions on a need-to-know basis with travel service providers. He noted that while individual pieces of information are straightforward, managing relationships between different data points is more complex. Makki suggested integrating AI to streamline profile creation through voice commands, which Neil agreed could be an effective approach. Neil also shared insights from Alex Beinfragers' experience with a simplified travel profile system, noting that most travelers are willing to provide only basic information, with more detailed data being optional.

### AI Schema for Trust Environments

Neil discussed the role of AI in simplifying complex information and creating profiles, emphasizing its natural fit in this process. He explained that the schema being developed is an exchange format that can be queried by any service, making it technology-neutral but particularly useful in trust environments. Neil also mentioned that this schema could be used by various identity systems, including OpenID Connect and sovereign-based SSI, and is intended to enable the efficient use of AI and other technologies.
