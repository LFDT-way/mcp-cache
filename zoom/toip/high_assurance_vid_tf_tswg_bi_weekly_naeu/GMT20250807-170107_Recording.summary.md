## Details

### High-Assurance VID Task Force Discussion

The meeting began with introductions from new participants, including Daniel Hardman and Vasily, who shared their backgrounds in organizational identity and SSI. Alex highlighted the progress made on the high-assurance vid task force specification and encouraged feedback. Daniel expressed concerns about bridging keys across different cryptographic methods or VID systems, using a personal anecdote to illustrate potential issues with cross-referencing identifiers. The group agreed to further discuss Daniel's concerns and explore the implications of the specification, with Drummond joining remotely despite a tight schedule.

### Complexities of Organizational Identity

Daniel discussed the concept of organizational identity and its complexities, emphasizing that while identity is defined as sameness across time and circumstances, it can also refer to sameness across different facets or roles. He used examples like Cecilia's multiple identities as a mother, employee, and patient to illustrate this point. Daniel also highlighted the challenges organizations face when dealing with different identifiers for various facets, such as Coca-Cola's multiple legal entities and brand relationships. He concluded by explaining how these complexities can lead to confusion and fraud in industries like telecommunications, where there is a lack of understanding between brands and legal entities.

### Secure Identifier Equivalence Framework

Daniel discussed the risks of equating different types of identifiers, such as domain names and SSH keys, which can lead to confused deputy problems and escalation of privilege issues. He recommended eliminating the cryptographic bridge and instead using a cross-endorsement mechanism where identifiers can be approximately equivalent, with clear statements about scope, governance, and lifespan. Daniel suggested formalizing the process of making equivalence statements, including a checklist for evaluating equivalence and a schema for safe statements.

### Modeling Token and Tag Relationships

The group discussed Daniel's recommendations and Tim's insights on the fundamental model needed for addressing disconnects and congruities. Tim proposed a model involving tag-to-token and tag-to-tag relationships, emphasizing the importance of defining a verifiable identifier as a unique reference that can be dereferenced to reveal information about a token's controller. The discussion highlighted the need to refine the specification and explore concepts like mutual attestation and third-party attestation to establish a robust framework.

### Public Key Sharing Confidence Discussion

The group discussed identifier control and public key sharing. Drummond emphasized the need to establish confidence in identifier control rather than equivalence, and argued that sharing a public key in multiple places increases, rather than diminishes, confidence in the controller's control over the private key. Charles agreed with Drummond's points, and suggested that Daniel's concerns about public key sharing might be addressed by considering the context in which the keys are shared.

### Key Verification and Authentication Discussion

Charles and Daniel discussed the use of keys for verifying habit links and certificates, emphasizing the need for clarity in the specification regarding the scope of key usage. Vasily highlighted the operational challenges and security risks of reusing keys across different containers, advocating for the practice of key rotation. The group agreed on the importance of cross-endorsing authentication methods, with Vasily suggesting the use of DNS for this purpose due to its distributed nature.

### DNS Identity Verification Goals

The discussion focused on the goals of DNS-based identity verification and the relationship between X.509 certificates and decentralized identifiers (DIDs). Alex explained that the effort aims to establish trusted relationships by linking DNS records to company identities and to create common patterns for interoperability between different types of identifiers. Vasily emphasized that DNS is well-suited for simple cryptographic proofs of identifier ownership but may struggle with complex schemas.

### DNS Security and Key Governance

The group discussed DNS security and key governance, with Vasily explaining that DNSSEC allows for simple cross-signing of keys and control over domain records through signature production. Daniel responded to Drummond's points about identifier equivalence, explaining that in practice, reputation is typically bound to the referent of the identifier rather than the controller, using examples like LEI identifiers for legal entities. The discussion focused on whether there was a pathway to allow certain key governance mismatches, with Vasily suggesting that simple cross-signing could address security concerns.

### X.509 and DID Governance Models

The group discussed the relationship between X.509 certificates and DID (Decentralized Identifiers), focusing on governance challenges and attestation models. Tim proposed using attestation rather than endorsement, explaining that different governance regimes can coexist with the same public key, and shared an example of using the same private key for both a website certificate and a PDF signing certificate. Alex presented a diagram showing how a certificate authority could manage both X.509 certificates and DIDs, though Tim emphasized keeping governance considerations separate from the technical specification while acknowledging their importance in practice.

### Cryptographic Key Bridge Specification Discussion

The group discussed the problem they are trying to solve, with Alex confirming it was the correct issue and mentioning additional layers involving business requirements and market trends. Daniel expressed concerns about the cryptographic key bridge, questioning the feasibility of a generic specification for bridging different identifier types. He emphasized the need for careful interpretation of the model to avoid misinterpretation. Alex agreed to be more cautious in distinguishing between requirements and recommendations in the specification, but did not suggest starting from scratch. The conversation ended with plans to reconvene in two weeks for further discussion, and Alex committed to sharing notes and thoughts with the group.
