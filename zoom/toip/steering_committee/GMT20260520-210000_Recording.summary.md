## Overview

The Trust Over IP steering committee meeting focused on several key updates and decisions regarding specification processes and standards. The committee approved removing permalinks in favor of DOIs for document identification, with Carly explaining that DOIs provide better long-term stability and are already being implemented through Zenodo. Drummond presented a new specification template based on ITF standards that includes guidance for using SpecUpT for glossary management and standard consideration sections. The committee discussed potential international standardization of the CARY specifications, with Carla reporting that costs for ISO adoption range from $2,250 to $800 depending on the process, while national standards bodies could potentially provide free access to adopted standards. The group also reviewed plans for upcoming conferences, including GDC where Scott has submitted a speaking proposal, and discussed recent insights from various spring conferences including progress on content authenticity and verifiable credentials implementation.

### Trust over IP DOI Migration

Carly proposed removing permalinks in favor of DOIs for Trust over IP deliverables, citing technical implementation challenges and the superiority of DOIs. The team agreed to this change, with Drummond expressing support and confirming that DOIs would provide better long-term stability. Carly explained that the process would involve using Zenodo to mint DOIs, with both web and archived versions of specifications being accessible through the DOI resolver.

### New Specification Template Implementation

Drummond presented a new specification template that has been developed in a repository, replacing previous templates that were either underutilized or outdated. The new template follows ITF standard structure and includes instructions for editors, standard sections, and guidance on using Spec Up T for glossary management. The team agreed to have Drummond send the template to Jory for review and to create either a presentation or intro video for working groups, while Carly's approved deliverable process document was approved pending minor permalink adjustments.

### International Standardization Options Discussion

Karla presented research on potential international standardization options for the Cary suite specifications, exploring three ISO scenarios with associated costs ranging from $2,250 to $800. Carly raised a question about ISO pricing and accessibility, which Karla clarified by explaining that while national standards bodies can provide free access to adopted ISO standards, ISO standards themselves are copyrighted and require payment. The discussion concluded with Makki inquiring about creating a web page for standards under the Linux Foundation to increase reach and impact.

### Trust over IP Standards Reference

The team discussed how to properly reference standards approved at Trust over IP, specifically whether they should be called Trust over IP standards, LFDT standards, or Linux Foundation standards. Daniela agreed to consult with Jory about the best practice for referencing these standards and will follow up via email and Slack. The group also clarified that national standards bodies can decide whether to charge their members for internationally adopted standards, but this process applies after standards are published at ISO, not during the ISO working group process.

### TrustRIP Protocol Specification Discussion

The team discussed the TrustRIP query protocol specification and its current status at TrustRIP. Steve inquired about how to refer to the CARIE suite of standards (including ACDC, CSER, and CARIE), which Daniela needs to follow up on. The group also discussed ISO specification purchase requirements, where Karla clarified that purchasing a standard includes a watermarked PDF and restrictions on redistribution. Makki suggested adding an "open issues" or "upcoming projects" section to the specification template, though Drummond advised against including implementation details in the specification itself to maintain separation between specifications and implementations.

### CARIE Standard Recognition Discussion

The group discussed the need for the CARIE standard to be recognized by a large international standards body like ISO or IETF for legal and contractual purposes, particularly for the state of Utah's implementation. Steve clarified that while the Linux Foundation produces standards, it may not carry the same weight as recognized standards bodies in contracts, leading to discussions about potentially rebranding the standard under a more recognized organization's name. The team also noted that the next all-members meeting would be scheduled for October, before the IW43 event on November 3-5 and the SETI Summit on November 16.

### SETI Credential Interoperability Discussion

The team discussed SETI credential interoperability, with Steve proposing a solution where foreign visitors exchange their credentials for temporary SETI credentials at entry points like airports, avoiding the need for local businesses to support multiple credential formats. Drummond volunteered to help champion this presentation, and the group explored technical implementation details including correlation risks and privacy considerations. Scott shared updates from recent conferences, including positive developments with the COG specification receiving industry endorsement and Google's announcement supporting C2PA on their products. The team also reviewed recent conference activities, including successful sessions at IAW and the new CARICOM conference, with particular emphasis on the growing momentum around open source development and implementation of decentralized identity solutions.
