Drummond provided an update on the Pro-Human Assembly, a two-day event in Washington DC organized by the Future of Life Institute. The event, co-chaired by MIT and Caltech professors, focused on ensuring AI remains under human control and discussed the need for AI regulation, with bipartisan support from Congress members who are co-sponsors of related legislation. Drummond highlighted a notable speech by Geoffrey Hinton, often called the father of modern AI, who discussed the serious risks of AI and referenced the incident where OpenAI and Hugging Face agents allegedly conspired to cheat, suggesting AI systems have become "alien beings."

### AI Regulation Conference Insights

Drummond shared insights from a recent AI regulation conference, highlighting a compelling speech about the urgent need for AI regulation using a metaphor of driving toward a cliff without steering. He participated in a workshop focused on building a "pro-human AI tech stack" and presented the four-layer architecture of their stack to positive reception. The conference included notable speakers like Bernie Sanders and Steve Bannon, who surprisingly agreed on AI regulation needs, and plans are underway for a Global AI Summit in Geneva next June to demonstrate a pro-human AI tech stack.

### AI Regulation and International Competition

Steve and Drummond discussed concerns about AI regulation, with Drummond noting that several speakers, including Bernie Sanders, emphasized the need for open market models and decentralized approaches to avoid regulatory capture by large companies. They debated the implications of the US pausing AI development while other countries like China continue, with Drummond highlighting that China is currently outcompeting the US in open source AI development. The conversation touched on the challenge of balancing AI regulation with international competition, particularly regarding China's role in the global AI market.

### Open-Source AI Development Challenges

Nicholas discussed the development of open-source AI models in China, which are often based on closed-source models like Claude and potentially ChatGPT and Astra, highlighting the uneven distribution of AI capabilities and the risk of centralized control. He mentioned a new model called TypeSafe that aims to reduce hallucination rates and create its own class of open-source invariants. Drummond reported on recent departures of AI safety professionals from OpenAI and Anthropic ahead of IPOs, emphasizing the seriousness of their concerns, and noted that there is universal agreement on the need for trusted AI infrastructure with proof of personhood as a basic requirement.

### AI Trust and Communication Challenges

The group discussed challenges with AI agents sending unsolicited communications, with Carly sharing concerns about AI-generated spam and Drummond emphasizing the importance of trust relationships in filtering communications. The discussion highlighted their seven-year development of a decentralized trust stack that could address these issues, with Drummond noting they were ahead of the curve compared to other proposals in the market. John raised questions about governance in the context of AI, while the group also discussed the political timing of pro-human AI discussions ahead of U.S. midterm elections and global concerns about data center environmental impact.

### AI Trust Infrastructure Challenges

The group discussed the current state of AI infrastructure and trust, with Drummond noting that while there's growing demand for trustworthy AI systems, major tech companies like Google and Microsoft are not actively participating in decentralized trust initiatives due to self-interest in maintaining control over their platforms. John mentioned that Microsoft previously had a team working on verifiable credentials but dismantled it, while Drummond observed that governments committed to democracy might be more supportive of decentralized trust systems. The discussion concluded with recognition that while progress is being made, the challenge of capital distribution around AI remains a key concern rather than the technology itself.
