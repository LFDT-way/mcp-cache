## Overview

The Trust Over IP Steering Committee met on March 18th to discuss several key topics including the scheduling of quarterly all-member meetings, updates on specification approval processes, and collaboration opportunities with the Linux Foundation Technical Advisory Committee (TAC). The group decided to move from quarterly to semi-annual all-member meetings, with the next meeting scheduled for April 15th to align with IIW and the Carrie conference. Carly announced that the Carrie specifications have been approved and received DOIs, while Darrell reported progress on the Trust Registry Query Protocol (TRQP) specification which is ready for review. Arun from the TAC discussed potential collaboration opportunities between Trust Over IP and other LFDT projects, suggesting regular check-ins to identify synergies and areas for joint work. Steve provided an update on Utah's state-endorsed digital identity bill (SB275) which passed unanimously and creates new requirements for government systems to accept digital identity credentials, including SETI. The conversation ended with discussions about the Global Digital Collaboration (GDC) event in Geneva and how to properly reference the newly approved Carrie specifications.

## Details

### Trust RIP Steering Committee Meeting

The steering committee meeting for Trust RIP began with roll call and a reminder to share the antitrust policy notice. Carly informed the group that the URN for Carrie Spech had been accepted by IATA, which Karla noted she would share with her team. Drummond, who was joining from a coffee shop, introduced the agenda and mentioned that Aaron would speak about the LFDT Technical Advisory Committee, though his timing was unclear. The meeting was set to start four minutes after the hour.

### Semi-Annual Meeting Frequency Change

The team discussed changing the frequency of all-member quarterly meetings from quarterly to semi-annual, aligning them with IIW events. Carly proposed holding the next meeting before the April 28-30 IIW event, with Drummond and Daniela offering to help coordinate logistics. The group agreed to focus on presenting significant updates and news from working groups rather than requiring every group to provide a report. Nicholas volunteered to help organize the meeting, and Carly mentioned she had updated the membership mailing list to about 80-90% accuracy.

### All-Members Meeting Scheduling Discussion

The group discussed scheduling an all-members meeting for April 15th at 10am PST, with Drummond and Martina confirming the time works for them. Karla and Nico were tasked with checking with the working group chairs, including Sam, Phil, and Kevin, to confirm the date works for them. The meeting will focus on showcasing the Carry suite and trust graph, with an invitation for other working groups to present as well.

### LFTT-Trust Over IP Collaboration

Arun proposed collaboration between LFTT and Trust Over IP, suggesting ways to support ongoing work and demonstrating Trust Over IP's value. Drummond and Carly agreed on the importance of identifying overlaps and synergies between LFTT projects, and suggested scheduling a meeting to discuss potential collaborations. Arun expressed interest in learning about other LFTT projects and their use cases, and proposed setting up regular check-ins to measure progress.

### Tax Channel and TAC Updates

The meeting focused on updates and discussions related to the tax channel, the Technical Advisory Council (TAC) annual report, and the specification approval process. Drummond emphasized the importance of scheduling exploratory meetings and highlighted the availability of the tax channel on Discord or the Steering Committee channel for discussions. Arun and David discussed feedback on challenges within the framework and the TAC annual report, with Drummond offering to coordinate and complete the report with Carly's help. Carly explained ongoing work to refine the specification approval process, which will be tested with the Carry specs and TRQP, and she plans to submit the updated process document to the Steering Committee for approval next month. The conversation ended with updates on the progress of the Carry specs and an open question about the next step for the blog post.

### Blog Post Planning and Updates

The group discussed plans for a blog post about ACDC, Caesar, and Cary's importance, with Nicholas seeking feedback on whether to include real-life implementation examples like VLEI and GSMA Open Verifiable Calling Project. Drummond suggested involving LFDT staff for guidance and emphasized highlighting real-world applications, while Eric agreed that practical examples would be beneficial. The team decided to aim for the blog post to be ready by the All Members meeting, potentially even before it, and to promote it as part of the meeting's advertising. Carly and Darrell then provided an update on the Trust Registry Query Protocol, noting its submission for committee review and approval, with Darrell highlighting the positive impact of the review process on the specification's clarity and organization.

### Trust Registry Protocol Review Approval

The committee approved Darrell and the Trust Registry Query Protocol V2 submission for review, with Darrell committing to provide necessary documents by the end of the day or tomorrow. Carly confirmed that the review committee needs to complete paperwork two weeks before the next meeting to allow time for committee review and approval at the April 15th Steering Committee meeting. The group discussed scheduling conflicts between the all-member meeting and the Steering Committee meeting, deciding to move the all-member meeting one hour earlier to accommodate both events within the same day.

### Global Digital Conference Planning Discussion

The group discussed the Global Digital Conference (GDC) scheduled for September 1-3 in Geneva, Switzerland, and its potential overlap with Identity Week America. Daniela requested support for Trust over IP community participation, including session nominations and attendance. Drummond and others expressed interest in supporting the event, with First Person Cooperative planning to attend and potentially help schedule sessions. The group also touched on recent successful sessions at Summit Freeman Agency and LF Decentralized Trust, with plans to continue developing specs and code. Finally, they discussed the target date for Linux Foundation's October meeting in Prague, aiming to present mature implementations by then.

### Digital Identity Bill Passage Update

Steve discussed the passage of Utah's state-endorsed Digital Identity Bill (SB275), which creates a user-centric digital identity framework. The bill, which passed unanimously through the legislature, will require SETI (State Endorsed Trust Infrastructure) for new government systems and healthcare organizations receiving over $10 million in public funding. Steve also mentioned the upcoming SETI summit on April 20-21, which will focus on engaging other states and public policymakers, though there was some discussion about scheduling overlap with the Cary Con. Carly announced the successful passing of the URN specification for Trust over IP, noting that LinkedIn is already using a URN in their URL, and Daniela agreed to provide guidance on how to refer to the specification going forward.
