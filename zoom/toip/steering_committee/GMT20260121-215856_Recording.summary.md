## Details

### Linux Foundation Steering Committee Meeting

The steering committee meeting began with introductions and a discussion about the Linux Foundation's training materials and GitHub usage for open standards development. Drummond led the meeting, noting that attendees have a license to eat, drink, or engage in other activities during the call. The agenda included reviewing the antitrust policy, discussing the ToIP approved deliverable process forms, and considering nominations for the steering committee. The group also planned to address annual and quarterly reports, prepare for the LFD Member Summit, and discuss the EU Digital Sovereignty Initiative and an upcoming conference on consumer agency.

### Deliverable Approval Process Updates

The team reviewed and discussed the forms and process for approving deliverables, with Carly explaining adjustments to the approved deliverable process and introducing a new request for initiation form. Scott and Carly's work on Google Forms for checklists and reports was acknowledged, and the group agreed these forms could be used without a separate vote since they implement the overall process already approved. The conversation ended with Drummond expressing gratitude for the team's efforts and noting that other specifications would follow this process in the coming year.

### Cary Suite Review Process Discussion

The team discussed the review process for the Cary Suite Working Group specifications. Nicholas presented a report summarizing the review of three specifications: Cary, ACDC, and CSER, all at version 1.0. They clarified the roles of steering committee members versus review committee members, with Karla and Nicholas confirmed as co-chairs of the review committee. The group agreed that while the working group had formally approved the specs, they would document in the report that no external authorities were consulted during this review phase, as per the checklist requirements.

### Review Committee Labeling Discussion

The group discussed how to label members of the review committee, confirming that Steering Committee members who serve as co-chairs should be labeled as both Steering Committee members and co-chairs of the review committee. They agreed to investigate whether the process document mandates including non-Steering Committee members, with Drummond suggesting they could waive this requirement given they are developing the process. Steve McCown offered to provide technical review despite not being part of the Cary Working Group, and the group discussed where to record these reports, with Drummond suggesting both meeting minutes and GitHub would be appropriate locations.

### Cary Suite Specifications Approval

The Steering Committee approved the Cary Suite technical specifications, marking the first Trust RAP approved deliverable specifications in their 5.5-year history. Steve made the motion to approve, which Drummond seconded, and after checking for objections and abstentions, the approval was confirmed. The team discussed how to publicly announce the approval, with Nicholas offering to have his team handle media communications, and Daniela's team will handle publishing the announcement on the blog and website.

### Trust Over IP Governance Overview

The meeting provided an overview of the Trust Over IP project's structure and governance, highlighting its transition under the Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust umbrella. Daniela explained the project's membership and Steering Committee processes, emphasizing that membership is open to all organizations, and clarified that Steering Committee members can be elected rather than requiring paid membership. Drummond discussed the current nomination process, noting 17 candidates, and clarified that proxies can be appointed to attend and vote on behalf of members. Eva expressed interest in joining the Steering Committee and confirmed her preference for both herself and her proxy, Karen, to participate, aligning with Scott's suggestion to ensure balanced representation from their company. The conversation ended with a call for brief confirmations from nominees, prioritizing efficiency while completing the nomination process.

### Steering Committee New Member Welcome

The meeting focused on welcoming new members to the Steering Committee, including Alan Davies, Makki, and Martina, who expressed their interest and commitment to contributing to the committee's goals. Drummond provided an update on the current membership, noting that while some members like Eric and John Phillips were absent, others like Alex were unresponsive. The discussion also touched on the importance of global collaboration and diversity in perspectives, with Martina highlighting her role in the Decentralized Trust Working Group. The conversation ended with a confirmation that all nominated members were interested in continuing their roles in the 2026 Steering Committee, bringing the total to 17 members.

### Steering Committee Expansion and Nominations

The Steering Committee agreed to increase its size to 16 members, with Carly seconding the motion. Daniela confirmed that all new members must have a contributor license in place. Drummond announced that the First Worse Cooperative and Affinity are joining as general and member members, respectively. The committee then nominated 16 people, including existing members and three new ones, as the Steering Committee for 2026, with Steve seconding the motion. Nicholas expressed curiosity about potential future involvement from the trust graph team, to which Drummond responded optimistically but noted that current members have enough on their plates.
