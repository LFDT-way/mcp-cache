## Overview

The Trust Over IP Steering Committee met to discuss several topics, including the formal closure of the inactive Utility Foundry Working Group, which was approved without objection. Diane from the Linux Foundation presented an updated format for the annual report, emphasizing the need for evidence of progress and community participation, and the committee agreed to adopt this approach. The group also discussed the upcoming TAC elections and the importance of having a Trust Over IP representative. The AI contribution policy was reviewed, with a focus on the contentious Section 2.2 regarding disclosure of AI assistance. Updates were provided on various projects, such as the Global Digital Conference (GDC) submissions, the Decentralized Trust Graph, and the Trust Registry Task Force. The committee decided to take a sabbatical in August, with the next meeting scheduled for September.

## Details

### Utility Foundry Working Group Closure

The steering committee formally approved closing the inactive Utility Foundry Working Group, which had not been active for several years. Diane, a Linux Foundation veteran, presented feedback on the annual report, explaining that she enhanced it with additional footnotes and context to make it more useful as a wayfinding document for TAC members who may not be familiar with Trust Over IP work. The meeting included casual discussion about World Cup soccer, with participants discussing upcoming games and tournament structure.

### Annual Report Template Discussion

Diane presented a detailed template for annual reports that includes metrics, evidence of progress, and supporting references, emphasizing the need for concrete data rather than generic updates. Carly suggested creating a skills markdown file for ChatGPT to streamline report generation and align it with the group's existing meeting cadence, including their two annual general meetings. The team discussed using new technologies for reporting, with Nicholas proposing the creation of cryptographic artifacts to ensure content authenticity, which Diane expressed interest in exploring further.

### TAC Meeting and Reporting Updates

Drummond discussed attendance challenges at TAC meetings and expressed interest in having someone from Trust River IP participate, with David confirming that nominations for next year's TAC will open in early fall. Diane requested that someone review and resubmit the current annual report in the correct format to get it approved at tomorrow's meeting, with Drummond and Carly agreeing to handle this task. The conversation ended with Drummond asking about potential collaboration between LF Decentralized Trust and other Linux Foundation AI interoperability efforts, particularly regarding agentic identity work.

### Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust Coordination

The group discussed the Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust (LFDT) and its role in coordinating with other communities across the open source ecosystem. Diane emphasized the need for better coordination of multiple identity-related initiatives, noting that it's challenging to track and participate in various working groups across different foundations due to resource constraints and overlapping efforts. The discussion highlighted the potential for LFDT to serve as a coordinating mechanism, though Diane expressed doubt that it would become the umbrella organization for all identity initiatives under the Linux Foundation.

### TAC Meeting Planning Discussion

Drummond and Carly agreed to prepare a report for the TAC meeting, with Drummond committing to attend at least the second half if possible. Diane discussed the potential for creating an Identity Working Group under LFDT to analyze the self-sovereign identity space and gather requirements from existing projects. Carly inquired about setting up a task force for skills.md and reporting, with Diane suggesting it would fall under the Governance umbrella, and Drummond offering to help organize it informally under working groups. The conversation ended with a discussion about AI contribution policies, noting that while DIF had issued a policy, the state of play at the TAC level needed clarification.

### HIRO Community Guidelines Discussion

Diane discussed the HIRO community's position on section 2.2 of AI-assisted development guidelines, explaining that the community generally agrees the section should be removed as it's ineffective and could be misused by companies. She noted that while the TAC hasn't voted on the guidelines yet, there's general consensus that the rest of the document is acceptable. David encouraged participants to share their input during the current review period, emphasizing that this is an opportunity for community feedback before any formal vote takes place.

### AI Interaction Guidelines Discussion

The group discussed guidelines for AI interactions in discussions, with Martina highlighting concerns about AI-generated content being copied and pasted, creating noise and making moderation difficult. Drummond noted that DIF policy prohibits AI-based submissions due to IPR concerns. The team agreed to add comments to the AI guidelines document, with Drummond offering to submit Martina's feedback if she couldn't do it herself. The conversation ended with plans to review a Trust Over IP report at the next day's tech meeting.

### Balazs Endorsement Decision Meeting

The group decided not to provide an endorsement to Balazs due to his failure to address governance concerns regarding his community-based domain application. Drummond noted that Balazs's deadline is August 12th and expressed concern about his lack of response despite being asked for more details about his proposed governance structure. The group also discussed potentially taking a sabbatical in August, with no strong objections raised, though they noted this would affect preparation time for an upcoming GDC meeting.

### GDC Prep and Agenda Updates

Drummond reported on the GDC prep calls and shared details about the updated three-day agenda structure, including the public session spreadsheet where submissions are being tracked. He explained that submissions require two officially recognized co-organizers to qualify, with 142 submissions currently made, and noted that sessions missing proper co-organizers are marked in red. Drummond confirmed that three Trust Over IP-related submissions have been successfully made, including sessions on decentralized trust graphs, keyring, and fiduciary AI, with potential speakers like Richard Witt now confirmed for the fiduciary AI session.

### Collaboration and Conference Planning

Drummond reported positive discussions with Ivan about collaboration opportunities and mentioned plans to connect with Chris Bramwell regarding AI fiduciary topics that align with Utah's SB 275 data loyalty legislation. The team confirmed several members including Drummond, Scott, and others will attend the upcoming conference in Switzerland, with at least four submissions from the First Person Cooperative team. Drummond indicated they may organize a First Person meetup the day before the conference and mentioned potential coverage of expenses for attendees.

### Trust Over IP Project Updates

The meeting focused on updates regarding Trust Over IP and related projects. Scott shared progress on the Trust Registry Task Force, which is working on governance and ecosystem trust, including the use of public certificates and governance-backed assertions. Drummond provided updates on the decentralized trust graph protocol, noting its adoption by OpenVTC and an upcoming presentation at the Linux Plumber Summit. The group discussed upcoming events, including sessions at GDC and the IETF, and confirmed that the next Trust Over IP meeting would be in September after the August hiatus.
