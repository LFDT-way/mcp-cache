## Details

### YouTube Recording Policy Discussion

The meeting focused on discussing the recording and uploading of Steering Committee meetings to YouTube. John noticed a new process where meetings were automatically uploaded to YouTube, which sparked a discussion about whether this was appropriate for Steering Committee meetings. The group agreed that the decision to record and upload meetings should be made by the Steering Committee, with Carly suggesting this could be addressed as a formal decision. Daniela offered to pause the recording for the current meeting and remove the YouTube upload, which the group approved. The Steering Committee was tasked with deciding on the future recording and uploading policy for their meetings.

### Specification Governance Process Review

The meeting focused on establishing a governance process for specification approval, particularly for the Carry specification. The group agreed to record and publish meetings by default, unless otherwise requested for off-camera discussions. They discussed the need for antitrust disclosures and voting restrictions in Steering Committee meetings. Carly presented a document outlining the specification approval process, which includes an external review committee chaired by a Steering Committee member. The group also touched on web page cleanup, GitHub cleanup, and continuing support for Confluence. Finally, they briefly discussed a third-person generation diagram and symposium follow-up items.

### Document Approval Process Reforms

The group discussed the approval process for documents, particularly the Issuer Requirements Guide, which was approved by a working group but not by the Steering Committee. Karla suggested that the Steering Committee review should be separate from the technical review by working groups, with external experts providing input after internal consensus is reached. Makki proposed a structured process involving draft production by co-chairs, working group consensus, cross-working group review, and finally Steering Committee approval, with the possibility of external expert feedback if needed.

### Working Group Review Process

The group discussed the review process for working group specifications before they reach the Steering Committee. Makki suggested that working groups should get internal consensus before proceeding, with an optional external review step managed through a simple process like a Google form. John emphasized that the Steering Committee should have the right to request additional reviews if needed, though the group agreed to avoid over-specifying the process. Karla and Carly proposed a stepped review process where working groups would complete their draft, invite other working groups and external experts for feedback, incorporate comments, and then seek final approval from the Steering Committee, with public review remaining an optional final step.

### Working Group Review Documentation Standards

The group discussed the need for a more detailed documentation of the process for working group reviews, including requirements for public review and submission to the Steering Committee. Drummond proposed creating a subcommittee to complete this documentation, with a focus on standardizing the process using GitHub. Karla suggested adding an extra review step for feedback from other working groups and external experts. The discussion also touched on the need to resolve all feedback received during public reviews, as per W3C requirements.

### Specification Approval Process Simplification

The group discussed the process for reviewing and approving specifications by the Steering Committee. Scott proposed a light-touch approval process where working groups must complete a checklist before presenting to the Steering Committee, with a sponsor from the committee providing oversight. Drummond suggested forming a committee of volunteers to quickly prepare and communicate the checklist to the working group, emphasizing the need to avoid creating an onerous process. The group agreed to finalize the checklist and present it to the Steering Committee for approval, establishing clear expectations for future specification approvals.

### Carrie Stack Working Group Checklist

The group discussed the creation of a checklist document for the Carrie Stack Working Group, with Drummond proposing a timeline for completion by the end of next week. Scott clarified that the checklist should cover two main categories: conformance to the JDF process and adherence to the housekeeping and style guide, including look and feel. Drummond suggested adding a third category to ensure mission alignment between the working group and TrustRIP. The team agreed to volunteer their time to help complete the checklist, despite some members being in Frankfurt for the week.

### Technical Specifications Review Process

The steering committee discussed the process for reviewing and approving technical specifications, with Drummond proposing a four-person committee to evaluate specifications against mission alignment. The group agreed to aim for steering committee approval of the first technical specifications by December 5th, which would be announced in January following the symposium, with Karla and Nicholas supporting the timeline. They also discussed the need for consistency across implementations, with Nicholas suggesting alignment on concepts like registries and glossary terms, which Karla noted would already be addressed in the Carrie Suite specs.

### Trust River IP Webpage Update

Carly proposed leading a committee to update and streamline the Trust River IP webpage by collaborating with the Linux Foundation, and several Steering Committee members expressed support. The group decided to continue using Confluence for Trust River IP due to its heavy usage and relatively low cost compared to GitHub, despite Rye's suggestion to switch to GitHub. Carly agreed to reach out to Ben Thomas at the Linux Foundation to initiate the webpage simplification project, and the Steering Committee agreed to invite other community volunteers to assist with the effort.
