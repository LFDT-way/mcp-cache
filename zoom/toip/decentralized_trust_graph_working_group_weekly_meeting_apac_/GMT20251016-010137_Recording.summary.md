## Details

### UNCFAC Trust Registry Project Discussion

John and Drummond discussed the cancellation of a planned meeting and instead had a separate discussion about the UNCFAC Global Trust Registry project, which involves similar challenges to those faced in other parts of the world. They also talked about a new automatic meeting minutes feature provided by the Linux Foundation, which generates summaries but lacks editing options. John expressed frustration with the inability to edit the generated summary and described his workaround of downloading the transcript and using his own Notebook LM instance to create custom meeting minutes, which he then plans to copy and paste into a Confluence wiki page for further editing.

### Travel Experiences and Future Plans

Drummond and John discussed their experiences with travel and living abroad, with Drummond mentioning his son's house-sitting opportunity in Hawaii and John recounting his time working in London and later moving to New Zealand. They briefly touched on the beauty of New Zealand and John's recommendation to visit, as well as Drummond's interest in planning a trip there in the future.

### Task Forces for Digital Credentials

Drummond shared positive updates about funding progress and mentioned a recent meeting where new participants from the Kwai open-source AI project and Now Genesis joined. He discussed plans to form task forces on credentials and digital cards, driven by the need to develop early versions of PHCs and VRCs for the Linux Foundation kernel implementation and experiments with the card exchange by Harvard Applied Social Media. Drummond committed to drafting charters for these task forces and will distribute a survey and article to the first-person mailing list for feedback.

### Task Force Collaboration Decision

Drummond explained that Scott and Nikki discussed their different approaches to risk assessment and threats prevention, with the possibility of either working together in one task force or separately in two. John expressed a preference for a single task force to benefit from diverse perspectives, warning against the risks of separate task forces leading to disjointed recommendations. Drummond acknowledged this concern but decided not to push either approach, leaving it to Scott and Nikki to decide within the next two weeks.

### First-Person Trust in Linux Kernel

John and Drummond discussed the progress of the first-person project and its potential to address trust challenges in the Linux kernel. They highlighted the collaboration with Jeff Turek, Glenn Gore, and the ZKP team at Berkeley, aiming to replace the GPG web of trust with first-person credentials. They also mentioned a milestone event scheduled for February 23-25 in Napa, where they plan to showcase the progress of the kernel project. John noted a parallel initiative by an Australian group called Astro Sync, which is working on similar trust challenges, and suggested directing them to the first-person project for insights.

### AI Agent Identity Management Initiatives

The group discussed various initiatives around AI agent identity management, including HCS14, NANDA, and the Loyal Agents coalition, which are all working on similar challenges. They noted that multiple companies and platforms are developing their own solutions for agent identification and trust scoring, but there's a lack of interoperability between these systems. The discussion highlighted that while these initiatives are focused on AI agents, the underlying identity and trust infrastructure could eventually be applied to other scenarios including people and devices.

### Trust Over IP Diagram Updates

John and Drummond discussed changes to Trust over IP foundation diagrams, where they reversed the direction of arrows to better reflect information flow between ecosystems and Trust over IP. They confirmed that the updated diagrams have been posted to Discord and need to be propagated through relevant foundational documents, with Drummond offering to handle this task. The discussion also touched on an IETF RFC about time zones and actions from a recent Graph meeting, with Scott Perry identified as the person who should follow up on the diagram updates.

### Symposium Planning and AI Discussion

Drummond and John discussed the upcoming symposium, where John will deliver a 30-minute talk on the TrustRIP stack, which will be recorded and used as a reference for others. They also talked about AI's impact on coding, noting that despite expectations, AI has not significantly improved coding speed. John shared an article from The Economist warning about a potential AI bubble that could result in a $35 trillion loss in wealth, which Drummond found interesting. They briefly mentioned the need to finalize the symposium schedule, which Drummond said would likely be set in about two weeks once all talks and speakers are confirmed.
